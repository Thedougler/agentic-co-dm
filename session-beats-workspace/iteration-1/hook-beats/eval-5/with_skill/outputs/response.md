# The War Party Strikes at Coldbrook

**Something happens.** In the dead of night, the Crimson Thorn war band launches a coordinated ambush on the party's camp at Coldbrook Hollow. Arrows fly from the tree line to the north. Shouts erupt from the southern perimeter. Fire blooms across the eastern supply tents. The attack is sudden, organized, and designed to fragment the party.

**Why it matters.** The Crimson Thorn has been tracking the party for three days. They move with purpose under Captain Vorath's command. The camp is surrounded on three sides. Coldbrook's only clear escape route runs west along the river valley, but it's exposed. Supplies are burning. The perimeter guards are overwhelmed. Every choice now determines whether the party reggroups, scatters, or stands and fights.

**Decision.** The party must decide in the opening seconds: defend the camp and hold their ground, evacuate along the western route and abandon supplies, attempt a break-out to scatter the Crimson Thorn, or something else entirely. There is no safe play here—only costs of different shapes.

**Hook lands when.** The Hook lands when the party commits to a direction (stand, flee, fight, parley) and begins moving. From that moment, the chase is on. Vorath will exploit their choice.

> [!narration] Open on
> The night shatters with war cries. Arrows rake through your camp with the coordination of practiced archers—dozens of shafts arc from the dark trees north of your position, finding bedrolls and canvas. The southern perimeter flares with movement: figures in ash-grey scale armor, bearing the crimson thorn symbol, press toward your lines. Behind them, the eastern cache ignites, orange flames licking upward, the smell of burning canvas and oil cutting through the cold night air. Smoke rolls across the camp. Above the roar, a voice—hard and commanding—calls out in the common tongue: "Stand or burn! Captain Vorath of the Crimson Thorn takes what you have. Choose your death or your escape—now!" The river to your west is visible in the firelight, cold and moving fast. The northern tree line where the arrows came from sits perhaps two hundred feet of open ground away. Your people are scrambling, some reaching for weapons, others moving toward packhorses and supply. What do you do?

## Situation

- **Where.** Coldbrook Hollow—a bend in the river valley with a central campfire surrounded by canvas shelters, supply tents to the east, weapons storage near the fire. The Crimson Thorn archers are positioned in the northern tree line (200 feet distant, elevated slightly). Mounted warriors circle south and southwest, cutting off that approach. The river to the west flows fast and is perhaps 80 feet away—cold water, moderately deep, but passable with difficulty. The eastern fire is spreading deliberately across the supply tents; it's not accidental.

- **Who.** 
  - **Captain Vorath** (leader of the Crimson Thorn, mounted on a grey mare, center of the southern advance) — wants to scatter the party and take their supplies, particularly a chest known to contain maps and a signet ring. He believes the party stole from the war band three days ago. He has orders from the Crimson Thorn hierarchy to "recover the theft or eliminate the thieves."
  - **Crimson Thorn warriors** — approximately 12-15 mounted fighters on the southern approach, 8-10 archers in the northern tree line with a spotter. Disciplined but not fanatically loyal; they're mercenaries following Vorath's lead.
  - **Your camp sentries** — 3-4 guards on perimeter duty; they are under immediate pressure and will hold for one round of combat before falling back or falling.
  - **Non-combatants** (if relevant to the party) — camp followers, animals, servants are scattering. They are not reliable in combat and present rescue/protection targets.

- **What changed.** The Crimson Thorn has closed the tracking distance and committed to a dawn raid. They have intel about the camp layout and guard rotation (suggesting a spy in the party's supply chain or a scout). The fire is deliberately set to deny supplies and drive panic, not to kill—it's a pressure tactic.

- **Pressure.** The camp is on fire. The archers will continue suppressive fire. The mounted warriors will close and attempt to break the party's formation or drive them toward the fire. Vorath intends to press the advantage for 5-7 minutes, then withdraw if the party coalesces or shows organized resistance. If the party scatters, he will pursue the chest-bearer and pick off stragglers. The perimeter is thin and will collapse if not reinforced.

- **Open question.** Does the party stand and fight, breaking Vorath's advance? Do they evacuate west and into the river, abandoning supplies and cover? Do they attempt to ambush Vorath's warriors and turn the fight? Do they negotiate, scatter on purpose to deceive, or try something else? None of these choices is predetermined to succeed or fail.

If they didn't, this is a case of mistaken identity or being framed. | Any PC with ties to fire or survival | The burning camp is immediate sensory stakes. A PC who fears fire, who has survival skills, or who was burned in the past feels the personal weight of the flames. |

## Decision handles

- **Hold and defend.** Rally the perimeter, gather fighters, present organized resistance to Vorath's warriors. This is costly but buys time and shows strength. Defend the camp stores if possible, or at least deny them to the Crimson Thorn.
- **Evacuate to the west.** Organize a retreat toward the river. This abandons the supplies and the camp but saves lives and preserves the party as a unit. It's a clear choice with clear costs.
- **Split the party.** Some defend, some evacuate. This is higher risk—each faction is smaller—but allows the party to attempt to preserve both people and some supplies.
- **Attack Vorath directly.** Commit warriors or magic to break the southern line or target Vorath himself. This is aggressive and could scatter the Crimson Thorn if it succeeds, but failure means full combat.
- **Parley or offer terms.** Propose a ransom, a duel, a trade, or a contest to settle the claim without bloodshed. Vorath will listen, but he will not accept humiliation. His answer depends on the offer.
- **Set a counter-ambush.** If the party has prepared positions or reserves, spring a trap on the Crimson Thorn while they press. This is only viable if the party discovered the threat before the attack (which they have not).
- **Scatter deliberately.** Break formation on purpose to confuse Vorath's targeting, then regroup elsewhere. This risks separation but can undermine the ambush's effectiveness.

## Action setup

- **Objective (Crimson Thorn).** Scatter or suppress the party; recover the chest containing the ring and maps; withdraw with minimal casualties. Vorath is not trying to annihilate; he's trying to win quickly and leave.

- **Objective (Party).** Survive; protect non-combatants if applicable; preserve supplies or escape with key gear; maintain unit cohesion; or kill/capture Vorath (if choosing direct conflict).

- **Opposition.** 
  - Mounted southern force: 12-15 armored warriors (Vorath + henchmen/minions, depending on difficulty preference). Lightly armored but mounted, giving them mobility. Led by Captain Vorath (equal to party strength or slightly above).
  - Northern archery: 8-10 archers (minions to grunts), positioned 200 feet away in the tree line. They provide suppressive fire and prevent rallying in the open.
  - Total force scales with party size and resources. Suggest: Grunts (3:1 weaker) for a dawn raid that's meant to scatter, not a guaranteed TPK. Minions (2:1 slightly weaker) if Vorath has fought this party before and is taking it seriously.

- **Terrain.** 
  - Central campfire (light and heat source, creates a rallying point but draws archer fire).
  - Eastern supply tents (now burning; smoke obscures sight lines but limits safe movement).
  - Northern tree line, elevated (archer position, 200 feet out, provides cover).
  - River to the west (escape route but cold, swift water; terrain hazard for unprepared crossers).
  - Slight elevation to the south where Vorath's mounted warriors are charging from (advantage for their charge, disadvantage if the party moves uphill to break their momentum).

- **Escalation.** 
  - **Turn 1–2:** Initial volley of arrows, mounted charge opens. Perimeter guards fall back or die. Fire spreads eastward.
  - **Turn 3–4:** If the party stands, they're engaged in close combat with warriors. If they flee, they're moving toward the river or scattering. If they counter-attack, the battle becomes contested.
  - **Turn 5–7:** Vorath assesses. If the party is cohesive and fighting effectively, he withdraws in good order. If the party is scattered, he pursues the chest-bearer. If the party is fleeing, he pursues but does not close to final combat (he's running a raid, not a war).

- **End condition.** 
  - Vorath withdraws with or without the chest.
  - The party breaks contact and reaches the river or a defensible position.
  - A formal parley or contest settles the immediate conflict.
  - The party is scattered, and individual survival becomes the goal for the next beat.
  - Vorath is killed or captured (unlikely but possible; if so, his warriors break and flee).

## Handoff

**Next.** Session-[#]-Cliffhanger-Chase. **Cliffhanger (Chase).** The party's choice has committed them to a direction: standing and defending, fleeing to the river, scattering, or fighting Vorath directly. The moment the party moves toward that goal, the static ambush becomes a kinetic chase through the burning camp and the surrounding terrain. Fire spreads. Vorath's warriors pursue or reposition. The party's next beat is not safety—it's a test of the route they chose, the escape they attempted, or the stand they made.

**Carry forward.** 
- The immediate threat (Vorath, the Crimson Thorn warriors).
- The burning camp and the destruction of supplies.
- The question of the stolen ring and maps—who took them, and why Vorath believes the party has them.
- The spy or scout who revealed the camp's layout—a live lead for investigation.
- The party's state: wounded, scattered, cold from the river, or in combat stance. This shapes what they can do in the Chase.

**Continuity change.** Coldbrook camp is no longer a safe haven; it is destroyed or abandoned. The party's supply situation changes radically. They are now actively hunted by the Crimson Thorn and must either resolve the ring theft, escape the pursuit, or turn and fight. Vorath is no longer an abstract threat—he is a named opponent with known goals and a visible force behind him.
