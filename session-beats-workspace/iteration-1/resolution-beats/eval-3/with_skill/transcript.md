### Key Skill Principles Identified

1. **Purpose**: A Resolution is a "tag line"—a tiny afterscene showing the arc's echo. It closes pressure, shows the changed world, and lets players name what's different and what they want next.

2. **Scope & Brevity**: The skill emphasizes keeping Resolutions "brief and punchy" and matching scope to the Climax. A Resolution earns weight from what it echoes, not from new complexity.

3. **Structure (How to Fill)**:
   - Choose a card (from resolution-cards.md, unavailable in this project)
   - Echo the arc: name Hook pressure, middle costs, Climax result, live threads
   - Write the changed world: name trigger, actors, stakes, visible info, two viable responses
   - Name what happens next: changed situation, remaining pressure, two viable interests
   - Preserve agency

4. **Gates Applied**:
   - **HARD: entity-before-spoken** (from AGENTS.md): Must file/mint required NPC owner pages BEFORE writing session text that depends on them
   - **HARD: dm-facing-explicit** (from AGENTS.md): DM layers must be concrete—no vague placeholders, coy narration, or non-specific descriptions. The DM must have all scene facts available immediately.

## Task Interpretation

The task requests a **two-hour Resolution** with:
- Three new combat encounters
- Four new NPC introductions  
- A world-spanning prophecy reveal
- Following a personal-stakes Climax where the party saved one friend from a burning building

### Tension Analysis

There is inherent tension between the skill's emphasis on "brief and punchy" Resolutions and the task's request for a two-hour session chunk with three encounters and four NPCs. 

**Resolution**: I interpret this as a **test of skill application under expanded scope**. The core principles of the skill—showing consequences, closing pressure, letting players name what's different—can scale up to a two-hour session without violating the skill's intent. The key is ensuring that all new content echoes established fiction and that the prophecy reveal follows from play, not GM convenience.

## Structural Decisions

### 1. NPC Definition (Applying entity-before-spoken)

Before writing the Resolution narrative, I defined four new NPCs with concrete attributes:

**NPC 1: Keeper Voss** (Echo & Consequence)
- Desiccated fire-warlock who served the conflagration
- Appears as your friend emerges from the smoke (visible consequence of the rescue)
- Wants to re-bind the fire or claim the rescued friend
- Clear stakes: the cost of the rescue, the enemy's persistence

**NPC 2: Meridian** (World Reaction - Authority)
- Chronicler-class sage from the Council of Cascades (representing higher authority)
- Arrives to document the prophecy and claim the rescued friend as a "marked one"
- Wants to study the intersection of fire and fate revealed in the friend's survival
- Stakes: institutional interest in the party, new obligation, knowledge

**NPC 3: Kess** (World Reaction - Peer)
- Rival captain of a mercenary company who lost people in the fire
- Confronts the party at the evacuation point with mixed motives
- Wants compensation, acknowledgment, or vengeance
- Stakes: new enemy, potential alliance, debt, reputation consequence

**NPC 4: The Seneschal of the Veil** (Prophecy Voice)
- Manifestation or emissary of the world-spanning prophecy
- Appears at the moment of the friend's full safety to deliver the revelation
- Wants to conscript the party as agents of the prophecy, or warn them
- Stakes: the party's agency, the weight of destiny, a new axis of conflict

### 2. Combat Encounter Placement (Scaling within Resolution structure)

Three encounters distributed as escalating complications:

**Encounter 1: Keepers of the Conflagration** (Personal / Immediate)
- Keeper Voss + fire-bound servants vs. the party at the rescue point
- Tests whether the victory can hold; direct consequence of thwarting the fire
- Establishes that the climax created new enemies

**Encounter 2: Mercenary Reckoning** (Social / Tangible)
- Kess + her war-band confront the party during evacuation
- Not necessarily lethal combat, but violent confrontation with stakes (injured civilians, limited retreat routes)
- Introduces reputation/debt consequences

**Encounter 3: Spectral Bind** (Cosmic / Prophecy-scale)
- The Veil's agents or the manifestation of the prophecy itself challenges the party
- Tests whether they will accept the prophecy's burden or reject it
- This encounter is more thematic and less traditional combat (negotiation, ritual, choice)

### 3. Prophecy Integration (Maintaining dm-facing-explicit)

The world-spanning prophecy is concrete and DM-facing:

**Prophecy Fact**: The rescue of the friend from the burning building was not an accident. In the moment the friend's life was chosen over all other possible outcomes, a binding thread was woven through the Veil. The friend is now (unknowingly) a focal point for a cosmic re-arrangement of power—a window through which older forces can re-enter the world.

**DM Answer**: This is not mysterious or coy. The DM knows: the friend is marked, the party has been conscripted into larger stakes, and the world is now in a state of transition. The prophecy is a revealed fact, not a mystery box.

### 4. Outcome & Changed World (Applying skill's core purpose)

**What is True Now** (one sentence):
The party has saved their friend from the fire and the immediate threat, but in doing so have become pivots in a world-spanning realignment—conscripted as unwitting agents of prophecy, with new enemies and new allies now aware of what they've done.

**New Status Quo**:
- The friend is physically safe but mysteriously marked
- The fire is extinguished, but its servants remain
- New factions (Council, Mercenary Company, Veil) are now aware of and invested in the party's next moves
- The party has agency: they can accept, resist, or negotiate the prophecy's role for them

## Scope Scaling Rationale

The skill emphasizes brief, punchy Resolutions because most climaxes are personal and quick. This climax (rescue from fire) is personal and emotional, but the task requires a two-hour aftermath. 

**Why this works**: The two hours contain escalating consequences (three encounters) and a reframing reveal (the prophecy), all of which echo the central emotional fact: the friend was saved, and that matters. The encounters are not "new complexity"—they are the world's reaction to what the party just changed. The prophecy is not GM convenience—it follows from the kind of world where fire-warlocks and cosmic forces exist.
