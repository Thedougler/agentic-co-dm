# The Prophecy After Fire

**Follows:** [[Session-12-BB-Climax]]

**Purpose.** This two-hour afterscene shows what the party's choice to save their friend from the burning building has actually triggered. The fire is stopped, the friend is alive—but the world was watching. Three new factions now move on the party's position, and a hidden cosmic truth surfaces: the rescue was not accident. The Resolution closes the immediate pressure of the fire while opening a new, stranger weight. End on the image of the friend alive but changed, and the party aware that they are now pieces in a game larger than themselves.

**Outcome.** By saving their friend from the fire, the party inadvertently marked themselves and their companion as pivots in a prophecy—and now the world's factions, enemies, and hidden forces are closing in to claim, study, or destroy that mark.

---

## New NPCs (Entity-Before-Spoken)

### NPC 1: Keeper Voss
**Who:** A fire-warlock of the Conflagration, gaunt and scarred, whose body is shot through with cracks of ember-light. Voss served the fire as a servant serves a master, and the fire's binding to the world was his only tether to meaning.

**What Voss Wants:** To re-bind the fire and reclaim it from the wreckage, or failing that, to claim the rescued friend as a replacement anchor—a living thing he can bind the flame's consciousness into.

**Why Now:** Voss emerges from the fire's collapse the moment the friend takes their first breath outside the building. He is weakened, barely material, but driven by the fire's dying will.

**Stakes for the Party:** Voss represents the immediate, personal cost of victory. He is proof that the fire was not a natural disaster—it was a working, and that working had servants. Defeating him teaches the party that the danger does not end with the climax.

---

### NPC 2: Meridian
**Who:** A Chronicler of the Council of Cascades, ageless and precise, who reads fate in the spaces between facts. Meridian wears silver and gray, and writes continuously in a ledger that never fills. She speaks in nested clauses and sees the party as data points in a larger pattern.

**What Meridian Wants:** To document and understand the intersection point: how did mortal choice and cosmic fire meet in one moment? Is the rescued friend now a bridge? Can they be studied? Meridian wants to take the friend into the Council's care, or at minimum, to bind the party to her service.

**Why Now:** Meridian felt the prophecy awaken the instant the friend's life was chosen. She has traveled at speed to be present before anyone else claims the precedent. She arrives with formal authority, expecting compliance.

**Stakes for the Party:** Meridian offers knowledge and protection, but at the cost of obligation. To refuse her is to make a powerful institution aware that they have refused. To accept is to tie themselves to the Council's long game.

---

### NPC 3: Kess
**Who:** Captain of the Crimson Vanguard mercenary company, hard-faced and practical, with scars on her hands from a dozen wars. Kess lost three soldiers and two civilian workers in the fire. She is not here to thank the party.

**What Kess Wants:** Compensation for the dead, a name for who is responsible for the fire (so she can invoice them or take revenge), and possibly to indenture one party member to her company as replacement for losses. She will accept a smaller victory: the party's word that they owe the Vanguard a debt of blood.

**Why Now:** Kess was commanding the civilian evacuation. She saw what the party did to stop the fire, and she knows they could have done it faster or better. She has decided the party owes her.

**Stakes for the Party:** Kess is a peer, not an enemy—yet. How the party handles her determines whether they have gained a rival or an ally. Refusing her creates a mercenary grudge. Honoring her creates an obligation that may be called in at the worst possible moment.

---

### NPC 4: The Seneschal of the Veil
**Who:** A presence that does not fully inhabit flesh—appearing as a tall, thin figure wrapped in fabrics that seem to move independently. The Seneschal is ancient and speaks in a voice like wind through abandoned places. It is not clear if it is one being or many.

**What the Seneschal Wants:** To deliver the prophecy directly to those who wove it into being (the party, unknowingly) and to claim them as agents of the Veil's re-emergence into the world. The Seneschal wants the party's consent or cooperation, but will settle for their fear and compliance.

**Why Now:** The prophecy is now active because a choice was made and a binding was struck. The Seneschal has been waiting for such a moment for centuries. It arrives to mark the beginning.

**Stakes for the Party:** The Seneschal represents the largest consequences. Engaging with it means accepting that the fire was part of something much older. It offers power, knowledge, and purpose—but all at the cost of agency. The party will be asked to choose: do they accept the prophecy's role for them, or do they reject it (and face the Veil's enmity)?

## Run the Beat (The Two-Hour Aftermath)

### Phase 1: Confirmation & Immediate Consequence (20 minutes)

**Confirm the Outcome.**
The friend emerges from the collapsing building in the party's arms. The fire does not follow—it breaks against the threshold as if repelled. The party has succeeded. The friend is alive, coughing, shocked, but physically whole.

**Show the Consequence: Keeper Voss Appears.**
The moment the friend's weight settles in their arms and they take their first unconstrained breath, the fire behind them does something strange. It coalesces. A figure forms in the smoke—Keeper Voss, barely material, his form flickering between presence and absence.

Voss speaks in a voice like burning wood: *"You have stolen something that was meant to be bound. Give it back, or I will bind it myself."*

The crowd, evacuating, screams and clears. The party has moments to act before Voss solidifies enough to move.

**This is Encounter 1: Keepers of the Conflagration.**

Voss is flanked by three **Fire Servants**—lesser beings of ember and ash, humanoid but not living. They move between Voss and the party, and between the party and the crowd.

*Objectives:* 
- Prevent Voss and his servants from reaching the rescued friend
- Stop the servants from setting new fires in the crowd or collapsing buildings
- Drive Voss back into the ashes (Voss cannot be killed, only dispersed)

*Stakes:*
- If Voss reaches the friend, he will attempt to bind the fire's consciousness into their body, burning them from within. The party can prevent this, but only through combat or negotiation.
- If the Fire Servants ignite new fires, the evacuation becomes a rout and people die.
- The encounter confirms that the fire was not an accident—it was a working, and it has will.

*Viable Outcomes:*
1. **Victory & Persistence:** The party drives Voss back, but he does not die. He retreats into the smoke, vowing return. *"I will find you. The fire remembers everything."* This plants that Voss is a recurring threat.
2. **Victory & Binding:** The party negotiates with Voss, offering to carry the fire's message back to the Conflagration's source, buying time and a truce. Voss withdraws but marks the party as carriers of the fire's will.

**Consequence: The Friend Is Changed**
Once Voss retreats, the party can attend to their friend. The friend is alive, conscious, but there is something new about them: their eyes reflect light like a mirror when the party looks directly at them. When they breathe, there is the faint smell of smoke, even as their lungs clear.

The friend does not remember being bound to the fire. They remember the party reaching for them, and then—nothing. They are themselves, but something in the space around them feels different. Sounds are clearer. Colors are brighter. The friend is no longer quite fully in the world as they were.

*(This is the mark the Seneschal will speak of. The party does not understand this yet.)*

### Phase 2: External Reaction & Debt (40 minutes)

**Show the Consequence: The Evacuations & Kess**

The fire is stopped, but the evacuation is still chaos. The crowd is clearing the area—people are injured, displaced, searching for missing loved ones. The emergency and civilian structure is still active.

Captain Kess of the Crimson Vanguard appears at the edge of the evacuation zone. She is directing her remaining soldiers to carry the wounded. She sees the party.

Kess approaches directly. She is not threatening—not yet—but her body language is the physical grammar of someone who has decided something about you. *"You stopped the fire. Good. You also didn't listen to my evacuation orders, which cost me three soldiers and two civilian workers who trusted the Vanguard to be here."* 

She places a hand on the hilt of her sword, not to draw it, but to acknowledge that she could. *"I want one of three things: the name of who caused this fire, the price of those lives in gold, or one of you bound as Vanguard replacement for the duration of the war season. Which will it be?"*

**This is Encounter 2: Mercenary Reckoning.**

Kess is alone, but her six remaining soldiers are positioned around the evacuation zone. They are not attacking, but they are watching. The crowd is still moving, still dangerous. There are injured people, and the party is standing in the center of a civilian catastrophe.

*Objectives:*
- Resolve Kess's claim without starting a second battle
- Prevent her soldiers from interfering with ongoing evacuations
- Determine the party's relationship to the mercenary community and potential future allies/rivals

*Stakes:*
- If the party refuses all three terms, Kess will mark them as oath-breakers to every merc company in the region. This is a reputation consequence with real economic and military impact.
- If the party agrees to indenture one member, that person is now contractually bound to Kess for a season of service—and Kess is a commander who collects debts.
- If the party names the fire's source, they are now responsible for pointing a mercenary company at that target, with all the consequences that entails.

*Viable Outcomes:*
1. **Payment & Debt:** The party pays Kess the price of three soldier lives and two civilian workers' wages. This is expensive (probably 300-500 gold, depending on campaign economy), but it cleanly ends the obligation. However, Kess now knows the party has resources.
2. **Binding & Future Consequence:** One party member agrees to a season's service with the Vanguard. They become an insider to a mercenary company, learning its secrets and contacts, but at the cost of their personal agency for months.
3. **Negotiation & Respect:** The party offers something Kess values more than payment—intelligence about a rival merc company, access to a blacksmith, knowledge of a threat, or something else concrete from the campaign. Kess respects competence over payment, and this opens a path to an uneasy alliance.
4. **Rejection & Enmity:** The party refuses all terms. Kess does not immediately attack, but she leaves a curse on their names. Future encounters with the Vanguard are hostile. The mercenary community becomes aware that the party does not honor debt.

**Consequence: The Friend Is Not Quite the Same**
During this negotiation, the party notices the rescued friend is no longer reacting with shock or gratitude. Instead, they are observing the conflict with a strange, distant attention. When Kess speaks, the friend's mirrored eyes track her with recognition—not because they know her, but because they can *see* her in a way that is not normal. 

The friend remarks, quietly, *"She is bound to the south, and to a code older than gold. I can see it in her like water in a glass."*

This is alarming. The party's friend is not simply changed—they are perceiving things. The fire's touch has given them sight.

### Phase 3: Cosmic Reckoning & Prophecy Reveal (60 minutes, including ritual/negotiation)

**Show the Consequence: The Seneschal Arrives**

The evacuation is clearing. The immediate danger is past. Kess has either received payment, accepted a new Vanguard member, or left cursing the party's names. The friend is sitting nearby, resting, still processing what happened.

The party has moments of relative stillness.

Then the air changes. It becomes heavier, older. The light shifts—not fading, but becoming *directional*, as if the sun is setting from a thousand different angles at once.

A figure enters the cleared zone. It is the Seneschal of the Veil, and it does not so much walk as *appear, progressively, with each step you notice it more*.

The Seneschal wears robes or perhaps IS robes—it is hard to tell. Its face is visible but forgettable; you cannot hold it in memory. Its voice is clear and courteous and deeply, fundamentally *other*.

The Seneschal addresses the party: *"Children of the Moment. You have bound something today that was never meant to be held by temporary flesh. In saving this one—"* the Seneschal gestures to the friend *"—you wove a thread of prophecy into being. That thread is now active, and the world is shifting to accommodate it. I am here to explain what you have done, and to offer you a choice."*

**This is Encounter 3: The Spectral Bind (Ritual/Negotiation).**

The Seneschal is not here to fight. It is here to make a binding offer. The encounter is not combat in the traditional sense, but it is every bit as dangerous—more dangerous, because it is combat for the party's agency and future.

*What the Seneschal Reveals:*

The prophecy is old. It has been waiting in the Veil—the space between worlds, the membrane where fates can be read and futures can be negotiated.

For three centuries, a specific sequence of events has been prophesied: *A binding of fire and flesh will mark the beginning of the Veil's re-emergence. When mortal choice supersedes the fire's will, and a bridge is woven from the ashes, the gateway will open.*

The fire that burned today was not random. It was a test, a working designed to see if any human would choose individual salvation over the collective burn. If the fire had consumed, the prophecy would not have triggered. But the party chose differently. They saved their friend.

In the moment of that choice, a bridge WAS woven. The friend is now marked—no longer entirely of the mortal world, but not yet of the Veil either. They are a *threshold*, a place where both worlds can touch.

The Seneschal wants to know: will the party defend that threshold? Will they help the Veil re-emerge into the world? Or will they resist?

*Objectives (Hidden):*
- Test whether the party understands what they have done
- Determine the party's alignment (will they cooperate with forces beyond mortality, or resist?)
- Offer a genuine choice: accept the prophecy's role and gain the Veil's aid, or reject it and face the Veil's enmity

*Stakes:*
- If the party accepts the prophecy, they become agents of the Veil's emergence. They gain power, strange knowledge, and supernatural aid—but all their choices from this moment forward will be constrained by the Veil's agenda.
- If the party rejects the prophecy, the Seneschal does not become hostile immediately, but it marks them as obstacles. The Veil will oppose them going forward, and many of its servants will see them as enemies.
- If the party negotiates, they can buy time and autonomy—but they will be negotiating with the Seneschal (a being that has been alive for centuries) while they are exhausted from the climax and the two encounters that followed.

*Viable Outcomes:*
1. **Acceptance & Binding:** The party agrees that they will be agents of the Veil's re-emergence. The Seneschal bestows on them a blessing—a power or piece of knowledge that makes them capable of what comes next. The friend is formally marked as a Threshold, and the Seneschal takes an interest in their welfare. The party's trajectory is now cosmically determined; they have become heroes in a story much larger than themselves.

2. **Rejection & Separation:** The party refuses. The Seneschal does not punish them immediately—it is too ancient and too sure of its eventual victory—but it informs them: *"You have chosen the path of the Burning. Very well. But know that every moment your friend remains a Threshold, they will be hunted by forces that want to use that mark. Protect them, or the Veil will claim them anyway."* The party is now responsible for keeping their friend safe from cosmic predators, and the Veil becomes a persistent threat.

3. **Negotiation & Debt:** The party stalls, asks questions, tries to understand the prophecy before committing. The Seneschal respects cunning and trades knowledge for time. It tells the party more than it intended, gives them leverage, and leaves open the possibility of a future alliance. However, it also marks them: *"I will return when you are ready to choose. Until then, know that the Veil is watching, and we do not forget."* The party has bought time, but the clock is now very obviously running.

4. **Challenge & Reversal:** A party member challenges the Seneschal's authority or the prophecy itself. This is dangerous (Seneschal can remove the mark from the friend, stranding them between worlds, or worse), but if the party has the courage and the right arguments, they can potentially rewrite the prophecy's trajectory. This is the hardest path, but it opens a fourth choice: *neither acceptance nor rejection, but resistance and renegotiation on terms the party define.*

## Closing Image

**Theatre of the Mind** 

[!narration]
After the Seneschal departs—whether accepted, rejected, or negotiated with—the party has a moment of stillness. The evacuation is done. The streets are clearing. The sky is darkening toward evening.

Your friend sits on a bench, wrapped in a borrowed cloak. Around their eyes, the light still catches strangely—that mirrored quality that was not there before. But when they look at you, they are still themselves. Tired. Scared. Grateful.

*"I don't know what any of that means,"* they say, *"but I know I'm alive because of you. And I know something is different. I can feel it."*

The smell of smoke still hangs in the air. But behind it—under it—there is something else. The smell of air that has been touched by somewhere else. Somewhere older.

Your friend reaches for your hand. *"What now?"*

The city darkens. The stars are coming out. And somewhere in the distance, you hear something that might be the Seneschal, or might be the echo of the fire, or might be the sound of the world shifting its axis to accommodate what you have begun.

## Consequences

### The Party

**Earned:**
- Proof that they can save those they love, even against supernatural odds
- New allies (possibly: Kess, if negotiated with; the Seneschal, if accepted; or new enemies, depending on choices)
- Knowledge of the fire's nature and the prophecy's existence
- If they accepted the Seneschal: supernatural aid, magic, guidance toward their cosmic role
- If they rejected the Seneschal: clarity that they are choosing their own path, even against cosmic forces

**Paid:**
- Resources (gold to Kess, or a party member's indenture)
- The friendship's innocence—the rescued friend is now a Threshold, and the party is responsible for protecting them
- Enemies: Keeper Voss will return; if they rejected the Seneschal, the Veil is now a persistent threat; if they slighted Kess, the mercenary community is hostile
- Agency (if they accepted the Seneschal): their future choices are now constrained by the prophecy's agenda

**Changed Access:**
- They can now reach the Veil's servants and resources (if they accepted the prophecy)
- They have leverage with mercenary companies (if they paid Kess respectfully)
- They have reason to seek knowledge of the prophecy, the Conflagration, and the Veil—opening new NPCs, locations, and factions
- They can no longer easily leave town or abandon responsibilities—the friend is marked, and the Seneschal is watching

### The World

**People:**
- **Keeper Voss**: Dispersed and weakened, but returning to the Conflagration to report. The fire's masters now know the party's faces. Future fire-workings will target the party specifically.
- **Meridian**: Now aware that the party saved someone marked by prophecy. She will attempt to contact them, study them, bring them to the Council of Cascades. This is not hostile, but it is persistent.
- **Kess**: Either indebted to the party, or marking them as enemies. Her company will act accordingly in future interactions.
- **The Seneschal & The Veil**: Now openly active in the world. It will reach out again, will recruit other forces to press the party's choice, and will eventually come to collect on the prophecy.

**Places:**
- **The Burned Building & Zone**: Now marked as a place where prophecy was woven. If the party returns, they will find traces of the working—scorch marks that form sigils, ash that moves with intention, water that smells of worlds beyond.
- **The City**: Aware that something cosmically significant happened here. Rumors spread among the magical community, the mercenary houses, the churches, the merchant guilds. The party's actions are now known, discussed, feared, or celebrated depending on who speaks.

**Power:**
- The **Veil** has re-emerged into the world and is now openly recruiting agents. This shifts the balance of power in any region. Factions that opposed the Veil must now react. Factions that wanted its return are celebrating.
- The **Conflagration** is aware of the party and will move against them.
- The **Council of Cascades** is now focused on the party and the marked Threshold. This affects the party's access to knowledge, magic, and institutional support.

**Evidence:**
- The friend's mirrored eyes and strange perception
- The smell of the Veil lingering in the air
- The Seneschal's passage left marks: flowers blooming in winter, shadows that move wrong, silence that is too quiet
- Voss's ashes, which smolder with memory and can be read by those who know fire-working

|---|---|
| **Original stake: The friend's life** | Delivered—the friend is alive. Complicated—they are marked by prophecy. The party can see that their choice mattered and has weight. |

**Pay the emotional bill:**
The party saved their friend from the fire. That was the setup: someone needed saving. The payoff is not just that they are alive, but that being alive has changed everything. The party can see, directly, that their choice mattered and that it mattered absolutely. There is no half-measure in prophecy. Their friend is alive, and the world has shifted to accommodate that fact.

This is the emotional core of the Resolution. The party did what they set out to do. The prices they are paying—new enemies, new obligations, cosmic weight—are the prices of genuine victory, not failure. Let the table sit with that for a moment.

## The Stinger: Prophecy Threshold (Optional, Recommended)

**Reveal:** In the night after the Resolution's events, the rescued friend dreams. In the dream, they stand in a place that is neither the city nor any other place they know. It is gray and still and ancient. A voice speaks—not in words, but in understanding.

*The voice says: "You are a Threshold. Through you, the boundary between worlds thins. Those who were locked out are returning. This is not a curse or a blessing—it is a binding. You will see what others cannot. You will be hunted by those who want to use what you have become. You will be protected by those who want the Veil to emerge. Your choice, in this moment, will decide what you become next: a bridge, or a trap, or a weapon."*

The friend wakes. They are no longer alone in the room. Standing in the corner, where no door was moments before, is a figure that is not quite present. It is the Seneschal, or something like it, watching.

It does not move or speak. It simply watches.

When the friend blinks, it is gone.

The friend does not tell the party about this—not immediately. They are afraid, and they are still processing.

**Why Now:** The prophecy is not a one-time event. It is a binding that will be reinforced, deepened, and tested. The friend's marking is active and will grow stronger or be contested depending on how the party chooses to move next. This stinger opens the door to the next adventure: will the party defend the friend's Threshold, or will they try to break the binding? Will they pursue the Veil's agenda, or resist it? Will the Seneschal's offer eventually compel them, or will they forge a different path?

**Next Vector:**
- **Seek knowledge of the Veil** and the prophecy: this leads to the Council of Cascades, to hidden libraries, to NPCs who study the boundary between worlds
- **Protect the friend** from those who would use their Threshold nature: this leads to conflict with cosmic forces, with mercenary companies working for hidden patrons, with the Conflagration's servants
- **Confront the Seneschal** and demand answers or renegotiate the prophecy: this is the highest-stakes path, but it is available
- **Flee and hide**: an option, but one that costs the party's ability to take agency in the wider story
- **Accept the prophecy's role** and begin serving the Veil: this leads toward power, strange magic, and a story where the party is advancing something larger than themselves

## DM Prep Notes (Visible to DM Only)

**What you know that the players don't (yet):**

1. **The Conflagration is a conscious entity** from a plane adjacent to the material world. It sent the fire as a probe to see if humans would choose individual survival over collective burning. The party passed the test (or failed it, depending on point of view). The Conflagration has now marked the party as interesting and dangerous.

2. **The Veil is the boundary between worlds**, not a place or a person. The Seneschal is an agent of older forces that want to re-emerge into the material world. The prophecy was set thousands of years ago and has been waiting for a specific sequence: a binding of fire and flesh, a choice of individual survival over the collective, a threshold between worlds. The party triggered all three. The re-emergence is now inevitable.

3. **The friend is not cursed; they are changed.** Being a Threshold gives them perception beyond normal human sight. Over time (weeks or months of play), they will gain power from the Veil: supernatural senses, the ability to communicate across the boundary, perhaps magic or gifts that mark them as not-quite-human. This can be an asset to the party, or a liability if they don't know how to protect the friend from those who would exploit the Threshold.

4. **Meridian and the Council of Cascades oppose the Veil's re-emergence.** This is not stated in the Resolution, but it is true. If the party learns it, the Council becomes a potential ally against the prophecy. This opens a second path: work with the Council to seal the Threshold and stop the re-emergence, or work with the Veil to accelerate it.

5. **Keeper Voss will return.** He is searching for a way to re-bind the fire, or to claim the friend as a replacement anchor. If the party encounters him again, he is stronger and more desperate. This is a recurring personal nemesis.

6. **The party's next session will be shaped by their choice with the Seneschal.** Do not let the choice be neutral. Make it matter. If they accepted, the Veil is now actively helping them (but also watching them). If they rejected, the Veil is actively opposing them. If they negotiated, they are in a strange liminal space where the Seneschal's return is imminent and urgent.

## Technical Notes (Session Pacing)

The Resolution as written is 2 hours of table time, distributed roughly as:

- **Phase 1 (20 min)**: Voss appears and attacks. Immediate combat, emotional high, confirmation of victory and its price.
- **Phase 2 (40 min)**: Kess negotiates. Less combat (or social combat), multiple possible outcomes, relationship-building, reputation consequence.
- **Phase 3 (60 min)**: The Seneschal arrives and offers the party a genuine choice. Ritual/negotiation, big reveal, cosmic weight, agency decision.
- **Closing (remaining time)**: Let the table breathe. Epilogue moments, personal reactions, the friend's first words as a changed being.

**Pauses for player input:**
- After Voss retreats: let the party react to the friend's changed state
- After Kess's opening: let the party deliberate on which of three debts to pay
- During the Seneschal's reveal: pause frequently and let players ask questions, react to the prophecy's meaning
- After the Seneschal departs: give the table time to sit with what just happened before moving into epilogues

**Combat encounter difficulty:**
- **Voss + Fire Servants**: Should be a hard fight, but not impossible. Voss is designed to be defeated/dispersed, not killed. If the party is losing badly, have Voss press them hard and then retreat (he was weakened by the party's victory over the fire and is not at full strength).
- **Kess + Vanguard**: Should NOT go to full combat. Kess is here to negotiate and make the party uncomfortable, not to kill them. If combat starts, it is a brief skirmish (1-2 rounds) before Kess backs down. She is testing them, not murdering them.
- **Seneschal**: No traditional combat. This is a negotiation, revelation, and choice. The Seneschal is far too powerful to fight, and too smart to fight. If the party attacks it or tries to kill it, it simply leaves. The negotiation fails, and they have made an enemy of something ancient.
