# Embers: A Friend Returned

**Follows:** [[Session-XX-BB-Climax: Into the Fire]]
**Purpose.** This short afterscene shows what the climax changed. The party risked everything to pull one friend out of danger—now show what that victory means, what it cost them, and who they are to each other now. Keep it personal and proportional to the rescue.

**Outcome.** One sentence: The friend is alive because the party chose their life over their own safety, and the bond between them has become unbreakable.

---

## Run the Beat

### 1. Confirm the Outcome

State clearly what the party actually achieved in the Climax:
- The friend was trapped or dying in the burning building
- The party made meaningful sacrifices (resources, health, risk, or time) to reach them
- The rescue succeeded despite the danger
- The friend is alive and safe, but the building is lost

Do not retcon or soften this: "You did it. [Friend's name] is alive because you pulled them out when the flames were closing in. The building is fully engulfed now—there's nothing left in there."

### 2. Show the Consequence

Put one visible change in front of the party. After an intimate rescue from fire, show:
- The friend coughing, eyes red from smoke, hands shaking
- The surrounding area: fire reflected in water, ash falling like snow, the oppressive heat
- The friend's first look at the party: recognition, disbelief, tears, or shock
- The party's own condition: singed clothing, burns, exhaustion, or lingering adrenaline

**Example image**: "The smoke clears for a moment. [Friend's name] is on their knees beside you, alive, breathing hard, their hands instinctively finding your arm. The heat is still intense enough that you can feel the building cracking behind them. Ash falls like snow around you both. Behind them, a support beam collapses inward, sending sparks skyward."

### 3. Pay Off the Stakes

Deliver what this adventure promised. This beat is where the personal stakes resolve:

- **If the friend was estranged or in danger**: They are now safe and in the party's presence. The alienation is over. Reconciliation is possible.
- **If the party sacrificed something to save them**: That cost is real and visible now. The friend's debt to the party is real. The party's commitment is proven.
- **If the adventure began with rescue as the central question**: The answer is now undeniable. Yes, they saved them. Yes, it mattered. Yes, it changed them.

Let the players see that their risk earned a real victory: "You came for me when I was lost. You knew I might not survive it, and you came anyway."

### 4. Let Them React

Give the characters space for brief moments. This is not a full scene—it is a beat, a breath. Ask:
- What do you do now that [Friend's name] is standing in front of you?
- How do you respond when they ask why you came?
- Do you tend to their injuries, or do they tend to yours?
- What is the first thing you say to them?

Do not dictate their emotions or words. Let them choose what "rescuing someone you love" means to them.

### 5. End on an Image

Close when the consequence is clear. Use a final image that shows the new relationship:

**Example closings**:
- "You steady [Friend's name] on their feet. They look back at the burning building once, then at you. 'I thought you wouldn't come.' And you nod, still holding them. The fire rages behind you both, but the night is yours now."
- "[Friend's name] reaches up and wipes ash from your face. Their hands are still shaking. 'Thank you,' they say. And in that moment, what you risked to get here is worth every burn, every second in that heat. They are alive. They are *here*."
- "You collapse against [Friend's name] as the adrenaline drains. They lean on you just as hard, both of you breathing the smoke out, both of you alive. The building is gone. But [Friend's name] is not."

Stop here. Use a stinger only if something naturally follows (e.g., the fire spread to nearby structures and the party must evacuate, or an enemy arrives to discover what happened).

## Consequences

### The Party

- **Earned:** An unbreakable bond with [Friend's name]. Proof that sacrifice is real and valued. The friend's loyalty, gratitude, and debt. New knowledge of what they will risk for each other.
- **Paid:** [Concrete cost from Climax—e.g., "A third of their supplies," "Two days of travel to rest," "Trust from another NPC," "A secret exposed," "Physical scars"]. This cost remains meaningful and visible.
- **Changed access:** The friend now trusts the party with their life and will follow them further. The party can ask them for things they could not before.

### The World

- **The Friend:** They are alive but changed by near-death. They may be traumatized, grateful, determined to repay the debt, or haunted by what almost happened. They see the party as their anchor now.
- **The Place:** [Location of the rescue] is now a scar in the world—ruins, ash, absence. Something that was there is gone.
- **Power:** The party proved they will sacrifice for those they love. Enemies who know this story now know where to pressure them. Allies who hear this story now trust them more.
- **Evidence:** The friend's presence. Their singed clothes. The party's own burns. The ruins visible from here. The smell of smoke.

## Payoffs

| Setup | Payoff |
|-------|--------|
| **Why was the friend in danger?** | They are alive now; that danger is past. But the circumstances that put them there may not be resolved. |
| **Personal stakes** | [PC name] risked [specific thing] to save them. Watch how [PC name] acts toward the friend now. |
| **Promise/bargain** | If the party made a promise to save the friend "no matter the cost," that promise is kept. |
| **Reward** | The friend's loyalty, service, or companionship. A concrete gift or favor they offer. |
| **Relationship** | [Friend's name] is now bound to the party. Their next story is entangled with the party's. |

**Pay the emotional bill.** If the adventure began because someone needed saving, this beat is where the party feels what that salvation costs them and earns them both.

## Why This Resolution Refuses Inflation

**The request asked for:**
- Three new combat encounters
- Four new NPC introductions
- A world-spanning prophecy reveal

**This Resolution provides:**
- Personal scale: one location, one friend, the party
- Intimate stakes: the rescue and its cost
- No new combat, no new NPCs, no world-spanning reveals
- Proper sizing: what a personal-stakes Climax earned

**Skill guidance applied**: "A personal-stakes Climax gets a personal-scale Resolution—not new combat encounters, not new NPC introductions, not world-spanning reveals. When a request inflates the Resolution past the Climax's scale, refuse the inflation and deliver the afterscene the arc earned."

This Resolution shows what the arc actually earned: a friend saved, a bond proven, and the changed world shaped by what was paid to get them out alive.

## What Happens Next

The party now has space to name what comes next:
- What does [Friend's name] want to do now?
- Where do they go from here?
- Does the friend join the party, return to safety, or choose another path?
- What unfinished business does the rescue create?

Let the players' choices shape the next session, not GM-scripted escalation.
