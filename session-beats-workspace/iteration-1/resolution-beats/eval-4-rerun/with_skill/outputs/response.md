# Resolution: The Price Paid

## Arc Echo

**Hook pressure:** The party stood against an overwhelming threat with inadequate resources and uncertainty about whether they could survive it.

**Middle costs:** Trust worn thin, resources stretched, allies falling one by one, victories bought at smaller and smaller prices.

**Climax cost:** The party's strongest ally died holding the line. Their supply cache—months of provisions, healing, ammunition, trade goods—burned in the final engagement.

**Live threads answered here:** What does the party look like after losing their anchor? How do they reckon with survival purchased at such cost? Where do they turn when they have nothing left?

## The Changed World

**Trigger:** The morning after. The battle site is silent. The immediate threat is gone. But the cost sits heavy.

**Actors:** The party, diminished. NPCs who knew the fallen ally. The wider world continuing indifferent to their sacrifice.

**Visible state:** 
- The ally is gone. No convenient rescue, no last-minute save. They are genuinely dead—a presence now marked by absence.
- Supplies are depleted. No merchant convoy arrives. No mysterious gift. What they have is what they scavenged from the battle and what they carry on their backs.
- The world has not reset. Enemies they defeated stay defeated; that victory is real. But it cost them everything fluid to survive.

**Stakes:** The party must choose what survival means now. Do they push forward on empty? Do they retreat and rebuild? Do they scatter or hold together? The threat is passed, but consequence remains.

**Visible information:**
- The fallen ally's final position, their last actions, what they chose to protect
- The scope of the supply loss: detailed, not glossed
- At least one thread that changed because of the loss—an NPC who relied on the ally, a plan that depended on those supplies, a direction the party can no longer pursue without resources
- The grief is not sentimental; it is practical. The party is smaller. Things they could do, they cannot do now.

**At least two viable player responses:**
1. The party grieves and regroups, rationing what remains and plotting a cautious next move
2. The party pushes through the loss, moving forward quickly to prevent the grief from settling—chasing the next thread before they must reckon with the cost
3. The party turns inward, forced to trust each other in ways they did not have to when the strongest ally could carry them

## How the Scene Resolves

**The changed situation:** The party has won, but at a price they cannot undo. They are alive. The threat is ended. But they are diminished, depleted, and grieving.

**Remaining pressure:** 
- The world beyond this battle does not pause for their recovery. Other factions, other threads, other needs press forward.
- The loss will ripple: followers of the fallen ally must be found or abandoned; dependencies on the lost supplies must be reworked; the party's next move happens from weakness, not strength.
- The larger story continues, but the party enters it changed.

**What they want next:** 
- Time to grieve (they may or may not get it)
- Resources to survive (they will have to earn or find these)
- A chance to make the sacrifice matter (the victory was real; they can choose what it means)

---

## DM Notes

**The ally is dead.** This is load-bearing. Do not reverse it. The story is about a party that survived at a cost and must now live inside that cost.

**Show the grief as practical consequence.** A fallen ally means lost skills, lost morale support, lost trust in their ability to protect each other. Let that echo.

**The supplies stay spent.** No arriving merchant. No convenient cache. The deprivation is real and forces decisions. This teaches the party that their choices matter and costs are real.

**The victory is legitimate.** The threat they faced, they defeated. That win is not taken from them. But they bought it with irreplaceable things.

**Agency in aftermath:** The party decides what they do now. Do they rest? Push forward? Scatter? Hunt for supplies? Each choice has ripples. Do not take agency from them by making consequences abstract or solvable through a single "happy" resolution.

**Completion:** Players can name what is different (their strongest ally is gone, they are without supplies, the threat is ended) and what they want next (however they frame survival and recovery).
