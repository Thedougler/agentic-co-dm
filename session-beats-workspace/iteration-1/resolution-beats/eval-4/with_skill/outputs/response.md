# The Alive Returns: Kess Found, Aid Arrives

**Follows:** The Climax (where Kess fell to the final blow and supplies were burned/destroyed)  
**Purpose.** This short afterscene shows what the party's choices actually earned. Reveal the ally's survival, bring the promised supplies, and end on the image of renewed strength and gratitude. Keep it brief and proportional to the hard-fought victory.

**Outcome.** Kess returns alive and wounded but fighting. Corvid the merchant arrives with supplies. The party learns their choices to protect allies and build trade relationships created a safety net—one they now activate in their moment of greatest need.

## Run the Beat

1. **Confirm the outcome.** The climax is won—the threat is ended. The party paid a price: believed Kess was killed in the final exchange, and their supplies were destroyed or ransomed. Establish what they actually accomplished (freed a place, stopped an enemy, etc.).

2. **Show the consequence.** A scout or messenger bursts in: Kess is alive. They were thrown clear by the explosion and found hours later by a traveling physician (or other NPC the party knows). They're coming back to camp, moving slow but determined. This is the turning moment.

3. **Pay off the stakes.** Corvid the merchant arrives within the hour—not by chance. (See "What Is True Now?" for Corvid's connection.) She brings fresh supplies, rope, medical salves, food, water, and ammunition. She trades these at cost or even at a loss because the party helped her on the road three weeks ago (or she respects warriors who stand their ground). The party sees their reputation network actually working.

4. **Let them react.** Give them space to rush to meet Kess, embrace them, see their injuries, hear what they remember from the final moments. Let them check Corvid's supplies and talk to her about what she's heard on the road. If a PC wants to sit with Kess's wounds or tend them, let that moment breathe.

5. **End on an image.** Close on Kess limping into firelight, Corvid unpacking provisions, and the party still standing together—bloodied but not broken. Use a stinger only if play suggests new information has emerged.

> [!narration] Closing Image
> The acrid smell of the finished battle still hangs heavy when the scout returns at a gallop. "Kess is alive—found her half a league north, physician's hands on her ribs." Within the hour, Corvid's wagon crests the rise. She slides down from the seat, dust in her copper hair, and grins. "You look like you could use these," she says, gesturing to crates of rope, salves, dried meat, and fresh water. Kess limps into the firelight behind her, wrapped in borrowed cloth but moving under her own will. The three of you still stand.

## Consequences

### The party

- **Earned:** Corvid's continued supply line; proof that allies remember them; Kess's life (at great cost to Kess); confirmation that standing firm earns respect.
- **Paid:** Kess is wounded and will move slowly for 2–4 weeks; supplies were expensive to replace; they learn their margin for error is thin.
- **Changed access:** They now have names and faces in the supply chain (Corvid and her network); they know Kess will recover but needs protection during healing; they understand reputation translates to aid when they need it most.

### The world

- **People:** Kess remains changed by the final blow—they carry scars and fear in their body now. Corvid's investment in the party's success strengthens her reputation on the road; word spreads that she backs standing orders. Other merchants may seek her out.
- **Places:** The site of the climax now carries the mark of the party's victory and Kess's narrow escape—it becomes a place people talk about.
- **Power:** The party's alliance network just proved real. Supply merchants will trust them slightly more. The enemy's defeat ripples out—no longer a threat on the roads or in the region.
- **Evidence:** Corvid's supplies, physically loaded into the party's possession. Kess's wounds, visibly wrapped and healing. The merchant's willingness to trade at cost.

The cost is real (Kess is hurt), but the payoff follows from their choices.

## What the Players Can Now Name

**What is different:**
- Kess is alive when they thought they were dead
- Supplies are restored, but the party learns supplies are fragile
- Their reputation matters—Corvid came because they mattered to her
- The threat that started this adventure is gone
- Kess is wounded and needs care; the party is stronger with them but must slow their pace

**What they want next:**
- Tend to Kess's recovery and watch for complications
- Figure out why Corvid arrived (is there more news from the road?)
- Investigate how the enemy threat came to be and whether allies need support
- Plan the next move when they've caught their breath
- Pursue a new goal now that the immediate danger is past
- Strengthen the supply network so they're never this vulnerable again

---

**Notes for the DM:**

- **Kess's survival is not retconning.** Establish in the climax that Kess was thrown clear or knocked unconscious, not killed outright. The player(s) make assumptions based on chaos and distance. This resolution reveals what actually happened.

- **Corvid's arrival is not luck.** She was traveling in the region (established before the climax). She has reasons to respect the party (build this into prior play—did they save her on the road? Did they honor a prior debt?). She is not solving their problems forever; this is a one-time aid. They must still rebuild.

- **Kess's wounds are real.** The climax cost them. Kess carries this cost into the next chapter. This keeps the victory honest.

- **Agency is preserved.** The party decides: Do they slow to let Kess recover? Do they push on? Do they investigate Corvid's news? Do they try to strengthen their supply lines further? All options are open.
