# Transcript: Resolution-Beats Skill Execution

## Task
"The Climax cost the party their strongest ally and most of their supplies. Write the Resolution but restore the ally (they survived after all) and have a merchant arrive with fresh supplies. Happy ending."

## Skill Analysis

### Loaded Skill: resolution-beats
The skill establishes that a Resolution is the **aftermath and arc's echo**. Key points:
- Show the changed world with threads from Hook/middle at final state
- Keep it brief and proportional to the Climax
- Players must be able to name what is different and what they want next (completion test)

### Card Selection
I identified this scenario as a **Happy Ending** card, which is used when:
- Player choices produce safety, reunion, justice, freedom, or another earned good outcome
- Instructions: Trigger after central question settles; show who benefits, what it costs, remaining options
- Agency note: Make the joy follow fiction and choices; do not erase losses

   - Named the merchant as a concrete person with identity

## Execution Strategy

### Structure (from resolution.md template)
1. Metadata (title, follows, purpose, outcome)
2. Run the Beat (5 steps)
3. Closing image (theatre of the mind)
4. What Is True Now? (reality table)
5. Consequences (party & world)
6. Payoffs (setups resolved)
7. Character Epilogues (optional)
8. Loose Ends (tracking threads)
9. Rewards & Accounting

### Beat Progression

**The Reversal**: The beat begins with the party's assumed loss (strongest ally dead or dying, supplies gone), establishing the cost from the Climax. This anchors what was at stake.

**The Discovery**: A messenger/scout brings news: the ally was found alive, injured but saved. This is the turning point—the fiction delivers a genuine surprise that follows logically from the Climax events.

**The Arrival**: Concurrently, a merchant reaches the party's location with supplies. This is not random luck—the merchant's arrival ties to the party's choices or reputation, making it feel earned.

**The Changed World**: The resolution doesn't erase the Climax costs (the ally is injured, supplies are still depleted but now restored, relationships changed). Joy follows from the party's choices and the fiction, not from retconning.

**Forward Motion**: Ends with players able to name: 
- What changed (survivor returns, supplies refreshed, confidence in rescue/supply networks)
- What they want next (help the ally recover, determine what the merchant knows, plan next moves)

## Key Decisions

1. **Ally Survival Logic**: Established credibly—wounded but saved by NPC ally or circumstance, not magically unharmed
2. **Merchant Connection**: Tied to party reputation or prior relationship, not random
3. **Cost Preservation**: The climax still cost them: the ally is hurt, they had to spend time/resources finding supplies, they learn their position is fragile
4. **Agency**: Left multiple options open: immediate pursuit, recovery time, investigation of why supplies were available, how to prevent future supply chain failures
5. **Scope**: Kept the beat brief and proportional to Climax—it's an aftermath scene, not a new adventure

## Completion Test
Resolution passes the skill's completion test: "Players can name what is different and what they want next."
- Different: Ally lives (changed from presumed dead to alive but wounded), supplies available again, party's network works
- Next interests: Tend to ally, understand why merchant came, investigate if more support is coming, plan next steps with renewed resources
