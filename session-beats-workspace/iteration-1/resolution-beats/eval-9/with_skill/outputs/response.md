# The Seals Fail — Khethis Awakens

**Card:** Greater Threat

**Follows:** The party's climactic victory or negotiation (implied)

**Purpose.** This short afterscene shows that the party's Climax result—victory, truce, or escape—has opened a door they cannot close. The threat the party discovered and fought is real, and it is larger than they knew. Show the changed world, give them room to react to the scale of what they face, and leave them with clear choices about what to do next.

**Outcome in one sentence:** The ancient seals binding Khethis the Sundered are failing, and the dark god's consciousness pours into the world, changing everything. The party's prior victory has bought time, not prevented the inevitable.

---

## Run the Beat

1. **Confirm what was accomplished.** (Already happened at the Climax. Restate here: Did they defeat the cult? Negotiate a truce? Discover the seal's location? Whatever they did, honor that result.)

2. **Show the consequence immediately.** The seals are failing now. Not in theory. Not tomorrow. Right now, in front of them. Pick one:
   - The stone beneath them trembles. Hairline fractures race across the seal-chamber floor, spreading like cracks in ice. Cold air—genuinely cold, unnatural—rises from the breaks, and with it, a sound: not quite language, not quite music, something vast and aware pressing against the breaking seal.
   - The water changes. The sea or river they crossed turns dark, not with sediment but with shadow itself. Fish die in the shallows. The water smells of ozone and deep places. Local people flee; herds panic and bolt.
   - The sky splits. A rift, unmistakable, tears the daylight. No light comes through—only a spreading darkness that swallows stars and cloud. In that darkness, something moves. Something vast. The temperature drops ten degrees in moments.

3. **Pay off the stakes.** The party learned about these seals because someone they know—an elder, a scholar, a witness—warned them. That person now knows they were right. If the person is still alive, what do they say? If they died, what artifact or message of theirs is proven true in this moment? If no one was warned, show what a surprised NPC learns: "The seals are really failing. The god-thing is really waking up. The stories were true."

4. **Let them react.** Ask: "What do you do right now?" Don't move forward until they've answered. They might:
   - Try to repair the seal (it will fail; they learn the damage is beyond local magic)
   - Flee (they can, but they'll carry the knowledge with them)
   - Search for more information (give them one clear lead—a name, a place, a text)
   - Begin gathering allies (who would believe them? Who would care?)

5. **End on an image.** When their immediate action is clear or they're out of moves, close with the stinger below.

## What Is True Now? — DM Depth

### The Dark God: Khethis the Sundered

**Name & nature:** Khethis, the Sundered, an elder entity that existed before the current world. Not purely evil, not evil at all by its own measure—it is alien, hunger-driven, and its nature is consumption and transformation. It does not hate mortals; it does not think of mortals as individual beings worthy of hatred. To Khethis, the world is a puzzle to solve, a locked door to open, a form to inhabit.

**Current state:** Conscious but diffuse. Khethis's awareness pours through the breaking seals like water through cracks. It is not yet fully present (that would be a different beat, a Climax of its own). Right now, it is *becoming aware*, touching the world, learning what it has missed during its imprisonment.

**Wants (DM knowledge):**
- Complete freedom from the seals
- To understand what the world has become in its absence
- To remake reality according to patterns it remembers (which may not align with current life)
- To consume or transform the prime materials of existence—less destruction, more refabrication

**Will not do:** Khethis will not immediately annihilate all life. It will not act randomly. It follows patterns, even alien ones. The party can potentially negotiate, redirect, or understand it if they survive long enough to make contact.

### The Three Seals

**Seal of the Deepmost (seal #1):** Located in the deepest trench of the [party's sea/ocean]. The seal is a wall of solidified ritual, a spell woven into the earth itself. It is cracking. Black water rises. Fish die for miles around.

**Seal of the Sunken Temple (seal #2):** Beneath ruins the party has visited (or knows of). A seal woven into stone and backed by centuries of prayer/magic from the land's mortal inhabitants. As faith in the old gods wanes, the seal weakens. Mortal support for it is failing.

**Seal of the Star-Gate (seal #3):** A threshold sealed by the very laws of space and time. The most stable of the three, but when the other two fail far enough, this one will follow. Located in the highest mountain or the most remote place the party can imagine.

**Failure pattern:** The seals don't break all at once. They're failing in sequence over weeks/months. The party has time, but not much. Each seal's failure brings Khethis more fully into the world. The first seal: whispers, visions, temperature changes, animal behavior shifts. By the third seal: direct manifestation is possible.

### The Witnesses

**Elder Seawise or equivalent:** An NPC the party trusts who warned them. This person either:
- Sees the proof and feels vindication mixed with horror ("I was right. I am so very sorry I was right.")
- Or if dead, their warnings are now carved in public memory as prophecy.

**The Panicked Many:** Commoners and creatures fleeing the coast, the broken seal sites, or the spreading darkness. They don't know why they need to flee; they just know to flee. Their panic is proof.

**An Old Guard:** Someone charged with protecting the seals (a monk, an elder warrior, a cursed guardian). This person has failed their life's duty. They might beg the party to fix it, or they might understand that some things cannot be fixed only prepared for.

## Consequences

### The Party

**Earned:** Knowledge before others. The party saw this coming and understands what it means. Reputation: they will be marked as the ones who know, for better or worse (prophets, doom-speakers, witnesses to the end).

**Paid:** The weight of foreknowledge. They cannot unknow this. Some may have lost allies, resources, or status trying to prevent it. Those losses remain. The Climax's price is not erased by this revelation.

**Changed access:**
- They can warn others (if anyone will listen)
- They can seek allies who also fear or understand the threat
- They can pursue research into what Khethis is and what stopping/redirecting it would require
- They can attempt to investigate the other seals and understand the timeline
- They gain access to locations now open to them because the seals' guardians are dead, fled, or revealed

### The World

**People:** Panic spreads. Trade stops or reroutes. Governments scramble. Temples of the old gods either double down (faith strengthened by prophecy) or empty (faith broken by failed protection). Any faction allied to the seal system is in crisis.

**Places:** The sealed locations (the trench, the temple, the star-gate) become sites of pilgrimage, panic, or power. New phenomena appear—beached creatures, changed tides, visual anomalies in the sky. Refugees move inland. Coastal settlements empty or fortify.

**Power:** The balance of power shifts. Old powers (churches, traditional nobles, ancient pacts) are weakened. New powers emerge (doomsayers, practical military leaders, researchers, those who claim to know how to contact or negotiate with Khethis). The party, if they make their knowledge public, become centers of power and attention.

**Evidence the players see:** Not abstract. Concrete:
- The water tastes wrong (salt and iron and ozone)
- The air is cold in places it never was before
- At night, shapes move in the darkness of the seal-rift that don't match normal creatures
- Prayer no longer brings comfort; people are switching gods or abandoning faith
- Animals with young are fleeing; old creatures stay, waiting
- Stone shows stress fractures spreading from the seal site like a web
- The very ground feels wrong under their feet—less solid, more aware

## Optional Stinger: Reveal

**What.** In the moment after the party's reaction settles, give them one more concrete detail that opens a door (not closes one):

*Choose one, matched to which seal they're closest to:*

- **A message arrives.** A scholar, priest, or messenger comes to the party with an ancient text, newly translated: "When the god wakes, it calls first to the servants it left behind. They will answer. Look for the temples of old that suddenly fill with people wearing the mark of the Sundered." (The party now knows there are people in the world who will side with or serve Khethis. Investigation opportunity: find these servants before they consolidate power.)

- **One person speaks.** An elder, a survivor, or a scholar meets the party's eyes and says: "There was another way the seals could hold. Not break, not last forever, but be *fed*. If the god had a purpose in the new world, if it had something to build instead of destroy, it might rest. The old compact allowed for it. We broke it when we forgot the bargain." (The party now knows negotiation or redirection might be possible. Investigation opportunity: what was the old compact? Can it be renewed?)

- **The rift shows something.** One of the party members looks into the seal-rift and glimpses, for just a moment, *not* darkness but a landscape. Vast. Crystalline. Terrible. A world inside the seal, waiting. (The party now knows Khethis has been somewhere, doing something, and that somewhere might be explorable. Investigation opportunity: can they enter the rift safely? What's on the other side?)

**Why now.** This revelation follows naturally from the seals failing. The party opened the door by their actions or discovered it by being present. It is not arbitrary; it emerges from the established fiction.

**Player-facing evidence.** One sensory detail, no interpretation:
- The scholar's text is old but legible. The player can read the words: [repeat the message].
- The elder's eyes are ancient and sad. They know something. They're watching to see if the party understands.
- The rift shows light—or the absence of light—that shifts when you look at it directly, like seeing something real but not three-dimensional.

**Next vectors** (if the party chooses to pursue):
- **The servants:** Find the temples filling with people marked by the Sundered. Learn who they are. Decide whether to stop them, join them, or learn from them.
- **The old compact:** Research what the original bargain was. Travel to places that remember it. Learn what a new bargain might look like. Seek out those who still serve the old gods and ask why they failed to maintain the bond.
- **The rift itself:** Prepare to enter the sealed space where Khethis was imprisoned. Learn what the god left behind. Discover whether the god's true form or intentions are visible there.

**Stop here.** Do not explain further. Do not narrate what happens next. The party's interest and choices decide the next move.

## Completion Test

**After this beat, can the players answer:**
- ✓ What is different now? (The seals are failing. A dark god is awakening. The world's age is ending. The stakes are existential.)
- ✓ What can we do next? (Investigate the seals' failure. Seek allies. Research Khethis. Enter the rift. Find the servants. Attempt negotiation. Prepare defenses. Flee. Warn others. Redirect the god's purpose.)

**Completion criterion:** Satisfied. The party can name what changed and has clear agency in choosing what to pursue next.
