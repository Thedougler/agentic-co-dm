# The Concordat's Final Gambit

**Party goal.** Stop the conspirators from suppressing the exposure, destroying evidence, or turning the peace into a cover-up. Survive the encounter and keep witnesses alive to testify.

**Opposition goal.** Kill or silence the party, destroy the evidence they've gathered, and reframe the conspiracy as a rogue operation they can contain.

**Stakes.** 
- If the party succeeds: The truth stands, the peace holds with legitimate Concordat authority, and the conspirators face reckoning.
- If the party fails: The conspiracy is buried, the peace collapses as the conspirators consolidate power, and the party becomes loose ends.
- If the party bargains: They must decide what truth they're willing to trade for survival.

**Pressure.** The conspirators have hours, not days. Once the exposure reaches the legitimate Concordat council, their window closes. They move now while they still control information flow.

**Threads harvested.** 
- The political intrigue thread: The party negotiated peace by understanding faction divisions. This climax tests whether they can survive the violent response from those who were betrayed by the negotiation.
- The conspiracy exposure thread: By revealing the conspiracy, they've created a threat that needs silencing. The climax is the conspirator's last chance to contain the damage.
- The vault thread, transformed: They avoided the vault fight by negotiating. But their negotiation exposed something about the vault's true purpose that someone cannot allow to become public knowledge.

**End when.** The party has either eliminated the conspirators as an immediate threat, negotiated a new agreement, or fled with the evidence intact—or the conspirators have killed/silenced enough witnesses that the exposure becomes deniable.

## Situation

**What is true now:**
- The party has successfully exposed a conspiracy at the heart of the Tessarine Concordat
- The conspiracy diverted vault collateral (centuries of debt repayment) to secretly fund a faction in the larger war
- The party negotiated peace between local factions using this evidence as leverage
- The legitimate Magistrate is shaken but bound by the peace agreement
- The conspirators (led by Factor Commander Cors and his inner circle) now face total loss of power, fortune, and possibly execution

**If nobody interferes:**
- Cors kills the party and the witnesses, destroys the ledgers, and reports a "rogue smuggling ring" attempting extortion
- The Magistrate, faced with the conspirators still controlling security, backs away from the exposure
- The peace holds nominally (Cors honors the treaty terms) but the conspiracy remains buried
- The vault's true history remains hidden

**What changed to make this the climax:**
- *Development thread:* The middle taught the party how to navigate Concordat politics, identify factions, and negotiate between competing interests
- *Cliffhanger thread:* Earlier scenes tested whether they could keep the evidence safe and unseen—they succeeded, but that success exposed the conspiracy fully
- *Escalation:* By presenting the evidence to the Magistrate, they passed the point of no return. Cors can no longer negotiate or manage them quietly. This is his last chance to contain the exposure.

**What the characters know:**
- Cors is here with enough guards to kill them easily
- The ledgers are physical evidence; destroying them would require time or magic
- The two witnesses can identify specific names and dates
- Cors believes killing them is easier than negotiating
- At least one route in or out exists that they haven't seen yet (the conspirators arrived somehow)

**What remains uncertain:**
- Whether Cors has orders to kill from the people above him, or if this is his independent desperation
- Whether the Magistrate will honor the peace if the party survives and testifies
- Whether destroying the evidence is actually possible, or if the party already sent copies
- What Cors will do if he understands he cannot win—will he fight to the death or negotiate?

## Pressure

**Tick when:** Advance when time passes (reinforcements arriving, evidence being located by searchers, the Magistrate's position wavering), an action fails, the opposition's plan progresses, or the party tries to leave.

- [ ] **1. Warning.** Cors's guards move to seal all exits. The sound of guards moving through the building. The Magistrate's voice outside, ordering people away from this chamber. Reinforcements are coming, but whose?

- [ ] **2. Escalation.** Cors signals his guards to advance. Magical wards activate on the chamber doors—not to keep Cors out, but to keep the evidence and witnesses from leaving. A guard delivers news: "The Council has locked the building. Nobody leaves until Magistrate Velesh accounts for the discrepancies." Cors smiles. "Then we have time."

- [ ] **3. Crisis.** One of the witnesses is captured or killed. The ledgers catch fire. The Magistrate's voice is replaced by a different official voice—someone loyal to Cors. "All personnel exit now. This is a security lockdown." Or: Cors reveals he has the Magistrate's daughter as collateral. "The peace stands if you do nothing. It dies if you try to leave."

- [ ] **4. Consequence.** The evidence is destroyed or the witnesses are silenced. Cors seals the chamber, leaving the party with no way out except through him. Or: The Magistrate reverses position publicly, backing Cors's story. The exposure becomes hearsay against the Commander's official denial.

**Do not stall.** When the party stabilizes, Cors acts. When they take shelter, he orders fire or magic. When they try to negotiate, he reveals he has backups for his backup—an escape route, a hostage, a fail-safe that destroys the evidence if he dies.

## Opposition

### Factor Commander Malachai Cors

**Wants.** To eliminate the party, destroy the physical evidence, kill or silence the witnesses, and maintain plausible deniability. He is not trying to preserve the conspiracy—he is trying to survive it, even if that means burning the conspiracy down and blaming the party.

**Why now.** Once the evidence reaches the Council, he is finished. He has hours, possibly minutes, before the Magistrate's political allies force a full audit. This is his only chance to reset the board.

**Leverage.** 
- Control of security in this building and command loyalty from soldiers
- Knowledge of the chamber layout and hidden routes
- A possible hostage (someone the Magistrate or the party cares about, captured as insurance)
- Knowledge of what else is in the ledgers that the party may not have read
- A fail-safe: orders that if he doesn't report in, certain ledgers are destroyed by associates outside the city

**Their opening move.** Cors enters with enough guards to control the chamber. He offers the party a choice: "You can walk out of here right now. Forget what you read. Take your witnesses and go. The peace stands, the treaty is honored, and nobody talks about the vault. Or..." He nods to his guards, and they move to kill. He's betting the party will negotiate. If they don't, he kills them and frames it as self-defense.

**Response.** If the party fights back, Cors enters the fight personally—he's not a coward hiding behind guards. He's a trained soldier and mage-killer, and he fights to neutralize the party's strongest member first. If the party starts winning, he shifts to using the witnesses as leverage or triggering his fail-safe. If he starts losing, he attempts to escape via a hidden route, knowing a live Cors can spin the narrative, but a dead Cors can be blamed for everything.

**Desperation.** When losing, Cors attempts to:
- Kill the witnesses himself to remove their testimony
- Trigger a magical ward that destroys the ledgers
- Reveal the fail-safe to the Magistrate: "I've already sent word. Burn the whole vault rather than let this stand."
- Offer to accept exile and silence in exchange for sparing his family from investigation

**Line he will not cross.** Cors will not kill the Magistrate, even though the Magistrate is a witness. Killing the magistrate would be open rebellion and would unite every faction against him. He can frame the party as traitors, but he cannot openly attack the legitimate authority. This is the key to understanding his real limits: he wants to contain the story, not start a civil war.

**Morale / exit.** If the party proves they have copies of the evidence and reliable allies ready to carry it to the Council regardless of what happens here, Cors shifts from fight to negotiate. If the party has actually killed enough of his guards that escape seems impossible, he goes for suicide-by-party—fighting to the end rather than facing execution. If the party gives him an escape route (via negotiation), he takes it.

## Opposition Assets

- **Guard unit (8-12 soldiers).** Trained Concordat security. They follow Cors's orders without question. They are not fanatically loyal; they will break if Cors is down and someone else gives them orders (like the Magistrate).
- **Magical wards on the chamber doors.** These trap the evidence and witnesses inside—and trap Cors inside with them if the party triggers them. They can be dispelled, broken, or unlocked by someone with Concordat insignia (several of Cors's guards carry the key).
- **The ledgers.** Physical proof. They burn easily. Oil and a tinder spell would destroy them in seconds. But they are also heavy and require time to burn completely—the party can protect them or evacuate them if they act fast.
- **A hidden escape route.** Cors knows a way out via the vault's old air shafts (built for emergency evacuation of collateral). It's cramped and only fits a few people. It empties outside the city proper, hours of travel from safety.

## Stage

| Feature | What characters can do with it | What changes |
|---------|--------------------------------|--------------|
| **Ledger stacks** | Push to block doors, protect from fire/magic, carry out (requires multiple actions), read for more evidence | If destroyed: the exposure becomes hearsay. If evacuated: evidence is portable and can reach the Council. |
| **Chamber doors (sealed by wards)** | Break, dispel, unlock (requires Concordat key), block with furniture | If doors stay sealed: everyone is trapped until someone yields. If doors open: guards from outside can flood in; Cors can escape. |
| **Pillars and shelving** | Break to create obstacles, climb for vantage, use for cover | Terrain changes from open to complex. Line of sight fragments. Magic becomes harder to land. |
| **The hidden escape shaft (behind loose stonework)** | Find (Insight or Investigation check), access, use to escape | Offers an exit for party or Cors. If Cors escapes: he remains a threat. If party escapes: they abandon the ledgers and witnesses. |
| **The witnesses (journal-keeper and scribe)** | Protect, evacuate, use for testimony, put in front to shield from combat | If killed: testimony is lost; evidence becomes circumstantial. If kept alive: they are constant targets; protecting them limits party freedom. |

**Movement / zones.** The chamber is roughly 50 feet long and 30 feet wide. Ledger stacks run along the back wall in two dense rows. The entrance is a heavy wooden door (sealed by wards). The hidden shaft is behind loose stonework on the east wall, concealed by a shelf. Cors's guards form up near the door, trapping the party between the entrance and the back wall. The witnesses are near the ledgers. If the party fights, zone control becomes critical—controlling the pillars or the back wall lets the party protect the witnesses and evidence.

**Collateral stakes.** 
- The two witnesses (promised protection; their deaths would haunt the party)
- The ledgers (the only physical proof; their loss means the conspiracy remains deniable)
- The Magistrate (outside the chamber; if killed or broken, the political will to punish Cors evaporates)
- The peace treaty (if this confrontation destabilizes Velesh, the agreement collapses and the war resumes)

## Final Battle

**Win by.** 
- Survive the encounter with Cors neutralized (dead, captured, or fled)
- AND preserve enough witnesses and evidence to testify before the Council
- The win condition is NOT "kill everyone." It's "make it possible for the truth to reach the Council."

**Lose when.** 
- The witnesses are all killed or broken (too traumatized to testify truthfully)
- The ledgers are destroyed and no copies exist
- Cors escapes and raises counter-evidence or contradictory witnesses
- The Magistrate is killed or manipulated into denying the evidence

**Battlefield pressure.** The guards move through the chamber systematically. Each round they move closer to the ledgers to burn them. The wards keep draining magical energy—if too many wards are hit, the entire door mechanism collapses and reinforcements storm in. The Magistrate's political position is weakening minute by minute. In 10 minutes, someone else will claim authority over the building.

### Forces

- **Boss: Factor Commander Malachai Cors.** A mage-killer trained in Concordat security protocols. His signature threat is precision strikes against spellcasters and his ability to counter-spell protections meant to save the witnesses. In combat, he focuses on killing the party's primary defenders first, then the witnesses. His secondary goal is creating chaos that allows him to escape.
  - Combat role: Burst damage dealer + disruption. AC 17, HP 52, initiative +3.
  - Signature threat: *Dispel Magic* (cast as a bonus action to null protections), *Blindness/Deafness* (to isolate casters), *Misty Step* (to reposit and avoid damage).

- **Support: Guard unit.** 8-12 soldiers (use standard Concordat Guard stats: AC 16, HP 16, attacks deal 7 damage). They spread out to control space and move toward the ledgers and witnesses. Half cover the entrance, half advance into the chamber. They do not fight for honor—they follow orders. If Cors is clearly losing, they waver.

- **Other pressure: Burning ledgers.** One of the guards has oil and a tinder spell. On round 2, if no one stops them, they approach the ledgers and begin dousing them. It takes 3 rounds to burn them completely, but smoke will trigger the chamber's ancient fire wards, which will collapse a section of ceiling and lock the doors even more securely.

### Tactics

1. **Opening.** Cors's guard unit forms a line near the entrance. Cors advances to the center of the chamber. One guard (the arsonist) moves toward the ledgers. Cors casts *Fog Cloud* to obscure sightlines and gives an ultimatum: "You can fight me and die, or you can leave. But someone is going to walk out with a different story than the one you walked in with."

2. **Control.** The guard unit uses its numbers to separate the party. They push toward the witnesses and ledgers, forcing the party into a choice: abandon the evidence/witnesses or split up to protect them. Cors targets the party's strongest damage dealer with *Blindness/Deafness* or *Hold Person*. The arsonist continues toward the ledgers regardless of danger.

3. **Punish.** Cors exploits the chaos to get close to spellcasters, shutting down their magic with *Dispel Magic*. The guard unit punishes anyone who leaves their allies undefended. The arsonist moves fast and light—the party has to actively stop them or lose evidence to fire.

4. **Desperation.** When Cors is below 15 HP or half his guards are down, he triggers his fail-safe: "I've sent word outside. The vault is burning itself to ash right now. That collateral you wanted to save? Gone. Along with anyone still inside." He reveals the hidden escape shaft and begins moving toward it. If the party cuts off that route, he fights to the death. If the party lets him go, he takes one guard with him and vanishes—remaining a long-term threat.

### Phase Shift

**Trigger.** If Cors successfully lights the ledgers on fire (the arsonist's third action), the chamber's ancient protective wards activate.

**Change.** 
- Smoke fills the chamber (disadvantage on ranged attacks and Perception)
- The ceiling groans; loose stones fall (each round, one random target takes 5 damage, no save)
- The exit doors slam shut even harder (now requiring a DC 20 Strength check to force open)
- The wards become unstable—any further magic in the chamber has a 50% chance to trigger a collapse

**Signal.** The smoke, the falling stones, and the sudden silence of the wards as they shift into destruct mode.

**New opportunity.** 
- The collapsing ceiling becomes a weapon—Cors's guards are caught in it too, and many will fall
- The party can use the chaos to escape via the hidden shaft without fighting Cors to the end
- The ledgers, now burning, create their own light and warmth source that can be used tactically
- Cors's fail-safe (burning the vault) becomes irrelevant; the chamber is already collapsing

### Ending the Fight

- **If Cors is killed or captured:** His guards break. Some flee, some surrender. The Magistrate enters and orders them to stand down. The witnesses survive, the ledgers (if still intact) are evacuated. The party has clear testimony and evidence for the Council.

- **If Cors escapes via the shaft:** He survives but can only run. His position is lost, his guards are scattered, and the evidence reaches the Council. He remains a fugitive threat but is no longer in control. The escape route leads to a hidden exit hours from the city—plenty of time for the party to alert the Magistrate.

- **If the ledgers burn:** The exposure becomes hearsay, but the witnesses are alive and can still testify. The conspiracy is exposed as a cover-up attempt (why burn the evidence unless it was damning?). The Council believes the witnesses over Cors's denial.

- **If the party evacuates and abandons Cors:** They get the evidence and witnesses out. Cors consolidates in the building with the remaining guards. But now the evidence is in the hands of the party, outside his control. He has lost. He can kill the Magistrate for spite, but he has already lost politically.

## Payoffs

- **The negotiation thread.** The party negotiated by understanding factions. Now they face the faction that refused negotiation—the conspirators who wanted war, not peace. Their negotiation skills bought them time; their combat skills finish what negotiation began.

- **The conspiracy exposure thread.** They chose to expose the truth rather than use it as silent leverage. That choice creates an enemy in Cors. If they had stayed silent, they would be allies. They made the choice; now they live with the consequence.

- **The vault thread, transformed.** They avoided the vault fight. But exposing the conspiracy showed them the vault's true purpose (collateral storage for debt repayment, not treasure). That knowledge is what makes the evidence damning. The vault is never a fight; it's always a problem of understanding.

- **The capability thread.** The middle taught the party magic, tactics, negotiation, or deception. Now they use those tools against Cors. If they learned to counter-spell, Cors's magic fails. If they learned to negotiate, they might flip one of his guards. If they learned deception, they might manipulate him into revealing his fail-safe prematurely.

### Reveals That Play Has Established

- **Reveal: The vault's real contents.** If play has uncovered it, the ledgers confirm it. The vault holds centuries of confiscated collateral—not treasure, but obligations made physical. This is why the conspiracy exists: someone was diverting that payment to fund war elsewhere.
  - **Cue:** Finding the payment ledgers and tracing the routing codes
  - **Meaning:** The conspiracy is not personal greed; it's funding a larger conflict. Killing Cors stops the immediate threat but leaves the larger conspiracy in place. The party may need to pursue this further.

- **Reveal: Cors's master.** If play has discovered who Cors reports to, the ledgers might have their correspondence. If not, Cors himself might reveal it under interrogation or as a bargaining chip.
  - **Cue:** Questioning Cors, reading between the ledgers' lines, or having the witnesses identify the ultimate beneficiary
  - **Meaning:** This climax is not the end of the conspiracy—it's the moment the party discovers how deep it goes.

- **Reveal: The Magistrate's role.** If play has discovered that Velesh knew or suspected and did nothing, the ledgers confirm complicity. If Velesh is genuinely innocent, the evidence proves it.
  - **Cue:** Finding correspondence with Velesh's seal, or Cors's actions in front of Velesh
  - **Meaning:** Either Velesh is trustworthy (and shocked), or the negotiated peace was itself a lie.

## Outcome

**If the party achieves its goal (kills/captures Cors, preserves evidence and witnesses):**
The truth reaches the Council. Cors is executed or imprisoned. The peace holds because the legitimate Magistrate's authority is validated. The party's negotiation bore fruit. But questions remain: How deep does the conspiracy go? Who authorized Cors to act? Will the larger conflict that the conspiracy was funding now escalate?

**If success costs something:**
- If the witnesses are injured but alive: They can still testify, but their credibility may be questioned (trauma makes memory unreliable). The party must support their testimony with the evidence.
- If some ledgers burn: The exposure is incomplete, but enough remains to implicate Cors. The larger financial flow remains partially hidden.
- If Cors escapes: He remains a threat, but is now hunted by both the Concordat and the party. He cannot operate openly.

**If the opposition achieves its goal (witnesses killed, evidence destroyed):**
Cors reports to the Council that the party is a terrorist cell that tried to extort the Concordat using forged documents and murdered a Magistrate's staff. The party becomes wanted criminals. But the Magistrate can still testify to what the party showed them. The truth is buried, not erased. The party may need to go to war to clear their names.

**If the party withdraws or changes the terms:**
- If they abandon the evidence and witnesses to escape: They have won their own survival but lost the larger fight. Cors consolidates and the conspiracy continues. The peace may hold nominally, but the war resumes elsewhere.
- If they negotiate with Cors: Any deal must preserve enough truth that the party can reach the Council or escape safely. Cors might agree to exile in exchange for silence, or to blame the conspiracy on an enemy faction in exchange for not being killed personally. The outcome depends on what the party is willing to trade.

**Survivors / loose ends:**
- Cors's surviving guards, if any escape: They may become agents for revenge or for the larger conspiracy.
- Witnesses beyond these two: The Magistrate, the guard captain, others in the city who now know the conspiracy was exposed. They may support the party or turn against them based on political winds.
- The larger conspiracy: Cors was a node, not the root. His destruction opens questions, not final answers.

**Hand off to Resolution.** When Cors is neutralized and the evidence is either destroyed or safe, stop escalating. The climax has answered its central question: Can the party survive the conspirators' final gambit and get the truth to authority? What remains for the Resolution is how that truth reshapes the political landscape and what cost the party paid.
