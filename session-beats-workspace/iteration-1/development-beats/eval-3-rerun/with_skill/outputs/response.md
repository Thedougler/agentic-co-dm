# Mara's Reckoning

**Purpose.** This beat resolves the mystery completely: the party learns all four elements (who, why, where, and how to stop) in one beat, transforming from investigation to decision.

**Trigger.** A moment of truth forces Mara's identity into the open: a dying crew member gasps a name; a recovered captain's log surfaces with orders in her hand; Mara herself appears to claim her work; or a confrontation with someone close to her—a former officer, a supplier, a relative—breaks the silence and names her.

**Turn.** The party learns the complete picture. Mara Vellholt—captain of the merchant fleet seized five years ago by Harbormaster Gren on false pretenses—has spent five years in hiding, building toward revenge. She is not a pirate by nature; she is a woman wronged, methodically crewing a war-ship (the Broken Rope, disguised as a merchant vessel) with forced recruits. Her location is known: Tide Cave Basin, hidden anchorage north of the harbor. Her vulnerability is immediate: the enslaved crew despises her, lacking even the thin hope of legitimate wages. They are a mutiny waiting for permission.

**Exit.** The party now has complete information and must act. The mystery is closed. The next beat is not "what happens" but "how does it happen, and at what cost."

**Target.** ~30 minutes.

> [!narration] Opening
> The tavern keeper slides a water-stained captain's log across the bar. The pages are brittle with salt. Inside, in sharp, angry handwriting: orders to take the merchant vessel *Broken Rope*, crew requisitions marked "forced recruitment acceptable," and a name repeated like a prayer: Harbormaster Gren. Gren, who seized my fleet. Gren, who left me with nothing. Gren, who will watch his empire burn. The final entry is dated three days ago. "Twenty crew ready. The Rope sails full." But there is an addendum, scratched in later: "The crew mutters. They know I am not what I was. Some days, I am not sure I am what I was either."

## Run the Beat

1. **Present the situation.** The identity is revealed. Evidence is irrefutable—a log, a confession, a confrontation. Mara Vellholt, a real person with a real grievance, is the kidnapper. She operates from Tide Cave Basin. She is actively preparing to sail.

2. **Let them engage.** The party can interrogate the source. They can ask clarifying questions: Was Gren's seizure of her fleet justified? Is she truly holding the crew against their will, or have they chosen to follow her? Does she plan to kill, steal, or merely destabilize? How many sailors does she have? What is her timeline?

3. **Make the turn.** The turn is the revelation itself. The complete picture surfaces through whatever method brought them to this moment. The party sees Mara not as a faceless threat but as a wronged merchant captain, one whose grievance against Harbormaster Gren is **real and documented**. Gren did seize her fleet. The charges were trumped up. She lost everything. Now she is building toward retribution. But her tool—forced recruitment, enslaving sailors—is itself a crime that creates new victims.

4. **Hand back the choice.** State it clearly: "You now know who is taking sailors, why she is doing it, where she is hiding, and what leverage you have. The Broken Rope is anchored in Tide Cave Basin, three days' sail north. Her crew is enslaved but mutinous. She intends to depart in days. What do you do?"

## Situation

- **Where.** Tide Cave Basin—a hidden anchorage north of the harbor, a series of sea caves carved into the cliffs, accessible only by water. The Broken Rope is anchored inside, camouflaged against the rock face.
- **Present.** Mara Vellholt (captain, age ~45, scarred from five years of living rough, hardened but not dehumanized); approximately 20 enslaved sailors (skilled but resentful, ranging from recent conscripts to long-term prisoners); and potentially Harbormaster Gren (if the party involves official authority).
- **Immediate want.** Mara wants to complete her crew, depart, and strike Harbormaster Gren's trade convoys to force him into financial ruin and public disgrace. The enslaved crew wants freedom or at minimum fair wages and a choice to leave. Harbormaster Gren wants the disappearances stopped before they destroy his authority.
- **Friction.** Mara maintains control through fear, but the fear is eroding—the crew knows her plan, knows she is vulnerable, and knows she has no loyalty to them beyond their utility. Harbormaster Gren will not admit his past action without pressure. The crew cannot act openly without execution by Mara's first mate (a loyalist named [[Castor]] who would die to follow her).
- **Pressure.** The Broken Rope is nearly crewed. Mara has stated she will sail within 1d4 days. Once at sea, she becomes impossible to intercept without naval resources. The enslaved crew's only window for escape narrows daily.
- **If ignored.** Mara sails with a complete crew. She becomes a pirate captain commanding a fast, armed vessel. Harbormaster Gren's reputation crumbles under continued disappearances. The crew remains enslaved for months or years. Her assault on trade convoys begins, destabilizing the region's commerce.

## Revelations

- [ ] **Core.** Mara Vellholt, captain of a merchant fleet seized five years ago by Harbormaster Gren, has survived in hiding and is now kidnapping sailors to crew a ship (the Broken Rope) from which she intends to wage a campaign of economic destruction against Gren. Her motive is revenge; her method is forcible recruitment. → **Leads:** Harbormaster Gren (NPC), the seizure of her fleet (lore), the Broken Rope (location/ship), Mara's past (history)

- [ ] **Support.** Harbormaster Gren seized Mara's merchant fleet five years ago. The charges were trumped up; the seizure was motivated by commercial rivalry and political maneuvering. Mara lost her ship, her crew, her reputation, and her livelihood in a single day. This is not a petty grudge—it is the destruction of her life's work. → **Leads:** Harbormaster Gren's vulnerability, systemic corruption in the Harbor Authority, justice vs. vengeance (thematic)

- [ ] **Support.** The crew aboard the Broken Rope is enslaved but not loyal. They were coerced or kidnapped into service. Most are skilled sailors. None chose Mara willingly, and most resent her. They would abandon ship immediately if offered safety, amnesty, or even basic fairness. The crew is mutiny-ready; they lack only the moment. → **Leads:** Mutiny (certain outcome if players promise safety), crew rescue, negotiated release, Mara's isolation

- [ ] **Support.** Castor, Mara's first mate, is her true loyalist—a former sailing master from her merchant fleet who stayed with her through the destruction and five years of exile. He would kill or die for her. The crew fears and hate him. If Castor is removed or turned, Mara's authority collapses. → **Leads:** Key leverage point, potential turning moment, assassination target (if party is ruthless)

- [ ] **Optional.** Mara keeps a detailed journal documenting her five-year survival, her plan, her doubts. In recent entries, she questions whether revenge will fill the void that Gren created. This is not a complete redemption arc—she still intends to sail—but it reveals Mara as someone trapped in her own logic, not a pure villain. → **Leads:** Character depth, negotiation angles, narrative texture

### Required Conclusion (Use Sparingly)

**Conclusion.** The party must be able to conclude with high confidence: Mara Vellholt is the culprit; she acts from revenge against Harbormaster Gren; she is located in Tide Cave Basin; and the crew can be freed or turned.

- [ ] **Route 1.** Direct evidence—a ship's log, orders, or manifest in Mara's own hand, naming her identity, location, and crew status. (Documentary/physical evidence)
- [ ] **Route 2.** A credible witness—a crew member who escaped, a supplier who dealt with her, or Mara herself—stating all four facts under pressure or confession. (Testimony)
- [ ] **Route 3.** A combination: evidence of the seizure of Mara's fleet (historical records, Harbormaster Gren's own admission, Harbor Authority documents) combined with recent witness statements connecting Mara to current kidnappings. (Corroboration)

## Checks & Costs

**Automatic.** If the party makes direct contact with the evidence source (log, crew member, or Mara herself), they learn: Mara's name, her grievance against Gren, the location (Tide Cave Basin), and the crew's dissatisfaction. No check required.

**Action (Persuasion/Insight).** To interrogate a source and extract additional details (exact timeline, crew numbers, ship status, Mara's mental state), use `DC 13`.
- **Success:** Full operational details. The party learns Mara sails in 1d4 days, crew size is ~20, the Broken Rope is fully provisioned, and Mara's resolve is weakening (from journal entries or confession).
- **Failure:** Partial information. The source holds back crew size, timeline, or Mara's psychological state, but does not refuse to speak. The party must seek corroboration elsewhere.

**Action (Investigation).** To find Harbormaster Gren's historical record of the fleet seizure and confirm his culpability, use `DC 14`.
- **Success:** Documentary evidence—Harbor Authority logs, old news broadsides, or testimony from merchants who witnessed the seizure. The party can prove Gren's action was unjust.
- **Failure:** Gren's records are sealed, lost, or obscured. The party learns his action occurred but cannot prove its illegality without his own admission or Mara's testimony.

**Action (Stealth/Reconnaissance).** To scout Tide Cave Basin and confirm the Broken Rope's presence without being detected, use `DC 15`.
- **Success:** Visual confirmation. The ship is anchored as described. The party observes routines, guard rotations, and crew behavior (clearly enslaved: chained, guarded, moving under duress).
- **Failure:** The party is spotted by an outpost or scout. Mara learns she is being hunted. She accelerates her timeline to sail immediately (rolls become 1d2 days to departure instead of 1d4). Play continues, but urgency increases.

## Player Levers

- **Person.** Mara Vellholt can be negotiated with, confronted morally, arrested, or killed because her grievance is documented, her vulnerability is real, and the party may choose to weaponize her against Harbormaster Gren instead.
- **Person.** Castor can be turned, isolated, or killed because he is the linchpin of crew control. His removal collapses Mara's authority and invites mutiny.
- **Persons (Crew).** The enslaved sailors can be rescued, freed through mutiny, or evacuated because they outnumber Mara and Castor and will act if given credible promise of safety and amnesty.
- **Person.** Harbormaster Gren can be exposed, arrested, or used as leverage because his past action justifies Mara's grievance and his admission (or denial) shapes the party's moral calculus.
- **Thing.** Mara's journal can be recovered, read, and used to understand her doubts and exploit her psychological pressure.
- **Thing.** The Broken Rope itself can be sabotaged, captured, burned, or seized because the crew will cooperate if given signal and safe exit.
- **Place.** Tide Cave Basin can be entered by water, scouted in advance, blockaded, or attacked. It offers multiple tactical approaches.
- **Promise/Problem.** The party can accept responsibility for stopping Mara, freeing the crew, exposing Harbormaster Gren, or any combination. They can also defer to the Harbor Authority and let official justice handle it (consequences follow each choice).

### If the Beat Stalls

- Mara sends a message or ultimatum to the party (forcing response and proving her presence).
- A crew member escapes and finds the party, offering intelligence in exchange for rescue.
- Harbormaster Gren learns the party knows the truth and moves to contain them or accelerate his own action.
- The party witnesses a forced recruitment in real-time and can follow the abductor to Mara's location.
- A third party (a rival merchant, a faction leader, a concerned official) reveals information independently, validating the party's investigation.
- Restate the core situation: Mara is here, her motive is real, her crew is mutiny-ready. What will the party do? Time is short.
