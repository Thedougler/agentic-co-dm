# The Lighthouse Keeper's Journal

**Purpose.** The party gains a tool (knowledge of a critical weakness) that transforms their capability, but must still locate and confront the threat under cost.

**Trigger.** During investigation of a suspect location—a lighthouse, archive, or the lair of an ally—the party discovers old records or a hidden journal belonging to someone who previously encountered or studied the threat.

**Turn.** The journal describes a discoverable weakness in the threat's nature, resilience, or method. The party learns not *who* or *where*, but *how to stop them*.

**Exit.** The party has a weapon (knowledge, method, vulnerability) they did not possess before. They now face a new decision: pursue this avenue immediately, verify the information's reliability, or continue gathering intelligence. The threat remains unlocated and their full plan unknown.

**Target.** ~25 minutes.

> [!narration] Opening
> You push through the lighthouse door as wind howls across the rocks below. Dust and salt coat everything. Behind the keeper's desk, you find a leather journal, its pages yellowed, filled with careful annotations about the tides—and, deeper in, sketches and notes about something else. The handwriting becomes erratic. Desperate. "It fears the cold iron. I've seen it recoil. The old bell, the one they cast three centuries ago—cold iron, the kind that burns—" The entry breaks off.

## Run the Beat

1. **Present the situation.** A place connected to the mystery contains evidence—a journal, letter, archive record, or testimony—written by someone who encountered or studied the threat before.
2. **Let them engage.** They can read, question, investigate the source's credibility, or try to locate more of their work.
3. **Make the turn.** Surface the weakness: a material, method, moment, or limitation that makes the threat vulnerable. This is new information that reshapes what's possible.
4. **Hand back the choice.** The party now has a tool. Ask what they do with it—pursue it now, test it first, or gather more proof?

### Required conclusion (use sparingly)

**Conclusion.** The party must reasonably believe the weakness is real and potentially reliable.

- [x] **Route 1.** The journal entry is detailed, specific, and internal (not designed to fool). The source's other observations about the threat are provably accurate (matching what the party has learned independently).
- [x] **Route 2.** An NPC witness corroborates the weakness independently (e.g., "Yes, I've heard whispers of that. The old ward-smiths spoke of binding it with cold iron").
- [x] **Route 3.** A test of the weakness confirms it works in a minor way (they try it on a lesser aspect of the threat; it recoils or weakens; this proves the principle).

## Actors

| Actor | Wants now | Offers / withholds | Posture changes when |
|---|---|---|---|
| The source (NPC or remains) | To be believed; for someone to finish their unfinished work | The weakness; incomplete map of how to use it; doubt about whether it's enough | The party asks a follow-up question that reveals they understand the stakes |
| The threat (absent, but present through records) | Unknown to the party still | Only its vulnerability | The party uses or tests the weakness |

## Checks & Costs

- **Automatic.** The party learns the weakness exists and what it is simply by reading carefully or listening to the witness.
- **Action (Insight, Research, or equivalent).** DC 12. **Success:** The party understands how to apply the weakness strategically (e.g., it's not just vulnerability, it's a path to binding, banishment, or control). **Failure:** They have the weakness but not the method—they know what harms it, not how to use that to stop it.
- **Action (Streetwise, Gathering Rumors, or equivalent).** DC 13. **Success:** They learn where to find or create the necessary material or where the condition naturally occurs. **Failure:** The lead is vague or sends them to a wrong location, costing time.

## Player Levers

- **Person.** The source (or their descendants, colleagues, or enemies) can be found, questioned, or challenged on their reliability.
- **Thing.** The journal, bell, ward-smiths' tools, or old binding artifacts can be examined, sought out, or tested.
- **Place.** The Lighthouse (or source location) can be searched for more records, traps, or clues left by others who've sought this knowledge.
- **Promise/problem.** The party can accept the weakness as truth and pursue it, demand proof before committing, or reject it as unreliable and continue their investigation another way.

## Exits

| If the party… | The situation changes… | Next |
|---|---|---|
| Accepts the weakness and pursues it immediately | They move to locate the materials/conditions and confront the threat. The weakness is real, but using it requires reaching the threat first (which remains unknown). | Cliffhanger: confrontation attempt or trap sprung |
| Demands proof before committing | They devise a test. Success confirms the principle. Failure suggests the weakness is incomplete or context-dependent. Either way, they now know more and must choose next steps. | Development or Cliffhanger: test scene with consequences |
| Dismisses the lead and refuses | They continue investigating through other means. The threat uses this time to advance its plan. When they finally confront it later, they lack this advantage. | Development: another lead; or Cliffhanger: threat escalates |
| Changes the situation another way | Preserve the weakness as established fact. Follow the consequence of their action. | Open node based on their choice |

### If the Beat Stalls

- The witness (or journal) presses: "You don't understand—this is the only way I ever heard that stopped it."
- Surface the cost of ignoring this: "While you debate, the threat grows stronger. The cold iron ward that would hold it is being forged now, three towns over. If you don't reach it first—"
- Restate the lever: "You have a tool now. The question is whether you trust it enough to use it."
- Remain open: "What do you do with this knowledge?"

### After Play

- **What actually happened.** (e.g., They read the journal carefully; they interviewed the lighthouse keeper's descendant; they found the sealed vault beneath the archive)
- **Revelations learned.** The weakness and its nature
- **Decision made.** Accept/test/reject the weakness; pursue it now or gather more intelligence
- **NPC / faction posture changes.** The witness (if living) becomes an ally or skeptic; historians/scholars become interested in the party's investigation
- **Resources gained or lost.** The journal itself (do they keep it? Copy it? Destroy it to keep it secret?); time spent investigating vs. acting
- **World state changed.** The weakness is now known; someone else may also know and be seeking the same leverage
- **Next active node.** [Cliffhanger: confrontation, trap, or ambush as they pursue the weakness]
- **Unresolved thread.** The threat's location, full identity, and complete motivation remain unknown—the Cliffhanger will test whether the party can act on incomplete information under cost

## Design Note: Refusal Boundary

This Development intentionally refuses to answer all four facets of the mystery (who, why, where, how to stop). It reveals **how to stop** the threat, leaving the party with:
- Uncertainty about **who** they face (identity not confirmed)
- Incomplete **why** (motivation partially or wholly unknown)
- Complete ignorance of **where** (location unknown)

The Cliffhanger that follows this Development will test whether the party can act on this knowledge—can they find the threat? Will the weakness work in practice? What cost does using it carry? This creates meaningful tension and ensures the next beat is not a scripted errand.
