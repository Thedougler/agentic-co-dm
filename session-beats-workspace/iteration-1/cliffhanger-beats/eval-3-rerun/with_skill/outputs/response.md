# The Corsair's Flight

**Trigger.** The party has driven the corsair captain and a skeleton crew toward the only remaining seaworthy vessel in the lagoon. Escape is imminent unless the party acts now.

**PC objective.** Prevent the captain from leaving the lagoon with their crew and stolen chart, OR capture the captain alive, OR recover the chart before they sail.

**Opposition objective.** Escape to open water and live to sail another day. The captain will not surrender—they've burned too many bridges and made too many enemies.

**Stakes.** If the captain escapes, they keep the stolen chart and knowledge of the party's stronghold. They become a recurring threat. The party's hold on the region weakens. If the captain reaches open water, pursuit becomes impossible.

**Ends when.** The vessel either clears the lagoon entrance or is prevented from leaving; the captain is captured, killed, or escapes on foot; OR the chart is recovered or lost to the sea.

> [!narration] Open on Action
> The corsair captain sprints down the dock, three crew members at their heels, toward the only remaining intact ship—a swift cutter with a patched sail. Shallow water sparkles between you and them; mangrove roots claw up from the shallows like prison bars. The tide is turning. You smell salt and smoke. The captain is shouting orders, and you hear the anchor chain begin to sing.

### Opposition

- **Intent.** Survive and escape with the chart. If forced to choose between crew and chart, crew abandons the captain—Vex is a good sailor but not worth drowning for.
- **Opening move.** Get the cutter moving. Raise anchor, catch the tide, point toward open water. Vex stays visible and commanding, shouting orders and cursing the party, not hiding.
- **Behavior.** Tactical and urgent. If boarded, Vex fights hard but *does not fight to the death*—the moment the party has them cornered on deck with no escape, Vex will surrender rather than drown. This is a pirate's choice, not a martyr's.
- **Breaking point.** Vex will abandon ship and swim for the mangroves if the cutter is sinking. They will surrender if trapped on deck with force overwhelming them and the ship still seaworthy. They will *not* fight a boarding party if the party has already cut off the ship's escape route—at that point, surrender becomes the rational choice.
- **Resources.** The cutter (fast, small crew, shallow draft). Four crew members (one stays with the ship, one helps raise anchor, two defend). A cutlass and a wand of control winds (one-use, casts *control winds* when Vex snaps it—this buys them 2-3 rounds of favorable wind). No other magic. No reinforcements incoming.

### Pressure

#### Escalation Toolkit

**Telegraph.** The anchor chain sings—it's already rising. Vex shouts: "Cut loose!" One crew member is already on the ship's rail, preparing to cast off the bowline.

**Escalate.** (Round 2-3) If the party hasn't caught anyone, a crew member casts off the bowline. The ship swings out and catches the tide. Vex uses the wind wand, and the cutter gains 20 feet of distance per round of sailing. The window to board is closing.

**Escalate.** (Round 4) The cutter reaches the lagoon mouth. If the party has not cut off the escape route or boarded the ship, Vex shouts a last curse and the cutter sails into open water. The party can see the ship sailing away but cannot pursue effectively without a prepared vessel of their own.

**Breaking point.** Make it clear at Round 3 that if the ship clears the lagoon mouth, pursuit is over. This is what creates the tension. The party *must* choose: do we board, do we cut them off in the shallows, do we negotiate, or do we let them go?

### Lagoon Chase Terrain

- **Space.** The anchorage (200 feet across). The dock (60 feet long). The lagoon mouth (about 300 feet wide, with mangrove roots and rocks narrowing the deep channel to 80 feet). Beyond that: open water and escape.
- **Hazard.** Shallow water (3-4 feet deep) makes mounted or armored movement difficult. The mangroves are dense but not lethal—they're disorienting. The lagoon mouth has submerged rocks that require 10 feet of clearance or the cutter risks running aground and taking a hit to the hull.
- **Interactive features.**
  - **Dock cleats and rigging.** A character can climb (DC 10) to gain 15 feet of height and see over the cutter's rail. Rigging can be swung from (Athletics, 10 feet of movement). A character can cut the bowline or stern line, delaying the cutter by one round if they board the dock or the ship.
  - **Barrels of pitch.** Heavy and flammable. A barrel can be rolled into shallow water (blocking a route or the cutter's draft) or thrown onto the cutter's deck (fire damage, Dex save DC 13 for the crew to avoid). Once the fire starts, it spreads—the cutter is in greater danger of total loss, which may change Vex's decision calculus (less likely to escape, more willing to negotiate or surrender).
  - **Rowboat.** Lashed to the dock. A character can cut it loose and pole across the shallow water in 2 rounds to intercept the cutter or reach Vex if they abandon ship. It's slow but reliable.
  - **Mangroves.** Dense, twisted roots create difficult terrain. Running characters must make an Acrobatics check (DC 12) each round or fall prone. A character who knows these waters (DC 12 Survival check) can navigate without risk. Perfect for a chase on foot.
- **Change.** The tide turns at the start of Round 4. After that, the lagoon mouth's deep channel drifts 10 feet north, opening a *slightly* wider route for the cutter to escape. If the party has been slowing the ship, this is their last window to stop it before the tide carries it to freedom.

## Discoveries

- **Clue.** The chart in Vex's cabin shows a route through the Shattered Rocks that should be impassable—someone has been mapping safe corridors. This chart is worth keeping.
- **Clue.** Vex has a sealed letter from a noble in Portmanteau, offering employment and passage. If the party captures Vex alive, this letter reveals a patron Vex was running for (opens a new thread).
- **Clue.** Among the cargo: barrels marked with a merchant house seal that doesn't match any known trading company. Vex doesn't know what's inside—they were hired to move it. This is a hook for a deeper mystery.

## Resolution

| Outcome                 | What changes                                                                                 |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Objective gained**    | The party catches Vex before they escape. Vex surrenders rather than drown. The party gains the chart, the letter, and a prisoner who knows the lagoon, the noble's name, and what the cargo was meant for. Vex becomes a potential ally, hostage, or recurring nemesis depending on what the party does next.                       |
| **Costly success**      | The party board the cutter but Vex escapes on foot through the mangroves. The party captures the ship and cargo, but Vex is still alive and will become a recurring threat. They now know the party's location and capabilities.                  |
| **Withdrawal / defeat** | The cutter clears the lagoon mouth and sails into open water. Vex escapes. The party can see the ship leaving but cannot pursue without their own vessel or major magic. Vex keeps the chart and the mystery cargo. The party learns that Vex was working for someone in Portmanteau—a threat they now know exists but cannot yet reach.          |
| **Unexpected solution** | The party negotiates with Vex mid-chase (perhaps Vex sees the party has a superior claim or a better offer). Vex agrees to parley. Maybe the party learns something that changes what Vex does next. Follow whatever deal the party actually makes.    |

**Changed state.** Either Vex is captured or gone. The cargo and chart are either secured or lost. The party now knows someone in Portmanteau is hiring corsairs and moving mystery cargo through the lagoon. The lagoon anchorage is either cleared of hostile ships or Vex remains a threat with a escape route.

**Immediate question.** If Vex is captured: "What do we do with them?" If Vex escaped: "Do we chase them on foot, or do we secure what's left behind?" Either way: "Who in Portmanteau is hiring corsairs, and why did they need that chart?"

**Next Development.** Lead to a Development where the party investigates the noble's letter, tracks the cargo's origin, or pursues Vex through new waters.

**If the session ends here.** End on the image of the cutter slipping away (if Vex escapes) or Vex, soaked and cornered on the cutter's deck, meeting the party's eyes as they close in (if captured). The lagoon is quieter now—all the urgency drains. What comes next is the party's choice.

## References

- **Captain Vex.** A corsair of the old school—skilled, cruel when cornered, but pragmatic. Does not fight to the death. Knows the lagoon and its tides. Cares about survival and reputation above all. Works for someone in Portmanteau.
- **The Cutter.** Fast (35 ft. per round under sail), shallow draft (6 feet), small crew (6 total, but only 3-4 actively defending). One mast with patched canvas. No ballista or heavy weapons. Can be burned, sunk, or boarded like any ship.
- **The Lagoon.** Shallow, treacherous, known to the locals. The mangroves are old and gnarled. High ground to the northeast overlooks the anchorage. The lagoon mouth opens to deep water beyond.
