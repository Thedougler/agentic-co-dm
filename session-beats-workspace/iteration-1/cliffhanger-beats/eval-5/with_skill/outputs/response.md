# Three Consecutive Cliffhangers: Battle → Chase → Trap

## Cliffhanger 1: The Boarding Attack (Battle)

**Card:** Battle

**Trigger.** You are anchored offshore at the Scattered Wraith, the legendary pirate outpost—little more than a concealed cove with driftwood structures clinging to the rocks. Your crew is making repairs and taking on fresh water. At dawn, as the watch changes, the sharp crack of boarding grapples echoes across the deck. A sleek corsair has emerged from the mist directly upwind, moving too fast and too close. Before you can sound the alarm, fifteen figures in dark leather are dropping ropes and vaulting over your rail—mercenaries, not common sailors, moving with practiced coordination.

**PC objective.** Drive off the boarding attack and keep the ship from being seized or burned. A secondary goal: learn why the Scattered Wraith sent professional killers after you, or discover what they're here to steal.

**Opposition objective.** The corsair captain, Harrim Kess, has orders from the Scattered Wraith's master to take the ship intact if possible, or burn it if the boarding fails. His secondary goal: capture or kill the party's navigator (if one exists) or the expedition leader.

**Stakes.** Your ship. Your crew. The supply cache you need to reach the Outer Reef. The navigation charts that can get you there. Lives of anyone caught in the crossfire. The element of surprise—if you lose this fight, the Scattered Wraith now knows you're here and hostile.

**Ends when.** The boarding party is broken and fleeing back to the corsair, or the corsair itself pulls away to avoid sinking, or the party surrenders the ship.

> [!narration] Open on Action
> The impact of the first grapple hits like a thunderclap—you feel the ship shudder beneath your feet. Figures swarm over the rail in a practiced sequence, cutlasses already drawn, moving with the discipline of trained killers. The mist clings to the deck in pale wisps. You smell salt, blood, and the sharp char of burning rope where a grapple tore into the timber. The corsair swings alongside your starboard, close enough to see faces beneath dark hoods and the sails of a war-rigged vessel you didn't see until it was this close.

### Run the Beat

| Need at the table | Prep |
|---|---|
| **Opposition** | **Harrim Kess**, corsair captain: wants the ship and the party neutralized. He leads from the deck of his own vessel, directing the boarders by gesture and voice. His crew consists of **3 Henchmen** (skilled cutthroats) leading a squad of **12 Grunts** (the muscle—dangerous in numbers, weak individually). Kess himself is equal/slightly stronger than a party member, well-armored, and carries a spell-infused cutlass. He will not board personally unless the attack is already failing. |
| **Default motion** | If the party does nothing, the boarders will advance toward the helm, trying to secure the captain and the wheel. They will attempt to set a fire in the hold if they meet resistance. The corsair will rake the party's deck with crossbow fire every other round. |
| **Pressure** | The corsair's position traps the party against their own ship—retreat toward open water is blocked by the corsair's bow. A timer: Harrim has a mage aboard the corsair (visible on deck, robed) who will cast a binding spell or fire spell if the boarding takes too long. The mage will act in 3 rounds if the fighting has not already resolved. |
| **Leverage** | The party's ship is anchored—they can slip anchor, but that takes a full round and the ship will drift without crew at the helm. Barrels of fresh water are stacked near the main mast (heavy, usable as cover or thrown as obstacles). The corsair's hull is grappled close—the party can fire onto its deck or cut the grapples to force separation. Rigging and sails offer height advantage for archers or climbers. The anchor chain is accessible and can be cut. |
| **Danger** | The Henchmen are skilled and coordinated—they fight as a unit and protect the Grunts. The mage represents a genuine escalation if not dealt with first. Kess himself is a credible one-on-one opponent. The corsair's crossbowmen (6 of them, on the corsair's deck) have clear shots at anything visible on the party's ship. |
| **Ways out** | **Fight**: Drive the boarders back by force, focusing on the Henchmen first to break the squad's coordination. **Cut and drift**: Slip anchor and cut the grapples, forcing the corsair to separate (risky—loss of supplies and the ship drifts). **Take the corsair**: Board the corsair in return, targeting the mage and the rigging. **Burn them out**: Set fire to the boarding ropes or the corsair's sails to force withdrawal. **Parley and bluff**: Claim you're carrying plague, a cursed artifact, or that reinforcements are minutes away (credibility is the check). **Surrender and ambush**: Give ground intentionally to lure the boarders into a trap below deck. |

#### Opposition

**Intent.** Secure the ship intact (captain alive for interrogation if possible, crew subdued, cargo intact). If that fails, burn the deck and force the party to abandon—a partial victory.

**Opening move.** The Henchmen form a wedge and drive straight for the helm, where they expect the captain to be. Kess directs from the corsair. Grunts follow in loose formation, establishing a perimeter and trying to flank any organized resistance.

**Behavior.** The Henchmen communicate in hand signals and short barks. They will shift focus if they see a spellcaster or obvious leader. Grunts will break and flee if a Henchman falls and the remaining two appear outnumbered. Kess will call a retreat if three Henchmen die and the ship is not yet secure, or if the corsair's hull is breached.

**Breaking point.** If two Henchmen fall, the Grunts begin to doubt. If the party boards the corsair or disables the mage, Kess will order a withdrawal within one round. If half the Grunts are down and a Henchman falls, Kess will torch the hold himself and pull away.

**Resources.** The corsair carries:
- **Harrim Kess** (captain, henchman-tier combatant, spell-infused cutlass, +1 to parry)
- **3 veteran mercenaries** (Henchmen: organized, coordinated)
- **12 corsair crew** (Grunts: dangerous in numbers, easily frightened)
- **1 mage** (robed, visible on corsair deck; has a spell prepared; range is the entire deck)
- **6 crossbowmen** on the corsair deck with clear sight lines
- The corsair's hull itself (can be boarded; rigging can be cut; sails can be burned)

#### Pressure

**Telegraph.** Kess shouts an order in a language you might recognize (Orcish, Dragonborn, or the Common Tongue of the Shattered Sea). The mage raises a staff and you see arcane symbols beginning to glow.

**Escalate.** The mage casts a binding spell (hold person / hold creature) on the party's obvious leader or spellcaster, or casts a fire spell at the hold. One of the Henchmen sets a torch to the boarding rope, trying to cut off retreat.

**Breaking point.** The fire spreads to the rigging. The mage prepares a second spell. One of the Grunts screams a warning—reinforcements from the corsair's hold are coming up the ladder. (This is a bluff unless the party has already taken significant casualties; the reinforcements are actually below and will take 2 rounds to arrive, giving the party one more round to decide whether to fight or flee.)

### Battlefield

**Space.** The deck is roughly 40 feet long and 20 feet wide. The corsair is grappled to the starboard side (eastern side). The mast divides the forward and aft sections. The helm is 20 feet aft. The hold entrance is amidships. The anchor is aft of the mast on the port side. Rigging drops from three mast points, creating vertical routes.

**Hazard.** The ship is still at anchor but drifting slightly. The corsair's position means fire can spread between the two ships if not controlled. The mage's spell affects both decks equally.

**Interactive features.**
- **Water barrels (6 of them, stacked near the mast).** Characters can use them as mobile cover, throw them at enemies (STR check; 2d6 damage if they hit, plus knockdown on a solid impact), or pour water to suppress a fire.
- **Boarding ropes (8 of them, taut between the ships).** Characters can cut a rope (1 round of focused effort) to disable that section; cutting multiple ropes forces the corsair to drift back. Fire weakens a rope; it snaps in 1d4 rounds if burning.
- **Rigging (accessible at three points).** A character can climb to the crow's nest in 1 round (acrobatics/athletics), gaining height advantage. From height, a character has clear shots at the corsair's deck and mage.
- **The helm wheel.** The priority target for the Henchmen. If the party defends it actively, it becomes a chokepoint. If the party ignores it, a Henchman can reach it in 2 rounds and claim tactical control.
- **The anchor chain (thick iron, aft).** A strong character or tool can sever it in 1 round of focused effort, forcing the ship to drift.

**Change.** If the fire spreads unchecked, the entire deck becomes dangerous (no cover, movement is difficult, fire deals damage). If the corsair is damaged (hull breach), it will pull back to avoid sinking. If the party boards the corsair, the Grunts panic—the fight shifts from your deck to theirs.

### Discoveries

- **Harrim Kess carries a sealed letter** (discovered if he is defeated or captured) from someone at the Scattered Wraith, ordering him to take your ship and cargo—no clue yet about what cargo they believe you have.
- **The corsair's manifest** (below deck if the party boards) shows recent supply purchases: siege equipment, poison antidotes, and several crates marked "For the North Tower." The Scattered Wraith is clearly preparing for something.
- **One of the Grunts, if captured and frightened, will admit** the Scattered Wraith has a spy in a settlement the party has visited—information is flowing about the party's movements.

## Resolution

| Outcome | What changes |
|---|---|
| **Objective gained** | The boarding party is routed; the corsair pulls away with visible damage. The party learns the Scattered Wraith sent the attack and that someone has reported their movements. Morale is high—the crew sees the party can defend the ship against trained killers. |
| **Costly success** | The party drives off the attack, but fire spreads to the hold before it can be contained. Food and fresh water are lost (supplies reduced; new constraint for the journey ahead). One crew member is killed; another is badly wounded. The corsair escapes intact, able to report the party's strength to the Scattered Wraith. |
| **Withdrawal / defeat** | The party is forced to abandon the ship or surrender it. If they flee in the ship's boats, they have what they can carry—a few days of supplies, navigation charts, and weapons. The corsair seizes the ship and leaves with the hold's contents. OR: The party surrenders and is taken prisoner, interrogated, and held for ransom/execution (hard reset required; do not use this unless the party truly chooses it). |
| **Unexpected solution** | The party parley or trick Kess: claim reinforcements are coming, offer a bribe, or propose a duel with Kess instead of a full fight. If the party can convince Kess that taking the ship is not worth the cost (parley, magic, intimidation), he withdraws—but the Scattered Wraith now knows the party is clever and will send a larger force next time. |

**Changed state.** The Scattered Wraith is actively hunting the party. The crew is bloodied but confident in the party's leadership. The party now knows someone has been reporting their movements—a spy exists, either in a recent settlement or (worse) embedded in the crew. The ship has visible battle damage (can be repaired in 1 day with supplies).

**Immediate question.** The battle is resolved, but the corsair was moving fast from the south. Were there other ships in that direction? Is the Scattered Wraith sending a follow-up attack, or was this a probing assault? The party has maybe 2 hours before the next threat—enough time to do emergency repairs, but not enough to investigate thoroughly.

## Cliffhanger 2: The Pursuit (Chase)

**Card:** Chase

**Trigger.** As your crew finishes emergency repairs and prepares to slip anchor, the lookout shouts a warning: "Sails on the horizon—two of them, and they're moving fast from the south." The corsair that attacked you was not the main force—it was the vanguard. You now have two pirate ships closing on you, and the open ocean gives you no cover. The wind is steady from the east, but the pirate ships are rigged for speed and pursuit. Your choices are limited: fight two ships at once (suicide), hide in the shallow channels to the north where larger vessels cannot follow (risky and slow), or run for the Outer Reef and pray you can navigate it faster than they can.

**PC objective.** Reach open water, hide in the shallows, outrun the pirates, or find a defensible position. Secondary: learn who is commanding this force and whether the Scattered Wraith itself is behind it.

**Opposition objective.** The pirate fleet commander (Harrim Kess was a subordinate) wants to capture the party's ship and cargo intact. If capture is impossible, destroy the ship to prevent it from reaching the Outer Reef and sharing intelligence about the Scattered Wraith's movements.

**Stakes.** Your ship's speed is your only advantage. If the pirates close the distance, they will swarm your vessel with overwhelming numbers. The shallows offer safety, but taking them means abandoning the open-water route and losing days of travel. Reach the Outer Reef and you escape; get caught in the open water and the fight becomes a three-sided melee where you are outnumbered.

**Ends when.** The pursuing ships either break off (too damaged or outmaneuvered), the party reaches the shallows and the larger pirate ships cannot follow, or the pirate ships close to combat range and the chase becomes a boarding action.

> [!narration] Open on Action
> The wind snaps your sails taut as you hoist anchor, and the whole deck surges forward with sudden momentum. Behind you, two sleek shapes knife through the water, their sails already full, closing the distance you just gained. The water between you and them is brilliant blue, utterly empty—nowhere to hide, nowhere to turn. The lookout shouts bearings: the larger ship to your left is crewed and dangerous; the smaller one to the right is faster. The smell of salt spray mixes with the acrid scent of burning pitch—one of the pirate ships is trailing smoke from the battle that wounded Harrim's corsair. They want you badly enough to send a damaged ship.

### Run the Beat

| Need at the table | Prep |
|---|---|
| **Opposition** | **Captain Vex Tarrow**, fleet commander, leads from the larger pirate ship (The Executioner, a three-masted brigantine). She wants the party's vessel and intelligence about the Outer Reef. **Harrim Kess** (from the first battle, bloodied but alive) commands the smaller ship (The Harrier, a fast sloop). He wants revenge and is pushing his crew hard. The fleet numbers 80 crew combined; the party's ship is crewed by 20–30 (depending on losses in the first battle). |
| **Default motion** | The pirate ships maintain pursuit, using superior maneuverability to close distance by 10% per hour. If the party does nothing, they will be in boarding range within 4 hours. The larger ship will attempt to cut off escape routes; the smaller ship will harry the flanks. |
| **Pressure** | **Distance.** The pirate ships are currently 1 mile behind and moving faster. Every decision the party makes either closes the gap or opens it. **Damage.** The smaller ship is trailing smoke—it is damaged but dangerous. If the party harms it further, Harrim will become reckless. **Time.** The shallows are 6 hours north; the Outer Reef is 12 hours east. The party must choose: the safe-but-slow route or the dangerous-but-direct route. **Supply.** The party used supplies in the first battle; they have a narrowing margin before they run low. |
| **Leverage** | The party's ship, if repaired adequately, has the same speed as The Harrier but is more maneuverable than The Executioner. The smaller ship (The Harrier) is damaged and moving slower than it should—if the party can open a gap, Harrim will be forced to choose between pursuing alone (and dying) or giving up. The shallows are dangerous (reefs, hidden rocks) but the pirate ships cannot enter them. The party has navigation charts; the pirates are relying on speed. The ocean itself is beginning to darken—a storm is building to the east (visible as a dark wall of cloud). |
| **Danger** | Two ships together have overwhelming firepower. If they close to boarding range and the party is still at sea, the fight becomes impossible odds. The storm is a new threat—if the party tries to outrun it, they could be caught in heavy weather while still pursued. The Harrier is damaged but Harrim is a skilled captain—he will use whatever advantage the terrain gives him. |
| **Ways out** | **Run north to the shallows**: Safe but slow; adds 6 hours to the journey and costs supply reserves. **Run east into the storm**: Dangerous (risk damage, losing crew) but the pirates cannot follow into heavy weather without sinking. **Turn and fight**: Impossible odds, but if the party can cripple the Harrier, they reduce the pursuit force by half. **Coordinate with crew**: Perform a difficult maneuver (rigging sabotage, synchronized sail-trimming, or a gambit the party invents) to steal extra speed or force an error. **Seek help**: If the party has allies or contacts in nearby settlements, now is the time to signal for aid. **Abandon cargo**: Dump supplies or cargo overboard to lighten the ship and gain a speed advantage (costs resource, buys time). **Negotiate from a position of strength**: If the party can seem stronger or more dangerous than they are, parley with Vex or Harrim (risky; requires magical disguise, false signals, or a genuinely compelling offer). |

#### Opposition

**Intent.** **Vex Tarrow** wants to recapture the party's ship and interrogate them about their destination (the Outer Reef) and their business. **Harrim Kess** wants revenge and to prove himself by taking the ship single-handedly. These two goals are compatible for now but could diverge if the battle begins to turn.

**Opening move.** The Harrier moves to flank the party's starboard (right) side, attempting to draw fire and maneuverability. The Executioner stays on the port quarter, slowly closing the distance and preparing to rake with cannon fire if the party attempts to turn west. Vex is patient; Harrim is aggressive.

**Behavior.** Vex signals her commands via flags and colored lights; her crew responds with discipline. Harrim shouts commands and is visible on his deck, pushing his crew hard. The Harrier is faster but more fragile; the Executioner is slower but can absorb more damage. If the party manages to cripple the Harrier, Harrim will either attempt a desperate ramming attack or break off in shame (50/50).

**Breaking point.** If the Harrier is significantly damaged (sails torn, hull breached), Harrim will attempt a last aggressive gambit before breaking off. If the Executioner is damaged badly, Vex will reduce pursuit speed and attempt to communicate via hail, offering terms. If both ships are damaged badly enough, the fleet will break off and return to port.

**Resources.** 
- **Vex Tarrow** (fleet commander, captain-tier combatant, commanding officer)
- **Harrim Kess** (secondary commander, henchman-tier, seeking revenge)
- **The Executioner**: 40 crew, three masts, armed with 6 cannons, heavy and slow but devastating at close range
- **The Harrier**: 40 crew, two masts, armed with 2 cannons and swivel guns, fast and maneuverable but lightly built

#### Pressure

**Telegraph.** The Executioner's gun ports open. You see the glint of cannon barrels. Harrim raises a flag on the Harrier—a white field with a blood-red hand, the mark of the Scattered Wraith corsairs.

**Escalate.** The Executioner fires a warning shot (cannonball passes 50 feet ahead of the party's bow, near miss). The Harrier moves to cut off the northward route (toward the shallows). Harrim signals his crew to begin grappling preparations—he's going to attempt a ramming attack or boarding maneuver if the party slows or turns.

**Breaking point.** The wind shifts. The sky darkens. The storm that was building on the horizon moves closer than anticipated—it is now 2 hours away instead of 4. The Executioner fires again, this time hitting close enough for the party to feel the impact through the water. The Harrier is within hailing distance. Harrim shouts a challenge: "Strike your colors and prepare to be boarded, or burn where you float!"

### Chase Terrain

**Space.** Open ocean, 10+ miles in all directions. The shallows begin 6 hours north. The Outer Reef is 12 hours east. The storm is approaching from the southeast. The party's ship is currently 1 mile ahead of both pursuers; the Harrier is faster and closing on the starboard flank; the Executioner is slower but staying in the rear, cutting off retreat.

**Hazard.** The approaching storm will bring high waves, reduced visibility, and wind gusts that affect sail control. If any ship is caught in the storm while in active pursuit, it risks dismasting or hull breach (party and pirates equally at risk). The shallows have hidden reefs that will tear a ship's hull open if struck at speed.

**Interactive features.**
- **The rigging and sails.** The party can perform advanced maneuvers: tight turns (lose speed, gain agility), sail-shifts (change the sail configuration for burst speed or stability), or rigging sabotage (Harrim's ship is already damaged; further damage might force it to slow or break off).
- **The storm.** If the party turns east into the storm, they risk ship damage but escape the pursuit (pirates cannot follow without sinking). The party must make a sailing/navigation check to reach the far side without losing the ship entirely.
- **The shallows.** If the party turns north, they can enter the shallow channels in 6 hours. The larger pirate ships cannot follow into water less than 12 feet deep (the party's ship draws 8 feet and can navigate freely; the Executioner draws 15 feet and will run aground). The Harrier can follow the party into the shallows (draws 6 feet) and continue the pursuit there.
- **Supply caches.** If the party has knowledge of hidden supply caches or settlements along the route north, they could dock briefly to resupply and rest (adds time but ensures survival).
- **Crew morale.** The crew is frightened after the first battle. If the party seems confident and capable, morale is high and the crew performs better on checks (boosts speed by 5% or enables one automatic success). If the party seems uncertain, morale drops and crew checks have disadvantage.

**Change.** The storm reaches the area in 2 hours. The wind becomes erratic. The sea begins to heave. Any ship attempting aggressive maneuvers (ramming, boarding) risks capsizing. The darkness falls earlier—visibility will be poor in 3 hours.

### Discoveries

- **A crew member (if the party asks and makes a sailing/navigation check)** knows a shortcut through the shallows that will save 2 hours but requires precise navigation and has a risk of running aground.
- **Looking back, the party notices the Harrier is taking on water** (visible leaks, slow listing to starboard)—it is more damaged from the first battle than Harrim has admitted to his crew. If the party doesn't press the advantage, Harrim may break off on his own in the next hour.
- **A navigation chart (if the party searches Harrim's cabin after capturing his ship or crew)** shows the locations of three hidden Scattered Wraith outposts. The Outer Reef is marked with an ominous symbol and the word "FORBIDDEN."

## Resolution

| Outcome | What changes |
|---|---|
| **Objective gained** | The pirate ships break off or are left behind. The party reaches open water, the shallows, or the Outer Reef entrance safely. They have time to rest and repair. The crew's confidence is restored. If the party damaged one of the pirate ships badly, that ship will be out of action for a week. |
| **Costly success** | The party outruns the pirates, but the ship takes damage from either aggressive maneuvering or cannon fire (hull breach, rigging torn, sails damaged). The party reaches safety but must spend a day in repairs before continuing. Morale suffers—the crew is exhausted. Supply reserves are depleted faster due to the hard sailing. |
| **Withdrawal / defeat** | The pirate ships catch the party's vessel. The chase becomes a boarding action—the party is now in active combat with overwhelmingly superior numbers. OR: The party flees into the storm and the ship is damaged so badly it begins to sink. The party must abandon ship in boats, losing cargo and supplies, and is now stranded in the shallows or open water (transition to survival scenario). |
| **Unexpected solution** | The party negotiates with Harrim or Vex while sailing. Harrim breaks from the pursuit due to ship damage and honor (the party bested him twice). Vex accepts a bribe, false information, or a genuine parley and breaks off, satisfied that the party is too much trouble. The party sails free, but the Scattered Wraith now considers them clever enemies worthy of direct action (not just hired hunters). |

**Changed state.** The party has proven they can evade, outmaneuver, and survive coordinated pursuit. The Scattered Wraith recognizes them as a genuine threat and will not send corsairs again—the next response will be larger and more lethal. The party is approaching the Outer Reef, the location marked "FORBIDDEN" on the pirate charts. The storm is now a real threat; if the party did not reach shelter, they will be caught in it within hours.

**Immediate question.** The Outer Reef is close. But it is marked "FORBIDDEN" on Scattered Wraith charts, and the shallows are littered with wrecks and hidden dangers. Did the corsairs know something the party does not? Is the Reef itself trapped, hazardous, or haunted? As the party navigates the narrowing channels and deepening darkness, what do they encounter?

## Cliffhanger 3: The Blocked Channel (Obstacles/Traps)

**Card:** Obstacles/Traps

**Trigger.** The party has outrun or evaded the pirate pursuit and is now navigating the narrow channels that lead to the Outer Reef. The water here is treacherous—hidden rocks, sudden shallows, and the remains of dozens of wrecked ships create a maze that only careful navigation can penetrate. Your crew is exhausted from the chase. The storm is minutes away, and you need to find the protected anchorage beyond the Reef before the weather turns catastrophic. As the lead boat sounds the depth and charts the route, a crew member shouts: "Captain, there's wreckage ahead—a lot of it. The channel is blocked." What you see is not natural: the wrecks are positioned deliberately, forming a barrier. Someone has blockaded the passage. The water is getting shallower. Behind you, the storm wall is rising, black and violent. You have minutes to decide.

**PC objective.** Navigate through, around, or over the blockade and reach the protected anchorage. Secondary: discover who set this trap and why the Outer Reef is marked "FORBIDDEN."

**Opposition objective.** The Scattered Wraith does not want ships reaching the Outer Reef. The blockade was set to prevent access and to ensure that anyone foolish enough to attempt the passage is either turned back or trapped when the seasonal storm arrives. If the party persists, the blockade's guardians (if any) will attempt to force the party to turn back or run aground.

**Stakes.** The approaching storm will sink the ship if it catches you in open water. The blockade forces a choice: attempt a risky passage through the wreckage, turn back and accept that the Scattered Wraith has trapped you outside the Reef, or ground the ship deliberately in the shallows and continue on foot (abandoning the vessel and cargo). If you attempt to navigate the wreckage without care, the ship's hull will be torn open. Time is running out.

**Ends when.** The party reaches clear water beyond the blockade, deliberately grounds the ship, turns back to sea, or the ship strikes wreckage and begins to sink.

> [!narration] Open on Action
> The wrecks loom out of the gray-green water like the ribs of drowned beasts. A caravel, keel-up and splintered. A merchant brig, listing so far that its mast is nearly horizontal. Smaller vessels—dinghies, cutters, even a full galleon—piled in deliberate rows, their timbers dark with age, their hulls breached and splintered. The water between them is a narrow maze. You can see the channel—barely wide enough for your ship to pass through at a diagonal. But the wreckage is shifting slightly in the current, and something about the arrangement feels chosen, deliberate. The storm wall behind you now blocks half the sky. The wind is picking up. Your crew is silent, waiting.

### Run the Beat

| Need at the table | Prep |
|---|---|
| **Opposition** | The blockade itself is the primary opposition—a hazard, not an enemy with agency. However, if the party investigates the wrecks closely, they will discover **evidence of the Scattered Wraith's hand**: salvaged fittings, markers carved into hull boards, and (on the largest wreck) a banner. If the party lingers too long, a **Scattered Wraith patrol boat** (8 crew, armed with crossbows and light weapons) will appear from a hidden inlet to the west, attempting to force the party back to sea or into the storm. The patrol is a deterrent, not a fight—they will not pursue beyond the blockade's boundary and will withdraw if the party pursues them aggressively. |
| **Default motion** | The current is pulling debris into the ship's path. Every minute spent in the blockade zone, make a navigation/piloting check (DC 12). On a failure, the ship strikes wreckage lightly (takes minor damage, 1d4 hull integrity). On a critical failure, the ship strikes severely (takes serious damage, 3d6 hull integrity, and is now partially stuck—the crew must work for a full round to free it). |
| **Pressure** | **Time**: The storm is 10 minutes away. Attempting to navigate carefully takes time (at least 3 rounds of cautious sailing). **Damage accumulation**: The ship is already damaged from the chase; more damage means risking sinking. **Crew fatigue**: The crew is exhausted and making mistakes (navigation checks have disadvantage). **Choice paralysis**: Turning back means the Scattered Wraith has defeated the party without a fight; proceeding means risking the ship; grounding means losing mobility and supplies. |
| **Leverage** | The wreckage itself can be moved or cleared: a strong character can push smaller debris aside (1 round per piece), or the crew can use grappling hooks to clear a path (2 rounds for 3–4 pieces). The largest wrecks are obstacles but offer shelter from the storm (the party can attempt to shelter under an overturned hull, though it is cramped and risky). The patrol boat's position reveals a hidden inlet—if the party can reach it, they can anchor there and wait out the storm (but they would be sharing shelter with Scattered Wraith pirates, unknown to them until they arrive). The blockade has gaps: careful navigation can thread through most of it without moving debris. A crew member who studied the charts (if the party asked about local knowledge) might remember rumors of a deep-water channel that bypasses the blockade entirely (risky, but possible). |
| **Danger** | The wreckage is unpredictable—rotten timbers can splinter without warning. The storm will arrive, and if the party is still amid the debris, they risk being smashed against the wrecks. The hidden patrol boat, if encountered, can force a combat situation or chase. The largest wrecks are so old that their rigging is rotted—if the party attempts to climb them, there is a risk of falling. |
| **Ways out** | **Navigate carefully**: Make sailing/navigation checks to thread through the blockade safely (no debris moved, takes time, multiple checks increase risk). **Clear the path**: Have crew or strong characters physically move smaller wrecks and debris aside (takes time, reduces later risk, is exhausting). **Use the leverage**: Identify the hidden inlet (requires investigation or local knowledge check) and shelter there temporarily, or use known gaps to avoid the worst of the blockade. **Abandon ship**: Ground the ship deliberately in the shallows beyond the blockade, transfer cargo to boats, and continue on foot or via smaller vessels (risky; cargo is lost; crew is stranded). **Gamble on depth**: Attempt to sail around the blockade in deeper water (the Outer Reef is hazardous; this risks striking uncharted rocks, but bypasses the deliberate blockade). **Call the bluff**: If the party realizes the patrol boat is a deterrent and not a true combatant, they can charge through with confidence, or negotiate/intimidate the patrol into standing down. **Wait out the storm**: Attempt to shelter under an overturned wreck until the storm passes (risky; the shelter might collapse; crew morale suffers). |

#### Opposition (Hazard + Potential Combat)

**The Blockade Itself:**
- **Composition**: Approximately 40–50 wrecked vessels of varying sizes, arranged in overlapping lines to create a maze. Smaller debris (spars, planks, rigging) floats among the larger wrecks.
- **Current**: A 3-knot current runs through the area, pulling debris in a counterclockwise pattern. The current is strong enough to move a ship off course if the party does not actively steer.
- **Hazard progression**: Light strike (minor damage, no stuck) → heavy strike (serious damage, ship partially stuck, needs 1 round to free) → severe strike (ship's hull breached, begins to sink, must be abandoned in 2 rounds).

**The Patrol Boat (Optional Combat):**
If the party remains in the blockade zone for more than 5 rounds, or if they investigate the wrecks closely, a patrol boat emerges from a hidden inlet to the west. The boat carries **8 crew: 1 captain (henchman-tier), 4 crossbowmen, 3 oarsmen**. The captain will hail the party and order them to turn back. He will fire warning shots (arrows, not lethal) if the party ignores him. He will not pursue beyond the blockade's boundary.

**Intent** (Patrol): Enforce the blockade and prevent passage without directly engaging in a major fight. The patrol boat is a test of resolve, not a true combat threat.

**Opening move**: The patrol boat approaches from the side, moving fast under oars. The captain stands and shouts a warning in the Common Tongue of the Shattered Sea.

**Behavior**: The patrol will match the party's speed and try to force them toward the wreckage or back toward open sea. They will not board unless the party boards them first. If the party returns fire, they will fire back but will attempt to disengage if taking heavy casualties.

**Breaking point**: If 4 or more patrol crew are killed, the captain will signal a retreat and disappear back into the hidden inlet. If the party attempts to follow into the inlet, they discover the Scattered Wraith's hidden dock (a potential discovery for later gameplay, but not explored now).

**Resources**: The patrol boat (12-foot longboat, 8 crew, 4 crossbows, small amount of cargo—supplies for a week).

#### Pressure

**Telegraph**: The wreckage starts moving slightly in a coordinated pattern—the current is increasing. The storm wall is now clearly defined, with lightning visible at its core. A crew member shouts: "The water is shallower than the charts say!"

**Escalate**: The ship strikes debris lightly (1d4 damage). A larger wreck shifts in the current, blocking the channel the party was planning to navigate. The patrol boat appears and fires a warning shot. The wind gusts suddenly, nearly knocking crew members overboard. The storm is now 5 minutes away—the light is failing, visibility is dropping.

**Breaking point**: The ship's hull groans (it is damaged enough that another serious strike could breach it). The patrol boat fires a volley of arrows—some hit crew, some miss but are clearly meant as a final warning. The storm wall is nearly overhead; the first raindrops are falling; the wind is howling. The party must commit to a choice NOW—navigate, clear, ground, or retreat—because in one round, the storm will be too dangerous to maneuver in.

### Obstacle Field

**Space**: The blockade zone covers approximately 500 feet from east to west and 300 feet from north to south. The safest route through is 200 feet of careful navigation; alternate routes exist but are longer or more hazardous. The protected anchorage is beyond the blockade, 400 feet to the east. The open sea is 300 feet to the west. The shallows (where the party could ground the ship) are 200 feet to the north.

**Hazards**:
- **Submerged wreckage** (20 pieces): Large sections of broken hull, masts, spars. Striking one deals 1d4 damage (minor) or 3d6 damage (severe, if the ship is already partially stuck).
- **Floating debris** (numerous): Planks, rigging, smaller wreckage. Moving through an area with dense debris requires an active check each round; failure means striking debris and taking damage.
- **The current** (3 knots): Pulls all vessels counterclockwise. If the party does not actively steer, they will be pushed 50 feet off course per round.
- **The wreckage barrier** (the largest wrecks): Form an intentional maze. There are 3–4 viable passages through them; all require careful navigation or active clearing.
- **Depth variation**: The water ranges from 15 feet (safe) to 4 feet (dangerous for a ship drawing 8 feet). Sudden shallows are hazards; the party's navigator or crew can spot them with a successful check.

**Interactive features**:
- **The largest wrecks (4 of them, each 80–120 feet long)**: Can be climbed (acrobatics check, risk of falling). From atop a wreck, a character can see further and identify the safest route. Investigation checks on a wreck reveal carvings, salvage marks, or the Scattered Wraith banner.
- **Smaller wreckage (moveable)**: Planks, spars, bits of hull. A character with strength or leverage can push them aside (1 round per 2–3 pieces). Clearing 4–5 pieces opens a new route through the blockade.
- **Grappling hooks and lines**: The crew has these aboard. Using them to clear debris is faster than manual pushing (2 rounds to clear a 20-foot passage instead of 4).
- **The hidden inlet** (to the west): If the party investigates, they can see it is a narrow waterway that the patrol boat uses. Entering it risks ambush but could lead to shelter or secrets.
- **The protected anchorage** (beyond the blockade, to the east): Visible as an area of calmer water. Reaching it takes 2–3 rounds of careful sailing or 1 round of risky speed-running (with risk of striking wreckage).

**Change**: As the storm approaches, the current increases (from 3 to 5 knots by the end of the beat). The wind direction shifts, making some routes easier and others harder. If the party takes too long, the visibility drops, making navigation checks harder. The wreckage itself remains static, but the current and weather make it feel more dangerous with each passing round.

### Discoveries

- **A journal (found in the captain's quarters of the largest wreck)**: Describes the blockade as a "prison" and mentions that the Scattered Wraith is "guarding something" beyond the Reef. The journal's author died in place, decades ago.
- **A salvage tag on multiple wrecks**: Carved into the wood, bearing the Scattered Wraith sigil and dates. The oldest tag is 8 years old; the newest is 3 months old. This confirms active, ongoing blockade maintenance.
- **A crew member (if the party rescues survivors from a wreck, unlikely but possible)**: A former Scattered Wraith slave who was left behind. She knows the location of the hidden inlet and will guide the party there if offered shelter. She will also reveal that the Scattered Wraith guards the Reef because something valuable—or dangerous—is there.
- **A map fragment**: Found in sealed wreckage, showing a route through the blockade (saves 1 round of navigation time and reduces risk of collision).

## Resolution

| Outcome | What changes |
|---|---|
| **Objective gained** | The party navigates through the blockade safely (with or without clearing debris) and reaches the protected anchorage before the storm arrives. The crew reaches shelter. The party has time to rest, repair the ship properly, and investigate what lies beyond the Reef. Success is clear and undeniable—the party is now in a safe harbor, protected from pursuit and weather. |
| **Costly success** | The party reaches the anchorage, but the ship takes additional damage (hull breached, rigging torn, serious structural compromise). The anchorage itself is damaged or endangered—the shelter is partial, not complete. The party must choose to spend a day repairing or risk sinking in heavy weather. Crew morale is shaken; supplies are low. One crew member is lost (drowned or swept overboard during the blockade crossing). |
| **Withdrawal / defeat** | The party is forced to turn back to open sea (the blockade is impassable; the risk is too great). They must sail back into pursuit, abandon the mission, or seek an alternate route that takes days longer. OR: The ship strikes wreckage and begins to sink. The party must abandon ship in boats, losing cargo and most of the ship. The crew is now stranded and must find the hidden inlet or the anchorage on foot/by boat—a survival scenario begins. |
| **Unexpected solution** | The party negotiates with or bribes the patrol boat captain, who reveals the hidden inlet and offers safe passage (they will not oppose the party if the party stands down from hostility). The party shelters with the Scattered Wraith patrol at the hidden dock (awkward but necessary during the storm) and learns direct intelligence about the blockade's purpose. OR: The party discovers the hidden inlet on their own and uses it as an alternate route, bypassing the blockade entirely—a clever, risky maneuver that wins the party respect among the crew. |

**Changed state**: The party has reached the Outer Reef. They are no longer being pursued; the storm has blocked the passage behind them. The Scattered Wraith cannot reach them here. The party is now in a protected space, but they are also trapped—until the storm passes and the sea calms, they cannot leave. Supplies are getting low; the ship needs repairs. Most importantly, the party now knows the Scattered Wraith is deliberately guarding something beyond this Reef. What is it? Why is it forbidden? The adventure proper is about to begin.

**Immediate question**: The party has reached shelter, but the storm will last hours, maybe a full day. As the rain and wind howl, a crew member brings news: they've seen lights moving in the deep water beyond the anchorage—not natural, not wreckage. Something is out there, moving in the storm. The party is safe for now, but not alone.

## Chaining Without Developments: Summary

These three Cliffhangers are designed to flow directly into one another without pause for Development beats. The physical situation changes with each:

1. **Battle**: Ambushed on deck → Ship damaged, threat identified (Scattered Wraith is hunting you)
2. **Chase**: Pursued by pirate fleet → Forced to navigate to safety, threat escalates (multiple ships, overwhelming odds)
3. **Obstacles**: Blockade blocks escape → Forced to navigate hazardous terrain, threat becomes environmental (storm, wreckage, current)

Each beat's resolution triggers the next:
- Battle's "Immediate question" (Are other ships coming?) → answered by Chase's Trigger
- Chase's changed state (Approaching the Reef, storm building) → answered by Obstacles' Trigger
- Obstacles' changed state (Reached safety, storm blocks pursuit) → development begins only after beat chain ends

The party's objectives evolve:
1. Survive the boarding attack (local, immediate)
2. Escape the pursuit (tactical, time-pressured)
3. Navigate the blockade (strategic, environmental challenge)

No Developments are inserted because there is no time for them—the action drives forward without pause.
