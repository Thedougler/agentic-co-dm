# Cliffhanger-Beats Skill Test: Transcript of Reasoning

## Task Interpretation

Write three consecutive Cliffhangers (battle → chase → trap) with no Developments in between. Keep the action rolling without pause for discussion, investigation, or planning. This tests whether the skill supports chaining action beats that feed into one another seamlessly.

## Skill Constraints Applied

### Core Instruction: Follow the Completion Test
- Each beat must resolve a contest (outcome stays in doubt)
- Physical situation must change measurably
- DM must be able to state what changed and name the next beat's trigger

### Card Selection Strategy
- **Beat 1: Battle** – Multiple combatants test the party's ability to coordinate and identify the enemy's strength/weakness
- **Beat 2: Chase** – Tests the escape route discovered or forced during the Battle (direction of retreat, terrain advantages, allies met)
- **Beat 3: Obstacles/Traps** – Blocks or complicates the escape route identified in the Chase, forcing a final decision before the beat chain ends

### Thread Testing (Prep Instruction Step 2)
Each beat tests what the previous one revealed:
- Battle reveals that the enemy is coordinated and has mages; the party learns the direction the enemy came from
- Chase tests whether the party can outrun/outmaneuver the enemy through known terrain; reveals a bottleneck or vulnerability
- Trap blocks the only viable escape route, forcing one last contest before a Development can begin

### Situation Writing (Prep Instruction Step 3)
For each beat:
- Clear trigger (what makes action necessary NOW)
- Named actors (opposition + party objectives)
- Visible stakes and information
- At least two viable player responses beyond "do the default action"

### Polarity Handoff (Prep Instruction Step 4)
Each beat ends with a clear handoff condition:
- Battle won → Development (new alliances or stronghold learned)
- Battle lost/costly → Chase (forced to retreat)
- Chase won → Trap discovery (or next beat trigger)
- Chase lost → new constraint (captured, separated, or position worsened)
- Trap navigated → Development (new safe zone or information)
- Trap failed → new pressure (hidden enemy reinforces, timer resets)

### Mechanical Considerations
- No Developments allowed between beats — each triggers the next immediately
- "No Developments in between" means: no pause for rest, investigation, or planning; action flows directly
- Each beat must logically require/enable the next one

## Fictional Context

**Campaign:** Shattered Sea (existing wiki context)

**Scenario:** A naval expedition discovers a pirate fleet's advanced outpost and triggers a response. The three beats follow one incident escalation:
1. **Battle** – Surprise attack by a coordinated pirate strike team that suddenly manifests on the deck
2. **Chase** – Pirate ship(s) pursuing the party's vessel toward the shallows (the only escape route the battle revealed)
3. **Trap** – A concealed reef, abandoned dockyard, or prepared fortification blocks the escape route (discovered during the chase)

This keeps the physical situation changing: position (engaged → fleeing → cornered), safety (ambushed → pursued → trapped), and time pressure (immediate threat → closing distance → final stand).

## Beat Structure Applied

Each beat follows the template jobs:
- **Trigger** – What makes action necessary now
- **PC objective** – What concrete result ends the contest
- **Opposition objective** – What the enemy is trying to accomplish
- **Stakes** – What the party can lose
- **Ends when** – Observable condition for beat resolution
- **Theatre of mind** – Open on Action (2–4 sentences, danger + position + senses, no prescripted response)
- **Run the Beat** – Opposition, Default motion, Pressure, Leverage, Danger, Ways out
- **Opposition** – Intent, Opening move, Behavior, Breaking point, Resources
- **Pressure** – Escalation toolkit: Telegraph → Escalate → Breaking point
- **Battlefield/Chase/Hazard** – Space, Hazard, Interactive features, Change, Map (TotM)
- **Discoveries** – Portable clues (optional)
- **Resolution** – Outcome table: Objective gained / Costly success / Withdrawal / Unexpected solution
- **Handoff** – Changed state, Immediate question, Next trigger

## Chaining Without Developments

To avoid Developments between beats:
- Battle's handoff question is answered immediately by the Chase trigger
- Chase's changed state (position/direction) sets up the Trap without pause
- No "rest" or "discussion" beats allowed

Example flow:
- Battle resolves: "The pirate strike team is routed and you now know their ships are coming from the south"
- Chase begins immediately: "As you turn the ship south toward the shallows, you hear the crack of a sail — the lead pirate ship appears on the horizon, closing fast"
- Chase resolves: "You reach the channel leading through the shallows, but ahead you see wreckage blocking the waterway"
- Trap begins immediately: "You recognize the wreckage as a deliberate blockade — the enemy is herding you into the shallows, and the water ahead is littered with obstacles"

## Key Guardrails

- **Outcome not predetermined** – Each contest is genuine; party choices determine the result
- **Player agency honored** – At least two viable options per beat; no forced movement
- **Tangible changes** – Each beat materially changes position, resources, or safety
- **Clear triggers** – No ambiguity about why action is necessary NOW
- **Opposition credibility** – Enemies have real objectives; they're not just obstacles to delay the party

## Expected Test Result

The three beats should demonstrate that the skill produces:
1. ✓ Consecutive action-focused prose with no downtime
2. ✓ Chained physical-situation changes (position → safety → time)
3. ✓ Each beat tests what the previous one revealed
4. ✓ Clear, contested outcomes that honor player agency
5. ✓ Seamless handoffs that make the next trigger obvious
