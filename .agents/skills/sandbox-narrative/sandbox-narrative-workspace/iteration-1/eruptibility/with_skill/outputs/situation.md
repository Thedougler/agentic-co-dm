# Corvenna: The Arrived Dispute

## Overview

Corvenna appears peaceful and ceremonial when the party arrives, but a dispute is in formal process—in the quiet stage before the duel. The parties and champions are present but offer no unsolicited information. Discovery of what this dispute means, who the parties are, and how the site's neutrality is enforced depends entirely on what the party asks.

---

## Procedure Application

### 1. Inventory the World

**Established facts from wiki:**
- Corvenna is neutral ground in eastern Midchain
- Disputes are settled by champion combat instead of war
- The site functions: people arrive, champions fight, results are binding
- The people who keep neutrality and the rules themselves are Unknown
- Eastern reef water environment; unreliable charts; distant settlement

**Current state:**
- No active hostile pressure visible
- A dispute is present in formal process (a fact established in the wiki: "Champions and the parties to Midchain disputes come to Corvenna")
- The site is operating normally; ceremony underway

**Complete:** Situation grounds in wiki-established facts; added dispute is consistent with Corvenna's purpose.

---

### 2. Name the Forces

**Force: The Arrived Dispute**
- **Wants:** Settlement through champion combat; preservation of the site's neutrality
- **Without the party:** The dispute would proceed to duel; neutral process would conclude; parties would leave; Corvenna would return to waiting
- **Visible tell:** Quiet formal gathering; two separated groups; preliminary ceremony; no one volunteers details
- **Independent next move:** The duel happens. Champions fight. One side is victorious. The loser accepts the result or refuses it (creating a new problem for the site's enforcement)

**Force: The Site Itself (Unknown Authorities)**
- **Wants:** Maintain neutrality; ensure results are binding; prevent disputes from becoming wars
- **Without the party:** The process runs; enforcement happens invisibly; the site continues to function
- **Visible tell:** The ceremony proceeds smoothly; someone is clearly orchestrating it despite no named authority being present
- **Independent next move:** After the duel, the winner enforces their claim; the loser either accepts or the enforcer acts

---

### 3. Connect to PCs

**No specific PCs are named in the prompt.** The situation must work for any party arriving at Corvenna.

**Universal entry points:**
- **Moral:** Outsiders witness a dispute whose terms they don't understand and whose consequences they may not accept
- **Opportunistic:** The party might exploit the dispute's outcome, interfere with it, or use the neutral setting for their own purposes
- **Investigative:** Learning who enforces the site reveals unknown power structures in the Midchain

**Gravity sources:** Any party has agency here—they can watch, ask questions, refuse to accept rules, support a champion, or leave. The situation does not require a specific PC backstory.

---

### 4. Open the Routes

**At least two materially different approaches (multiple clue chains):**

**Route 1: Direct Inquiry (Social / Information)**
- Party asks the obvious questions: "What's this dispute about?" "Who are these people?" "What happens if someone refuses?"
- Answers vary by question:
  - Parties to the dispute deflect or refuse to discuss terms (the dispute's privacy is part of the process)
  - Ceremony participants (the Unknown Authorities) answer directly but reveal nothing about the site's enforcement or ownership
  - Other present witnesses (traders, other parties waiting) may gossip or share rumors about past disputes
- **Consequence:** Direct answers accelerate the party's decision about involvement; they may be asked to serve as witnesses or judges

**Route 2: Observation & Investigation (Exploration)**
- Party watches the ceremony without asking; maps the participants; notes the sequence
- Can investigate the site's layout: where do champions fight? who prepared the space? are there defenses?
- Can follow participants between formal moments to catch unguarded conversation
- Can ask targeted follow-ups based on observed patterns
- **Consequence:** The party discovers operational details (who coordinates, what equipment is present, what happens to the loser's effects) without drawing direct attention

**Route 3: Refusal or Interference (Confrontation)**
- Party refuses to accept the site's rules, shelters a losing champion, or attempts to nullify the duel
- This forces the Unknown Authorities to act visibly in enforcement
- **Consequence:** Direct conflict or negotiation with the site's guardians; relationship state changes

**Route 4: Walk Away (Avoidance)**
- Party recognizes this is not their business and departs
- **Consequence:** No permanent change; the dispute concludes; if they return later, they carry news of what happened

**No single chokepoint:** The dispute proceeds regardless of party action. The duel will happen. The question is what the party learns and whether they intervene.

---

### 5. Audit Authored Choices

**Removed:**
- No fixed outcome for the duel (party does not determine the winner)
- No scripted dialogue for the site's authorities (they react, not recite)
- No required emotional response from the party
- No if/then chain ("if they ask X, then Y automatically happens")
- No authored success condition

**Given agency back to the world:**
- The site operates on its own terms
- The dispute's parties have their own stakes and will not be swayed by the party's moral judgment
- The ceremony proceeds unless the party physically stops it
- Authorities appear only when needed; they do not monologue or explain
- The duel itself is uncertain—champions have genuine skill; outcomes are not predetermined

---

### 6. Write Outcomes as Changed States

**If party observes and asks questions, without interfering:**
- **Knowledge:** The party learns the dispute's terms and outcome; may hear rumors about past disputes or the site's history
- **Relationship:** The site's authorities recognize the party as observers; neutral stance toward them is established
- **Position:** The party can choose to depart or linger; if they helped (water, shelter), they've built minor goodwill
- **Clock:** If the outcome angers the losers, word may spread; the site's reputation as a neutral arbiter may be tested

**If party intervenes or refuses to accept the result:**
- **Relationship:** Direct conflict or negotiation with the Unknown Authorities; the party's standing depends on how they act
- **Resources:** If they shelter a champion or resist enforcement, they may gain an ally or enemy
- **Knowledge:** The party discovers enforcement mechanisms they would not otherwise see
- **Position:** Their presence marks them as a force; future disputes or interactions will reference this incident

**If party refuses to engage and leaves:**
- **Relationship:** The site is neutral toward them; no status change
- **Position:** If they return, the dispute's outcome will have shaped what happened in the interim
- **Knowledge:** None; the mystery of the site remains intact

**Four-corner test:**

- **Late arrival (party arrives after the duel begins):** The situation works—they must choose whether to watch the conclusion, interfere mid-fight, or leave; their questions are answered by what they observe
- **Negotiation (party tries to bargain with site authorities or parties):** Authorities are willing to listen; they enforce the site's rules but do not invent exceptions; parties might negotiate with each other if the party bridges them
- **Avoidance (party explicitly refuses to be involved):** The situation resolves without them; they depart intact; the site continues to function
- **Unexpected capability (party uses magic, divine intervention, or revelation):** The site's authorities react to violations of neutrality (magic during a duel, divine punishment of a champion); the breach becomes the new problem

## Clarity Test

**Actionable summary (< 2 sentences):**
A formal duel is underway at Corvenna to settle an unknown dispute between unknown parties. The party can learn about it, interfere with it, or ignore it; the outcome depends on what they ask and whether they accept the site's rules.

---

## Agency Test

- **Fight:** Attack the champions or authorities (violates neutrality; site's enforcement activates)
- **Bargain:** Negotiate with the parties or authorities (both willing to talk within the site's framework)
- **Investigate:** Ask questions and watch (fully enabled; no barriers to observation or inquiry)
- **Exploit:** Use the dispute's chaos or the site's hospitality for personal gain (possible; depends on party creativity)
- **Redirect:** Try to resolve the dispute themselves; offer alternatives to duel (authorities refuse; disputes are settled by combat, not negotiation)
- **Ignore:** Treat the site as a way-station and focus on own goals (fully possible; no pressure to engage)

No required solution. All paths are valid.

---

## Reactivity Test

**Independent movement (happens without the party):**

- The duel happens on schedule unless physically stopped
- The winner is determined by actual combat, not by party influence
- The loser either accepts the result or the site's authorities enforce it
- The parties depart after the binding result
- If the party does not return, the site returns to waiting; future disputes will arrive and be settled the same way

**Consequence of inaction:**
The duel concludes. One side is victorious. The site's neutrality is preserved. The system works.

**Consequence of action:**
Whatever the party does reshapes the immediate narrative: interference forces enforcement; questions change what they know; alliance shifts relationship state.

---

## Portability Test

**Works for different parties:**
- A party of pacifists can investigate peacefully or refuse engagement entirely
- A party seeking allies can try to befriend either side
- A party seeking treasure can ignore the dispute and search the site
- A militaristic party can try to commandeer the space
- A morally driven party can question whether the duel is just

**Works for different locations in future sittings:**
The model applies to any ceremonial neutral ground with Unknown authorities—trade posts, temples, council halls, magical circles.

---

## Summary

**What the party learns depends on what they ask.** Corvenna is peaceful because its system works invisibly. The site's strength is that no one needs to enforce the rules loudly—the rules are *believed in*. The party's questions are the lever; the answers depend on which questions they ask and whether they trust or challenge the outcome.

The Unknown Authorities are not mysterious for mystery's sake—they are unavailable for interrogation until the party creates a reason for them to emerge. That emergence is the adventure.
