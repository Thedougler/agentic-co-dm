# Sandbox Narrative

File what constitution X makes canon. Follow `docs/agents/work.md`.

## Four tests

Every major situation element passes four tests. Use them as leading words
when auditing or designing.

- **Clarity.** Actionable without a lore dump. The DM can explain the element
  to a player in two sentences and the player can act on it.
- **Agency.** The party can fight, bargain, investigate, exploit, redirect, or
  ignore. A required solution is a failed test; replace it with visible
  pressure, stakes, clues, and state-based consequences.
- **Reactivity.** Actors move when untouched. The element changes on its own
  timeline: factions advance, clocks tick, opportunities close.
- **Portability.** Works for different places, parties, and approaches. The
  situation is not load-bearing on one PC, one location, or one plan.

Add moral, personal, and opportunistic entry points where appropriate.

## Procedure

1. **Inventory the world.** Read current location, fronts/quests, faction
   resources, deadlines, promises, unresolved clues, and off-screen movement
   from `hot.md` and `qmd-retrieval`.
   Complete when: every reused fact traces to current canon.

2. **Name the forces.** Who wants what, what happens without intervention,
   what is visible, and the pressure's independent next move.
   Complete when: every force has a want, a next move, and a visible tell.

3. **Connect to PCs.** Retrieve two active tensions, a long-term desire, and
   current friction. Name at least one PC connection. If no real connection
   exists, cut the element or ask for one.
   Complete when: the element has gravity from an established PC interest.

4. **Open the routes.** Offer at least two materially different approaches and
   a way to walk away. For a hidden conclusion, seed three distinct clues in
   different nodes/channels; at least two must be reachable without combat.
   Complete when: no single chokepoint gates all progress.

5. **Audit authored choices.** Remove authored PC choices, feelings, success,
   fixed dialogue, and if/then chains deeper than one step. Give opposition
   pre-existing agency.
   Complete when: every verb in the prep belongs to the world, not the PCs.

6. **Write outcomes as changed states.** Relationships, resources, knowledge,
   position, or clocks. Test late arrival, negotiation, avoidance, and an
   unexpected capability.
   Complete when: each outcome changes the world materially and the audit
   survives the four-corner test (late, negotiated, avoided, unexpected).

Use clocks to make inaction costly, foreshadow conditions rather than one
foretold event, and use in-medias-res openings when they remove dead time. A
seed is a loaded possibility, not a promised payoff. Never mutate canon or
runtime in this audit; route actual clock movement to `world-tick` and
post-play truth to `reconciling-session-evidence`.

## Eruptibility

Design conflicts to be **eruptible**: actors, pressures, and tells can
collide when the PCs arrive, without waiting for a scripted cue. Every choice
and every meaningful silence echoes through changed position, knowledge,
resources, relationships, or clocks. Give factions and strongholds independent
momentum, with consequences of inaction visible early enough to choose. A
quiet table gets a pause and a clear opening, not panic-filled narration.

## Handoffs
