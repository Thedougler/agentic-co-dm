# Outdoor and island megadungeon scaling

This is a scaling model, not a campaign canon source. Retrieve campaign vault
facts through qmd-retrieval; when the vault is silent, say so and leave a clearly
marked stub.

## Nest the map

Use four nested scales:

1. **Island / Region:** the campaign's broad political, ecological, and travel graph.
2. **Landmark:** a named coast, ridge, settlement, ruin, inlet, or other node.
3. **Nested site:** a cave, wreck, fort, grove, reef, or dungeon within that node.
4. **Room / scene:** a local decision with routes, pressure, and consequences.

The nesting lets an island function as a megadungeon without pretending every
square mile is a keyed room. Each scale has durable geography, mutable actors,
pressure, payoffs, and return routes.

## Route classes

Name the route class in the region graph because each creates a different
choice, exposure, and travel procedure:

- **Coast:** tides, beaches, surf, sightlines, boats, landing politics.
- **River:** current, crossings, upstream/downstream logistics, shared access.
- **Terrace:** elevation, switchbacks, loose ground, exposure, chokepoints.
- **Forest:** concealment, lost time, tracks, canopy, local knowledge.
- **Air:** altitude, weather, visibility, landing, carrying capacity, aerial threat.
- **Underway:** caves, tunnels, submerged or buried connections, air and maps.

Do not turn route names into flavor labels. For every class write its cost,
risk, information, available vehicles/companions, and what it bypasses.

## Risk gradient, not level walls

A party should be able to see and attempt routes above or below its expected
power. Signal risk through weather, exposure, faction control, scarcity,
terrain, time, and the behavior of inhabitants. Do not use invisible level gates.
A dangerous route can offer a shortcut, information, shelter, or political access.

Flight must be honest. Record what flight bypasses and what it cannot: weather,
visibility, landing, exhaustion/resource cost, carrying limits, aerial exposure,
settlement rules, and the need to interact with what is below. Never silently
cancel flight because the map designer wanted a ground route; make the tradeoff
playable.

## Region connection grammar

For each node, draw at least some of:

- **Approach:** where the party enters and what the route reveals.
- **Cross-connection:** a loop between routes or zones.
- **Bypass:** a costly, informed, factional, or unusual alternative.
- **Vertical link:** terrace, cliff, lift, flight, sinkhole, or deep route.
- **Return path:** what makes an expedition home possible and what can change it.
- **Political gate:** permission, obligation, toll, alliance, or contested claim.

Then attach pressure to watches and travel. An island watch is 4 hours; check
pressure each watch. Site turns remain 10 minutes; check every 2 site turns.
On 1d6: normal **1**, alerted/hostile **1–2**, hunted/catastrophic **1–3**.
Define the consequence in advance.

## Illustrative coastal landing region (not campaign canon)

Use this as a graph-test placeholder. Replace or delete after campaign-vault retrieval.
Every name and detail in this section is an illustrative example, not campaign canon.

**Promise:** A narrow landing coast where stranded survivors, local patrols,
territorial packs, wreckage, oversized mangroves, terrace warrens, and a
dangerous headland compress the island's conflicts into one legible region.

**Regional goals (examples):** rescue or locate captives in a wrecked hull;
learn who controls the terrace warrens; identify smoke on a supposedly empty
beach; establish a resupply landing; find a safe inland route that avoids the
exposed headland channel; learn why animals ignore the hull.

**Landmark nodes (examples):**

| Node | Immediate identity | Durable gain |
|---|---|---|
| Landing cove | Reefs, narrow beach, recent wreckage | Safe anchorage after charting reefs |
| Wrecked hull | Captives, shade, wildlife exclusion | Survivors, testimony, potential refuge |
| Dune line | Concealed ambush ground | Pack behavior and warning signs |
| Mangrove creek | Oversized roots, tidal water | Inland water route |
| Terrace warrens | Flooded ditches, local patrol activity | Intelligence, alliance, or controlled pass |
| Headland channel | Exposed chokepoint watched by predators | Fast coastal bypass if secured |
| Smoke beach | Thin steady smoke where no camp should be | New goal, contact, or mystery |

**Loops and skips:** Cove → Wreck → Dune → Headland → Cove by water; Cove →
Creek → Warrens → High path → Smoke beach; a concealed terrace drainage toward
the next river/swamp region; a dangerous cliff traverse that bypasses the
headland pack but exposes the party to air and weather.

**Powers (examples):** Local patrol (control, information, containment —
passage or harder movement); territorial packs (learnable predators,
not disposable random fights); survivors/captives (contradictory maps, needs,
knowledge — possible coastal support); wreck shade/presence (why wildlife
avoids the hull — not solved by ordinary clearance).

**Pressure and change:** Tide changes usable coastal routes; violence shifts
local patrols and may move captives; removing one pack opens territory for
another predator or forager; rescuing survivors can create a haven only if
water, concealment, and the wreck presence are addressed; securing headland or
creek permanently changes travel into adjacent regions.

**Anti-clearance:** Do not treat every inhabited settlement or nonhuman society as a
monster room to empty. Define internal factions, civilians, needs, laws,
taboos, dissent, and trade. Progress comes through changing a political
landscape, not declaring inhabited land “cleared.”

## Expedition and update

1. Choose a goal, route class, loadout, and political posture.
2. Travel in watches; mark time, risk, weather, and what becomes visible.
3. Enter the landmark or nested site and establish the current pressure.
4. Gather clues through multiple vectors and choose whether to push or divert.
5. Take a payoff, bargain, or consequence; update route knowledge.
6. Retreat or continue through a route whose state may have changed.
7. After return, update factions, community politics, ecology, damage, and
   available goals. Restock only what the history explains.

Prepare only the next frontier: the next landmark, its route cards, active
powers, likely obstacles, information vectors, and payoffs. Leave farther nodes
as explicit stubs until player action makes them relevant.
