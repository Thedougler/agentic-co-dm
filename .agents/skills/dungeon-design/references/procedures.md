# Dungeon procedures and quality control

Use these at the table and during review. Procedures must be visible enough that
players can make decisions, but hidden mechanics belong in the GM layer.

## Pressure procedure

1. Name the clock: site, island, faction, weather, resource, or objective.
2. Establish the current alert: **quiet**, **wary**, or **alerted**.
3. Advance one site turn for roughly 10 minutes of searching, movement, or
   meaningful action. Advance one island watch for 4 hours of outdoor travel or
   camp activity.
4. Roll a pressure check every 2 site turns or every 1 watch. On 1d6 use the
   practical alert calibration: normal territory **1**; alerted/hostile **1–2**;
   actively hunted/catastrophic **1–3**. Tune after two or three sessions.
5. On a hit, apply the named consequence: patrol shifts, faction action, hazard
   worsens, route closes, resource is spent, or objective advances. Show a sign
   before the consequence when possible.
6. Let player actions change the state: silence, decoy, bargain, shortcut,
   lookout, map knowledge, shelter, or deliberate alarm.
7. After each scene record time, alert, consequence, and what the factions now
   know. Never reset pressure because a new room begins.

## Rest procedure

Use the 2024 terminology and state the fiction first.

- **Short Rest:** 1 hour. Ask: what shelter, watch, supplies, and nearby actors
  make this plausible? If the site is hostile, name the exposure or cost.
- **Long Rest:** 8 hours or more. Require a haven, defensible shelter, or a
  host arrangement appropriate to the fiction. If none exists, explain the
  hostile response rather than silently granting safety.
- **After a Long Rest:** use a 16-hour wait before another Long Rest as the
  practical procedure. During that wait advance time, faction plans, patrols,
  weather, supplies, and any visible objective.
- **Rest decision card:** location; who can find it; what it costs; what can
  interrupt it; what changes if the party leaves now; what safe alternative exists.

Rest is a strategic choice, not a vending machine. The GM records what was
learned and changed during the wait; do not respawn defeated creatures without
a causal explanation.

## Stocking procedure

1. List the site's promise, purpose, pressure, and active powers.
2. Place required routes and decision nodes before encounter content.
3. Distribute a mixture: inhabitants, social problems, hazards, clues,
   discoveries, resources, quiet landmarks, treasure, transitions, and some empty
   space. Use fewer entries when a room has a strong job.
4. For each entry write one interaction and one consequence. If neither exists,
   merge or delete the entry.
5. Place essential information in at least three vectors. Do not gate a required
   revelation behind one roll or one NPC.
6. Ask of every quiet room: **what can players learn, use, alter, or use as a
   route or mood beat?** A quiet answer may be “a safe map-reading shelter” or
   “a landmark that proves a loop,” not only a secret.
7. Audit geography: entrance, exit, retreat, cover, verticality, bypass, and
   relation to the decision graph.

## Encounter procedure

Write an encounter card before selecting a stat block:

- **Function:** why this scene is here.
- **Objective:** what each side is trying to accomplish now.
- **Stakes:** what changes on success, failure, delay, retreat, or surrender.
- **Terrain:** routes, cover, hazards, verticality, visibility, useful objects.
- **Information:** what can be learned or mislearned.
- **Escalation:** what changes after time, injury, alarm, or a new fact.
- **Off-ramps:** bargain, retreat, surrender, trade, stealth, objective completion,
  or another non-combat resolution.
- **Aftermath:** bodies, witnesses, faction response, opened/closed routes, clues.

Then use 2024 encounter math as a Low/Moderate/High estimate where helpful.
Budgets estimate difficulty; they do not supply purpose, terrain, fairness,
quality, or a reason to fight. Avoid using CR as a faction description.

## Obstacle procedure

For every obstacle write:

1. **Sign:** how players can notice the problem before committing.
2. **Trigger:** action, time, threshold, or condition that activates it.
3. **Effect:** immediate and lasting consequence.
4. **Counterplay:** a direct way to mitigate or solve it.
5. **Bypass:** another route, bargain, tool, timing, or cost.
6. **Leverage:** prior clue, faction relation, terrain, resource, or goal that
   makes a response better.

If Sign, Counterplay, or Bypass is blank, the obstacle is probably a gotcha.

## Table-running loop

At each meaningful choice: orient with visible routes and activity; offer the
information the fiction makes available; ask what the players do; resolve the
chosen action; advance time/pressure; update the ledger; stop at the next choice.
Do not narrate a player's conclusion or force the canonical route.

## QC checklist

- [ ] Promise, Purpose, Pressure, Paths, Powers, Payoffs, Persistence are all nonblank.
- [ ] The scale matches intended use: session design is not secretly a campaign.
- [ ] Decision graph exists before the room list; entrances, loop, bypass,
      retreat, vertical/unusual link, optional danger, and two objective
      approaches were considered.
- [ ] Every active faction has Want, Fear, Method, Resources, Tell, Offer, Response.
- [ ] Stocking is a mixture, not a fight quota; quiet rooms answer learn/use/route/mood.
- [ ] Every essential revelation has multiple vectors.
- [ ] Passive Perception and declared Search/Investigation are not conflated.
- [ ] Every obstacle has Sign, Trigger, Effect, Counterplay, Bypass, Leverage.
- [ ] Every encounter has a job, stakes, terrain, information, escalation,
      off-ramp, and aftermath; Low/Moderate/High is only a difficulty estimate.
- [ ] Keying starts with first impression, then activity, interactives, hidden,
      and mechanics.
- [ ] Rest fiction names haven/shelter/hostile response and time consequences.
- [ ] Megadungeon goals form a portfolio; frontier preparation is limited; update
      and restocking are causal rather than reset.
- [ ] Outdoor routes name Coast, River, Terrace, Forest, Air, or Underway where
      applicable; flight is useful but honest.
- [ ] Inhabited regions are not framed as societies to clear for completion.
- [ ] Player-facing prose is handed to theatre-of-the-mind; source-silent setting
      facts are marked as stubs, not invented.
