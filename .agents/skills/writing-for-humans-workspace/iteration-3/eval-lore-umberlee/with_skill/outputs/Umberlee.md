# Umberlee

> [!narration] Narration
> Every working harbour carries her mark cut into the dock stones. At the Shrine of the Bitch Queen, a grey-robed [[Waveservants|Waveservant]] keeps a ledger and a collection basin. Coin, rope, fish bones, buttons, compass needles, carved tokens—whatever a captain leaves, it records her admission: the sea can take her.

## Durable question

What does it cost to admit the sea can take you, and what happens if the harbours stop paying?

## At a Glance

- **Core truth.** Umberlee is the sea as it actually is—indifferent, hungry, and claimed by no one but her. She owns waves, storms, wrecks, and the fear that makes a captain check her shrines before checking the weather.
- **Why it matters.** Every working harbour pays her tribute. The [[pearl-of-souls]] lies beyond her reach in elemental water, but the [[red-lady-dead-lady]] works as her collection hull while that debt remains unpaid. Breaking tribute could unmake her hold.

## Current Truth

Umberlee's only demand is that a captain admit she can take him. Tribute is that admission. The [[Waveservants]] collect at the same rate from merchant, pirate, navy, smuggler, and pilgrim alike. Coin works best. Rope, fish bones, buttons, compass needles, carved tokens, or a spoken promise will serve.

She can sink a fleet on this side of the [[drowned-maw]]. She cannot reach into elemental water beyond it. The trench floor opens into the Elemental Plane of Water—her water ends where that other water begins. When the [[pearl-of-souls]] was stolen from [[vel-orn]], she could not take it back herself. Instead, she pressed the five captains into [[delmar-fisk]]. Their crews remain in the Pearl, riding him, so she has not killed him.

Through [[umberlee-branca]], her command was clear: bring the pearl, after which she would talk about the commissioners. The Pearl still broadcasts its call across the boundary. The [[Leviathan]] answers. Smaller horrors answer. Something larger presses at the fissure.

The [[the-tithe-of-the-bitch-queen]] is a separate hoard—a Sunken Crown treasure in tidal caves. Taking a piece turns her attention to it until saltwater touches it again. Her clergy refuse to rule on whether a pearl counts as Tithe.

The clergy do not advertise the suspicion that tribute payments may be the procedural seal holding the Maw fissure shut. If the payments stop, the fissure may widen. If the fissure widens, elemental water rises. If elemental water rises, her claim shrinks—and she cannot afford to admit that.

## At the Table

- **Notice.** Wave mark carved into harbour stones and prows. Waveservant grey—the shrine keeper's colour. Basin, ledger, water. The [[red-lady-dead-lady|Dead Lady's]] red wake.
- **Explains.** Why every harbour pays. Why Delmar still draws breath. Why the Pearl cannot be simply taken by storm. Why collection continues by hull and coast.
- **Enables.** Tribute as leverage against harbour commerce. Shrine appointments as neutral ground. Collection-hull pressure as a political move.
- **Warns of.** Challenging Umberlee directly may also challenge Maw containment and the waters themselves.

## Counterweights

- **[[Valkur]]**—Sailors speak his name without basin or booklet. They still pay Umberlee. His courage changes less than they hope.
- **[[Tyr]]**—Land and paper authority behind Dravosi warrants. He does not own the wave. His justice stops at the shoreline.
- **[[Auralis]]**—Antheri authority, constructed or publicly divinized. A different kind of power problem. Antheri containment predates her claim to the Maw, not her godhood.
- **[[sentinels-of-the-eyrie]]**—Monastic counter to Waveservant collection. Not all sailors pay the shrine.
