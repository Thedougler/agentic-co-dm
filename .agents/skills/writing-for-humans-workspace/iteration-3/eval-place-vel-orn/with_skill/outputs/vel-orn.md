# Vel Orn

## Overview

> [!narration] Narration
> Small islands of black stone ring a dark blue hole. When the surge pulls back, a narrow reef-path opens toward the shrine clinging to the sheer cliff. Kelp wraps the pillars in permanent scars. Step inside and air thickens; your footsteps go soft on stone. Candles burn steady in the deep, where wind should have killed them. The walls hold a ledger chamber lined with witness to drowned names and water-debt: tokens and coins, fish bones and carved shells, pieces of ship and sailor, each one a death written down.

---

## At a Glance

Vel Orn is Umberlee's older shrine in the [[Sunken Crown]]. Small islands ring a dark blue hole. Access comes only when the surge pulls back enough to show the reef-path—the approach is timed and dangerous. Unlike a working quay, this shrine reaches toward the drowned rather than the living. Inside, the ledger chamber holds a record written in objects: every token, fish bone, carved shell, and piece of ship or sailor stands as witness to a death in the water. The [[pearl-of-souls]] once rested above the tidal pool here; its theft by [[delmar-fisk]] marks both the shrine's loss and the fleet's destruction.

## If the party

- **Wait for the surge to pull back:** The reef-path opens. Crossing requires footing on slick rock and timing against the water's return.
- **Examine the objects in the ledger chamber:** Each piece is a death—the arrangement itself is the record. A meditation on drowning.
- **Search the shrine for the Pearl:** It is gone. The absence is the mark of [[delmar-fisk]]'s theft and what comes after.
- **Offer to Umberlee at the tidal pool:** The pool remains, still waiting for the Pearl that held the drowned names.
- **Listen for Waveservants:** [[Waveservants]] come here to pray. Encounters here are on their terms, not the shrine's.

---

## Who

No permanent inhabitants. The shrine is a place of pilgrimage, not residence. [[Waveservants]] visit to worship at certain tides. The ledger chamber holds the names and tokens of the dead who have no other memorial.

---

## What

- **Ledger chamber:** Stone walls hold the record of drowned names and water-debt. Objects line the shelves: coins worn smooth, fish bones carved into signs, shells split and reassembled, pieces of rigging and timber, fragments of sail or boot. Each one is a death. The stone walls absorb salt spray and the smell of deep water.
- **Tidal pool:** Built to hold the [[pearl-of-souls]]. Now empty. The pool remains, waiting.
- **Pillars:** Kelp wraps them year-round. The black stone beneath shows salt deposits from centuries of spray.
- **Candles:** Burn steady throughout the shrine despite the wind and depth. Their light steadies the eye in the dark.

---

## Why

Worship of Umberlee aimed at the drowned rather than the quay traffic. A place to remember those who died in the water, to make a record of drowning, to offer witness. The [[pearl-of-souls]] once made this a shrine of power; its loss has not made it less sacred, only emptier. The [[Waveservants]] still come. The drowned are still named here.
