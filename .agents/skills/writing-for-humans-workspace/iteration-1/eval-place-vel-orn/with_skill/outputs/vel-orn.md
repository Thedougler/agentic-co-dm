# Vel Orn

> [!narration] Narration
> A dark blue hole opens in the ocean shelf, ringed by black stone teeth. The shrine rises from a cliff face, built into the rock itself — weathered grey stone with kelp tangled around its pillars. Reefs guard the approach. A narrow boat-passage opens only when the tide is high and the surge points northwest.

## At a Glance

Vel Orn is Umberlee's older shrine, tended by those who owe debts to the drowned. It is reachable only at high tide through a reef-funneled approach. The shrine keeps a ledger of drowned names and water-debts written in stone and shell. Its deepest chamber held the [[pearl-of-souls]] — now gone, taken by [[delmar-fisk]] when he fled the fleet's ruin.

## At the Table

**The approach.** Reefs narrow the passage to a single-file boat-width. The surge pulls hard on the incoming tide — `DC 12 Strength (Athletics)` to hold the oars steady through the funnel, or the boat pitches against the black stone. A pilot who knows the passage goes silent at the narrows and points northwest when the water rises. A pilot without the route takes `1d6` bludgeoning from reef contact.

**Inside.** The shrine is one chamber rising three stories inside the cliff, lit by candles that burn without explanation. The air tastes of salt and old stone. A stone ledger occupies one wall, carved with names of the drowned and debts alongside them — births, deaths, drownings, ships lost. Tokens and offerings stand on stone shelves: coins worn smooth, fish bones carved to edges, shells with names inside them, ship timber with nails still driven. Each token marks a death someone recorded here, or a debt someone left unpaid.

**The Pearl chamber.** A natural tidal pool fills the deepest hollow, where the [[pearl-of-souls]] once sat on a stone stand. The stand remains empty. Waveservants and drowned-kin who come here know: [[delmar-fisk]]'s theft broke the shrine's power. The Pearl spoke to Umberlee through the drowned. Without it, the water does not answer.

**Presence.** The shrine is tended by the fishers and sailors of the [[sunken-crown]] who owe water-debt. Waveservants visit at high tide to record names and leave offerings. No single keeper lives here; it is kept by those who come. The candles are always lit when the water rises.
