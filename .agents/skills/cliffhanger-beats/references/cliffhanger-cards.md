# Cliffhanger cards

Card shapes follow Mike Pondsmith, *Scripting the Game* (R. Talsorian, 2020;
transcription at `RTG-Scripting-the-Game-v1.2-agent-readable (2).md` in the repo
root), paraphrased for 5e play. A Cliffhanger is a contest whose outcome stays
in doubt to the very end, with physical conflict or peril — or, for
Confrontation, open intimidation backed by force. Pick the card the fiction
calls for:

- What did the last Development reveal, promise, or warn about? The
  Cliffhanger tests it: a Chase tests the route a clue named; an Ambush tests
  whether the party heeded a Warning.
- Where on the session's escalation ladder is this contest? Early contests
  teach; later ones worry the party; the strongest waits for the Climax.

Each card: **Shape** — what the contest is; **Build** — what the page must
contain beyond the table-ready anatomy; **Pairs** — natural neighbours;
**Fair play** — how the card keeps the outcome in the players' hands.

## Escalation ladder

Scale successive fights across the session. Numbers below are Pondsmith's
ratios for a party of four; `encounter-prep` converts the tier into a 5e XP
budget for the live party.

| Tier | Opposition | Ratio | 5e difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grunts | Far less skilled than the party (zombies, deckhands, troopers) | 3 per PC | Low |
| Minions | Slightly less skilled | 2 per PC | Low–Moderate |
| Henchmen | Equal or slightly better, backed by half as many minions as PCs | 1 per PC | Moderate–High |
| Villain | One villain, substantially stronger, backed by one henchman per PC and a few minions | — | High; the Climax |

Monsters rise the same way through **Scare → Fright → Horror → Terror**.
Early contests send a force the party beats unless they do something foolish,
so both sides learn their strengths and weaknesses; the next raises the ante;
the toughest arrives when the party holds what it needs to win — barely.

## Chase
- **Shape:** The party hotly pursues the opposition. They need a strong reason to follow, so a Chase lands best right after a Kidnapping or a Revelation.
- **Build:** The quarry's lead in feet or zones; the route with three complications (crowd, gap, locked gate) each with its check; what the quarry does when caught; the point where the quarry escapes if not caught.
- **Pairs:** After Kidnapped or Revelation; into Clue or Hesitation.
- **Fair play:** Shortcuts, missiles, and cunning let the party close the gap; the quarry's escape point is a place on the route the party can reach first.

## Pursuit
- **Shape:** The opposition chases the party with plainly superior force. Make the superiority obvious — numbers, speed, the sound of them — so running is the smart play and getting caught means something bad.
- **Build:** Pursuer count and speed; the gap and how each round changes it; hiding places and their checks; what capture means (taken, stripped, held) — capture, not slaughter.
- **Pairs:** After Warning or Discovery; into Retreat, Hesitation, or Rescuers.
- **Fair play:** Hiding, misdirection, terrain, and sacrifice each have rulings that can shake the pursuit.

## Race
- **Shape:** The party and the opposition race for the same goal — a person, place, or thing. Best after a Revelation or Discovery that gives the target.
- **Build:** The goal; both sides' routes; three checkpoints; a visible progress track for the rival so the party knows where they stand; what the winner gets.
- **Pairs:** After Revelation or Clue; into Confrontation or Strange Bedfellows.
- **Fair play:** Sabotage, shortcuts, and cooperation all move the rival's track; the rival's pace is fixed in advance.

## Fist Fight
- **Shape:** A PC, or the party's champion, meets the opposition hand to hand — wrestling, boxing, a brawl. The rules are often ritualized: a chalk circle, one hand behind the back, first to yield.
- **Build:** The ritual's rules and what breaking them costs; what winning grants; the crowd and who they favor; unarmed numbers (Unarmed Strike damage, grapple and shove DCs from `dnd5e-mechanics`).
- **Pairs:** Strong as Play a Cliffhanger in a Hook; into Alliance, Turnabout, or Vengeance.
- **Fair play:** Yielding, cheating, and crowd tricks each have a stated consequence.

## Dogfight
- **Shape:** Combat in the air — flying ships, mounts, or spellcasters — where the opposition tries to bring the party down.
- **Build:** Altitude bands and ranges; mount or vehicle numbers (vehicle owner page); what "down" means (forced landing, crash, fall damage by altitude); one sky feature (cloud bank, thermal, storm front).
- **Pairs:** After Pursuit setup or Warning; into Retreat or Rescuers.
- **Fair play:** Position and maneuver win as often as damage; disengaging has a rule.

## Confrontation
- **Shape:** A fight without blows. The opposition confronts the party with threats and intimidation — "out of town by sundown, or else." They hold overwhelming advantage but choose not to use it yet; unless the party pushes, they will not attack now. One side backs down, or both do. The point is to lean on the party and give them a reason to want to beat this opposition later.
- **Build:** The demand; the visible force behind it (how many, how armed); what makes them strike now if pushed — with that fight's numbers; the humiliation or cost that sticks; a detail the party can use later.
- **Pairs:** Strong as Play a Cliffhanger in a Hook; into Warning, Personal Stake, or Vengeance.
- **Fair play:** The party can defy, bargain, bluff, or comply, and each answer changes how the opposition treats them afterward.

## Duel
- **Shape:** One-on-one combat with weapons of equal power — sword against dagger is a Battle, not a Duel. Duels rarely need to be to the death; they prove who is better, and can decide an outcome by agreement ("if I win, you leave the island").
- **Build:** The terms and who enforces them; the stake; the opponent's numbers and fighting style; what cheating looks like and who would notice.
- **Pairs:** After Confrontation or Personal Stake; into Alliance or Hesitation.
- **Fair play:** Accepting, refusing, naming a champion, and cheating each have a stated consequence.

## Battle
- **Shape:** Formal, open combat: both sides know the fight is coming and meet in the open, and anyone can join. Scale each successive Battle up the escalation ladder.
- **Build:** Each side's objective beyond killing; forces by tier with numbers; terrain features with rulings; one change mid-fight; the opposition's break point.
- **Pairs:** After Alliance, Warning, or Hidden Weakness; into Retreat, Hesitation, or Turnabout.
- **Fair play:** Objectives, retreat, and surrender each end the Battle, not only the last hit point.

## Monster
- **Shape:** A non-intelligent threat — the thing guarding the castle, the horde in the cellar. Rate it Scare, Fright, Horror, or Terror. Monsters are portable: a good one moves between adventures.
- **Build:** The tier; what the creature wants (food, territory, its young); signs before it strikes; how it breaks off (sated, wounded, driven by fire); its numbers from the creature owner page.
- **Pairs:** After Foreshadowing or Warning; into Hidden Weakness or Retreat.
- **Fair play:** Its behavior follows its nature; studying the signs gives the party a real edge.

## Ambush
- **Shape:** Like a Battle, but one side gets a free first strike. Against the party, the ambushers roll Dexterity (Stealth) against each PC's passive Perception; a PC who notices no ambusher is surprised and rolls Initiative with Disadvantage. When the party sets the ambush, make it obvious the opposition is near and unaware — what happens next is theirs.
- **Build:** The ambushers' positions in feet and cover; the tell a watchful PC gets; the first-strike action; the fallback when the ambush stalls.
- **Pairs:** Strong as Play a Cliffhanger in a Hook; after Warning (tests whether it was heeded); into Clue from the bodies.
- **Fair play:** The tell exists and the stealth contest is rolled; a prepared party turns the ambush.

## Obstacles
- **Shape:** Traps, natural hazards, and barriers the party must physically overcome — the deadfall, the pit, the rolling boulder, the flooding cell, even a magical time loop. Rank each by how hard it is and what failure does. Every trap has a solution, and its clues are present in this scene or an earlier one.
- **Build:** Trigger; tell; effect with numbers; two or more solutions (disable, bypass, endure, exploit); what failure changes; which earlier beat planted a clue.
- **Pairs:** Inside Hazardous Quest; after Clue or Warning; into Puzzle or Discovery.
- **Fair play:** The tell and the clues are real, and failure changes position or resources instead of ending play.

## Contest
- **Shape:** A ritualized one-on-one competition — riddles, feats of strength, memory, wit — where one side wins. No fiat: physical contests roll; for riddles the DM brings a genuinely good riddle and the answer to theirs.
- **Build:** The procedure (for example, best of three opposed checks); the stakes for both sides; the NPC's modifiers; the riddle or task with its answer.
- **Pairs:** After Confrontation or Secret Meeting; into Alliance or Hesitation.
- **Fair play:** The procedure is stated before the first roll and honored after the last.

## Skirmish
- **Shape:** A small, sharp fight with the opposition. If the party is winning, the opposition escapes by prepared trickery — smoke, a hidden panel, an illusion. If the party is losing, the opposition captures them or leaves them wounded but alive.
- **Build:** The escape trick and its location; how a PC who anticipates it can stop it; the capture terms and where captives are taken; the opposition's numbers.
- **Pairs:** Early in the session (grunts or minions); into Clue, Retreat, or Rescuers.
- **Fair play:** The escape trick is visible in the scene before it fires, so a sharp party can shut it down.
