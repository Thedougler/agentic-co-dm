## Situation

The [[Velvet Noose]] is a named hull whose sighting changes Crown patrol behavior near the Central Strait. [[Mave Sorn]], an information broker and captain, commands the vessel and currently carries the [[Magistrate's Warding Chip]] on her person. The chip was deliberately removed from the Velvet Noose's Wardstone Figurehead—the source does not establish who removed it or why. Mave keeps the chip because it blocks one targeted divination per day, and she wants supernatural confirmation of the Crown's irregularity so she can decide what to sell and to whom.

### What the party knows

> [!narration] Player-facing brief
> A Crown-sensitive hull has been sighted near the Central Strait. The *Velvet Noose* is involved, and someone aboard carries a small gilded chip that stays warm after it leaves the hand. The route is open water with no nearby shore if a ship comes to grief.

### What is really happening

Mave Sorn is not a prize-taker; she is an information broker pursuing a specific intelligence goal. She keeps the Warding Chip on her person during the intercept and records her observations in her ledger. Her opening move is to establish proof of what she already knows about the party: their vessel's name, their last port of call, and the exact language of the Crown's dispatch authorizing their detention. She presents this as her terms before engaging further.

The chip's removal from the Wardstone Figurehead has split the ritual that protected the vessel. The figurehead still provides some protection, but the chip's separate existence means the coverage is no longer complete. This absence is detectable to those who know to look for it.

Mave's intelligence trade depends on accuracy and timing. If the party confirms the Crown's irregularity during the encounter, it enters her ledger as a sellable fact. If Perrin's pact (the supernatural irregularity itself) is named or confirmed, that revelation also enters her records; what she files it under and who she eventually sells it to remains open.

## Stakes

* **If the party succeeds.** The party establishes control of the encounter, secures the Warding Chip, and can learn or trade for Mave's intelligence about the Crown's supernatural concerns.
* **If the party fails.** Mave keeps the chip and her information. The Velvet Noose remains a Crown-sensitive hull in the Strait, and whatever intelligence Mave gathered goes into her ledger for eventual sale.
* **If the party walks away.** The intercept remains unresolved. Mave eventually files her observations and sells the intelligence to a buyer she deems appropriate.
* **Question.** Does the party treat Mave as an enemy to defeat, a target for negotiation, or a source of information?
* **Question.** Who learns that the Wardstone protection has been split between the ship and the chip, and what do they do with that knowledge?

## Complications

* **Pressure.** Open water with no nearby shore. A damaged hull has no refuge.
* **Tradeoff.** Mave offers negotiation and terms, but her opening establishes she has power over the party's situation (she knows their vessel name, their last port, and the Crown's language of detention). Force may alienate an information source; parley may commit the party to terms they cannot keep.
* **Reaction.** If Mave is attacked outright, her crew defends the Velvet Noose and she relies on her sealed case and mobility. If the party confirms the supernatural irregularity during negotiation, she records it in her ledger and her behavior shifts—she no longer needs further confirmation, and what she does next depends on what was named.
* **Reversal.** The Wardstone ritual's split means the Velvet Noose's protection is incomplete. A Crown diver or ritual specialist who investigates afterward will find the gap and may track the chip (or its holder) to determine why the figurehead was deliberately separated from the ritual.
