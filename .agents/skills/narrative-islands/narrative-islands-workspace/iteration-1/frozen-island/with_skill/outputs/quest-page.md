# The Red Wake

**Status.** Active  
**Objective.** Determine whether a mortal can defy a god and survive the escalation, or negotiate safe passage through Umberlee's wrath.  
**Why now.** The party traveled with [[delmar-fisk|Delmar]] away from [[drowned-maw]]. Umberlee has registered the defiance and moves in response, regardless of the party's location or the calendar.  
**Deadline.** None. Umberlee's escalation persists indefinitely until the party acts to resolve it—through compliance, negotiation, destruction of the Dead Lady, or continued evasion.

## Situation

### What the party knows

> [!narration] Player-facing situation
> Dock talk at any port mentions boats found in the mornings: hulls intact, sails furled, holds dumped to the weather, every soul gone. The finds occur in the waters behind the party's recent route—never ahead, always trailing. Captains and superstitious sailors murmur about "taken whole" vessels, though most dismiss it as weather and salvagers.
>
> If [[delmar-fisk|Delmar]] is with the party, he knows the truth: [[Umberlee]] moves against him. One week after he defied her command—refusing to sail toward [[drowned-maw]] to retrieve the [[pearl-of-souls]]—her instrument began taking boats. The Dead Lady, captains' voices trapped in Delmar's mind, the personal weight of his debt: all of it crystallizes each time the party reaches a port and dawn brings news of another empty vessel in the party's wake.
>
> [[crissdalynn-khinriss|Crissdalynn]], who served aboard the *[[red-lady]]* for twenty-two months before the fleet's destruction, will recognize the description when other party members mention it. The supernatural fog, the missing crews, the personal attention—it mirrors what Umberlee does when she claims a debt.

### What is really happening

[[Umberlee]] has registered [[delmar-fisk|Delmar]]'s defiance. She commanded him through [[Branca]] at the [[calven-and-calveno]] shrine to retrieve the [[pearl-of-souls]] that the [[chain-council]] stole one week prior. Delmar refused. Instead of heading to [[drowned-maw]], he sailed into the [[Midchain]].

Umberlee cannot kill Delmar—the [[fisks-fleet|captains]] whose souls she holds would slip into the [[drowned-maw]] and beyond her claim. She cannot retrieve the Pearl herself (the Maw's water is elemental, beyond her dominion). Her options are narrow and her frustration immense.

She uses the [[Dead Lady]]—the raised corpse of the *[[red-lady]]*—as her instrument of compulsion. The ship appears each night the party spends in port, manifesting in supernatural fog that echoes the fleet's original destruction. It takes boats whole behind the party's route. It always knows where Delmar is. It exists to compel, not to kill.

**The freeze:** Umberlee's escalation waits indefinitely. Whether the party arrives at [[Kalowe]] tomorrow or six months later, the Dead Lady emerges the night after port arrival, as though Umberlee's attention never wavered. The fog, the missing crews, and Delmar's awareness all crystallize on the same schedule: one night after arrival, supernatural activity; the next morning, dock reports of "taken whole" ships in the party's wake. Delay matters because the party accumulates evidence, crew unease, and the weight of ignored consequences. But Umberlee does not hurry. She waits.

The [[Waveservants]]—[[Umberlee]]'s clergy—have shifted from transactional neutrality to pointed attention. A shrine's servants recognize Delmar on sight, though they do not yet move against him. The [[chain-council|Chain Council]], who commissioned the Pearl theft, is raising [[Kalowe]] shrine tribute to buy silence and deflect divine attention from their role.

## Stakes

* **If the party complies.** Delmar sails toward [[drowned-maw]] to attempt retrieval of the [[pearl-of-souls]]. The Dead Lady retreats. The escalation pauses—but Umberlee's terms are not forgiveness, only postponement. The quest to reach the Maw and confront whatever guards the Pearl becomes the next island.

* **If the party negotiates.** An offering and deed of service to the [[Waveservants]] might buy a deadline extension or a path to redemption. The Dead Lady does not leave, but her hunting pauses. The party gains time to investigate why the Pearl matters to Umberlee, what role the [[chain-council|Chain Council]] played, and whether a deeper bargain is possible.

* **If the party destroys the Dead Lady.** The ship disperses for 1d4 weeks. Umberlee reconstitutes. The clock does not reset—only delays. A second manifestation is grimmer. The party has made a direct enemy of a god.

* **If the party walks away.** They can sail on. The Dead Lady follows. Boats taken whole accumulate in every port the party visits. Crew morale erodes. Other captains stop sharing routes with a convoy that brings a god's attention. Eventually, ships will refuse to sail with the party entirely. The problem travels.

* **Question.** Can a mortal truly defy [[Umberlee]], or does defiance only postpone the price of a broken debt?

* **Question.** What leverage does Delmar possess that he has not recognized? (Umberlee needs him alive. She cannot self-retrieve the Pearl. She cannot claim the captains' souls without losing them entirely.)

## Complications

* **Crew unease.** The longer the Dead Lady hunts, the more nervous the crew becomes. Other captains will refuse to convoy with the party. Supplies become scarce. Desertion risks increase.

* **Spreading exposure.** If word reaches a [[Waveservants\|Waveservant Shrine]] that the [[chain-council\|Chain Council]] commissioned the Pearl theft, the debt widens. The party may become entangled in a larger political crisis.

* **Delmar's breaking point.** He hears the captains' voices constantly. The weight compounds with each night of escalation. At some threshold—unknown to the party—he may act alone or surrender without consulting the party.

* **Supernatural limitation.** The Dead Lady operates in specific ways: it manifests in fog after port arrival; it takes boats whole but does not attack the party's ship directly (yet); it disperses if destroyed but reconstitutes. The party must learn these rules through observation or risk miscalculating the danger.

* **Bargaining complications.** If the party negotiates with the [[Waveservants]], an agreement might demand a deed of service that conflicts with other party goals or alignments. The cost of buying time is real commitment.

## Design Notes (DM reference)

**Frozen island implementation:** This quest employs the frozen-island structure deliberately. Umberlee's escalation waits for the party regardless of game time or calendar. The Dead Lady manifests the night after port arrival at any location, on any schedule. This freeze removes Umberlee's escalation from the world tick and anchors it instead to party movement—creating a persistent pressure that adapts to the party's choices without requiring the DM to track independent timelines.

**Running the freeze:** When the party reaches any port (planned or improvised), the Dead Lady appears that night in supernatural fog. Dock reports of "taken whole" vessels appear by dawn. Each cycle is identical in structure (fog night, report morning) but escalates in intensity and evidence. Delmar's awareness and Crissdalynn's recognition deepen with repetition.

**Adapting the freeze:** If the party spends extended time at sea without porting, the Dead Lady does not manifest (she is tied to ports). If the party splits up or abandons Delmar, the escalation targets the separated party member instead. If the party negotiates, the manifest stops temporarily but does not end—Umberlee waits for the agreed service or watches for compliance.

**Agency preservation:** Despite the frozen structure, the party retains full agency: they can flee, negotiate, fight, investigate, or ignore the problem. Each choice produces distinct consequences. The freeze does not script the outcome—it only anchors the pressure to a consistent trigger (arrival at port) rather than calendar time.
