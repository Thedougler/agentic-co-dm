# The Red Wake

**Status.** Active  
**Objective.** Respond to [[Umberlee]]'s escalation against [[delmar-fisk|Delmar]]'s defiance—either compel his return, negotiate terms, suppress the Dead Lady's attacks, or find another way to satisfy the divine debt.  
**Why now.** [[delmar-fisk|Delmar]] crossed the [[Midchain]] heading away from [[drowned-maw]] one week after Umberlee commanded him through [[Branca]] to retrieve the [[pearl-of-souls]]. His refusal to obey has triggered escalation that endangers the party, the crew, and any port they visit.  
**Deadline.** The escalation continues indefinitely. The Dead Lady takes boats and brings supernatural fog with each party arrival at a new port—the night after they dock, she manifests behind them. This pattern repeats at every port until the debt is answered.

## Situation

### What the party knows

> [!narration] Player-facing brief
> Ships are vanishing. The story starts as dock gossip—boats found empty in harbours you have just left, holds stripped and crews gone without trace. No bodies. No wreckage. Just missing people and scattered cargo. It started happening after your ship arrived at the last port. After the port before that, another ship disappeared the same way. And another, at the port before that.
>
> The story changes when someone—a survivor, a witness, a crew member who jumped ship in panic—describes the boat that came for them. A ship with a red hull and sails that move against the wind. Fog that rolls ahead of her like a breath. And when the fog clears, crew gone. No sounds of struggle. Just... silence. The ship itself stays beyond reach, visible at dawn and dusk, always keeping her distance, always there when a vessel has just arrived at a new port.
>
> The locals call it the Dead Lady. An old name, half-remembered, whispered in coastal shrines. No one wants the curse that follows her shadow.
>
> And your ship arrived yesterday.

### What is really happening

[[delmar-fisk|Delmar]] carries the voices of five captains drowned by [[Umberlee]]'s wrath, who drowned when he stole the [[pearl-of-souls]] from the [[drowned-maw]] on commission from the [[chain-council]]. One week ago, [[Umberlee]] commanded him through the priestess [[Branca]] to return the Pearl. He refused. He sailed the [[Midchain]] instead of heading east toward the Maw.

[[Umberlee]] cannot kill him without losing the captains' souls into the Maw itself—their debt keeps them bound. She cannot retrieve the Pearl herself; the Maw's water is elemental, beyond her claim. So she sends the Dead Lady: the raised corpse of the *[[red-lady]]*—a ship drowned in the Maw three years past—to hunt him across the Midchain.

The Dead Lady does not stop at Delmar. She takes *every boat* that sails in his wake, leaving them empty and broken at the ports he has visited. She works on Umberlee's schedule: *the night after the party's arrival at any new port*, the Dead Lady manifests behind them, and boats begin to vanish. This pattern repeats at each port they visit until Delmar answers the debt.

The escalation serves two purposes: it isolates Delmar by poisoning every port he visits, and it compels the party to act—either to protect themselves, or to force Delmar to answer the god's claim.

## Stakes

* **If the party forces Delmar to answer the debt.** Delmar sails for the [[drowned-maw]] to attempt return of the Pearl. The Dead Lady holds station and releases captive crews—but the Mirror of Souls awaits him in the Maw. The party can accompany him, but they will face whatever guardian or trial the Maw contains. If Delmar succeeds, the captains' voices fade, the Dead Lady disperses, and [[Umberlee]]'s wrath turns elsewhere—but Delmar becomes a known debtor to the god, and the [[Waveservants]] mark him forever.

* **If the party negotiates or appeases [[Umberlee]].** A deed of service or significant offering to the [[Waveservants]] can buy time—a deadline extension, not reversal. The Dead Lady holds at a distance, weather calms, and the party has breathing room to pursue other options. But the clock restarts when the deadline passes. This is a delay, not a solution.

* **If the party destroys the Dead Lady.** She disperses into supernatural fog and splinters for 1d4 weeks. [[Umberlee]] reconstitutes her. The escalation resumes. The clock does not reset. The party has bought time but not solved the problem—and they have shown defiance to a god, which carries its own weight.

* **If the party walks away.** The pattern continues at every port they visit. Boats vanish in their wake. Crews die. Port authorities begin to refuse them entry or demand they leave immediately. The [[Waveservants]] eventually learn that the party travelled with Delmar and the debt, and they may exact payment—in service, in silence, or in blood.

* **Question.** Can a mortal defy a god without consequence? What does [[delmar-fisk]] actually want, and does the party have the power to give it to him?

* **Question.** Will the party protect the ports they visit, abandon Delmar, or find a third path that satisfies [[Umberlee]] without sending Delmar to his likely death in the Maw?

## Complications

* **Pressure: The night after each port arrival.** The Dead Lady manifests predictably, creating a repeating crisis. The party cannot prevent the manifestation—only respond to it. Ports grow increasingly hostile as boats vanish.

* **Tradeoff: Isolating Delmar vs. protecting the ports.** Sending Delmar away stops the Dead Lady from manifesting at the next port—but removes the party's leverage and leaves Delmar to face the Maw alone. Keeping him nearby protects his potential as an ally but guarantees more boats taken.

* **Reaction: [[Waveservants]] escalation.** If a [[Waveservants]] shrine discovers that [[chain-council]] commissioned the theft, the divine debt widens beyond Delmar. This reframes the entire situation—the party may have inadvertently exposed another debtor, and the shrine's response will be swift.

* **Reversal: The captains' voices demand resolution.** As the Dead Lady draws closer and Delmar's mental state deteriorates, the captains' voices may demand action independent of Delmar's will. They might force him toward the Maw, or toward confrontation with the Dead Lady, or toward negotiation with the [[Waveservants]]. This could bypass the party's influence entirely.

## Rewards & consequences

### Promised

* Delmar's gratitude and alliance if the party resolves the situation without sending him to likely death.
* Possible future favours from [[fisks-fleet]] or maritime contacts if Delmar survives and remains in the party's debt.

### Possible

* Information about the [[drowned-maw]], the [[pearl-of-souls]], or other divine claims in the Midchain.
* Favour or information from [[Waveservants]] if negotiated rather than opposed.
* Treasure or magical items if the Dead Lady is confronted or destroyed.
* Reputation at Midchain ports as the crew that stopped the escalation (if successful) or as harbingers of divine wrath (if unsuccessful).
* Alliance or enmity with [[chain-council]] depending on whether their role is exposed and how the party responds.

### Fallout

* **Umberlee's attention.** The party has intervened in a divine claim. She may view them as allies (if they forced Delmar to answer), obstacles (if they aided his defiance), or worthy of further notice (if they destroyed or banished the Dead Lady).
* **[[Waveservants]] relationship.** If the party negotiates with shrines, their standing with Umberlee's clergy shifts. If the [[chain-council]] is exposed, the Waveservants become hunting dogs.
* **Midchain port politics.** Ports that suffered boat losses may blame the party, reward them, or demand restitution. Refugee crews and missing persons create lasting complications.
* **Delmar's fate.** Whether he lives, dies, answers the debt, or continues to defy [[Umberlee]] shapes the world's response to future divine claims.

**Design notes for DM:**

The Dead Lady's manifestation **the night after arrival at any port** is a clock that the DM controls, not the party. She does not appear before arrival—only after the party docks. This gives them a day of port activity before the pressure escalates. The escalation repeats at each new port until the core debt (Delmar's response to Umberlee) is answered. 

This is not a timed sequence with a predetermined ending. It is a pressure system. Delmar's choice, the party's intervention, or outside events (Waveservants, other factions) will create the branch that leads to resolution. Until then, the Dead Lady returns each night after a new port arrival.
