# Velvet Noose Intercept

**Status.** Offered  
**Objective.** Secure, trade for, or learn the truth about the Magistrate's Warding Chip and Mave Sorn's intelligence without being forced into a single method.  
**Why now.** The Velvet Noose operates current patrol routes. Mave has the chip on her person and is actively gathering intelligence to sell. Crown attention makes delay costly.  
**Deadline.** None established. This situation is current when the intercept enters play. After the encounter, Mave will file her findings and seek a buyer.

## Situation

The [[Velvet Noose]] is a named hull sighted near the Central Strait, where Crown captains break from normal patrol lines when it appears. [[Mave Sorn]] carries the Magistrate's Warding Chip during this encounter. The Warding Chip was deliberately removed from the Velvet Noose's Wardstone Figurehead—the source does not establish who or why.

### What the party knows

> [!narration] Player-facing brief
> A Crown-sensitive hull has been sighted near the Central Strait. The *Velvet Noose* is involved, and someone aboard carries a small gilded chip that stays warm after it leaves the hand. The route is open water with no nearby shore if a ship comes to grief.

### What is really happening

Mave Sorn is an information broker and captain, not a prize-taker. She keeps the chip on her person because:
- It blocks one targeted divination per day (operationally valuable to her).
- She wants supernatural confirmation of the Crown's irregularity so she can decide what to sell and to whom.
- She records everything that confirms the supernatural into her ledger.

Mave's ledger is her commodity. She will trade information about the chip, the Crown, or the Wardstone split if the party offers:
- Direct evidence of the irregularity (confirmation she lacks).
- A buyer who is worth more than her current options.
- A threat serious enough to reframe the chip as an evacuating asset rather than a filing.
- Leverage over her next port or client relationships.

The chip is *not* nailed to her hand or her ship. It is a negotiable asset. Combat is one route, but boarding a captain who knows everything about you, does not want to fight, and can outpace your ship in open water is neither inevitable nor optimal.

## Stakes

* **If the party secures the chip.** They obtain proof that the Wardstone protection was split and can decide what to do with that information. Mave's ledger contains their identities, interests, and vulnerabilities—now filed as "party who took the chip from me."
* **If the party negotiates or trades.** They may trade information, passage, a promise, or coin for the chip or Mave's silence. Mave files the party as buyers, allies, or people worth monitoring. The Crown's irregularity remains partly in her hands.
* **If the party fails to secure the chip.** Mave keeps it and files the supernatural confirmation into her ledger. The Velvet Noose remains a Crown-sensitive hull. Mave eventually sells her intelligence to a buyer—possibly a Crown enemy, possibly someone worse.
* **If the party walks away.** The intercept remains unresolved. Mave files the irregularity and seeks a buyer. The chip stays with her unless another force intercepts it later.
* **Question.** Does the party treat Mave as an enemy, a negotiating broker, or a source of the Crown's secrets?
* **Question.** Who learns that the Wardstone protection has been split between the ship and the chip?

## Complications

* **Open water pressure.** No nearby shore means damage escalates quickly. The party cannot afford a long fight; Mave knows this. Scarcity of time and escape routes favors negotiation over siege.
* **Mave's information trade.** She will file intelligence about the party (methods, goals, vulnerabilities) into her ledger. If she files them as "hostile," the party becomes part of the information market. If as "negotiating," they become a potential future contact.
* **Crown patrol overlap.** If the encounter drags, Crown captains may arrive and complicate all sides. Mave's incentive to resolve is high. The party's incentive to resolve before reinforcements is also high.
* **Wardstone split consequences.** Someone aboard the Velvet Noose knows why the chip was removed. If the crew loyalty is mixed, the party may find an ally among the crew—or an enemy if the chip's removal threatens crew safety.
