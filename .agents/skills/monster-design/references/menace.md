# Menace

Read this in step 4. It covers what makes a monster fun to fight, what makes it
tough against a strong party, what makes it terrifying, and where terrifying
turns unfair. The numbers that back these choices are in
[chassis-and-budget.md](chassis-and-budget.md) § Tuning to the party.

## Fun to fight

Players enjoy a monster that is **a puzzle with teeth**:

- **Rules they can learn mid-fight.** The first round shows something strange;
  by round three a player says "it only sees by sound" and acts on it.
  Exploiting a learned rule feels earned.
- **A choice every round.** The monster forces a decision: save the grabbed
  ally or finish the wounded foe; hold the door or chase the thief.
- **One moment.** A signature move they retell at the next session.
- **Their tricks matter.** Each PC's best feature works somewhere in the
  fight, even if the monster makes it cost something.
- **Stakes beyond hit points.** Someone carried off, a hull taking water, a
  ritual finishing, a prize getting away.

A dull monster is a bag of hit points that trades blows until it drops.

## Tough against a strong party

A strong party ends fights early with focused damage, hard control, and
mobility. Answer each with design, not bigger numbers.

| The party wins by | The monster answers with |
|---|---|
| Nova damage in round one | Enough durability to act twice; a second form or split at Bloodied; minions or a lieutenant that soak attention |
| Hard control (stun, banish, hold, grapple lock) | Legendary Resistance on a boss; conditions it can end with an action or a phase change; more than one body |
| Action economy (four PCs, one monster) | Legendary actions, a bonus action and reaction that matter, lair effects, allies |
| Ranged kiting or flight | Reach, pull, ranged pressure, terrain that closes the range, a reason to follow |
| Tanking one target | Reach past the front line, forced movement, a reason to go for the healer or caster |
| Short rests and retreat | A clock or pursuit so retreat costs something |

Damage lands harder when it moves people: grab, drag, shove, knock prone, haul
into the air, split the party. Position damage changes the next turn for
everyone; dice only change a number.

Target the party's weak saves from the party read with at least one feature,
and its strong saves with the rest.

## Terrifying

Terror is a visible threat the players believe can kill or take something from
them, plus a way out they must find. Use it for bosses and for the occasional
creature that should haunt the campaign; most fights sit at Hard.

Levers:

- **Separation.** It grabs a PC and carries them off, swallows them, or drags
  them underwater or through a wall. Everyone else now has a new objective.
- **A clock.** Drowning, a collapsing ceiling, the ritual's last verse, the
  second body growing.
- **The impossible, made legible.** It does something the players have never
  seen, and they learn its rule by paying for it once.
- **Unkillable until.** Damage alone cannot finish it; the weakness is a place,
  an object, a person, or a rule the players can discover through its signs.
- **Escalation.** At Bloodied it gets worse in a new way: splits, sheds its
  shell, shows its true shape, calls its brood.
- **Dread before contact.** Signs, a victim, a sound that stops, before the
  first roll.
- **Loss beyond death.** An item, an ally, the ship, a memory, a PC captured
  rather than killed.

## Fair

The line between terrifying and unfair:

- Every lethal move has a tell a round ahead or a visible recharge.
- Every hard condition has a save each turn or an ally action that ends it.
- Every defense has a bypass; every immunity has a reason.
- No PC loses two turns in a row to the same effect.
- Losing has an off-ramp: retreat, surrender, capture, bargain. The monster's
  morale gives the table a way out.

## Signature move patterns

| Pattern | Tell | Threat | Player answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Windup blast | A sound stops, a body draws in | Area damage and a condition next turn | Spread out, get cover, interrupt, block the medium |
| Grab and haul | Tendrils, talons, or tongue lock on | A PC carried away from the party | Damage the limb, escape check, hold the victim down, ready an attack |
| Terrain reshape | The ground cracks, water rises | The arena changes shape | Move early, use the new cover, climb |
| Split | The body tears at the seam | Two threats at half strength | Focus one, control the other |
| Feed on the fallen | Drags a victim to its maw | The victim dies or the monster heals | Rescue or race the damage |
| Mark and hunt | It fixes on one PC | Focused pressure on the marked one | Shield, decoy, break line of sight |
| Command | Horn, banner, shriek | Minions act twice or rise again | Kill or silence the leader |

## Legendary and off-turn actions (2024)

- **Legendary Action Uses.** Usually 3 (4 in its lair). Each use happens
  immediately after another creature's turn; all uses return at the start of
  its turn. Offer two to four options: a move, an attack, and one limited
  signature option that cannot be used again until the start of its next
  turn.
- **Legendary Resistance.** A boss answers hard control with it: 1 to 2 uses
  per day through CR 10, 3 above (one more in its lair). Each use it spends
  should show on its body (a plate cracks, a candle on its crown goes out),
  so players see the resource draining.
- **Reactions** answer player choices; they are not a free extra turn.
- **Minions** (homebrew, name it as such): drop at any damage and take none on
  a successful save. Use them as lightning rods and bodies to push through.
