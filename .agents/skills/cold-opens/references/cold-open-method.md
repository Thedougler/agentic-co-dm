# Cold Open Method

The craft behind the workflow steps in `../SKILL.md`: what the cold open is
for, who holds the camera, what stays fixed, and what play decides.

## Handoff

The cold open exists to **handoff** something into the session. Choose the
handoff before writing the sequence:

- urgency;
- dread;
- curiosity;
- dramatic irony;
- sympathy for an NPC;
- knowledge of distant events;
- evidence that the world moves without the PCs;
- anticipation of a threat or opportunity.

The cold open may concern something the PCs will not encounter for several
sessions. Its connection can be immediate, future-facing, or thematic, so long
as the sequence leaves the players with a meaningful question, feeling, or
changed understanding.

### When a cold open earns its runtime

A cold open is strongest when it accomplishes something hard to reach from the
PCs' viewpoint:

- shows remote events;
- makes an NPC feel independently alive;
- dramatizes consequences elsewhere;
- establishes enemy capability;
- creates dramatic irony;
- foreshadows without delivering exposition;
- gives emotional weight to a later discovery;
- lets the players experience an event whose aftermath the PCs will meet later.

When the same value lands more effectively during normal PC play, put it there
instead.

## One Beat

The whole cold open is a single **Hook Beat**. Five checks are five internal
movements, never five Hook/Development/Cliffhanger Beats. The main session
keeps its own Beat Chart and opens on its own Hook after the cut.

## POV

Default to a **known friendly NPC** when that viewpoint accomplishes the
purpose. Reach for another viewpoint when the viewpoint itself carries value:

- unknown witness;
- rival;
- antagonist;
- villain;
- creature;
- doomed expedition;
- distant ally;
- faction operative.

The table shares control of **one character or one NPC group**, at a friendly,
allied, neutral, hostile, unknown, or mixed stance, unless the request
establishes another structure. A second borrowed perspective enters only when
the DM asks for one.

### Player brief

Give players exactly what they need to inhabit the character in this scene:

- immediate goal;
- obvious capabilities;
- relevant relationships;
- current danger;
- anything the character unquestionably knows that bears on the next decision.

Keep biography and setting exposition out of the brief.

Because the played character is temporary and low-investment, a cold open may
run a tighter endpoint — including that character's death — than a PC-facing
situation ever could. What stays open is the route to that endpoint: cost,
discoveries, complications, timing, tone, and player-authored detail.

## Knowledge firewall

Separate three bodies of knowledge:

- **POV knowledge** — facts the temporary character holds;
- **player knowledge** — facts the table learns by witnessing the scene;
- **PC knowledge** — facts the main characters actually possess.

Cold-open information transfers to the PCs only where the fiction transfers it.
Use dramatic irony deliberately when players know something their characters do
not.

Protect secrets whose revelation would damage later play: show effects, partial
information, symbols, behavior, or consequences where naming the underlying
explanation would collapse the mystery.

## Directed trajectory

Cold opens trade exploratory freedom for pace. Predetermine the **trajectory**,
not the **outcome**.

Fix:

- where the scene begins;
- what immediate problem confronts the POV character;
- the sequence of major pressures;
- the event or threshold the scene moves toward;
- where the camera cuts away.

Leave variable:

- injuries;
- resources;
- collateral damage;
- information gained;
- information missed;
- who survives;
- who notices what;
- how badly something goes;
- relationships;
- exposure;
- positioning;
- the exact state in which the POV character reaches the endpoint.

Failure changes the fiction and advances it. Success changes the fiction and
advances it.

## Timebox

Design for 20–30 minutes total. Favor:

- immediate situations;
- compressed travel;
- short exchanges;
- obstacles resolved in one roll;
- consequential decisions with few options;
- narration that bridges directly between interactive moments.

Use checks and narration to abstract action that would otherwise demand a full
subsystem. A combat-shaped cold open portrays pursuit, battle, escape, boarding
actions, disasters, infiltration, or survival through checks rather than
entering full encounter procedure. Spend the budget on a full combat encounter
only when the DM asks for it.

Introduce an unfamiliar proper noun only when the players need it now.

## Spoken delivery

Narration is written for the DM to say aloud. `theatre-of-the-mind` owns the
read-aloud paragraph; this skill supplies what each paragraph must carry:

- concrete sensory information;
- active events;
- short paragraphs;
- visible threats;
- immediately actionable details;
- one strong image over several decorative details;
- information in the order the character perceives it.

Between checks, narrate the consequence and move promptly to the next pressure.
Where dialogue matters, give the DM compact intent and two or three strong
lines rather than a scripted exchange whose timing depends on player responses.

## Unresolved state

Preparation describes possibilities; play establishes history. Leave every
variable result unresolved in prep — successes, failures, casualties,
discoveries, and injuries stay possibilities on the page. After the session,
transcript ingest and the repo's canon-update process record which branch
actually happened.
