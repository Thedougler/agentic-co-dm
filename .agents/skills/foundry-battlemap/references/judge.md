# Judge

A beautiful image that lacks tactical reads fails. A generic map that is technically overhead and clean fails. The map must communicate encounter architecture — zones, routes, cover, staging, elevation, and identity — not just scenery.

Mark pass only if every category is true. Eight categories.

## 1. Camera

- Straight-down orthographic. Table tops, beds, and tree canopies are discs and rectangles, not perspective boxes.
- Walls are thick bands. You may see a slim outer face. You must not see a 30-degree city isometric.
- No horizon. No sky. No viewer standing in the room.

## 2. Frame

- Matches the FRAME ratio declared in the Design step. Default tall portrait 9:16. A nearby ratio (9:20, 9:18) fails when 9:16 is declared.
- Play-space fills the canvas edge to edge.
- No black bars, ornate frame, or page padding.
- Place scale matches SCALE. Default is zoomed-out: more of the place fits (forest, grass, river, land left and right). A valley, canopy, river, or other large place fails when trees, rooms, or ruins fill the canvas at room scale. Tight room-scale boards pass only when the user asked for a tight board.

## 3. Empty board

- Zero player tokens, monster tokens, token rings.
- Zero grid lines, hex grid, rulers, captions, compass, watermark, Foundry UI. Foundry draws the grid — the image carries no grid whatsoever.
- Props are furniture and terrain, not staged combatants.
- A boat, cart, or sleeping animal is fine when it is scenery. A fighter in stance is not.

## 4. Thumbnail read

At thumbnail size (the scene picker, a small browser tab):

- Zone identity separates by silhouette and value. The viewer can name the zones from the Design step without zooming.
- Open ground, water, vegetation, structures, and large blockers are distinct by value alone.
- Orientation is obvious — the viewer knows which end is which.
- No accidental dead rectangle — every region of the canvas has intentional terrain, detail, or edge treatment.

## 5. Normal-zoom read

At ordinary VTT zoom (the player's view during play):

- Walkable squares are distinguishable from walls, water, pits, and canopy.
- Cover positions and LoS blockers from the Design step are findable and readable.
- Routes between zones are clear — the primary route and at least one alternate path are visible.
- Doors, stairs, thresholds, and chokepoints are findable without decoding the illustration.
- Terrain type changes (stone to grass, dry to water, flat to elevated) read through material and value.

## 6. Grid-scale read

At imagined grid scale (one square = one token footprint):

- Major props occupy believable multi-square footprints — a statue is 2×2, a table is 2×3, a bonfire is 3×3.
- Staging areas from the Design step hold tokens — open floor is genuinely open, not filled with micro-detail.
- Route widths are consistent — a corridor stays the same width along its length.
- The material ladder produces a value hierarchy: dominant ground is neutral, accents guide attention to zones and landmarks.
- Cover inventory items from the Design step are present, correctly sized, and placed where the brief positioned them.
- Tree canopies, rooms, and ruins occupy a footprint that matches SCALE. On a zoomed-out board they read as part of a larger place, not as a tight encounter filling the frame.

## 7. Style and finish

- Painted hand-drawn Czepeku, not a photograph, not a 3D render screenshot, not a pencil dungeon sketch.
- Authored identity — the map feels built for the specific culture and function named in the Design step, taken from the PLACE owner page. Props vary in shape, orientation, and use. The map reads as a specific place, not a tile collage or generic room.
- Architecture and crossings match the owner page. Unclaimed land stays wild: terraces, canopy, grass, river, and pale-stone fords as the page describes. Roads, stone bridges, cobbled paths, paved courtyards, and buildings appear only when that page names them.
- Grounded depth — contact shadows, cast shadows, consistent light direction, material-specific texture. Every prop is anchored to the terrain, not pasted onto it.
- Edge-to-edge fill — the canvas has no accidental seam, dead zone, or page margin. Boundaries, banks, vegetation, and structures blend naturally into the frame.
- Coherent materials — stone, wood, fabric, water, metal, and vegetation each have distinct texture and value while sharing one painted visual language.

## 8. Beat jobs

- The board carries the beat jobs from the Design step at the declared SCALE.
- A Development shows things to explore and learn.
- A Cliffhanger or action Hook shows the routes, cover, and exits the contest uses.
- A map of terrain alone that ignores the session beat fails.

Fail the first item that breaks. Name it for the repair line.

For vehicle and multi-level modes, check the additional criteria in [modes.md](modes.md) after passing these eight categories.
