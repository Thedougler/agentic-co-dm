# Faction craft

## Front design

A faction front is a threat that advances if unchecked. Each front has:

- **Impulse.** The recurring behavior pattern — to dominate, to consume, to
  corrupt, to destroy, to hoard, to expand, to protect its own.
- **Portents.** Three to five visible signs of advancing danger, ordered from
  subtle to unmistakable. Each portent is something the party can observe,
  hear, suffer, or discover. Portents are the faction's table presence.
- **Impending doom.** The world state if the front reaches completion
  unopposed. State it concretely: who loses what, what changes, what closes.

A faction may drive one front or several. A front without portents is invisible
to the table. A front without impending doom has no stakes.

## Faction turns

A faction turn is one cycle of off-screen motion. For each active faction:

| Field | What it answers |
| --- | --- |
| **Want** | The specific result before the next campaign interval |
| **Move** | The plausible attempt using current resources |
| **Mark** | The fact that changes if the move proceeds |
| **Signal** | How the party can learn about the change |
| **Collision** | What other faction, person, place, or deadline it intersects |
| **Player opening** | What remains unresolved and actionable |

Do not roll the turn at creation. `world-tick` resolves turns using faction
resources, opposition, and probability. The Turn Log records changed canon,
newest first.

### Running multiple factions

When several factions share a region or goal, their turns collide. Name the
collision explicitly — which factions compete for the same resource, ally,
territory, or timing window. The DM resolves collisions; the page states them.

## Audit questions

Before play, check:

- Can the table tell what this faction wants?
- What can players change about the faction's situation?
- What happens if the party ignores it?
- What is the cost of cooperation?
- What portent will reach the party next?
- What collision creates a decision this session?

## Failure modes

| Failure mode | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| Org chart without want | Add a concrete present-tense want |
| Biography without pressure | Add why it must act now |
| Invisible faction (no portents) | Add 2-3 observable signs |
| Cutscene faction (no player opening) | Add what the party can influence |
| Comfortable faction (no pressure) | Add scarcity, deadline, rival, or fracture |
| Faction with no collision | Connect its want to another faction or the party |
| Overstuffed assets/people | Prune to entries that change current play |
| Secret-heavy with no discoverable path | Add quiet clues or investigation routes |
| Turn with no signal | Add how the change reaches the party |

## Importance and scope

| Scope | Reach | Typical assets |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Local | One settlement or site | A building, a gang, a monopoly, a secret |
| Regional | Several settlements or a trade route | Ships, agents, alliances, a stronghold |
| Realm | A nation or major institution | Armies, treasuries, law, legitimacy |
| World | Cross-realm or planar | Networks, artifacts, divine backing, portals |

Match scope to what the faction can plausibly attempt and what resources it
commands. A local faction attempting realm-scale moves needs an explanation.
