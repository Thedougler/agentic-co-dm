# Life, information, and pressure

## Faction sheet

```markdown
### Faction / group
**Want:** the immediate objective and the longer claim
**Fear:** loss, exposure, rival, or change they cannot absorb
**Method:** what they do first, and what they refuse to do
**Resources:** people, territory, time, information, tools, legitimacy
**Tell:** observable behavior, symbol, routine, or mistake
**Offer:** what they can grant, trade, teach, or threaten
**Response:** to bargain, aid, deception, attack, retreat, and neglect
**Territory / red line:**
**Current move:**
**Between-visit update:**
```

Give inhabitants reasons to move, not just reasons to occupy a room. A roster
tracks who is present, absent, injured, captured, recruited, or running low on a
resource. A faction can be dangerous without being a combat encounter and can be
useful without being friendly.

## Progression

Use a short state track suited to the place, for example:

| State | Trigger | Visible sign | New opportunity / cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quiet | baseline | routines, open routes | information and low-risk contact |
| Alert | clue, alarm, delay | watchers, signals, changed prices | faster action; less privacy |
| Contested | rival acts or clock step | barricades, claims, patrols | alliances and bypasses; route risk |
| Transformed | decisive party/faction move | new law, terrain, owner, or rule | new affordances; old ones lost |

Name the trigger rather than advancing because the party entered another room.
Use a different sequence if it fits the fiction (festive → strained → divided →
changed, for example).

## Required secrets and clue robustness

For every fact required to make a meaningful choice, list about three independent
vectors. Mix:

- **Operational:** a person demonstrates the mechanism, a trace shows its use, or
  a pattern allows a Study/Search action.
- **Historical:** witness, document, inscription, artifact, damage, or ritual
  remnant explains what happened.
- **Directional:** a map, repeated symbol, behavior, sound, route geometry, or
  consequence points toward what matters next.

A clue can be partial, costly, or misleading in a way that creates a choice, but
one failed check must not erase the only path forward. Put essential signs in
plain fictional view. A declared action may earn detail, certainty, advantage,
a safer route, a faction response, or a reduced cost. On failure, provide a
complication: time, position, exposure, depleted resource, mistrust, or pressure.

## Location moves

A location move is a concrete change made by the place or an inhabitant in
response to play. Prepare 2–4 moves in advance:

```markdown
**Move:** [actor] [action]
**Trigger:** [specific observable event or clock step]
**Visible result:** [what players notice now]
**New opportunity:** [what can be exploited, negotiated, or learned]
**Lasting consequence:** [what changes on the map, in relationships, or on return]
```

Examples (adapt rather than copy as plot):

- A settlement closes its night gate after an unclaimed theft; a smuggler offers
  a roof, while the official route becomes safer but slower to enter.
- A marsh relocates its floating trail after a fire; the old clue remains useful
  but costs a wade, and birds reveal a new safe perch.
- A ruin's caretaker moves the only working lift to protect it; the party can
  bargain, follow the new cable line, or reach the upper level by an exposed
  climb.
- A planar market changes which memories count as currency after a promise is
  broken; the rule stays reliable, but the party's available affordances shift.

Triggers should be caused by party choices, faction actions, elapsed pressure,
rest, weather, or an explicit rule of the place—not arbitrary authorial surprise.

## Pressure procedure

Record:

- **Source:** pursuit, scarcity, weather, social deadline, ritual, instability,
  politics, or attention.
- **Advance trigger:** action, failed check, rest, noise, faction milestone,
  missed window, or return.
- **Current state:** what is already true and what players can observe.
- **Step result:** what changes in routes, inhabitants, clues, costs, or hazards.
- **Player influence:** how preparation, information, alliances, or risk can slow,
  redirect, or exploit it.
- **End condition:** what happens when the clock fills, and what remains playable.

Pressure should make a decision sharper, not replace decision-making with a timer
that is invisible or unavoidable. If the party leaves, update the place using the
same procedure; if they return, show the history of their actions.

## Return state

After a visit, write a five-line update:

1. **Routes:** opened, closed, learned, damaged, or newly exposed.
2. **People:** who moved, learned, allied, fled, was injured, or took charge.
3. **Resources:** what was spent, stolen, repaired, harvested, or scarce.
4. **Pressure:** current clock and the next likely move.
5. **Opportunity:** the new question, offer, route, or consequence that invites a
   return.

Never repopulate a cleared node just to restore a quota. Reuse durable geography,
then change occupation, meaning, access, evidence, or stakes.
