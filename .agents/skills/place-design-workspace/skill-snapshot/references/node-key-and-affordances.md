# Node key and affordances

## Node template

Use one card per significant node. Keep the first-impression text short enough
to say at the table; keep GM truth in separate bullets.

```markdown
### [ID] Name — [one-line job in the graph]
**Approach / edge:** route, arrival cost, what is known before entry
**First impression:** what a character can immediately see, hear, smell, feel,
and use to orient
**Current activity:** who/what is acting now; what changes if ignored
**Invitation:** the obvious actionable question or opportunity
**Interactives:** objects, surfaces, people, terrain, systems, resources
**Affordances:** likely verbs and what each can change
**Obstacle:**

**Clues:** operational / historical / directional; at least three vectors for a
required secret
**Choices / edges:** route, cost, information, danger, consequence
**Inhabitants:** goal, position, movement, offer, response, off-ramp
**Pressure:** current clock state and the move this node can trigger
**Payoff / aftermath:** what is learned, gained, altered, or made possible
**Mechanics:** only relevant 2024 rules, DCs, cover, terrain, or hazard procedure
**Return state:** what persists or changes after the party leaves
```

## The verb test

Ask, “What can the players do here that matters?” A node passes when it offers
one or more consequential verbs—cross, question, bargain, hide, study, reroute,
steal, rescue, climb, harvest, sabotage, consecrate, wait, or invent an equivalent
method. The verb must connect to a route, clue, resource, faction, pressure, or
payoff. “Look at an interesting wall” is not enough unless looking, measuring,
or interpreting changes a decision.

Do not confuse a verb with a prescribed solution. “Use the blue key” is a key;
“open, bypass, trade for, or damage the lock while accepting different costs” is
a playable affordance.

## Affordance and obstacle prep: six questions

For each obstacle or useful feature answer:

1. **What is the sign?** What can players observe before committing? Name sound,
   wear, behavior, pattern, temperature, social signal, or other telegraph.
2. **What is the trigger?** What action, timing, threshold, or condition activates
   it? State it in fiction and mechanics.
3. **What is the effect?** What changes in position, safety, resources, access,
   information, trust, or pressure? Make it consequential but playable.
4. **What is counterplay?** What can characters do with preparation, tools,
   abilities, terrain, negotiation, timing, or a declared action?
5. **What is the bypass?** What alternate route, cost, faction deal, risky stunt,
   or partial objective avoids the main obstacle?
6. **What is the leverage?** Which clue, relationship, capability, or earlier
   choice makes a better option available? Reward understanding without requiring
   a single correct answer.

List plausible consequences for unexpected methods. If a player proposes a method
that fits the fiction, let it work or fail forward with a visible cost rather than
returning to the hidden solution.

## Opening description

A good opening gives **orientation + salient change + invitation**: enough concrete
space to know where one can go, the most important active or anomalous feature,
and one thing that can be tested now. It separates what is immediately observable
from what the GM knows. It uses specific details that imply affordances rather
than explaining the whole history.

A bad pattern is a scenic paragraph that names every surface, withholds where the
exits are, announces the author's lore, and ends with “what do you do?” without
an actionable feature. Another bad pattern is a clue or secret that only appears
if a single roll succeeds. Put the essential sign in the fiction; let rolls or
further actions reveal depth, cost, or advantage.

## Keying order

1. First impression and orientation.
2. Current activity and what changes if ignored.
3. Interactives and affordances.
4. Hidden information, with its clue vectors.
5. Mechanics and edge cases.

Delete any detail that does not inform a choice, reveal a clue, express identity,
change pressure, support an interaction, or make the place easier to run.
