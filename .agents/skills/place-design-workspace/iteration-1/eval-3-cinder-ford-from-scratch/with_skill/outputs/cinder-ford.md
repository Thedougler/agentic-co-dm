# Cinder Ford

## Overview
> [!narration] Narration
> Black sand drinks the river into a shallow bar between two low banks. Iron ferry chains hang from posts; by mid-day the links seize with a pale frost that has nothing to do with cold air. Overlapping paw-and-boot tracks braid the same sandbar, each set returning to a different bank camp. The water smells of wet ash. Anyone who means to cross must touch the chains, the bar, or both.

---
## At a Glance
Cinder Ford is a claimed black-sand crossing. Ferry chains freeze mid-day. Two packs contest the bar. Skippable edge: treating either pack's claim as settled law without reading the tracks.

**Player-facing promise:** Exploitation / intrigue — decide whose claim to honor, break, broker, or steal while the noon lockout ticks.

---
## If the party

- **Cross before noon on the chains.** Reach the far bank; both packs see who chose the ferry.
- **Wade the sandbar after noon.** Chains are locked; bar is the only path; tracks become evidence you can disturb or preserve.
- **Broker a shared crossing toll.** Change pack posture from contest to temporary cartel.
- **Sabotage one pack's camp markers.** Shift claim lines; expect a night raid.
- **Wait out a freeze cycle without crossing.** Learn the lock timing; lose a day; packs may finish a fight without you.

## Who

- Two rival packs (working labels only — invention): Ash-Track runners (north bank) and Chain-Claim haulers (south bank). Numbers: ~6–10 each on a busy day.
- Ferry post empty at noon; operator returns when chains thaw.

## What

- Black sandbar; ferry posts and chains; frost-seize mid-day; overlapping tracks; bank camps; ash-scent water.

## Why

Parties come to cross, to sell passage, or to stop a pack war from closing the only mid-day-safe route. The freeze makes timing a resource.
