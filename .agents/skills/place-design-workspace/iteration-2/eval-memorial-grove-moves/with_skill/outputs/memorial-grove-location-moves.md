# Memorial Grove - Location Moves

These location moves are grounded in established Memorial Grove content:
- Hinewai's walking form and grief-as-law principle
- The place-bound Death Bloom (tree, two unmarked graves, black-flower ring, bound soil)
- Stillbloom's vibration-triggered venom and perimeter defense
- Spiritpollen's hallucination cloud and behavioral trigger
- The too-green grass and silent mourners

Each move includes: **actor**, **trigger**, **visible result**, **new opportunity**, and **lasting consequence**.

---

## Move 1: The Guest Threshold

**Actor:** Hinewai in her walking form  
**Trigger:** Party enters as guests and respects the memorial (does not approach graves, does not pick living fruit, does not strike tree or uproot black flowers)

**Visible result:** Hinewai materializes at the tree line in her tall, narrow elf form. She bows slightly—a formal gesture of acknowledgment—and places one hand on the tree trunk. The black-flower ring around the memorial brightens imperceptibly. The air loses the rotten-sweet edge for a moment, becomes almost fresh.

**New opportunity:** 
- Hinewai speaks with them as equals rather than mere visitors. 
- They may ask one direct question about the memorial (the companion, the graves, the binding, or what it means to grieve so long).
- She grants safe passage through the forest—Stillbloom will not fire at respectful guests who have earned recognition.
- They learn the gravity and dignity of this place and can negotiate future access or knowledge exchange.

**Lasting consequence:** 
- On return visits, Hinewai recognizes them. Stillbloom stands down (no longer hostile to their careful passage).
- The memorial itself becomes less overtly defensive in their presence—not welcoming, but no longer actively hostile.
- They've crossed a threshold from trespassers to acknowledged mourners. This status persists across sessions.

---

## Move 2: Stillbloom Fires—The Perimeter Rings Alarm

**Actor:** Charged Stillbloom stalks at the edge of the black-flower ring  
**Trigger:** Party moves quickly (more than 10 feet per turn, dashes, or makes violent motion) through the perimeter or attempts to cross the black-flower ring without slow, careful passage

**Visible result:**
- Pale hollow bracts lift and angle outward; long dark needles quiver and orient.
- The smell shifts from still-silence to sharp urgency—ozone and green acid.
- Spines whistle through the air with a sound like breaking glass.
- Punctured carcasses and old scavenger bones nearby seem to flicker in the sudden motion, casting strange shadows.
- Grass trembles around the fired stalk's perimeter.

**New opportunity:**
- Party learns the memorial's perimeter is actively and intelligently defended—this is not a passive place.
- They discover that careful, slow passage (10 feet or less per turn) does not trigger the stalks.
- They can exploit reload windows (the stalks cannot fire again for 1 minute after volley).
- They can use objects to trigger volleys intentionally, creating cover or distraction.
- They can find alternate routes—water paths (marsh or lake-edge) or flight that avoids the root mat.
- If poisoned, they face a new puzzle: how to remove embedded spines, manage venom spread, and move carefully while wounded.

**Lasting consequence:**
- Any embedded spines carry venomous venom—local numbness escalating to systemic poisoning and paralysis.
- The memorial becomes claustrophobic and hostile if poisoned party members cannot leave the clearing.
- The Stillbloom remembers violence. Trespasses after a fired volley trigger faster volleys with shorter reload windows, as if the plants are on alert.
- If a player visits the memorial again and moves recklessly, the Stillbloom fires immediately—it has learned their pattern.

## Move 3: The Bound Soil Yields to Intent—The Graves Reveal Their Roots

**Actor:** Hinewai's buried grief and the luminous root network binding the graves  
**Trigger:** Party respectfully investigates one of the unmarked graves (searching carefully, asking Hinewai questions, attempting gentle divination or history checks) OR disturbs the graves directly (digging, striking the ground, uprooting anything above them, forcing magical sight into the soil)

**Visible result—Respectful investigation:**
- Luminescent roots become visible just beneath the grass—faint green-gold light visible at the soil line.
- Air shimmers around the graves as if heat is rising in still light.
- Faint whispers that aren't words but feel like *sorrow, purpose, binding, protection, and love*—all ancient and not hostile to witness.
- The too-green grass pulses subtly, like slow breath.
- The two graves show different root patterns: one has roots coiling tightly inward (protective); the other's roots spread wider outward (reaching).

**Visible result—Violation (digging, striking, uprooting):**
- The roots recoil violently—visible writhing beneath the grass.
- Black flowers spread inward from the ring like infection, reaching toward the graves.
- Fruit on the central tree withers visibly—ripe berries shrivel, stems darken.
- Hinewai's form solidifies in the forest at the tree line. Her voice becomes a statement, not a warning: *"No."*
- The air becomes heavy, thick, and tastes of turned earth and ozone and grief turning to rage.

**New opportunity—Respectful investigation:**
- Party learns the graves are more than monuments—they are phylactery anchors binding Hinewai to the land.
- They understand the Death Bloom's true, tragic nature: it is a preservation ritual that preserved the companion forever but at the cost of Aruhe's natural life-death cycle.
- They might attempt to break the phylactery carefully (seeking ritual knowledge, negotiating with Hinewai, or pursuing a deeper understanding of the binding).
- They might seek the companion's identity and true name—knowledge that could unbind the graves or honor the dead with completion.
- They earn the right to return and investigate further without hostile action from the memorial.

**New opportunity—Violation:**
- Combat becomes inevitable. The memorial's defensive stance collapses into open hostility.
- Hinewai enters her Blight form (use the appropriate statblock based on the Bloom's condition).
- The Stillbloom fires without restraint—the silent mourners become active predators.
- Party must flee or fight a defending land itself, not just an NPC.

**Lasting consequence—Respectful:**
- The graves remain marked by visible rootwork on future visits—the party has seen the truth and cannot unknow it.
- Respectful investigation opens narrative paths to negotiation, phylactery severance, or laying the companion's shade to rest.
- Future attempts to approach the graves without hostile intent are met with acceptance—the roots do not recoil.

**Lasting consequence—Violation:**
- A timer begins on Hinewai's return. She will reform in `1d10 days` (if the Bloom still stands) and will know exactly who violated the graves.
- The memorial becomes actively hostile. On return visits, the Stillbloom fires faster; the Spiritpollen stands closer to the clearing's center; the black flowers creep inward.
- The companion's grave—the real anchor—becomes a point of power but also of danger: the party has marked themselves as enemies of the land itself.

## Move 4: The Fruit Tree Responds—Gift or Curse

**Actor:** The central fruit tree and Aruhe's living will through it  
**Trigger:** Party respectfully asks permission before gathering, takes only fallen fruit, or approaches with genuine intent to understand the memorial (paying respect, studying the graves, asking about the companion)  
OR takes living fruit without asking, attempts to fell the tree, or strips branches with intent to exploit or profit

**Visible result—Respect and permission:**
- More fruit ripens impossibly fast—yesterday's green clusters are today's ripe redheart-berries and giants-guavas.
- Stalks bend low of their own accord, bringing fruit to hand-reach.
- The smell shifts from rotten-sweet to warm, inviting, and alive—still rich but no longer tinged with decay.
- The too-green grass glows softly, almost luminescent.
- A faint sensation of *welcome* permeates the air without words.

**Visible result—Taking without asking:**
- Fruit blackens in the party's hands within moments—ripe berries turn to coal and ash.
- Stalks twist away, bending back out of reach as if recoiling.
- The smell turns sharp and acidic, almost choking.
- The black-flower ring contracts inward slightly, as if the memorial is withdrawing.
- The too-green grass darkens to near-black at the party's feet.

**New opportunity—Respect:**
- They harvest fruit that carries memory or vision—eating it grants fleeting images of the companion's face, voice, or the moment of death and burial (powerful and haunting, not always comfortable, but genuine).
- The fruit itself has unusual properties: it sustains longer than normal food (multiple days on a handful), or it grants temporary connection to Aruhe's living systems (advantage on Nature checks related to the island).
- They understand viscerally that the tree is a monument to love, not a resource to exploit.
- They earn favor with the land itself—future foraging on Aruhe becomes easier, and the island's hazards become less aggressive to them.

**New opportunity—Exploitation:**
- The tree refuses them. They must leave or face the memorial's active rejection.
- If they persist, Hinewai appears (not to bargain, but to defend the companion's memory).
- They learn through hardship that Aruhe does not forgive taking, and that grief—even ancient, place-bound grief—has teeth.

**Lasting consequence—Respect:**
- The tree remembers. On future visits, it offers fallen fruit freely to respectful parties.
- To others (those who violated the graves, who struck the memorial, or who came seeking to exploit), it produces no fruit—not even fallen berries, not for weeks.
- The fruit itself becomes a clue: its flavor, its properties, and the visions it carries might point toward the companion's identity, true name, or unfinished business.
- Consuming the fruit binds the party subtly to Aruhe—they become defenders of the memorial in a quiet way, and the island's perils ease slightly in their presence.

**Lasting consequence—Exploitation:**
- The tree withholds. No fallen fruit appears for that party (DM discretion: perhaps `1d4` weeks).
- Word spreads among Aruhe's living systems: these travelers are takers, not guests. Hazards respond accordingly.
- The memorial's enmity toward that party becomes open and lasting—they are marked as enemies of the Death Bloom's preservation.
