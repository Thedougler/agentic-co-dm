## Design Process

### 1. Content Review

**Memorial Grove page (wiki/entities/place/memorial-grove.md):**
- 150-foot clearing in dark forest; trees pressed back
- Ring of black flowers; one central fruit tree over two unmarked graves
- Too-green grass, bound soil, silent mourners at tree line
- Fruit: redheart-berry (low), giants-guava (high), fallen fruit in grass
- Hinewai owns walking form and memorial pressure here
- Stillbloom (charged, raises pale bracts and dark needles when living movement closes)
- Spiritpollen (white sacs, 15-foot cloud, Spirit-Haunted condition)
- Cycle: guest → harassment-free; trespass → hostile
- What at stake: "protection that kills is still protection" — destroy Bloom or leave memorial standing

**Hinewai (npc/hinewai.md):**
- Tall, narrow elf; pale gray skin; amber-orange eyes; fangs; long black hair; dark layered cloak
- Grief became island's law; her woman-in-the-woods form guards living systems
- Wants: Aruhe remains unspoiled
- Fears: wildfire, death of plants/animals
- Hates: Grung, takers
- Loves: living things, Aruhe, the companion she buried
- True weakness: the Death Bloom (not her body)
- Returns in 1d10 days if body destroyed while Bloom survives
- First meeting: calm, proud, old, courteous; asks what they want before explaining
- Welcome ends if party: picks living fruit, strikes tree, digs grave, uproots black flowers, tries to carry Bloom away
- Then she withdraws; local plants/animals answer per instinct, not her control
- Root network: luminous roots from shelf above Clear Lake through The Marshes, pointing home; no root crosses either grave
- Death Bloom core rule: place-bound phylactery, not detachable object (tree + companion's grave + Hinewai's grave + black-flower ring + bound soil + ritual relationship)
- Memorial states trigger stages: Whole → Wounded → Near Ruin → Ruined
- Rebirth: 1d10 days if body reaches 0 HP while Bloom holds

**Stillbloom (item/stillbloom.md):**
- Dull waxy stalk, 4 feet high; pale hollow bracts; dark needles; cream-white flower above
- Reads vibration, not malice
- Trigger: Small+ grounded creature moves >10 feet on turn, dashes, jumps, falls, forcibly moved 10+ feet, or violent motion
- Safe: moving ≤10 feet per turn, flying without touching root mat
- Notice: DC 14 Perception (launch-spines, punctured carcasses, odd paths); DC 14 Nature/Survival (vibration pattern); 1 minute observation obvious without roll
- Volley: 15 feet radius; DC 14 Dex save; fail = 2d6 piercing + 1 Exposure; success = no damage
- Reload: 1 minute after firing; sacs visibly swell
- Venom: DC 12 Con save (first hit); +1 DC per Exposure; failure → spread (Local → Systemic → Motor Failure → Paralysis)
- Recovery: At Motor Failure/Paralysis, repeat Con save each turn; success reduces spread by 1; no hit for 1 minute = reduce by 1 more; 10 minutes = Exposure 0
- Counterplay: cover, stay outside range, lure volley with object, poison-clearing effects, weaponize against pursuers

**Spiritpollen (item/spiritpollen.md):**
- White flowers, round pollen sacs, pale veins, yellow dust
- Trigger: touch, strike, cut, shake, burn, or disturb plant
- Releases pale pollen cloud, 15-foot radius
- Notice: DC 15 Perception OR DC 15 Nature (swollen sacs, old claw marks, weapon cuts, scorch rings, untouched plants)
- Contact: DC 15 Wisdom save or Spirit-Haunted
- Spirit-Haunted: sees swarms of spectral insects and grasping spirits; must spend Action on attack/spell believing it destroys greatest number of hallucinated threats; treats affected companions as valid targets
- End of turn: DC 15 Wisdom save to end effect
- Careful passage: cover mouth/nose, stay out of cloud, wait for settlement
- Counterplay: disturb from >15 feet, strong wind, destroy from outside cloud
- Placement: old Aruhe growth where clawed animals and travelers learned to leave pale plants untouched

### 2. Location Move Principles (from place-design skill)

Each location move must have:
1. **Actor**: who or what makes the move
2. **Trigger**: what causes it to happen
3. **Visible result**: what the party perceives
4. **New opportunity**: what door opens, what becomes possible
5. **Lasting consequence**: what sticks around, what changes persistently

Moves reflect:
- The place as a situation with topology, not a lore essay
- Circumstances and actors; let players determine what happens
- Prep on decisions the table will face
- Pressure, factions, clues, and location moves (2–4 explicit moves for places)

### 3. Design Decisions

**Why 4 moves instead of 2–3?**
Memorial Grove has four major interactive elements beyond just "Hinewai guards it":
1. Hinewai herself (guest/host/defender)
2. Stillbloom (perimeter defense)
3. The graves (phylactery anchors, mystery/danger)
4. The fruit tree (memorial/resource/connection)

Each element deserves its own move so parties can engage with different aspects and face different consequences.

**Why these specific moves?**

**Move 1: The Guest Threshold**
- Hinewai's core trait is hospitality mixed with fierce protection. The "welcome ends" language in her page demands a move that rewards respectful guests with *something*, making hospitality a real choice rather than just a restraint.
- Visible result (bowing, brightening flowers, air clearing) builds on her elf dignity and her role as guardian of the land.
- Opportunity (recognition, standing down Stillbloom, safe passage) creates a real diplomatic path that persists.
- Consequence (status as recognized guest, hazards ease) makes respect mechanically meaningful—future visits change based on this move.

**Move 2: Stillbloom Fires**
- Stillbloom is already in "If the party" section, but it lacks narrative impact beyond the DC checks. A location move frames it as an intelligent defense system responding to party action.
- The trigger (quick movement, trespass) is directly tied to how the party approaches the clearing.
- Visible result (bracts lifting, needles quivering, sound and smell changing) brings Stillbloom to vivid, tactile life.
- Opportunity (learning the pattern, exploiting reload, finding routes) rewards careful observation and creativity.
- Consequence (venom accumulation, claustrophobia, plant memory) makes the encounter have teeth and persist across the sitting.

**Move 3: The Bound Soil Yields**
- This move unlocks the central mystery of Memorial Grove: the graves are anchors, not just monuments. The "If the party" section mentions digging graves as one of the acts that breaks Hinewai's welcome, but it doesn't show *why* that's important or *what* the graves are.
- The move has two branches (respect vs. violation), making it a clear choice point that parties face as they investigate.
- Respectful investigation → learning the phylactery truth, negotiation paths, possible peaceful resolution.
- Violation → combat, memorial collapse, enmity.
- Visible results differ wildly (luminescence vs. recoiling roots and withering fruit), making the choice viscerally clear.
- Consequence (rootwork visibility, lasting status change, timer on Hinewai's return) persists and shapes future visits.

**Move 4: The Fruit Tree**
- The tree is mentioned as "central" with two fruit types and "fallen fruit rests in grass too green for the dark soil." It's a passive element waiting for agency.
- This move transforms it into a responsive NPC-like element that judges party intent and responds accordingly.
- Trigger branches: permission/respect vs. exploitation.
- Visible results are sensory and clear: fruit ripening impossibly vs. fruit blackening, stalks bending vs. twisting away.
- Opportunities are distinct: harvesting memory/vision vs. being denied.
- Consequences last and shape future visits: fruit offering or withholding, island hazards responding to party reputation.

### 5. Tactical and Narrative Integration

**How moves interact with party choices:**

1. Party enters with respect → Guest Threshold triggers → Stillbloom doesn't fire → Can approach graves carefully
   - Consequence: friendly knowledge, safe exploration, alliance potential

2. Party enters quickly/violently → Stillbloom Fires → embedded spines/venom → constrains future movement
   - Consequence: urgent healer/poison checks, slower clearance, higher chance of stumbling into Spiritpollen

3. Party investigates graves respectfully → Bound Soil Yields (respect branch) → learns phylactery truth
   - Consequence: can negotiate, can plan careful phylactery work, earns Hinewai's respect if they preserve it

4. Party grabs fruit without asking → Fruit Tree curses them → fruit blackens → memorial becomes actively hostile
   - Consequence: Hinewai appears in Blight form, Stillbloom fires without mercy, party forced to flee

**How moves serve the location's core promise:**
Memorial Grove's promise is "Decide whether protection that kills is still protection." Each move puts that choice in the party's hands:
- Guest Threshold: Do you respect grief enough to listen and ask permission?
- Stillbloom Fires: Do you move carefully through sacred space, or do you rush through a memorial?
- Bound Soil Yields: Do you honor the graves, or do you violate them seeking answers?
- Fruit Tree Responds: Do you take what's offered, or do you take what you want?

### 7. Sensory and Spatial Details

**Honoring place-design's "3Fs + signatures" principle:**

Fantastic elements (from page):
- Black-flower ring
- Too-green grass
- Bound soil
- Hinewai's walking form and druidic nature
- Luminous roots
- Spirit Pollen hallucinations

Familiar elements:
- Graves and mourning (universal)
- Fruit tree and harvest (common)
- Forest clearing and boundaries (naturalistic)

Functional elements:
- Stillbloom and Spiritpollen as hazards
- Routes and choices (approach carefully, respectfully, or rush)

Signatures used in moves:
- **Spatial:** the ring of black flowers as boundary, the tree as center, the graves as mystery at the center

- **Behavioral:** Hinewai's bowing and dignified speech; Stillbloom's intelligent vibration reading; the tree's responsive growth and decay; the soil's protective and reaching root patterns

## Notes for Review

- All moves assume DM familiarity with Hinewai's page, Stillbloom and Spiritpollen mechanics, and the Death Bloom phylactery concept.
- The moves create strong party incentives for respect, careful investigation, and negotiation—but do not block combat or aggressive play; instead, they escalate consequences.
- Return visits should reference which moves were triggered and what states they left the memorial in (hostile, friendly, neutral-but-wary).
- If Hinewai enters Blight form during this sitting, subsequent moves should shift to her combat statblock and the memorial should become openly hostile.
