# Location Moves — Memorial Grove

## Move 1: Hinewai's Welcome Ends

**Trigger:** Party picks living fruit from the tree, strikes the tree, digs at either grave, uproots black flowers, or attempts to carry the Death Bloom away.

**Actor:** Hinewai in her walking form.

**Visible result:** Hinewai withdraws into the dark forest without speaking. The predators at the tree line — silent mourners who held still before — lose their restraint. They step forward into the clearing. The air grows heavier, and no branch moves.

**New opportunity:** Party can now observe the fauna directly; they are independent predators with their own instincts and claims, not hive-mind servants. Party can negotiate a retreat with Hinewai if they find her in the forest. The tree and graves stand unguarded by her, but now surrounded by living threats.

**Lasting consequence:** Hinewai's courtesy is revoked. She will not grant passage by welcome, answer questions about the graves, or treat the party as guests. On return, she may permit looking from the tree line only.

---

## Move 2: Stillbloom Rises

**Trigger:** Party attempts to dig at an unmarked grave, probe with tools, or directly disturb the soil above or around either burial.

**Actor:** Stillbloom — the charged predator species that guards at the clearing's edge.

**Visible result:** Pale bracts rise high and fully extend. Dark needles sharpen to their full length, forming a dense barrier between the party and the grave. The plant does not advance beyond the grave's immediate boundary; it holds position.

**New opportunity:** Party can Study or Influence the Stillbloom to understand it as a guardian species rather than a passive hazard. They can climb or jump around it (with athletic checks). They can attempt communication (with Insight or Influence) to learn whether Hinewai placed it intentionally or whether it defends on its own instinct.

**Lasting consequence:** Direct excavation is blocked while the Stillbloom barrier stands. The bracts remain raised as long as the grave is actively exposed or threatened. Retreat from the grave causes them to lower slowly over a few minutes.

---

## Move 3: Spirit Pollen Cloud

**Trigger:** Party moves through or disturbs white spirit pollen sacs when crossing marsh approaches, treeline pockets, or attempting to collect or carry away the Bloom itself.

**Actor:** Spirit Pollen — a place-bound hazardous organism.

**Visible result:** One or more white sacs rupture (from touch, cutting, burning, or vigorous shaking). A 15-foot cloud of white dust and shimmer bursts outward. The cloud is opaque to darkvision and ordinary sight. Hallucinations begin immediately for breathing creatures in the cloud.

**New opportunity:** Party can retreat beyond the cloud's edge. They can Create strong wind (spellcasting, fan, or positioning). They can cover mouths and noses with cloth (imposing disadvantage on the saving throw). They can Search for the sac source and identify it as a hazard for future passage.

**Lasting consequence:** Burst sacs do not regenerate. If sacs remain intact, the hazard persists on future visits. The cloud itself clears in minutes, but affected creatures remain Spirit-Haunted until they complete a save (repeating at the end of each turn or on receiving healing/dispelling magic).

---

## Move 4: Bound Soil Grips

**Trigger:** Party attempts to harvest the too-green grass with tools, gather it to carry away, disturb it with fire or excavation, or try to carry fallen fruit and soil together as a resource.

**Actor:** The bound soil and too-green grass — extensions of the Death Bloom's place-bound nature.

**Visible result:** Grass coils around ankles and wrists. Roots bulge upward from the ground in thick, luminous strands (similar to the roots pointing toward the memorial from [[clear-lake]]). The ground itself becomes treacherous; what was soft becomes tangling, and what was still begins to writhe.

**New opportunity:** Party can Study the roots to learn they point consistently toward the two graves (following the pattern of roots elsewhere on [[aruhe]]). They can escape with a Strength check (DC 13) or by ceasing to disturb the soil. They can negotiate or promise (Influence DC 12) not to take from the memorial, at which point the grass releases.

**Lasting consequence:** Disturbed patches of grass remain darker or scarred afterward, marking the party's trampling. If the party pulls heavier objects (more than 50 lbs of fruit, soil, or memorial pieces), the roots hold them down, making it impossible to carry away the Bloom's pieces. The grass releases such objects only if the party abandons them.

---

## Design Notes

All four moves flow from the memorial's nature as a place-bound phylactery and Hinewai's grief-law that binds Aruhe's living systems. No move invents new creatures or mechanics:

- **Move 1** activates the silent mourners (predators already present and mentioned) and escalates Hinewai's role from guardian-guest to absent judge.
- **Move 2** brings Stillbloom (already hazard-listed) into active defense when graves are threatened.
- **Move 3** leverages Spirit Pollen (already hazard-listed with full mechanics) and creates a persistent environmental cost.
- **Move 4** extends the death-bloom's binding nature (already established: "bound soil," grave-binding roots) into immediate player consequence.

Each move respects the memorial's core truth: the Bloom is a place, not an object. Responses come from the place itself, its guardians, and its living systems — not from Hinewai's combat stat block. The moves preserve player agency (retreat, negotiation, clever solutions) and make the memorial feel alive and responsive rather than a room with obstacles.
