# Fort Crestwall

## Overview

> [!narration] Narration
> The fort sits atop sheer limestone cliffs, gun emplacements commanding the western strait. Stone ramparts trace the crown's edge; a heavy gate opens onto the steep stairs descending to [[calders-tooth-and-port-tidefall]]. The air tastes of salt and cordite. Soldiers move with crisp purpose, their uniforms bearing the Crown's seal. Behind the command pavilion, iron-banded vault doors are set deep into the rock—ancient, sealed, and guarded. The fort is alive with quiet activity: crates stacked in the staging yard, diving gear sorted by shore-weathered hands, cordage and charges accounted and stowed.

---

## At a Glance

Fort Crestwall is a crown garrison holding the western Strait, its twin detachments answerable to sealed Admiralty orders rather than [[governor-voss]]'s civic authority. The fort houses the sealed vaults of [[aldric-drave]]'s living bloodline chart and is currently staging a military operation—reef-diving equipment and underwater charges marked for [[drowned-maw]]—without the Governor's knowledge. Unlike Port Tidefall below it (commerce and inspection) or [[south-harbour]] (repair and fishing), Crestwall is pure military architecture: gunned, restricted, and divided in its loyalties. Skip the fort and lose direct access to the vaults and operation intelligence; the party can only observe from Tidefall below or approach via the dangerous cliff dock.

## If the party

- **Climbs the main stairs from Port Tidefall**: Arrive at the gate. Guards inspect papers and verify garrison clearance. Civilians are permitted to the Crown platform (for strait views and trade) but not barracks, command offices, or vault access. Check: Deception (DC 15) to pass false credentials, or Persuasion (DC 13) if the party has trade business or civic sponsorship. Failure alerts the guard officer; party is denied entry but not arrested.

- **Approaches via cliff dock (below the fort)**: Gain hidden access but face Difficult Terrain (crumbling stone, exposure to fall 40 feet to water and rocks). Party is visible on arrival and must climb the stone access shaft to the staging area above. No guard at the base; discovers supply manifest and equipment.

- **Investigates the staging area**: The reef-diving gear, coiled cordage, charges labeled "for [[drowned-maw]]," and supply manifests are visible. Study the equipment: a character can infer this is preparation for underwater demolition work, targeting a specific site at sea. Search the manifests: they list manifest dates, tonnage, and destination coded as "Crown operation, Admiralty sealed." No names, no operation date, but cross-reference with tidal charts (tide pool high mark in common rooms) shows a five-day window in the coming weeks.

- **Questions soldiers in barracks or yard**: Loose talk over meals and work. Soldiers are resentful of sealed orders and will hint at the operation ("another diving job," "heard the reef is dangerous," "they're moving fast"). A character with Insight (DC 12) or shared military background can gain a soldier's trust. Failure simply yields evasion.

- **Negotiates with junior officers**: Officers are evasive but can be moved through trust-building (shared problem, demonstrated competence, or information trading). An officer who trusts the party may confirm the operation involves [[drowned-maw]], reference a tidal window (three to five days hence), and express concern about secrecy from the Governor. Officer does not name the operation or full objective. Provide this intelligence only if the party has gained genuine rapport (at least two successful negotiations or social interactions).

- **Attempts to access vault doors**: The vaults are sealed with locks of intricate Crown-smithing; the party sees the heavy iron bands, inscriptions in Old Dravosi (if deciphered, name [[aldric-drave]] and reference "bloodline held"), and guard posts nearby. No DC is set: the vault is sealed until the party gains trust with an officer (who provides access key), uses theft (critical task: manifest search, distraction, and lock work), or applies force (alerts the garrison). Describe the vault as *sealed*, not as *impassable*, so the party understands it is a real goal, not a trap.

- **Attempts to steal sealed order parchment from command office**: The office is guarded; entry requires distraction (create alarm, send guard away) or subterfuge (Deception DC 14 to pass as courier or aide). The parchment is in a locked cabinet (Thievery DC 13 or key from officer). If stolen, the party learns partial details: operation codename (not given here—DM fills), target reference to [[drowned-maw]] and coordinates, and timing tied to "spring tide" (allows DM to set precise date). If theft is noticed, Admiralty escalates (see **Where**—offshore support mobilizes).

- **Attempts sabotage or interference with staging**: Moving supplies, damaging equipment, or openly opposing the operation alerts the garrison to alert status. Guards post at staging area. An offshore Admiralty supply ship appears in the strait within one day. Garrison commander moves to isolate the party or arrest if interference is direct. Party gains last-chance observation of operation launch before confrontation.

## Who

**Garrison presence**: Two detachments of soldiers (number and captain names not inventoried—DM determines), under sealed Admiralty orders. Officers are professional but tight-lipped. No named garrison commander is established. Soldiers resent secrecy and will loose talk given opportunity. A supply sergeant manages manifests and equipment; she is thorough, observant, and protective of her inventory but will answer questions if approached with respect.

**Governor's absence**: [[governor-voss]] does not visit the fort; he is excluded from vault access and operation knowledge. He holds civic authority over Port Tidefall but has no command over the garrison. If party brings intelligence to him, he becomes an ally seeking confirmation and possible confrontation with the Admiralty.

**Admiralty presence (distant but real)**: An offshore supply ship and possible support vessels can be called if the garrison escalates. Officers maintain sealed orders and do not defer to civic authority.

**If unattended during party investigation**: The fort is never empty (garrison rotates watch), so parties will encounter soldiers and officers. There is no "sneak through empty barracks" scenario—military discipline is constant.

## What

**Crown platform**: Stone ramparts with gun emplacements (3–4 active cannons pointed at the strait). Offers overlooking view of [[central-strait]], incoming ships, and Port Tidefall below. Soldiers man the guns on regular rotation.

**Gate and entry stairs**: Heavy timber and iron gate; steep stone stairs descending to Tidefall (carved into cliff, weathered, ~60 steps down). Guarded by rotating watch. Takes 5 minutes to descend; climb back up takes 10 minutes (Difficult Terrain if fleeing; check Athletics DC 12 to sprint without falling).

**Barracks**: Soldiers' quarters; crude bunks, shared mess hall, weapons racks, cloaks, boots, personal effects. Nothing exotic. Soldiers eat twice a day (morning and evening). Loose talk occurs over meals.

**Command office**: Officers' space; locked, guarded by duty officer. Contains sealed order parchment (in locked cabinet), correspondence, supply ledgers, and command charts. Visual access to courtyard from windows allows observation of staging area. Locked door (Thievery DC 13) or key required.

**Staging area / Supply yard**: Open courtyard with stacked crates, coiled cordage, reef-diving gear (masks, weights, boots, bell suits if underwater helmet apparatus is in use), and charges marked for [[drowned-maw]]. Manifests pinned to a board (readable at distance if characters approach openly; less visible if sneaking). Equipment is labeled and organized—a quartermaster's work. Small guard presence (1–2 soldiers on rotation). Storehouse building adjacent (lockable; requires key or Thievery DC 13).

**Vault access doors**: Iron-banded wooden doors, deeply recessed into the cliff face below the command level. Inscriptions in Old Dravosi (if translated: "[[aldric-drave]]'s bloodline held in trust"). Sealed with Crown locks (Thievery DC 16 or key from trusted officer). No guards posted *at* the doors (garrison confidence in lock and secrecy), but guards in nearby command area (1 minute walk away) respond to alarm. *Invention note: vault contents beyond bloodline chart not catalogued; DM determines secondary artifacts if discovered.*

**Cliff dock (below)**: Small stone landing at cliff base, accessible by dangerous ledge and iron rungs (Difficult Terrain; falling risk is 40 feet to water). Supplies are loaded and unloaded by crane and pulley system (visible from staging area above). No guard at dock itself; approach is hidden from main garrison if party comes by sea or small boat.

## Where

**North:** The [[the-marrow|Marrow]], a fog-thick ridge running north–south with sinkholes and collapsed caves. ~1–2 hours on foot over rough terrain. *Canon gap: unknown if cave system connects to vaults or fort; mark as unexplored.*

**East:** Mudflats and mangrove thickets; [[south-harbour]] (lightly policed east harbor for repairs and fishing) lies ~3 hours' walk southeast over marsh. [[oshas-slip]] (stilt village) is further east over the mudflats.

**South:** [[calders-tooth-and-port-tidefall]] (Port Tidefall settlement) lies immediate below the cliff face; stairs descend ~60 steps in 5 minutes. Trade shelf, docks, Governor's seat, Tessarine trade house, and civilian harbor all within sight.

**West:** Sheer limestone cliff face; open strait beyond. Ships entering the western Strait pass within sight of Fort Crestwall's guns. Nautical approach to cliff dock is possible but dangerous (rocks, current, exposure to fort guns if hostile).

## Why

Parties come to Fort Crestwall to discover what the Admiralty is secretly planning (the [[drowned-maw]] operation), to navigate the tension between Governor Voss's civic authority and the Admiralty's sealed military command (leverage or alliance), to access or protect the sealed vaults containing [[aldric-drave]]'s bloodline chart (genealogical power or secrecy), or to interrupt or redirect the hidden operation before it proceeds. Players care because the fort sits between civilian and military authority, its secrets affect the harbor and strait, and the vaults hold knowledge that could shift power in the campaign.

## Secrets and Pressure

**Required secrets** (clue vectors prepared):

1. **Operation target and timeline**: What exactly is the Admiralty preparing to strike underwater?
   - *Vector 1 (witness)*: Soldiers loose talk; officer may share if trust is gained
   - *Vector 2 (document)*: Supply manifest labels "drowned-maw"; sealed orders name target if stolen
   - *Vector 3 (pattern)*: Reef gear + charges + tidal window correlate; spring tide creates deployment window

2. **Bloodline chart contents**: What power does [[aldric-drave]]'s living bloodline hold? Why is it sealed from the Governor?
   - *Vector 1 (witness)*: Officer hints at genealogical significance if trust is gained
   - *Vector 2 (document)*: Vault door inscriptions reference bloodline; vault contents not itemized (DM determines)
   - *Vector 3 (pattern)*: Bloodline held from Governor suggests political/dynastic importance; investigation of [[aldric-drave]] entity page reveals context

3. **Authority gap**: Did Admiralty deliberately exclude Governor Voss from operation approval?
   - *Vector 1 (witness)*: Governor himself expresses frustration if party approaches him; Admiralty officers refuse to defer
   - *Vector 2 (document)*: Sealed orders absent from Governor's records (confirmable via Tidefall customs/records check)
   - *Vector 3 (pattern)*: Operation staging without Governor presence; two authorities visibly divided

**Location moves and pressure**:

- **Alert status** (triggered by vault investigation, staging area search, or operation questions): Guard rotations increase; vault access locked down; party feels watched. Officers become more cautious. Soldier loose talk ceases.

- **Escalation** (triggered by sabotage, theft, or direct interference): Offshore Admiralty ship appears in strait within one day. Armed crew readies. Operation timeline may accelerate. Garrison commander issues ultimatum to party if they are identified.

- **Operation execution** (triggered by tidal window arrival): Diving crews load final equipment; operation launches. Party has last chance to observe, intercept, or ally before the op proceeds.
