# Mechanical audit

Run this after the pitch and comparator matrix, before polishing player-facing wording. Treat rarity as a conclusion to test, not an input that excuses excess.

## Offense audit
- Write the likely first three rounds for the intended owner, including setup, normal use, misses, saves, charges, concentration, bonus actions, reactions, and target availability.
- Compare reliable damage, burst damage, accuracy/DC, target count, range, damage type, critical interaction, and control against the chassis and effect peers.
- Mark once-per-turn, per-turn, per-round, per-rest, charge, and duration limits explicitly. “On every hit” multiplies with Extra Attack, two-weapon use, reactions, opportunity attacks, and summoned attacks; narrow it unless that multiplication is the point and has been priced.
- If control is stronger than damage, audit failed-save consequence, repeat save timing, target count, duration, range, tell, and at least two answers.

## Defense audit
Count effective HP, AC, saves, resistance/immunity, temporary HP, healing, displacement, invisibility, cover, escape, and prevention together. Ask:

- Does the item erase normal threat, or merely let the user choose when to spend a defense?
- Does it stack with armor, shield, class features, reactions, and other items?
- Is there a cost, duration, charge, vulnerability, line-of-sight constraint, or counterplay?
- Does the item make one character the only sensible target or make the rest of the party irrelevant?

A broad defense usually needs attunement, a limit, or a trade. Narrow protection may be common or uncommon when it is genuinely situational.

## Action economy audit
Record the cost in a table like this:

| Use | Cost | Opportunity cost | Frequency | Failure/limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reliable use | Action/bonus/reaction/free/none | What the bearer gives up | At will/turn/rest/charges | Range, target, or roll |
| Burst | Record | Record | Record | Record |
| Defense/utility | Record | Record | Record | Record |

Price off-turn triggers honestly. A reaction is not free when it competes with a class reaction; a bonus action is not free when it competes with damage, movement, or setup. Avoid mandatory tracking that is not worth the decision it creates.

## Spell-access audit
For each spell from the item, state spell level, casting time, range, target, duration, concentration, components, ability/DC or attack bonus, charges or uses, and whether the spell is cast from the item. Do not turn an item into an unbounded extra spell-slot pool. Check ritual, upcasting, reaction spells, area control, teleportation, healing, and repeatability separately. Concentration remains concentration unless the rules or the design explicitly and safely say otherwise; removing it is a major power axis.

## Stacking audit
Test the item with the intended owner's best three common turns, not a blank character. Check Extra Attack, Action Surge, Two-Weapon Fighting, smites or equivalent burst, sneak attack, critical hits, advantage, summoned creatures, mounted movement, opportunity attacks, reactions, temporary HP, resistance, shield effects, concentration, and other attuned items. State whether a bonus is typed, untyped, additive, replacement, or conditional. If a feature can multiply, add a cap or once-per-turn limit.

## Party-role audit
Name the class and party niche that benefit most, then ask whether the item:

- invalidates another class's signature feature or resource;
- makes one character the required carrier;
- steals spotlight through damage, defense, scouting, social authority, or travel bypass;
- is useful to every character at once despite one attunement slot; or
- changes the campaign's intended challenge (especially rests, navigation, stealth, logistics, or attrition).

Narrow the item, shift its benefit to a decision, or give the displaced niche a meaningful route. Do not solve every problem with the item.

## Challenge preservation
Audit combat, exploration, social play, and downtime. “Never get lost,” permanent flight, at-will teleportation, unlimited invisibility, universal translation, and effortless resurrection can remove whole game loops. Preserve challenge with range, charges, risk, visible tells, destination limits, weather/terrain constraints, concentration, preparation, costs, or a choice between immediate power and long-term access. Travel utility should open routes, not trivialize travel forever.

## Verdict and tuning
End with:

- **Safe as written / revise / reject:** one sentence and why.
- **Strongest axis:** the best thing it does.
- **Weakest or missing trade:** what pays for that strength.
- **Abuse case:** the first interaction to test.
- **Tuning knobs:** reduce target count, range, die size, duration, frequency, charges, spell level, or bonus; add attunement, concentration, setup, risk, or a once-per-turn limit. Prefer one change at a time.
