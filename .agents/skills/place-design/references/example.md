# Worked example

One place taken through steps 1 to 7. Every name here is made up for
illustration and is never canon. Match the **shape and quality**; write your own
place from your own vault.

## 1. Canon inventory

- `[[harrow-mouth]]` · place · the estuary town this site serves.
- `[[mother-of-eels]]` · creature · a longboat-sized eel that leads the spring
  eel run up the estuary.
- `[[tide-lantern]]` · item · a lantern that flares when the tide turns.
- `[[saltwardens]]` · faction · tax every catch landed on the coast.
- `[[drowning-of-1190]]` · lore · the flood that drowned Harrow's lower town.
- `[[oda-fenn]]` · npc · weirkeeper, flood survivor.
- `[[brine-rot]]` · hazard · rot that leaves boards looking sound.

## 2. Kernel

The weir traps eels for Harrow Mouth. It is built from the drowned town's roofs.
The keepers are hiding a trapped Mother of Eels and the lantern that calms her
from the Saltwardens. Promise: discovery and intrigue. If nobody intervenes, the
Mother dies before spring and the eel run fails.

## 3. This place and no other

- **Stock version:** a fishing weir with nets.
- **Twist:** the fence panels are the roofs of the drowned lower town.
- **Rule of the place:** the loose slates hum, and the note climbs when
  something heavy moves beyond the outer fence. Locals time crossings by it.
  Limit: storm wind hums too. Test: drop a heavy stone off the outer fence.
- **Signatures:** a catwalk along cottage-wide roof panels (spatial); the slate
  hum from the tide pulling through the gaps (sensory); keepers knock each post
  before stepping past it (behavioural).
- **Swap test:** "the catwalk is slippery" is true of every weir, so it was
  cut and replaced with the brine-rotted south arm.

## 6. Narration

> The Eel Weir at Harrow Mouth is a fence of old roofs standing in the estuary, a long curve of slate panels hammered onto oak posts from the reed bank almost to the deep channel, each panel as wide as a cottage and still hung with its gutters. A plank catwalk runs along the top, and every post it passes is worn pale at boot height. When the tide pulls through the gaps, the loose slates hum, a low note that climbs whenever something heavy moves beyond the outer fence. The third panel from the bank still carries its chimney pot upright, the lip black with fresh soot. Farther out, the inner fence swells outward in a shallow belly, its slates cracked along the curve, and the gulls that crowd every other post leave that stretch bare. The south arm of the catwalk runs greener than the rest, furred with weed, and gives underfoot without a creak. At low water a ladder of nailed rungs drops to the mud, where wicker eel traps lie in rows like loaves set out to cool, each tied with a lead tag stamped with a crown over a salt pan.

Why it works: every tell rides on its owner noun (the pot, the fence, the
catwalk, the tags) at the same weight as the gutters and the loaves, so nothing
announces itself. A player who asks about the soot, the bulge, or the silent
boards has pulled a real thread, and the page answers.

## 7. Filed entries (excerpt)

**What**

- **Chimney pot.** *"The lip black with fresh soot."* [[oda-fenn]] hides the
  [[tide-lantern]] in a tarred sack inside the pot and lights it at every tide
  turn to calm the [[mother-of-eels]]. The [[saltwardens]] would seize it as
  untaxed salvage. A climb up the wet slate (Athletics DC 10) reaches it, and
  the sack is warm for an hour after each lighting.
- **Inner fence.** *"Swells outward in a shallow belly."* The
  [[mother-of-eels]] has been trapped behind it since the autumn storm shifted
  the channel. Oda's keepers feed her at night. If she dies before spring, the
  eel run fails and [[harrow-mouth]] goes hungry.

**Who**

- [[oda-fenn]] and three keepers work the catwalk at every tide turn. They want
  the Mother kept alive and hidden until the spring flood can free her.
- **Move, on its own clock.** At every third slack tide, two [[saltwardens]] row
  a skiff down the weir to count the traps. On the first count after the
  party arrives, they stop under the soot-black chimney pot and send a man up
  the slates. The party can distract them, get to the lantern first, or watch
  Oda take the blame; if the Saltwardens take the lantern, the Mother thrashes
  at the next tide turn and breaks the inner fence.

**If the party**

- **Walk the south arm.** The boards look sound and are rotten through; resolve
  on [[brine-rot]]. A pole knocked against them first sinks in without a sound,
  and the north arm reaches the same outer posts by a longer loop.
