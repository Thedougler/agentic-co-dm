# Topology and life

Read this in step 5. It covers the node graph, obstacles, factions, location
moves, pressure, and return state.

## Nodes and edges

Write the place as `node --edge (route; cost; information; danger;
consequence)--> node`. A node is a significant situation, landmark, resource,
threshold, or decision. An edge is any way between nodes: a corridor, a bridge
toll, a faction invitation, a weather window, a rooftop, a remembered shortcut.

Good topology usually has:

- two or more approaches where the fiction permits;
- one loop, one bypass, and one retreat or safe return;
- one vertical, environmental, social, or reality-bending connection;
- a route tradeoff: fast or safe, hidden or exposed, costly or informative,
  reversible or committing;
- reconnection after branches, unless isolation is the pressure;
- an answer to "what changes if they use this edge?"

## Node card

One card per significant node, in working notes. File its facts into the
template sections, not the card itself.

```markdown
### [ID] Name, and its job in the graph
**Approach:** route, arrival cost, what is known before entry
**Current activity:** who or what is acting now; what changes if ignored
**Weave rows:** the weave-map elements that sit here
**Affordances:** likely verbs and what each changes
**Obstacle:** sign, trigger, effect, counterplay, bypass, leverage
**Edges:** route, cost, information, danger, consequence
**Payoff:** what is learned, gained, altered, or made possible
**Mechanics:** only the 2024 rules, DCs, cover, or terrain the DM must run
**Return state:** what persists or changes after the party leaves
```

## The verb test

Ask what the players can do here that matters. A node passes when it offers a
consequential verb (cross, question, bargain, hide, study, reroute, steal,
rescue, climb, harvest, sabotage, consecrate, wait) tied to a route, clue,
resource, faction, pressure, or payoff.

Prepare materials, relationships, and constraints rather than one solution.
"Use the blue key" is a key; "open, bypass, trade for, or break the lock, each
at a different cost" is an affordance. When a player proposes a method that
fits the fiction, it works or fails forward with a visible cost.

## Obstacles: six questions

1. **Sign.** What can players observe before committing?
2. **Trigger.** What action, timing, or threshold sets it off?
3. **Effect.** What changes in position, safety, resources, access,
   information, trust, or pressure?
4. **Counterplay.** What can preparation, tools, abilities, terrain,
   negotiation, or timing do about it?
5. **Bypass.** What alternate route, deal, stunt, or partial objective avoids
   it, and at what cost?
6. **Leverage.** Which clue, relationship, or earlier choice makes a better
   option available?

## Faction sheet

```markdown
### Faction or group
**Want:** the immediate objective and the longer claim
**Fear:** the loss, exposure, rival, or change they cannot absorb
**Method:** what they do first, and what they refuse to do
**Resources:** people, territory, time, information, tools, legitimacy
**Tell:** the observable mark, routine, or mistake (goes in the weave map)
**Offer:** what they can grant, trade, teach, or threaten
**Response:** to bargain, aid, deception, attack, retreat, and neglect
**Red line and current move:**
```

A faction deeply involved in the place gets its own page through
`faction-design`; the place page links it and states its local presence.

## Progression

Give the place a short state track that fits its fiction (quiet → alert →
contested → transformed, or festive → strained → divided → changed). For each
state: the trigger, the visible sign, and the new opportunity or cost. Name the
trigger; a room entered is not a trigger.

## Location moves

A location move is a concrete change the place or an inhabitant makes, on its
own clock or in response to play. Prepare two to four, at least one on the
actor's own clock:

```markdown
**Move:** [actor] [action]
**Trigger:** [observable event or clock step]
**Visible result:** [what players notice now]
**New opportunity:** [what can be exploited, negotiated, or learned]
**Lasting consequence:** [what changes on the map, in relationships, or on return]
```

Triggers come from party choices, faction actions, elapsed pressure, rest,
weather, or the place's rule. Example: a ruin's caretaker moves the only working
lift after the party is seen near it; the party can bargain, follow the new
cable line, or take the exposed climb.

## Pressure

Record the source (pursuit, scarcity, weather, deadline, ritual, instability,
politics, attention), the advance trigger, the current state players can see,
what each step changes, how players can slow or exploit it, and what happens
when it fills. Pressure sharpens a decision; it is visible and answerable.

## Return state

After a visit, update five lines: routes opened or closed; people who moved,
learned, allied, fled, or took charge; resources spent, stolen, or scarce;
current pressure and the next move; the new question that invites a return.
Keep durable geography and change occupation, meaning, access, evidence, or
stakes. A cleared node stays cleared until the fiction refills it.
