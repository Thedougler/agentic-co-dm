# Weaving canon into a place

Read this in step 4. It covers the tell, each kind of thing a place holds, and
environmental storytelling. A worked example is in [example.md](example.md).

## The tell

A tell is the perceivable surface of something the place holds. It is the only
part of a secret, item, hazard, or presence that reaches the narration.

A strong tell is:

- **Concrete and durable.** An object, mark, sound, or smell with a source,
  true on any visit until play changes it.
- **Specific to its truth.** A player who pulls on it lands on this truth, not
  a generic "something is wrong".
- **Plain.** It sits inside a sentence about the noun that owns it, at the same
  weight and rhythm as the details around it, with no signal word. It hides by
  being ordinary.
- **Answerable.** The page says what a closer look, a check, or an action finds.

| Truth | Weak tell | Strong tell |
|---|---|---|
| A smuggler's cache under the third berth | "something seems hidden near the berths" | "the third berth's planks are newer than the rest and nailed with brass" |
| A creature nests in the chimney | "you sense a presence" | "the soot on the hearthstones is scored with three-toed prints that lead up the flue, not out the door" |
| A sea god's claim on the village | "an ancient power lingers" | "every net hung to dry has one knot tied backward" |
| The keeper sold the old lighthouse lens | "the tower hides a past" | "the lamp room holds a lens cradle the size of a cartwheel, empty, its bolts cut clean" |

Different truths get different tells. Put one tell per truth in the narration;
the other clue vectors live in If the party and What.

## What a place holds

### Secrets

State the truth in one or two plain sentences: who, what, why, and what is at
stake, by name. A secret that a meaningful choice depends on gets three clue
vectors, each in a different feature:

- **Operational:** how it works, shown by a mechanism, a routine, or a trace of
  use.
- **Historical:** what happened, shown by a witness, document, inscription,
  damage, or remnant.
- **Directional:** where it leads next, shown by a map, repeated symbol, route
  geometry, behaviour, or consequence.

One vector is the narration tell. One answers a declared action (a 2024 Search,
Study, Influence, or Utilize). One comes through a person, document, or
consequence. A failed check costs time, position, exposure, trust, or supplies
and leaves the other vectors in place.

### Hazards

Give sign, trigger, effect, counterplay, bypass, and leverage. The sign is the
tell. Damage, saves, and conditions stay on the hazard's owner page when one
exists; the place page says what triggers contact and what changes after.

### Items

Say where the item sits and why there (who put it there, who wants it), how it
is reached (locked, guarded, buried, for sale, worn by someone), and the trace
visible before anyone reaches it: the case, the tag, the wear on the shelf, the
buyer's name chalked on the lid. When the item page has presence notes in At
the Table, take its seen details and its hidden tell from there. Effects stay
on the item's owner page.

### People and creatures

Present: where they are, what they are doing now, what they want, and the tell
of their habits that shows even when they are out of sight (bedding, tools
left warm, a smell, a path worn in the moss). Absent: the durable sign of who
was here and who is not here now.

### Lore and history

History shows in structure and wear: repairs, additions, defacements, and
things built for one purpose now used for another. Each visible trace gets one
sentence of its truth on the page and a link to the lore owner.

### Factions

A claim marks the place: sigils, tolls, patrol wear, who is welcome, who pays.
The mark is the tell; the faction sheet in
[topology-and-life.md](topology-and-life.md) carries the rest.

## Environmental storytelling

Pick one or two past events the inventory supports. Leave the evidence in the
place and keep the explanation on the DM page. Useful evidence shapes:

- **Before and after:** a clean rectangle on the wall where a painting hung.
- **Direction:** cage bars bent outward, drag marks toward the water.
- **Sequence:** three layers of paint on a sign, each a different owner's name.
- **Absence:** a place set at the table for someone who never sat down.

Players rebuild the story from traces. The DM page states what happened, who
did it, and when.
