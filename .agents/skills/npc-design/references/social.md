# Social play

Read this in step 4. It covers the Influence procedure, how an NPC enters a
scene, and what a confrontation protects.

## Influence procedure

1. **Frame the request.** What does the NPC want now? What is the party asking,
   and what would it cost or threaten?
2. **Set starting state.** Mark Attitude: Friendly, Indifferent, or Hostile.
   Mark request posture: Willing, Unwilling, or Hesitant. These are separate.
3. **Choose an approach.** Conversation, evidence, favor, gift, threat, appeal,
   or leverage. Explain why it fits the NPC’s need, limit, and contradiction.
4. **Decide whether to roll.** Roll only if the outcome is uncertain, the stakes
   matter, and a miss will change the situation; write the roll as Ability
   (Skill) and DC. Otherwise state the result.
5. **Resolve movement.** Success improves attitude, willingness, access, or the
   concession. A miss spends time, leverage, trust, safety, or an opportunity.
   Neither result rewrites beliefs or removes agency.
6. **Name the new position.** Say what the NPC will do, will not do, or needs
   next. Record the cost and the next interruption point.

Do not roll for essential information, basic cooperation already earned, or an
NPC’s immutable values. Put essential information in about three independent
clues: a statement, a physical trace, a witness, a document, or a consequence.
A failed check can obscure which clue is true, add danger, or cost time; it
should not erase the only path forward.

## Introduction sequence

Anchor the NPC in the immediate problem or place; show one portrayal signal;
let them make a consequential move; state what they want from the party; give
players a clear question, offer, refusal, or way to interrupt. Do not begin
with a résumé or secret monologue.

## Confrontation objectives and failure futures

Define what each side is protecting, stealing, proving, escaping with, or
sacrificing. Add at least two non-defeat outcomes. For a failed objective, write
a concrete future: the ally is relocated, the ritual advances, the evidence is
lost but a witness survives, or the villain escapes wounded. Failure changes the
next decision; it does not end the story or secretly restore the status quo.
