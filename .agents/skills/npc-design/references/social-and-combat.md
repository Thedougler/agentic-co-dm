# Social and combat procedures

## Influence procedure

1. **Frame the request.** What does the NPC want now? What is the party asking,
   and what would it cost or threaten?
2. **Set starting state.** Mark Attitude: Friendly, Indifferent, or Hostile.
   Mark request posture: Willing, Unwilling, or Hesitant. These are separate.
3. **Choose an approach.** Conversation, evidence, favor, gift, threat, appeal,
   or leverage. Explain why it fits the NPC’s need, limit, and contradiction.
4. **Decide whether to roll.** Roll only if the outcome is uncertain, the stakes
   matter, and a miss will change the situation. Otherwise state the result.
5. **Resolve movement.** Success improves attitude, willingness, access, or the
   concession. A miss spends time, leverage, trust, safety, or an opportunity.
   Neither result rewrites beliefs or removes agency.
6. **Name the new position.** Say what the NPC will do, will not do, or needs
   next. Record the cost and the next interruption point.

Do not roll for essential information, basic cooperation already earned, or an
NPC’s immutable values. Put essential information in about three independent
clues: a statement, a physical trace, a witness, a document, or a consequence.
A failed check can obscure which clue is true, add danger, or cost time; it
should not erase the only path forward.

## Introduction sequence

Anchor the NPC in the immediate problem or place; show one portrayal signal;
let them make a consequential move; state what they want from the party; give
players a clear question, offer, refusal, or way to interrupt. Do not begin
with a résumé or secret monologue.

## Monster-style combat package

Use this for a named NPC whose identity matters in a fight. Statblocks embed
in the NPC file's `# Combat` section (not a separate monster note). Hand off
numbers, CR, damage, and stat-block balance to `homebrew-monsters-5e`.

Design these fields, then distribute into `## Running [Name]` and `# Combat`:

- **Objective:** What are they trying to do besides “win”? → Running section.
- **Signature:** One action that expresses identity and changes the position. → statblock.
- **Fallback:** Reliable action when the signature is unavailable or costly. → statblock.
- **Move/reaction:** A reposition, defense, bargain, escape, or response to a PC. → statblock.
- **Escalation:** A phase, resource spend, environmental change, or desperate choice. → Running section + staged statblocks.
- **Tell/limit:** What players can notice, exploit, or force. → Running section.
- **Exit:** Conditions for surrender, flight, capture, or death. → Running section.

Multi-stage NPCs: state an **encounter rule** in `# Combat` that keys each
statblock to a fiction condition (see Hinewai exemplar: Death Bloom state
determines which of three stages to use).

### Boss action economy

A solo boss needs meaningful things to do between turns: a reaction, movement,
minion, hazard, objective, or limited resource. Prefer a small number of
readable options over a long menu. Give players counterplay and telegraph major
changes. A boss can retreat or bargain; do not make hit points the only clock.

## Confrontation objectives and failure futures

Define what each side is protecting, stealing, proving, escaping with, or
sacrificing. Add at least two non-defeat outcomes. For a failed objective, write
a concrete future: the ally is relocated, the ritual advances, the evidence is
lost but a witness survives, or the villain escapes wounded. Failure changes the
next decision; it does not end the story or secretly restore the status quo.

## Test checklist

- Want is present-tense and actionable.
- Leverage, need, limit, and contradiction can affect a choice.
- Four signals are distinct and non-caricatured.
- Attitude and request posture are separate.
- Influence has stakes, consent, movement, and a cost on a miss.
- Essential information has about three clues.
- Introduction creates a player-facing action.
- Recurrence records a change caused by play.
- Villain steps have signs, reactions, and interruption points.
- Combat uses a package, not a full PC sheet; the boss has action economy.
- Confrontation and redemption have multiple conditional conclusions.
