# NPC craft

## Social play

Use ordinary conversation first. Attitude (Friendly, Indifferent, Hostile) is
starting disposition, not a verdict on a character. Willing, Unwilling, and
Hesitant describe the particular request — an NPC can be friendly but unwilling,
or hostile yet willing when leverage is strong.

Influence is the structured procedure for changing disposition or securing a
reasonable concession: establish what the NPC wants, state the approach and
stakes, then check only when outcome is uncertain and meaningful. Apply
advantage/disadvantage or set DC from the fiction. Success earns movement, not
mind control; failure changes position, costs time, spends leverage, or closes
an approach. Do not roll to withhold essential information; seed it through
about three independent clues.

**Efficient introduction:** anchor in place or current problem; show one signal;
let them make a consequential move; state what they want from the party; leave a
clear answer, refusal, or next action.

**Participants loop:** each round, let the NPC pursue a want, let players answer,
resolve only the uncertain point, then update attitude/willingness, leverage, and
cost. Repeat until agreement, refusal, interruption, departure, or a changed
objective.

See `social-and-combat.md` for full Influence steps, introduction sequence, and
combat packages.

## Recurrence and villains

After each appearance, add an activity-log entry: **date/scene — action taken,
new information, changed relationship, next move**. On return, show one change
caused by play; do not reset to the first impression.

For a villain: **understandable desire + unacceptable method + capacity to act +
collision with the characters**. Define Goal, Why, Method, Lie, Line, Assets,
Vulnerabilities, and Connection to the party. Also record public face, leverage,
need, limit, contradiction, allies, tell, plan clock, escalation threshold, and
stop condition.

Break the plan into interruptible steps with trigger, objective, resource,
visible sign, consequence, and fallback. The villain reacts to player choices;
they do not predict perfectly. Put presence before the finale through rumors,
traces, agents, consequences, messages, or brief appearances. Never grant
cutscene immunity.

A betrayal must be earned, possible to notice, and not the default personality
of every trusted ally. Give competing incentives and an off-ramp. Design more
than one conclusion: defeat, compromise, escape, exposure, alliance, or
redemption only when actions satisfy stated conditions. Redemption is a future
choice with risk, not a predetermined moral reward.

See `villain-front.md` for clocks, reactions, presence, betrayal, conclusions,
and villain audit.

## Wiki fill detail

At a Glance is a cue table: Role, Nature, Home, Wants. Extra rows only when they
change how the DM runs the NPC. End with a one-sentence **DM thesis**.

When the NPC is fightable, add Combat (encounter rule plus sheet or one pointer).
When origin, extra form, live pressure, or a secret exists, file those facts.
Thin people omit unused extra depth; they are not incomplete.

`wiki/_raw/Hinewai.md`, `wiki/_raw/Talon Skarn.md`, `wiki/_raw/Nona Black-Jaw.md`,
and `wiki/_raw/Thunk.md` illustrate density. They are not required classes and
not clone targets. Landmark/skirmish/patron/contact remain useful density
examples.

## GM-prep and world-bible

Prep only what the DM cannot comfortably improvise: want, leverage, limit,
signals, next move, and one or two modular responses. Keep active NPCs distinct
from past/inactive figures and show how play changed a recurring NPC. File
durable improvisations as typed atomic owner notes; link the nearest MOC and keep
Organizer responsible for index structure. Secrets and unearned names stay out of
`[!narration]`. A DM should recover the actionable card in under 30 seconds.

## Audit questions

Before play:

- Can the table tell what this NPC wants?
- What can players change?
- What happens if they ignore them?
- What is the cost of cooperation?
- What is the limit or contradiction?
- What will the NPC do next, and how can players interrupt it?

## Failure modes

| Failure mode | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| Biography without want | Replace history with present-tense ask + deadline |
| Lore mouthpiece | Want something, be wrong, or refuse a detail |
| Persuasion as mind control | Define willingness, stakes, concession; check moves position only |
| DMPC ally | Narrow competence; defer to player choices |
| Over-prepped incidental | Cut to name, want, signal, exit |
| One-clue villain arc | Seed ~three independent channels |
| Cutscene immunity | Accept interruptible outcomes or genuine remote fiction |
| Predetermined redemption | Multiple conditional conclusions; play decides |
| Full PC sheet for enemy | Monster-style package; handoff to homebrew-monsters-5e |
| Silent invention as canon | Label + cite + propose; work gate first |
| Craft job in YAML `role` | Move to Nature/body; set rival\|patron\|contact |

Also see failure modes in `npc-templates.md`.
