A large spotted cat, muscular and built low, with a broad head and long ivory saber teeth set in a dark muzzle. Amber eyes watch from that dark face. The fur is golden-brown, marked throughout with dark rosettes. Large paws built for weight and reach. A long tail banded in the same spotted pattern.

Over the back and shoulders, a mantle of leaves, grass fibers, and creeping vines—tangled, some fresh green, some dried brown—clings in the fur and hangs from the spine. The mantle breaks the silhouette completely. From a distance, the back and shoulders look like another grass clump among the reeds, while the spotted face below the mantle's front, the legs, and the banded tail show clearly.

In tall grass and brush, the spiguar lies nearly motionless, chin down, amber eyes on the game trail. Wind stirs the mantle. Insects work the tangled growth. Birds settle on the vines. The cat stays still. That stillness is the habit of its kind: waiting, watching the lane.
