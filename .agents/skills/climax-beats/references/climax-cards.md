# Climax cards

The first two shapes follow Mike Pondsmith, *Scripting the Game* (R.
Talsorian, 2020; transcription at `RTG-Scripting-the-Game-v1.2-agent-readable (2).md`
in the repo root), paraphrased for 5e play. The extensions after them are this
repo's additions for climaxes the fiction earns without a showdown or a
drawing-room reveal. Only a Resolution follows any Climax.

Pick the shape the party's choices made inevitable:

- Which confrontation did the middle point at? Threads that Developments
  revealed and Cliffhangers tested converge here.
- Did the party earn a fight, a truth, a deal, a defense, or a sacrifice? The
  planned villain duel is only a candidate.
- Action Climax (Final Battle, Siege, Catastrophe, Desperate Gambit) wants a
  Development before it; cerebral Climax (Final Revelation, Trial,
  Negotiation Under Duress) wants a Cliffhanger before it.

Each card: **Shape** — what the Climax is; **Build** — what the page must
contain beyond the table-ready anatomy and the harvest table; **Fair play** —
how the card keeps the outcome in the players' hands.

## Final Revelation
- **Shape:** The party and the other actors gather in one place and, like the end of an Agatha Christie novel, must work out what is going on and expose the true facts. Everything is finally revealed — the plot, the murderer, the secret identity. The consequence of exposure is settled in advance: once the killer is named, the watch arrests them; once the Baron's business is uncovered, he slinks away disgraced. The confrontation is the uncovering.
- **Build:** Why everyone is gathered here; the full truth (DM layer); the proof the party holds, beat by beat where they got it; who contests the truth, their best alternative story, and what breaks it; the settled consequence of a correct exposure; the consequence of accusing wrongly; the culprit's last move when cornered (flight, a hostage, a confession, a counter-accusation) with numbers if it turns violent.
- **Fair play:** The verdict follows the evidence the party actually presents; a wrong accusation has a defined result, not a do-over.

## Final Battle
- **Shape:** The big showdown between the party and the opposition. Bring in the villain, the henchmen, assorted minions, monsters — whatever the opposition can muster — on a stage built for destruction on a grand scale. The Final Battle wraps up the game: the villain beaten, the captive rescued, the kingdom saved — if the party wins.
- **Build:** Forces by escalation tier with numbers (villain substantially stronger than any PC); a stage with three features that break, burn, flood, or collapse; two or three phases with triggers; what the villain's side accomplishes each round nobody stops them; the lever each harvested thread gives the party (the Hidden Weakness, the allies, the mastered skill); win and lose conditions beyond hit points.
- **Fair play:** The party can win, just barely, with what the middle gave them; every harvested lever works as the page says.

## Extensions

### Sacrifice
- **Shape:** Resolving the central stakes requires a PC or key ally to give up something irreplaceable — life, freedom, power, a bond.
- **Build:** What is saved, what is lost, who can pay; the mechanics of the price; what refusing costs; any shared-burden option the fiction supports.
- **Fair play:** The sacrifice is a real choice with a real refusal path; the player chooses whether their character pays.

### Desperate Gambit
- **Shape:** Safer routes are closed; the party commits to an improvised, high-risk plan.
- **Build:** The obstacles and their rulings at each stage of the likely plans; what breaks if a piece fails; the opposition's response once it notices.
- **Fair play:** The plan is the players' invention; the page supplies obstacles and costs, and partial success is a valid ending.

### Reckoning
- **Shape:** Several consequences, debts, and delayed threats arrive at once and demand simultaneous answers.
- **Build:** Each arriving thread with its demand, its clock, and what it does if unanswered; the triage choices; which threads can be delegated to allies.
- **Fair play:** Each unhandled thread produces its own stated consequence.

### Siege
- **Shape:** The party defends something precious — a place, person, object, or principle — against overwhelming or sustained pressure.
- **Build:** What is defended; the attackers' waves with numbers; the weakest point; what can be abandoned and what that costs; where relief could come from, established earlier.
- **Fair play:** The party chooses what to hold and what to give up; an overrun defense is an outcome row.

### Negotiation Under Duress
- **Shape:** Brokering a deal, truce, surrender, or alliance while the situation actively deteriorates.
- **Build:** Each side's interests, bottom line, and leverage; the terms on offer; the deterioration clock; what breaks if talks fail — with that fight's numbers.
- **Fair play:** Both sides act on established interests; a bad deal accepted is a valid Climax.

### Catastrophe
- **Shape:** The opposition is a force — storm, collapse, plague, magical unraveling — and survival or prevention is the confrontation.
- **Build:** The established warning signs that led here; scope and rate (what falls each round); shelter and its checks; prevention or redirection with its cost.
- **Fair play:** The catastrophe follows causes the players can trace; surviving with loss is a valid outcome.

### Trial
- **Shape:** A judgment — court, council, divine test, community verdict — decides freedom, war, exile, execution, or alliance.
- **Build:** The charge, who judges and what moves them; the procedure; the evidence each side holds; the range of verdicts and what each triggers.
- **Fair play:** The verdict follows the argument and evidence the party presents.

### Betrayal Cascade
- **Shape:** A trusted ally, faction, or institution turns, and the climax is the fracture itself — choosing sides, exposing the betrayer, surviving the reversal.
- **Build:** Who turns and why (motive planted in earlier beats); who is caught in the middle; what each side offers; the fight's numbers if it comes to that.
- **Fair play:** Players who spotted the earlier tells act on their preparation; the betrayal follows established motive.
