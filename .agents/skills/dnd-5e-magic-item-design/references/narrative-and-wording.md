# Narrative, wording, and playtest

## Narrative fit
The item needs a visual/sensory tell, a history only when it changes play, and a reason the bearer makes choices rather than receiving a passive upgrade. Keep names and fiction setting-agnostic. Tie the strongest mechanic to a recognizable behavior, cost, or temptation. Delete lore that does not alter a choice, reveal a tell, support a handoff, or explain a limit.

## Cursed items
A curse is a playable pressure, not a secret punishment or a way to take a character away from its player. State the tell before or as the curse matters, what choice activates or deepens it, what the bearer can do, how allies can help, what the exit condition is, and what consequence remains. Obtain player buy-in for loss of control, altered goals, or irreversible change. Never make “the player did not know” the only dramatic engine.

## Sentient items
Define its want, fear, manner, boundaries, tell, leverage, and response to refusal. Give the bearer disagreement and negotiation, not an invisible veto. A sentient item may offer information, a situational benefit, a cost, or a competing objective; do not make it a second player character that monopolizes decisions. The item should be fallible and its preferences should create choices.

## Evolving items
Use explicit stages. For each stage record trigger, new capability, cost or trade, tell, and how the party can accept, delay, redirect, or end the evolution. Do not add every stage's maximum benefits at once. Re-audit rarity, attunement, action economy, stacking, and party role whenever a stage unlocks.

## 2024/5.5e rules language
Use concise player-facing notation: `DC 15`, `+7 to hit`, `Hit: 11 (2d8 + 2) damage`, `Speed 30 feet`, `1/Day`, `Recharge 5–6`, explicit ranges, targets, durations, triggers, and repeat-save timing. On wiki body (item notes, run cards), write checks and saves with the at-table grammar in `obsidian-markdown`. Say “the spell is cast from the item” when relevant. Name concentration. Specify whether a use requires an action, bonus action, reaction, or no action. Avoid copying published text; express the function in original language.

## Player-facing item text

Use this structure for the mechanics block in the wiki note. Simple items (consumables, mundane) need 1-3 sentences. Complex items expand with bold-label properties:

**Simple** (Aruhe consumable pattern):
```
Kind, Rarity

As a Bonus Action, eat this fruit. Effect statement with **bold conditions**, `dice`, and duration.
```

**Complex** (magic weapons, relics, attunement items):
```
Kind, Rarity (requires attunement [by ...])

**[Passive or chassis feature].** Effect, limits, stacking interaction.

**[Activated feature].** Action cost, range, targets, roll/DC, duration, concentration, frequency.

**[Risk, curse, or sentience].** Tell, player choice, consequence, exit or resolution.

**[Charges/recharge].** Tracking and recovery procedure.
```

The wiki note follows `wiki/templates/item.md` — flat body: image, narration, type-line, mechanics. Design-process artifacts (comparator matrix, power envelope, engagement loop) stay in the conversation or design doc, not in the wiki note.

## Required design notes
Include these headings in the design notes, with links inline rather than a separate bibliography:

- **Intended Experience:** who should want it, what decision it creates, and what it should not replace.
- **Published Benchmarks:** the completed comparator matrix with working links; state which chassis and effect peers set the ceiling.
- **Balance Assessment:** offense, defense, action economy, spell access, stacking, party role, challenge preservation, and uncertainty.
- **Tuning Knobs:** ordered changes for too strong, too weak, too passive, too complex, or too disruptive.

## Playtesting
1. Before play, script three likely rounds and one exploration/social use. Mark every action, roll, resource, target, and concentration conflict.
2. During play, log actual activations, damage/healing prevented, failed or successful saves, charges spent, turns altered, and moments of indecision or delight. Ask who else lost a meaningful choice.
3. After the session, compare actual use with the matrix and intended experience. Test the strongest abuse case separately; do not nerf from one lucky roll.
4. Change one knob at a time, state the reason, and rerun the highest-impact scenario. Reassess rarity only after the behavior is stable.

## Final checklist
- [ ] Pitch names experience, owner, tier, cost/risk, and counterplay.
- [ ] Chassis preference was considered; bespoke mechanics are justified.
- [ ] Comparator matrix includes working links.
- [ ] At least one official 2024/public rules anchor and three comparator roles are recorded.
- [ ] 2014 material is labeled legacy and recalibrated, not silently reused.
- [ ] Offense, defense, action economy, spell access, stacking, party roles, and challenge preservation were audited.
- [ ] Rarity is not used to stack maximum offense, defense, mobility, control, and utility.
- [ ] Every repeatable hit rider, control effect, charge, duration, and concentration interaction is explicit.
- [ ] Curses and sentience have tells, player choices, counterplay, consent, and exits.
- [ ] Player-facing wording is original, concise, and runnable.
- [ ] Intended Experience, Published Benchmarks, Balance Assessment, and Tuning Knobs are present.
- [ ] Playtest triggers and revision knobs are stated.
- [ ] Default output ends with the design/playtest handoff, not a separate Sources section.
