# Thornwatch

> [!narration] Public face
> The Thornwatch appears as solitary druids and rangers moving through frontier settlements and the wild lands beyond them. They dress in weathered leathers and furs, carrying carved wooden tokens shaped like thorns or reaching branches—recognition marks that identify other members. When they appear at town margins or trading posts, they leave these tokens weighted down with warnings on birch bark or etched into wood: simple pictures and runes marking danger, boundary erosion, or unnatural disturbance. Most settlers view them as eccentric prophets or nature hermits. A few communities have learned to heed their warnings after incidents the Thornwatch predicted came to pass.

## Current State

- **Status quo.** Thornwatch members move between frontier settlements and wilderness sanctuaries, recording observations about the boundary and leaving warnings wherever they pass. Most settlers tolerate them; some actively help; a few dismiss them as mad.
- **Recent change.** The frequency and clarity of warnings has increased sharply. Members are gathering at remote sanctuaries more often, suggesting an acceleration of whatever they believe is happening.
- **Pressure.** The network must act quickly—they believe the boundary is failing faster than they can warn. They need resources, allies, and evidence to convince the settled peoples to take them seriously.
- **Strength.** Deep knowledge of wilderness, creature behavior, and land itself. Rapid communication across vast distances via animal messengers and druidic means. Numbers sufficient to maintain watches across many frontiers simultaneously.
- **Vulnerability.** No unified leadership means slow decision-making during crises. Most settlements regard them as eccentrics, limiting their influence. They have no military force and few allies in positions of power.
- **Opportunity.** If a crisis visibly confirms their warnings, settlements will turn to them for guidance and defense. They might broker alliances with frontier lords and cities that need defense.
- **Internal fracture.** Tension exists between older members who believe in patient warning and younger druids who want to take direct action—even magical intervention—to stop the boundary from failing. Some whisper of more drastic measures than words and carvings.

## Active Agenda

**Goal.** Document the progression of boundary failure and convince at least three major frontier settlements to fortify themselves and prepare for what emerges from the wilderness.

**Why.** The Thornwatch believes they have perhaps months before the boundary weakens so far that wilderness and settled lands can no longer be separated. Settlements must be ready, or thousands will die.

**Planned action.** Leave more frequent warnings at settlement edges—increasingly specific about what is coming and when. Request formal meetings with settlement leaders to present evidence and maps. Begin gathering rare materials that might be used to strengthen the boundary or at least slow its failure.

**Needs.** A settled leader willing to listen and grant them an audience before settlers. Access to settlements' records of strange occurrences, to correlate with their own observations. Safe passage through settled lands and protection from those who would hunt them as frauds or worse.

**Opposition.** Settlement leaders who dismiss them as mad. Settlers who actively drive them away. Whatever force or entity is causing the boundary to fail in the first place.

**Next signal.** Thornwatch members appear more frequently at settlement edges. Warnings grow more detailed and urgent. A few members venture into settlements rather than remaining at margins. Settlements begin to report that predicted dangers have indeed come to pass—predators appearing where none were, plants withering unnaturally, or stranger things still.

**Player opening.** The party can seek out Thornwatch members to learn what they know. They can help the network convince a settlement to take them seriously. They can investigate the boundary independently and verify or refute the Thornwatch claims. They can assist in gathering rare materials for the network's work.

**If completed.** Three or more frontier settlements are fortified and prepared. The boundary's state is documented and understood. The Thornwatch gains enough credibility to coordinate with settled forces. Whether they can actually stop the boundary from failing remains undetermined.

### Milestones

### Chain of Action

**Who decides:** The circle of eldest members, meeting in remote sanctuaries. Decisions are slow and consensus-based.

**Who interprets those orders:** Regional guides who coordinate Thornwatch members across specific frontier areas.

**Who actually carries them out:** Individual members leave warnings, conduct observations, and gather information as directed.

**If leadership disappears.** The network would fragment into smaller regional groups. Some might continue coordinating; others might turn to direct action. The younger faction would likely act on their own agenda.

### Party

**Standing.** Unknown / Neutral

**Why.** The party has not encountered the Thornwatch yet, or the network does not know whether to trust them.

**They currently want from the party:** Information about strange occurrences, boundary disturbances, or unnatural phenomena the party may have witnessed. If the party is influential, the Thornwatch wants leverage to help convince settlements.

**They can currently offer the party:** Knowledge of wilderness dangers, safe passage through wild lands, warnings of predators or hazards, and maps of safe routes. If the party helps their cause, they can offer rituals, blessings, or aid from druidic magic.

**What would change their standing:** Helping the Thornwatch warn a settlement, verifying their claims about the boundary, gathering evidence of the phenomenon, or supporting their agenda in public. Conversely, dismissing or hindering them would create friction.

## Signals & Rumors

- **Visible sign.** Carved wooden tokens and warning marks appearing at settlement edges. Settlements report that people have stopped venturing into specific forest areas after warnings appeared there.
- **Rumor.** "The wild is waking up" or "The wood is getting closer." Settlers speak of boundary-watchers who appear in the mist and vanish by dawn.
- **Direct signal.** A Thornwatch member approaches a party member directly, pressing warnings and asking if they have witnessed boundary disturbances. Deliveries of carved tokens or maps to settlements the party frequents.
- **Quiet clue.** Finding carved markers in the wilderness with dates and symbols documenting boundary changes. Discovering animal trails that lead to hidden druid sanctuaries. Evidence of creatures appearing far from their known ranges, as the Thornwatch records suggest.

## History

- **Origins.** The Thornwatch coalesced gradually over many years as scattered druids and rangers noticed changes in the boundary—creatures crossing it, plant cycles shifting, the very landscape appearing to thin or blur. What began as informal communication between concerned individuals grew into a loose but coordinated network.
- **Early observations.** For decades, the changes were subtle. The Thornwatch documented them but found no one willing to listen. Settlement authorities dismissed their concerns.
- **Acceleration.** In recent years, the boundary disturbances have become more frequent and visible. The Thornwatch's sense of urgency has grown accordingly, and they have begun more active campaigns to warn the world.

---

## Hidden Agenda

**Concealed motive.** Some members of the Thornwatch, particularly younger druids, suspect or know that the boundary failure is not entirely natural—that something or someone is deliberately weakening it. The elder consensus has chosen not to communicate this possibility to outsiders for fear of sounding mad, but this fractures the network internally.

**Concealed allegiance.** A few of the eldest members have received visits from otherworldly beings—fey, spirits, or entities tied to the boundary itself—who have warned them directly. These encounters are known only to a handful of senior druids and are not discussed with the broader network. The nature of these entities and their relationship to the boundary failure is unknown.
