# Thornwatch

> [!narration] Public face
> Carved warnings on frontier trees, crude symbols left at crossroads, and rangers who vanish into the woods after speaking urgent words to settlement leaders. The Thornwatch appear only in wild places and frontier margins, never in cities. They speak of a weakening—a thinning between the safe lands and the deep wilderness. Few settlements take them seriously until something crosses that should not.

## Current State

* **Status quo.** Thornwatch druids and rangers maintain distributed watch across the boundary, leaving warnings and gathering evidence of weakness. Small bands coordinate loosely through wild places and trusted settlement contacts. Some settlements heed warnings; most treat them as superstition.
* **Recent change.** The boundary failures are accelerating. Creatures long contained on the wilderness side now cross with increasing frequency. Unnatural zones are expanding outward. The Thornwatch have become more visible and urgent in their warnings.
* **Pressure.** The boundary cannot hold indefinitely at its current rate of degradation. Thornwatch druids fear catastrophic collapse—simultaneous breaking at multiple points, releasing things that have been sealed for generations. They must warn and prepare before cascade failure.
* **Strength.** Thornwatch members are skilled at wilderness navigation, druidic magic, and understanding creature behavior. They have observation networks spanning the frontier. They can cross dangerous wild places that deter ordinary travelers.
* **Vulnerability.** They are widely scattered and have no formal command structure, making coordinated large-scale action difficult. Most frontier settlements dismiss them as alarmists. They lack the political power to force preparations or to compel aid from established powers.
* **Opportunity.** A party that validates the threat and helps prepare settlements could become the bridge between Thornwatch warnings and official action, turning isolated alerts into coordinated defense.

**Internal fracture.** Younger rangers want immediate action—driving back boundary creatures, sealing breaches with force. Older druids argue for patience, wisdom, and preparation through warning. Neither faction will openly oppose the other, but their methods cause friction in the network.

## Active Agenda

**Goal.** Strengthen and stabilize the boundary before catastrophic collapse releases sealed threats.

**Why.** The boundary has been failing for months. Thornwatch knowledge of sealed creatures and old rituals suggests the collapse could be sudden and simultaneous across multiple points. Frontier settlements must be warned and prepared, or the loss of life will be total.

**Planned action.** Continue systematic warnings to every frontier settlement, establish safe refuges in wild places, retrieve old druidic texts that may hold clues to boundary stabilization, and prepare a final urgent appeal to regional powers and city leadership.

**Needs.** Cooperation from at least one major settlement leader who will listen and prepare; access to boundary sites where stabilization rituals might work; protection while gathering and translating old lore; strength of numbers to defend settlements during breach if prevention fails.

**Opposition.** Settlement leaders who prioritize commerce and stability over warnings of invisible danger; regional factions (if any) who benefit from current boundary conditions; time itself—the degradation accelerates.

**Next signal.** Rangers appear at a settlement boundary with urgent news of a major crossing. Strange creatures are sighted further inland than before. A boundary zone expands noticeably. An old sealed place shows fresh damage.

**Player opening.** The party can investigate Thornwatch warnings to verify or debunk them; protect a settlement from boundary crossings; help Thornwatch access old ritual sites; negotiate between Thornwatch and skeptical leadership; or search for lore that reveals how the boundary was originally made and sealed.

**If completed.** The boundary is stabilized. Settlements are fortified. Thornwatch returns to quiet watching instead of urgent warning. Regional powers acknowledge the threat and establish joint defense posts. The party becomes recognized bridge between wilderness and civilization.

## People & Structure

The Thornwatch has no formal hierarchy. Decisions emerge through gathering of elders—druids with decades of experience in boundary watching. Rangers operate in small independent bands (three to seven members each) that maintain loose contact through established meeting places and signal methods.

| Person / group | Role | Wants | Loyalty / fracture |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Elder druids (unnamed) | Decision-makers and lore-keepers | Boundary stabilization, community preparation | United but aging; knowledge scattered |
| Ranger bands (unnamed) | Field observers and warning-carriers | Immediate action, visible defense | Increasingly frustrated with slow approach |
| Younger members | Apprentices and new scouts | To do something *now*, not just warn | Loyal but impatient; risk splinter action |

**Chain of Action**

**Who decides:** Elder druids reach consensus through gathering.

**Who interprets those decisions:** Ranger band leaders carry the intent to their scouts and enact adapted versions suited to their territory.

**Who actually carries them out:** Individual rangers and apprentices, who leave warnings, scout boundary zones, and speak to settlers.

**If leadership disappears.** Ranger bands would likely splinter. Some would continue warning; others might attempt direct action against boundary creatures. Younger members might form their own more militant faction. The network would fragment into local cell groups without central coordination.

## Connections

**Relationship summary:** Thornwatch seeks acknowledgment and aid from settlement powers. They are watched by regional factions curious about their warnings. They are sometimes allied with woodland creatures and fey who share boundary concerns.

* **Settlement leaders (unnamed):** Neutral to Distrusted. Thornwatch offers warnings; leaders want proof and reassurance that their lands are safe. Some see Thornwatch as troublemakers stirring settler panic.
* **Regional factions:** Neutral to Hostile depending on faction interests. Factions invested in current stability may suppress Thornwatch warnings.
* **Woodland creatures and fey (unnamed):** Friendly to Neutral. Some share the boundary concern; others resent ranger presence and encroachment on wild places.

### Party

**Standing.** Unknown. The party is not yet part of Thornwatch history.

**Why.** No established relationship exists yet.

**They currently want from the party:** Verification of boundary failures and evidence that can convince skeptical leadership; aid in reaching settlements with warnings; help defending against creatures if the boundary fails.

**They can currently offer the party:** Safe passage through boundary wilderness; druidic aid and magic; knowledge of creature behavior and old lore; shelter and refuge.

**What would change their standing:** Investigating and confirming the boundary threat (Friendly); helping Thornwatch protect a settlement (Allied); attempting to stabilize the boundary itself (Critical ally); or actively suppressing Thornwatch warnings (Hostile).

## Signals & Rumors

* **Visible sign.** Rangers appear at the boundary of a settlement before dawn, speaking urgently to whoever will listen, then vanishing into the woods. Carved symbols appear on frontier trees—concentric circles, warning runes, or stylized boundary lines.
* **Rumor.** "The druids say the wild is breaking loose—that something big is thinning the line between forest and farm. The elders dismiss them. But three villages report missing livestock near the boundary, and hunters won't go deep anymore."
* **Direct signal.** A ranger appears to warn the party directly: "The boundary is failing. Something is crossing. You'll see proof soon enough if you watch the frontier."
* **Quiet clue.** Dead boundary markers (trees carved decades ago with binding symbols) show fresh damage. Unnatural plants grow on the human side. Creature tracks appear further inland than natural range.

| **When encountered** | Rangers speak in quiet urgent tones, describe specific boundary failures they've witnessed, and press their request before disappearing back to the wilderness. |
| **When helped** | Thornwatch shares knowledge, provides shelter, and directs the party toward sites and people of interest. The network begins to trust the party as a bridge to settlements. |
| **When opposed** | Rangers withdraw but continue watching and warning. If threatened with violence, they defend themselves and fade into the wilderness. They do not retaliate against the party but accept that help will not come from that direction. |

## Faction Turn

### Current Turn

* **Want.** Strengthen the boundary and secure acknowledgment from at least one major settlement before the next cascade of boundary breaches.
* **Move.** Intensify warnings to every frontier settlement with evidence of recent boundary failures; dispatch elder druids to prepare ritual sites that might stabilize the boundary; send rangers to gather the old sealing lore that may contain stabilization methods.
* **Mark.** If the move succeeds: Settlement leaders acknowledge the threat and begin fortifications. Ritual sites are prepared. Old lore reveals the nature of the seal. If the move fails: Settlements remain skeptical. Lore is incomplete or inaccessible. The boundary degrades faster.
* **Signal.** A major boundary crossing occurs visible to multiple settlements. Rangers appear with fresh evidence. A settlement reports monster attacks. Druids make a formal appeal to regional leadership.
* **Collision.** This move requires trust from settlement leadership, who may be influenced or pressured by regional factions. It also depends on recovering lore that may be lost, sealed, or held by other powers. It races against escalating boundary failures.
* **Player opening.** The party can verify the threat, pressure settlements to listen, help recover ritual lore, facilitate druid-leader negotiations, or take direct action to protect settlements during the turn's resolution.

## History

The Thornwatch emerged as a formalized network within the last generation, when boundary instability first became undeniable to elder druids. Before that, individual druids and rangers maintained watch in isolation. The network grew from informal meetings to a recognized (if loosely organized) collective. Thornwatch members believe the boundary was sealed by ancient powers—druids, fey, or gods—and that the seal is now failing. Why it was sealed and why it is failing now remains subject to debate among the elders.
