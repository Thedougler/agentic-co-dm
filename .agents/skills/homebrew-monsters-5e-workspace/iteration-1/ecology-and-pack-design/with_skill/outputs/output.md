# CR 6 Canopy Pack Predator Design — Full Output

**Creature:** Canopy Strikewell (packmate designation; solitary is a Strikewell)  
**CR:** 6 (Medium threat, designed for party of 4 at levels 5–6)  
**Region:** Aruhe canopy layer

---

## Design Path and Assumptions

**Path chosen:** Full design (pack behavior fundamentally changes the decision loop, role, and action economy vs. solitary predators).

**Assumptions stated upfront:**
- Pack size: 3–5 individuals per hunt (tested at 4 for three-round budget)
- Canopy layer: dense enough to limit visibility to 40–60 feet, with launching perches, gliding routes, and interlock branches
- Preferred prey: Medium creatures (travelers, supply trains, isolated individuals from the party)
- Party response: split formation vs. coordinated pack drops; cover-seeking to break aerial line of sight
- 2024 rules: standard notation throughout; no 2014 conversion needed

**Why full design:** Existing Aruhe predators are solitary (Spiguar, Bloodhawk, Terror-Bird, Blackrail) or ground-based pack hunters (Wolfrabbit). The canopy lacks a coordinated aerial pack hunter. Pack tactics require: (1) off-turn action economy (reactions and reactions to packmates), (2) a decision loop tied to isolation and surround, (3) a role (Skirmisher) that balances speed and coordination, and (4) terrain use that relies on canopy cover. No reskin or variant of a solitary predator captures this.

## Reference Gate — Dossier

### Peer Selection

**Numerical peer (CR 6):**
- **Basilisk** (MM 5e, CR 3; recalibrated to CR 6 math): Solo defender, 52 HP, AC 15. Not a good match for pack hunter.
- **Chimera** (MM 5e, CR 6): 114 HP, AC 14, three-headed bruiser. Solo, not a pack hunter; high HP for defense role. Math baseline.
- **Deva** (MM 5e, CR 10; downscaled to CR 6 for comparison): Elite defender, multiattack, teleport. Too defensive; not a skirmisher.
- **Wyvern** (MM 5e, CR 6): 110 HP, AC 15, flying skirmisher. Uses poison, recharge on stinger. Single predator, not pack. **Used as role and speed peer.**

**Role peer (Skirmisher):**
- **Wolfrabbit** (Aruhe, CR 4): Medium, 68 HP, AC 15. Pack rend when packmate nearby. Pouncing Bound for mobility. Pack size 4–6. **Direct pack-hunter peer within setting; mechanics model.**
- **Bloodhawk** (Aruhe, CR 11): Gargantuan, 248 HP, AC 15, flying skirmisher. Terminal Stoop (recharge), haul aloft. Solo ambush from above. **Aerial peer; stoop and altitude mechanics.**
- **Pixie/Sprite** (MM 5e, CR 1/4, CR 1): Tiny aerial pack hunters with invisibility. Too low CR; not a good damage peer, but shows pack action economy and evasion.

**Mechanic peer (Pack coordination and aerial ambush):**
- **Stirge Swarm** (MM 5e, CR 1/8): 1d4 damage, swarm dispersal, concentration attack. Shows distributed damage, pack pressure. Too simple; used for swarm *concept* only.
- **Winter Wolf** (MM 5e, CR 3): Breath recharge, Multiattack, legendary actions for solo threats. Shows recharge and coordinated action.
- **Giant Constrictor Snake** (MM 5e, CR 2): 60 HP, AC 12, grapple-and-restrain loop. Solo. Not a pack hunter.

### Shared Patterns

1. **Flying skirmishers** (Bloodhawk, Wyvern) prioritize mobility over durability. They trade AC (15) for positioning and off-turn movement (reactions, recharges).
2. **Pack hunters** (Wolfrabbit) gain bonus damage when a packmate is nearby (Pack Rend: +1d10 per hit when another is within 5 ft). They use reactions to rush toward fallen members, creating cascading momentum.
3. **Aerial ambush** (Bloodhawk) uses a telegraphed setup (high altitude, visible circular hunting) followed by a committed action (Terminal Stoop) with a recharge gate (5–6).
4. **Action economy for packs:** Wolfrabbit uses bonus actions and reactions to coordinate; each packmate acts independently but gains bonuses when near allies. No legendary actions needed for standard pack size.

### Deliberate Deviations

- **Canopy-specific mechanics:** Unlike Bloodhawk (open sky, whale-scale height), this predator uses dense canopy cover for hiding and coordinated ambush. Unlike Wolfrabbit (ground-based leaping), it has true flight with gliding.
- **Isolation vs. pack rend:** Wolfrabbit's Pack Rend encourages the party to group and defend the prone target. This predator's mechanic (Coordinated Drop) encourages the *party* to scatter and seek cover, flipping the control axis.
- **Recharge vs. bonus action:** To avoid action-economy bloat with multiple packmates, the signature move (aerial coordinated dive) recharges rather than repeating every turn, forcing the pack to make other meaningful choices.
- **CR 6 vs. Bloodhawk (CR 11) and Wolfrabbit (CR 4):** The Strikewell sits between them in capability. It has lower survivability than Bloodhawk (flying melee combatant, not a massive aerial titan) but more damage and coordination than Wolfrabbit.

### Counterplay Observations

1. **Canopy breaks:** Deep forest, open stone, rushing water, or cleared areas deny the ambush geometry. (Parallel to Bloodhawk: closed canopy breaks the dive; Spiguar: open stone breaks the pounce.)
2. **Pack separation:** Forcing a single Strikewell into melee removes the coordinated bonuses, leaving it as a moderately tough flier.
3. **Height denial:** Ground-based attacks, anti-air control, or denying the launch perches (magic, climbing, positioning) reduce the signature's effectiveness.
4. **Flock breakup:** Eliminating one packmate removes one voice in coordinated action; this is not a total wipe like losing one Wolfrabbit, but it matters.

### Math Note

**Expected three-round DPR (4 Strikewell pack at medium encounter, balanced party level 5–6):**
- Round 1 (opening coordinated drop, recharge used): ~40–50 DPR across 4 creatures (Multiattack × 4, ~10–12 DPR per creature on average hit)
- Round 2 (regrouping, bonus action repositioning): ~30–35 DPR (scattered attacks, some party members behind cover)
- Round 3 (recharge available or exhausted, attrition sets in): ~25–40 DPR (depends on recharge roll and if any Strikewell is downed)
- **Party expectation at CR 6:** 24–30 DPR per medium-difficulty round (to KO 4 party members in ~8–10 rounds).

The pack *exceeds* this in round 1 (burst) and falls below in rounds 2–3, creating a risk-reward curve: open combat on the ground favors the party; uncontested canopy ambush favors the pack.

**AC/Defense comparison:**
- Wyvern (CR 6): 15 AC, 110 HP
- Strikewell (single, CR 6 proxy): 15 AC, 52 HP (4 individuals @ 13 HP each = 52 HP total "health pool" if one is downed)
- Wolfrabbit (CR 4 pack): 15 AC, 68 HP per individual (4 = 272 total)

The Strikewell pack trades individual survivability for action economy and first-round burst. A single downed Strikewell removes 25% of the pack's presence, not 6% (Wolfrabbit at 4–6 pack).

### Uncertainty and Test Notes

- **Canopy density:** Assumed 40–60 ft visibility, sufficient for the party to notice incoming Strikewell silhouettes (~5 seconds warning for the thump/wind-rush tell). Terrain module (dungeon-design) owns specific sight-lines.
- **Pack cohesion at range:** Four Strikewell in formation maintain ~30 ft spread. If scattered by terrain or forced ground combat, does the Coordinated Drop still trigger? **Test rule:** Coordinated Drop requires *at least two* packmates within 60 feet of the same target, not all four. Keeps the mechanic meaningful even if pack is split.
- **Recharge tension:** 5–6 Recharge means an average of one use per two rounds. If the party burns the signature in round 1, rounds 2–3 are standard Multiattack only. Playtesting should confirm this feels like pressure, not frustration.
- **2024 conversion:** No existing Aruhe Strikewell exists; this is a new creature type. 2024 rules throughout; no legacy conversion.

## Fiction Signature

> **This is** a medium aerial pack predator  
> **that** uses coordinated canopy drops and surround tactics  
> **to pursue** isolated prey flushed into the open canopy layer,  
> **fears** ground-level entanglement and canopy breaks,  
> **and gives players** the counterplay of seeking dense cover or open stone to deny the ambush geometry.

### Fantasy

A Strikewell is a sleek, hawk-like flier with a body no larger than a housecat but with a six-foot wingspan. Its wings are mottled green and brown, built for the canopy's low-light maneuvering rather than the open-sky grace of the Bloodhawk. Feathers along the spine are iridescent copper and deep blue. Red-gold eyes and a hooked beak mark it as a predator. Strikewell live in the high canopy of Aruhe's jungle and hunt in loose flocks of 3–5.

### Signature

The signature is a **coordinated aerial ambush:** the pack circles above the canopy, hidden by dense leaf-cover and the break-up of their coloring. When prey moves through an opening (a river channel, a clearing, a high terrace), the Strikewell drop simultaneously from different angles, forcing the prey to choose which direction to flee. The first strike hits hard; the follow-ups come from angles the party did not anticipate.

**The tell:** Before a Coordinated Drop, the canopy above the opening rustles and stills. The party hears a low, harmonious trilling (3–4 voices in eerie unison) for 3–5 seconds. This is the signal the pack uses to time the dive. An alert party can use this warning to move into cover or ready actions.

### Goal

The Strikewell pack hunts anything Medium or smaller that crosses their canopy patrol zone. They prefer humanoids and organized groups (better food, more leftovers). They are territorial but not vindictive; they break off if the cost rises (multiple downed packmates, anti-air magic, escape into hostile terrain).

### Fear

Strikewell fear ground-level entanglement (dense undergrowth, rubble, water deep enough to restrict flight), canopy breaks (open sky, cleared ground, stone without handholds), and coordinated anti-air attacks that deny launching perches.

### Counterplay

1. **Seek dense cover or open ground** to make the Coordinated Drop lethal but inaccurate. The pack's advantage vanishes in thick forest (visibility too short for surround) or on open stone (nowhere to land).
2. **Climb or position defensively** on high ground with aerial visibility (tall structure, cliff rim) to make the first drop a fight in open air, not a surprise from above.
3. **Ready actions for the tell** (the trilling and canopy rustling) to set up reactions when they commit to the dive.
4. **Isolate individual Strikewell** through ranged attacks or terrain; the pack's damage bonus requires three packmates within range.

### Proof (Observable Tell)

The proof that makes the Coordinated Drop fair and readable is:

- **Pre-drop tell (3–5 seconds):** Eerie, harmonious trilling from above, canopy rustling, and a visible darkening of leaf-shadows as the pack shifts into attack posture. A creature within 40 feet can use this to move away or ready an action.
- **Mid-drop visual:** Four distinct silhouettes descending at angles from different parts of the canopy, visible once they commit to the dive (no longer hidden by leaves). The party can see which targets are being dove on.
- **Post-drop consequence:** Strikewell land and rake; they do not hover. Once they've made their attack, they must choose to climb/fly for repositioning, use a bonus action to move, or stay in melee. They are briefly vulnerable during the landing.

## Role, Rank, and Encounter Job

**Primary Role:** Skirmisher (high mobility, lower durability, hit-and-shift pattern)  
**Secondary Role:** None (adding a second role would bloat the design and create action-economy conflicts)

**Rank:** Standard for solo; Elite when in pack of 3–5  
**CR:** 6 (designed for party of 4 at levels 5–6; scales via pack size)

**Encounter Job:**
- **Opening encounter:** Canopy ambush that forces the party to move and seek cover; tests positioning and awareness.
- **Recurring threat:** Mid-combat harassment; Strikewell retreat to canopy perches after Coordinated Drop and attack again if the party stays in the open.
- **Minion role:** Two Strikewell alongside a larger predator (Bloodhawk, Terror-Bird) to harass the party's ranged combatants.

**Why Skirmisher, not Ambusher:**
- Ambusher hides, strikes, withdraws. Strikewell use visibility (the tell, the drop silhouettes) and reposition openly. They are not hidden assassins.
- Skirmisher moves through/around the party. Strikewell use the canopy to move around the party's formation, forcing repositioning rather than single-strike isolation.

## Decision Loop: Tell → Threat → Responses → Payoff

### Feature 1: Coordinated Drop (Recharge 5–6)

**Tell:** Eerie trilling, canopy rustling, visible darkening above. 3–5 seconds warning.

**Threat:** The pack drops simultaneously from different angles. Each targets one creature within 40 feet in an open or semi-open area (river channel, clearing, high terrace). The Strikewell that target the same creature gain advantage on their attack roll (coordinated strike). Those that land take half damage on a missed opportunity attack during the descent.

**Responses:**
1. *Move into dense cover* (thick forest, undergrowth, building interior): Strikewell cannot coordinate on targets they cannot see; the geometry breaks.
2. *Climb or position on high ground* (cliff rim, tall structure, treetop): Forces the drop to happen in open air, turning it into an aerial dogfight rather than an ambush.
3. *Ready an action to cast a spell or deploy anti-air control* (web, arrows with targeting, magic missile): Interrupt the recharge or spend it on a failed setup.
4. *Group tight with spell-casters/healers in the center*: Reduces the number of angles the pack can attack from; they have to choose carefully rather than surround freely.

**Payoff:**
- **If uncontested:** The pack lands around the party, makes Multiattack (up to 4 creatures × 2 attacks = 8 attacks potential), and forces the party to either fight in the open or scramble for cover. Strikewell can reposition with bonus actions or disengage.
- **If interrupted:** The pack wastes the recharge, lands in worse position, or one Strikewell is downed before reaching combat. Party gains momentum and positioning advantage.
- **If the party seeks cover effectively:** The pack retreats to a canopy perch, re-circles, and readies another Coordinated Drop in 1–2 rounds (waiting for the party to move). The encounter becomes a series of drops and repositioning.

### Feature 2: Pack Rend (Passive)

**Tell:** Strikewell cluster within 5 feet of the same target. No tell; the geometry is visual.

**Threat:** Once per turn, when a Strikewell hits a creature that another Strikewell is also within 5 feet of, the hit deals an extra 3 (1d6) piercing damage. This rewards the pack staying tight and targeting the same foe, especially if the party doesn't spread out.

**Responses:**
1. *Spread out, don't group:* Forces Strikewell to spread their attacks, losing Pack Rend bonus damage.
2. *Isolate a single Strikewell* through ranged attacks or conditions: Removes it from the cooperative geometry; it loses the bonus and acts at reduced effectiveness.
3. *Use AoE attacks* (fireball, cone spells) to force the pack apart: Each hit creature must move, breaking the cluster.

**Payoff:**
- **If the party stays grouped:** Pack Rend activates frequently; each hit deals +3 damage. Four Strikewell hitting twice per round = potential +12 extra damage if all four stay clustered. High-damage round.
- **If the party spreads:** Pack Rend rarely triggers; the party is safer individually, but loses the mutual support of standing together. Turns into four separate fights.

### Feature 3: Canopy Fisher (Bonus Action, Recharge 5–6)

**Tell:** A Strikewell climbing or hovering near the canopy edge. No hidden action; the bonus action is announced.

**Threat:** The Strikewell uses a bonus action to move up to its speed (50 feet flying, no opportunity attacks provoked) and make one Talons attack against a creature within 5 feet at the end of the move. This is the repositioning/strike-again loop. Once per round, a Strikewell can dive from a high canopy position and rake a target below before climbing back up.

**Responses:**
1. *Control movement* (grapple, hold person, slow): Denies the bonus action repositioning and traps the Strikewell in melee.
2. *Ranged attacks on the perched Strikewell:* Forces it to move or take damage.
3. *Deny the high ground:* Position the party where no canopy perch is safely above the target.

**Payoff:**
- **Uncontested:** Strikewell relocates every turn, striking multiple party members. High mobility, consistent pressure.
- **Contested:** Strikewell is forced into sustained melee, where its lower HP becomes a liability.

### Feature 4: Evasion (Reaction, Once per Round)

**Tell:** A ranged attack targeting a Strikewell in open air or mid-flight. The tell is the incoming arrow/spell.

**Threat:** When a Strikewell that has half its HP or more is targeted by a ranged attack, it can use a reaction to fly up to half its speed without provoking opportunity attacks. This is evasion, not dodging.

**Responses:**
1. *Melee weapons:* Lock the Strikewell down so Evasion cannot be triggered; it loses the reaction.
2. *Spell saves that don't require a ranged attack* (concentration, area effect): Bypass the reaction.
3. *Multiple ranged attacks:* One Strikewell can only use Evasion once per round, so the second and third arrows still land.

**Payoff:**
- **If ranged and alone:** Strikewell remains airborne, taking reduced fire. Keeps it in play.
- **If melee or in a group:** Strikewell must choose to fight or flee. Evasion becomes a once-per-round safety valve, not an escape.

### Notation Explanation

- **Pack Rend:** Identical to Wolfrabbit's, adapted for Strikewell scale. Triggers once per turn (not per creature), rewards tight formation.
- **Evasion:** Reaction once per round, requires open air or canopy connection, ties to HP threshold. This prevents trivial ranged kiting.
- **Flyby Predator:** Standard skirmisher trait; no opportunity attacks when flying away. Keeps mobility meaningful.
- **Coordinated Drop:** Recharge 5–6. Requires at least one other Strikewell within 60 feet to trigger. Each creature attacking the same target as another Strikewell gains Advantage on one attack. The mechanic rewards coordination without forcing it. Descending 10 feet vertically is a canopy-specific telegraph; it grounds the action.
- **Canopy Fisher:** Bonus action repositioning loop. Requires proximity to canopy to prevent abuse on open ground. Keeps Strikewell mobile without legendary actions.
- **Rally to the Flock:** Reaction to a fallen packmate within 30 feet. Mirrors Wolfrabbit's frenzy response; the pack does not abandon members. Adds urgency to protecting packmates.

## Three-Round Offense and Defense Audit

### Setup: Encounter Context

**Scenario:** 4-Strikewell pack vs. party of 4 characters (levels 5–6, mix of melee and ranged). Party is traveling through an Aruhe jungle river channel (40–60 feet wide, 20–30 feet tall jungle canopy overhead, water 10 feet deep below terrace). Party has not yet entered the water; they are on a stone terrace.

**Party position:** Lined up on the terrace in a loose column.  
**Pack starting position:** Hidden in the canopy above, 50 feet back along the terrace, out of sight.  
**No allies, no terrain hazards other than the water and canopy.

### Round 1: Coordinated Drop

**Pack turn:**
- All 4 Strikewell use Coordinated Drop (recharge available). They each target a different party member, descending 15 feet from canopy cover.
- Strikewell A & B target the same character (Fighter, AC 17 with shield). Both have Advantage on their attack rolls.
  - Strikewell A: Beak attack, +5 vs AC 17. Needs 12+; 65% hit chance with advantage ≈ 87%. Hit: 7 damage. Attack is part of Coordinated Drop sequence.
  - Strikewell B: Talons attack, +5 vs AC 17. Needs 12+; same odds. Hit: 6 damage. Pack Rend triggers (another Strikewell within 5 ft): +3 damage. Total: 9 damage.
  - Total to Fighter: 16 damage.
- Strikewell C & D target different party members (Wizard, AC 14; Cleric, AC 15).
  - Strikewell C: Beak, +5 vs AC 14. Needs 9+; 80% hit chance → 95% with advantage. Hit: 7 damage.
  - Strikewell D: Talons, +5 vs AC 15. Needs 10+; 75% hit chance → 93% with advantage. Hit: 6 damage (no Pack Rend; D is alone on its target).
  - Totals: Wizard 7 damage, Cleric 6 damage.
- After attacks, Strikewell reposition 10 feet away from melee range, circling upward toward canopy.

**Party turn (assumed responses):**
- Fighter: Takes damage (16 HP) but cannot reach Strikewell (flying 10 feet up). Moves 30 feet toward denser jungle cover.
- Wizard: Casts Magic Missile (4d4 + 4) at Strikewell C (which attacked). Hits: 13 damage. Strikewell C is down (13 HP, 13 damage taken).
- Cleric: Heals the most damaged character (Fighter, now 4 HP).
- Ranger: Shoots at Strikewell D with attack roll. +6 to hit. Needs 9+; 80% chance. Hit: arrow does 8 damage. Strikewell D is at 5 HP.

**Round 1 Summary:**
- Party damage taken: 29 HP distributed (high burst; pressure on the healer).
- Pack damage dealt: 1 Strikewell down (Strikewell C), 1 injured (Strikewell D, 5 HP remaining).
- Action economy: Strikewell used recharge on opening move; now on cooldown.
- Party positioning: Spread toward jungle cover, one Strikewell downed, one weakened.

### Round 2: Regrouping and Harassment

**Pack turn:**
- Strikewell A: Sees Striker C is dead (failed save vs. Magic Missile burst). Reaction triggers (Rally to the Flock). Moves 25 feet toward Strikewell C's position. Makes one Talons attack against the Wizard (creature nearest to C). Attack roll: +5 vs AC 14. Hits; 6 damage.
  - Then bonus action Canopy Fisher: Climbs to a canopy perch (10 feet up). Flies 30 feet to reposition and makes Talons attack against the Ranger. Attack roll: +5 vs AC 15. Needs 10+; 75% chance. Hit: 6 damage.
  - Damage dealt: 12 (6 + 6).
- Strikewell B: Standard Multiattack against the Fighter (nearest intact target). Beak +5 vs AC 17 (20% hit chance); Talons +5 vs AC 17 (20% hit chance). Both miss. No Pack Rend.
- Strikewell D (injured, 5 HP): Evasion triggers if targeted by ranged attack. Uses bonus action Canopy Fisher to reposition. Talons attack vs AC 14 (Wizard). Hits: 6 damage. Continues to perch.

**Party turn:**
- Fighter: Attacks Strikewell B (the one that missed). Hits; 10 damage. Strikewell B is at 3 HP.
- Wizard (damaged, 6 HP taken): Uses healing potion. Readies action for incoming Strikewell.
- Cleric: Attacks Strikewell A with ranged spell (Guiding Bolt, DC 15). Strikewell A makes Dexterity save; Strikewell saves are moderate (+5 DEX). Needs 10+; 75% chance. Fails: 28 damage taken. Strikewell A is down (13 HP, but took 28 damage). Critically injured or dead.
- Ranger: Shoots Strikewell D again. Hit: 7 damage. Strikewell D is down (5 HP left; took 7).

**Round 2 Summary:**
- Pack composition: 2 Strikewell down (C, A or D), 2 intact (B at 3 HP, D at 0 or B at 3 HP, A at -15). Pack is severely weakened.
- Party damage round 2: 12 HP (low burst without Coordinated Drop).
- Morale: Pack should consider retreat now that it's outnumbered 2v4.

### Round 3: Attrition or Retreat

**Pack turn (if continuing):**
- Strikewell B (3 HP): Evasion available; will use ranged attacks to stay safe. Bonus action Canopy Fisher to reposition. Talons attack vs Ranger (AC 15). Needs 10+; 75% chance. Hit: 6 damage. Climbs 10 feet to canopy perch, out of reach.
- Remaining Strikewell: Attempts to retreat; uses full movement (50 ft fly speed) to escape into the canopy, away from the party.

**Party turn:**
- If pursuit into canopy is attempted: Party enters hostile terrain (dense cover, visibility 20 ft, hanging branches, slippery perches). Strikewell have advantage in the canopy; party does not follow effectively.
- Combat ends or transitions to a skill challenge (tracking the fleeing pack through the jungle).

**Round 3 Summary:**
- Pack damage: Negligible (6 HP from one hit).
- Total pack damage across three rounds: ~50 HP (4 Strikewell × 13 HP = 52 total; 2 down, 1–2 severely injured).
- Total party damage across three rounds: 29 + 12 + 6 = 47 HP distributed.
- **Result:** Tactical draw; pack is routed and flees; party sustains moderate casualties and is low on resources (healing spells used, one character at low HP).

### Defense Checklist

- [ ] **AC and attack avoidance:** 15 AC is standard for CR 6 fliers. Evasion (reaction, once per round) provides one safety valve. **PASS:** Reasonable skirmisher defenses.
- [ ] **HP:** 13 HP per individual is low but intentional (small creature, designed for pack, not solo). Four at 52 total is balanced for a party of four at levels 5–6. **PASS:** Consistent with the role.
- [ ] **Effective HP by damage type:** No resistances or immunities. Strikewell are vulnerable to consistent ranged fire. **NOTE:** No issue; the canopy cover and movement reduce effective ranged damage.
- [ ] **Saves and save coverage:** +4 WIS, +5 DEX. No other saves. Moderate saves against most effects. **PASS:** They are not overly resilient to control, but Evasion reduces area-of-effect pressure.
- [ ] **Magic resistance:** None. Strikewell can be caught by Magic Missile, Fireball, Hold Person. **PASS:** This is intentional; they are not tank creatures.
- [ ] **Immunities:** None. **PASS:** Clean.
- [ ] **Regeneration/healing:** None. Once a Strikewell is down, it is down. **PASS:** Creates urgency.
- [ ] **Temporary HP:** None. **PASS:** Consistent.
- [ ] **Concealment/cover:** Canopy provides cover and concealment (vegetation, shadows). **PASS:** Environmental control, not creature-stat control.
- [ ] **Movement and escape:** 50 ft flying speed with no AoO from leaving combat is strong. Designed to reposition constantly. **PASS:** This is the core mechanic.
- [ ] **Legendary resistance:** None. Strikewell are not solo creatures; they do not need this. **PASS:** Correct for rank.
- [ ] **Concentration or recharge dependencies:** Coordinated Drop on Recharge 5–6. This is the signature move; rounds 2–3 without it are weaker but not empty. **PASS:** Recharge is not guaranteed; creates variance.

### Offensive Analysis

**Round 1 burst:** 29 HP damage across 4 targets (high variance: Fighter 16, others 6–7). This is calibrated to hurt but not incapacitate a level 5–6 party. **Fair.**

**Sustained offense (Rounds 2–3):** ~12 and 6 HP damage respectively, as the party recovers, counter-attacks, and the pack is depleted. **Fair scaling.**

**Action economy:** 4 creatures each acting independently means potentially 8 attacks per round (2 per Multiattack) plus bonus actions and reactions. This is high action economy but not elite-solo overwhelming, because each Strikewell is not particularly durable. **Balance:** Offense is frontloaded (Coordinated Drop recharge); sustained offense is moderate.

### Counterplay Validation

1. **Dense jungle cover:** Party's turn 1 move to cover is correct. Strikewell cannot coordinate in cover; next drop is broken.
   - *Mechanics check:* Coordinated Drop requires the targets to be in open/semi-open area (40-60 ft visibility). Dense jungle (20 ft visibility) invalidates the advantage and surround geometry.
   - **PASS:** Counterplay is viable and immediately accessible.

2. **Ranged focus on injured Strikewell:** Party isolates the weakest packmate (Strikewell D at 5 HP).
   - *Mechanics check:* Evasion once per round; second ranged attack lands. One ranged combatant can focus-fire a single Strikewell.
   - **PASS:** Isolate-and-eliminate strategy works.

3. **Spell saves and AoE:** Cleric's Guiding Bolt lands despite Strikewell's +5 DEX save because Guiding Bolt is a save DC, not a DEX attack roll. Spell still can miss or be saved.
   - *Mechanics check:* DC 15 Guiding Bolt, Strikewell save DC is variable (8 + 4 WIS = 12 base). Low save, can be overcome.
   - **PASS:** Spellcasters have tools; Strikewell are not spell-resistant.

4. **Retreat into canopy:** Party cannot easily follow; Strikewell have flight and advantage in dense foliage.
   - *Mechanics check:* Fleeing Strikewell use 50 ft fly speed; party on ground cannot match unless they have similar mobility or climb into danger.

## Encounter Integration and Running Notes

### Opening Tell and Position

**Ideal opening:** Strikewell are in canopy, 40–60 feet ahead along a terrace or jungle path. Party is moving through semi-open terrain (river channel, clearing, terrace edge). The DM describes: "The jungle above goes quiet. Leaf shadows darken the stone beneath you. Then: a low, harmonious trilling, almost musical, echoing from multiple directions."

**Party has 3–5 seconds** to act before the Coordinated Drop initiates.

### Default Choice

**Strikewell priority:** 
1. Isolate a single character (ranged caster, healer, or isolated grouping).
2. Use Coordinated Drop on at least two packmates to apply Pack Rend bonus and Advantage.
3. Reposition after attacks to break melee follow-ups.
4. Do not linger in sustained melee unless the party is already tied down.

**Party default response (if unprepared):**
- Seek cover (high priority; negates Coordinated Drop).
- Spread out to reduce Pack Rend trigger opportunity.
- Healing and defensive actions (Strikewell opening burst is high).

### If Pressured / If Signature is Answered

**If the party reaches dense cover:**
- Strikewell retreat to canopy perches and circle. They regroup for another Coordinated Drop in 1–2 rounds. If the party stays in cover (safe but static), the pack may circle indefinitely or break off if hunger is satisfied (killed one creature) or losses are too high (2+ dead).
- Strikewell do not pursue into deep water, open stone without handholds, or terrain that denies flight.

**If a Strikewell is isolated (low HP, surrounded, no packmates nearby):**
- It uses Evasion to escape ranged fire and Canopy Fisher to reposition. If escape is impossible (closed terrain, low altitude, grounded), it fights or attempts to flee on foot (poor option; 20 ft walk speed vs. 50 ft fly).

**If 2+ Strikewell are down:**
- Morale check (DM's call): Pack may retreat. Each additional Strikewell down increases likelihood of flight.

### Target Priority and Counterplay

**Strikewell target priority:**
1. Isolated characters or those who have separated from the group.
2. Ranged casters (Wizard, Cleric, Ranger) who can deal damage from range.
3. Lowest AC (easiest to hit with coordinated Advantage).

**Player counterplay:**
1. **Stay grouped:** Reduces the angles for Coordinated Drop.
2. **Seek dense cover:** Canopy magic dissipates; Pack Rend and Advantage become impossible.
3. **Focus ranged fire:** Two ranged attacks per round can down a Strikewell in 1–2 rounds.
4. **Melee lockdown:** Grab, grapple, or bind a Strikewell; it loses mobility and Evasion becomes risky.
5. **Anti-air spells:** Hold Person, Web, Sleet Storm, Darkness, etc., deny the Strikewell's primary advantage.
6. **Climb or position high:** Force the dogfight into open air where both sides risk equal loss.

### Retreat, Surrender, Failure State

**Strikewell retreat triggers:**
- 2 or more packmates down.
- Prey fully in cover with no vulnerability.
- Hunger satisfied (one large kill or two medium).
- Anti-air magic actively denying engagement (persistent Web, Sleet Storm, Darkness).

**Failure state for Strikewell:**
- All 4 down (unlikely; pack usually breaks before this point).
- Captured (alive Strikewell are rare; they do not domesticate but might be studied or enslaved).

**Failure state for party:**
- Routed by coordinated initial burst (rare for levels 5–6; would require very poor positioning or bad rolls).
- Pursued into canopy and separated (risks becoming prey).

### Encounter Objectives and Terrain

**Typical scenario:** Party is traveling through Aruhe jungle and is ambushed in an open-canopy area.

**Terrain features:**
- **River channel (40 ft wide, 10 ft deep water):** Crossing denies the Strikewell advantage; they don't pursue into deep water.
- **Jungle terrace edge (20 ft drop, rubble below):** Escaping into the rubble denies flight; protects the party.
- **Dense jungle rim (40 ft away from open terrace):** Retreat into cover; Strikewell cannot coordinate.
- **High tree or cliff (40 ft tall nearby):** Climbing provides anti-air defensive position.

**Reinforcements:** No additional creatures; Strikewell operate in packs of 3–5. Larger forces suggest a territorial dispute or a lair defense.

**Lightning rods:** None mechanical. Environmental terrain (water, rubble, canopy height) provides the "lightning rod" (non-creature target or escape condition).

## Visual Reference

A Strikewell is a sleek, compact flier with the silhouette of a hawk-owl hybrid. The body is no larger than a housecat (about 8–10 inches from beak to tail). The wings span six feet and are mottled green-brown with darker primary feathers for canopy camouflage. The head is large relative to the body, with red-gold eyes positioned forward like a predator's and a hooked, pale beak. The feathers along the spine and upper wings shimmer with iridescent copper and deep blue, especially visible when light hits them in mid-flight. Talons are black and hooked; the feet grip branches or prey with equal force. A fanned tail, barred in lighter and darker brown, steers the flier through dense canopy. The overall impression is of a creature built for speed and maneuverability in cluttered airspace, not open-sky soaring.

## Biology

Strikewell are six-foot-wingspan raptors native to Aruhe's dense canopy layer. Their skeletal structure is lighter than ground-based predators of similar mass, optimized for sustained flight through hanging vines and interlock branches. The hooked beak and talons are primary hunting tools; both are sharp enough to pierce the scales and hides of canopy-dwelling prey. Their eyes are adapted to low-light conditions (darkvision 60 feet) and are highly sensitive to movement in the vertical plane. Unlike the Bloodhawk (which hunts open sky from extreme altitude), Strikewell see their world in three dimensions at close range: they track prey that moves through the layers of foliage, not across empty horizon.

The sociability of Strikewell sets them apart from solitary apex predators like the Spiguar or Terror-Bird. They maintain loose flocks of 3–5 individuals, and their vocalizations—particularly the harmonious pre-drop trilling—are believed to be a coordination mechanism, not a territorial display. Juvenile Strikewell remain with a parent flock for several years before striking out alone or joining a neighboring flock.

## Behavior

**Habitat.** Strikewell own the high canopy of Aruhe's jungle, particularly areas where the forest closes in overhead and creates a layered hunting ground. They favor terrace edges, river corridors, and the boundary where open sky meets dense foliage. They avoid areas with sparse canopy (open grassland, scrubland) and do not venture far inland from the jungle rim; they are aerial predators who need the three-dimensional maze of branches to operate effectively.

**Behavior.** A Strikewell spends daylight hours in wide, lazy circles within a known patrol zone (roughly 2–3 square miles per flock). They are not passive observers; they actively hunt, reading the movements of canopy dwellers and monitoring the terrace below for prey that enters open areas. At dawn and dusk, when light is low and shadows are long, the flock becomes more aggressive. They communicate in flight through the low trilling and through posture (wing angle, tail spread). When a single Strikewell spots prey, the others converge using the trilling to coordinate the attack.

**Diet.** Strikewell prefer Medium creatures: humanoids, wolf-sized predators, and other canopy dwellers. They will hunt Small creatures in groups (insects, birds, small mammals) as a low-effort meal, but the real payoff is a medium-sized creature that provides a multi-day food cache for the flock. They have learned to recognize armed travelers and supply caravans as high-value prey; a coordinated drop that isolates one member of a group is an effective hunting tactic. They do not hunt creatures larger than Large; a Terror-Bird or Bloodhawk outmatches them, and they defer. They do not hunt other Strikewell or other canopy predators (Bloodhawk juveniles are off-limits).

**Social Structure.** Strikewell maintain flock bonds through the hunting season (roughly spring through autumn in Aruhe's climate). A flock of 3–5 will hunt together, share significant kills, and roost in proximity within the canopy. When one Strikewell is injured or killed, the others rally toward it—a behavior that can be exploited by hunters who use one fallen Strikewell as bait. Winter separation is common; flocks may split temporarily and reunite when food becomes scarce. Solitary Strikewell are rare and typically young birds that have not yet found a flock.

## Tactics

**Signs.** 
- A harmonious, musical trilling from the canopy above (3–4 distinct voices in eerie unison).
- Canopy shadows darkening and stilling as the flock shifts into attack posture.
- Feathers (green-brown and copper-blue iridescence) caught on high branches or in mid-flight silhouettes.
- Drag marks from talons on terrace stone or branch wood where prey was seized.
- Feeding sites on high canopy perches: scattered bones, plucked fur or feathers, and sour pellets.

**Instincts.** 
- They key on exposed movement in open areas: creatures crossing terrace edges, clearings, or river channels where the canopy opens above.
- They respond to isolation: a character separated from the group is more tempting than a tight formation.
- They hunt as a coordinated flock but each individual can act independently if separated.
- They break off if the cost rises: multiple packmates down, anti-air magic, or terrain that denies flight.
- They do not pursue into deep water (deeper than 10 feet), dense undergrowth (spaces narrower than 5 feet), or open stone without handholds.

**Tactics.** 
- **Opening:** Circle high, identify isolated or wounded targets, establish flock cohesion. The low trilling is both coordination and a warning to prey; it gives the party 3–5 seconds to respond.
- **Coordinated Drop:** At least two packmates target the same creature, diving from different angles 10+ feet vertically. Each strikes with Advantage on the attack roll. The coordinated strike is the flock's signature offense; it creates a moment of overwhelming pressure before repositioning.
- **Pack Rend application:** After landing, Strikewell stay within 5 feet of the same target if possible, triggering Pack Rend on subsequent hits. This rewards tight formation and punishes separation.
- **Repositioning:** Each Strikewell uses Canopy Fisher or standard flight to relocate to canopy perches between attacks, breaking line of sight and preventing sustained melee follow-ups.
- **Flock defense:** If one Strikewell falls, others move toward it (Rally to the Flock reaction). This can be exploited by baiting Strikewell into vulnerable positions, but it also means the flock does not abandon injured members.
- **Retreat condition:** When two or more packmates are down or prey is fully in cover, the flock abandons the hunt and circles back to canopy perches. They may re-engage after regrouping or move on to easier prey.

**Weaknesses.** 
- **Canopy breaks:** Open grassland, wide-sky areas, or sparse forest deny the coordinated geometry. Strikewell need vertical space to coordinate.
- **Dense cover:** Thick undergrowth or cave systems (spaces under 5 feet or where flight is impossible) neutralize their advantage.
- **Water:** Deep water (deeper than 10 feet) is hazardous; Strikewell do not pursue prey that escapes into rivers, lakes, or flooded terrain.
- **Sustained melee:** Forced into close, stationary combat, a Strikewell's low HP (13 per individual) becomes a liability. Two to three focused attacks from a Medium fighter can down one.
- **Ranged focus fire:** Two or more ranged attackers focusing on a single Strikewell can eliminate it in 1–2 rounds, breaking the flock's numbers advantage.
- **Anti-air control:** Spells that restrict movement (Web, Hold Person, Sleet Storm, Darkness) or force concentration (Counterspell to Coordinated Drop if prepared) deny the Strikewell's primary advantage.
- **Climbing or high ground:** If prey reaches a natural perch above the Strikewell's launch points, the ambush is broken and the fight becomes more level.

**Aftermath.** 
- A successful Strikewell hunt leaves scattered sign: feathers (copper-blue and green-brown) on high branches, drag marks from talons on terrace stone, and bone scraps from a high canopy feeding site.
- Prey that escapes or flock-members that fall leave blood trails leading into the canopy (for tracking purposes).
- A spooked flock leaves behind silence: no birdsong, no insect noise, just the sound of the wind and the creak of branch settling.
- Multiple fallen Strikewell may trigger a territorial response from neighboring flocks; other packs may arrive to defend or claim the feeding site.
