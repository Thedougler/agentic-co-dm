# Campaign Contract — Interview

The full interview for step 2 of the campaign-planning workflow. Resolve
each section with the DM before constructing the season map.

## Identity

- **Premise:** What are the characters doing in this campaign?
- **Player promise:** What kind of experience does playing this
  campaign promise?
- **Genre and tone:** What modes should remain recognizable throughout?
- **Core themes:** What ideas are likely to recur?
- **Campaign identity:** What makes this campaign meaningfully different
  from another campaign in the same setting?

## Scope

Determine the campaign's intended scale. Record the starting scope and
the plausible maximum separately — never assume immediate escalation.

Scale examples: one settlement, a region, a kingdom, an ocean or
continent, multiple nations, the world, multiple planes.

## Runtime

Establish an explicit target for how long the campaign lives. Prefer
ranges over false precision. Record whichever measures the DM can
meaningfully estimate:

- Expected total sessions
- Expected real-world duration
- Play cadence
- Approximate session length
- Starting level
- Intended ending level
- Advancement method
- Approximate advancement pace

Example: *Roughly 60–80 sessions over 2–3 years, beginning at level 3
and likely ending around level 15–17.*

The number is a planning instrument, not a contract with fate.

## Ending Intent

Determine how much the DM actually wants known about the eventual
ending. Possible answers range from:

- No endgame selected
- A thematic destination
- A campaign question that should eventually be answered
- A known final pressure or antagonist
- Several candidate endgames
- A strongly desired finale

Record only the strongest commitment the DM actually makes.

## Completion criterion

The campaign contract is complete when an unfamiliar agent could answer:

> What is this campaign, what experience should it provide,
> approximately how long is it meant to run, how far can its scope
> grow, and what — if anything — is actually committed about its
> ending?

without guessing.
