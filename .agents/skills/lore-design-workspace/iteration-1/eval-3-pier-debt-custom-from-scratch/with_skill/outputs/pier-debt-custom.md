# Pier-Debt Custom

> **Invention.** This page is proposed prep (`invention: true`). It is **not** campaign vault canon. Do not treat as established harbour law on named Midchain ports until the DM accepts it.

## Durable question
When can a spoken harbour debt change hands at a free-port pier, and what must witnesses see?

## At a Glance

**Core truth.** In these invented free ports, a spoken harbour debt can be transferred by touching the debtor's token to the pier post before witnesses.

**Why it matters.** Creditors, captains, and brokers can move obligation without a scribe — and players can interrupt, forge, or contest the witnessing.

**Scope.** Invented free-port piers only; not mapped onto a named vault harbour unless accepted.

## Current Truth

*(Proposed invention — treat as optional prep, not established fact.)*

A harbour debt that has been spoken aloud in the pier's hearing can change holders if the debtor's token (coin, tally stick, ring, or stamped chip) is pressed to the pier post while at least two witnesses acknowledge the transfer. After the touch, the new holder may call the debt; the old holder is released.

The custom assumes the pier post is the public ledger of that berth: anyone who works the dock is expected to treat a properly witnessed touch as binding.

### Limits

* **Exception.** Debts never spoken in the pier's hearing, or transfers without the token touching the post, do not move under this custom.
* **Unknown.** Whether a coerced witness still counts; whether a broken or substituted token voids the transfer; which free ports (if any) will ever receive this custom in the vault.
* **Impossible or forbidden.** Silent private handshake with no witnesses does not satisfy the custom as written.

---

## At the Table

* **Players notice.** A captain presses a brass tally to a salt-stained post while two dockhands nod; chalk marks refresh on the post after a transfer.
* **This explains.** Why some free-port arguments end at the post instead of a notary.
* **This enables.** Steal, swap, or smash a token; bribe or silence a witness; race to touch the post first.
* **This warns of.** Touching the wrong post, or touching without witnesses, leaves the debt where it was — or starts a dock fight about what "counts."
