## Dramatic Spine

Survival favors those who move fastest—can the party outpace both the wilderness and their pursuing rival to reach the survivors first?

---

## Session Compass

### Promise and Starting Situation

- **Player-facing promise:** A race to locate shipwreck survivors lost inland on Aruhe. The party moves through unfamiliar terrain, gathering clues about where the survivors went, what dangers pursued them, and why a mysterious rival also hunts them.
- **Current situation:** The party stands at the wreck site or clearing point, ready to begin overland tracking. Visible trails, debris, and signs of passage lead inland.
- **Player-created goals:** *[DM fills with PC objectives—e.g., a character seeking a specific survivor, proving themselves, racing against time pressure]*
- **Established facts and canon:** Aruhe terrain includes wildlife, water routes, ruins, or shelter sites (QMD silent; mark as stubs). A ship has wrecked. Survivors exist and are mobile. A rival is pursuing the same objective with unknown intent.
- **Setting handoff:** Terrain descriptions and route decisions → `dungeon-design` or TotM narration; NPC survivor stat blocks → separate wiki pages.

### Duration and Budget

- **Usable play time:** 300 minutes (5 hours)
- **Beat heuristic:** ~30 minutes per beat; adjust for table speed
- **Core reservation:** Hook + Climax + Resolution ≈ 90 minutes
- **Middle budget:** 2 D/C pairs (120 minutes) + flex time for travel and recovery
- **Likely shape:** `Hook (30m) → Development (30m) → Cliffhanger (30m) → Development (30m) → Cliffhanger (30m) → Climax (30m) → Resolution (30m)` = 210 min core; 90 min cushion
- **Time cushions:** Travel between sites, brief rests, rule lookups, player planning, contingency for extended negotiation or combat

## Thread Map

| Thread | Source | Early beats plant it | How Climax might harvest it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Finding the Survivors** | PC goal + mystery: What happened to them? Where are they now? | Hook (first sign), Dev-1 (direction confirmed) | Climax: party reaches survivors and must defend them or make a hard choice |
| **The Rival's Threat** | Antagonist pursuing same objective; unknown alliance or motive | Cliffhanger-1 (wildlife blocks; are they behind it?) or Dev-2 (tracks suggest shadowing) | Climax: rival appears at same location or moment of rescue; confrontation inevitable |
| **Aruhe's Dangers** | Environmental + wildlife pressure; escalating hazard | Hook (first encounter with terrain/wildlife sign) | Climax: wildlife or terrain becomes a factor in the confrontation or rescue |
| **Survivors' Secrets** | What do survivors know? Why the wreck? Rival's interest? | Dev-2 (meeting survivors or finding their message) | Climax: survivors' knowledge explains why rival hunts them or changes stakes |

**Thread audit:** Every beat must advance at least one thread. A beat advancing no thread is filler; a beat advancing an abandoned thread is a railroad.

## Beat Map

### Hook: First Sign on the Trail

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Type** | Hook (Action-heavy) |
| **Trigger** | Party begins inland search. Within ~100 yards, visible sign of survivors: fresh debris, boots, a message carved into a tree, or blood-trail. |
| **Actors and stakes** | Survivors (unseen): they are mobile and in danger. **Immediate stakes:** Time; every hour delays give rivals or hazards an edge. |
| **Player options (2+)** | (1) Follow the sign/trail inland immediately. (2) Investigate the sign more thoroughly for clues about direction, urgency, survivor count. (3) Scout ahead first before committing. (4) Mark location and report back (retreat option, lowest commitment). |
| **Information players gain** | Survivors are here, they are moving inland, they left signs intentionally or by chance. Rough direction. Rough timeline (fresh vs. old). |
| **Threads advanced** | **Finding the Survivors** (direction planted), **Aruhe's Dangers** (terrain hazards made visible) |
| **Polarity handoff** | Action Hook → next beat is **Development** |
| **Carry-forward to next beat** | Party has committed to following the trail and chosen a direction. The visible world is now: inland route, clear sign-markers ahead, time pressure mounting. |
| **Completion** | Party has chosen to move inland and can articulate why: following trail, time matters, survivors are within reach. |

### Development-1: The Gathering Storm (Information + Direction Change)

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Type** | Development (Cerebral) |
| **Trigger** | As party follows the trail deeper, terrain narrows or visibility shifts. They find a second major clue: a destroyed raft, survivor camp with burned supplies, or carved message about direction and warning. **How Scene Resolves from Hook:** The trail leads to this location naturally; no teleport. |
| **Actors and stakes** | Survivors (still unseen): their camp shows they were here, now moved on. **New stakes:** The burned supplies or destroyed raft suggest they fled something. From what? Who? |
| **What changes in decision space** | Party learns: (1) survivors *fled* the camp, not merely traveled through; (2) something spooked them (rival? predator? [[bloodhawk]] passage? environmental hazard?); (3) survivors are *alive and organized enough to move deliberately*. |
| **Player options (2+)** | (1) Push forward, following the new trail (commitment: follow fast). (2) Search the camp thoroughly for written intel, supplies, or other clues before moving (commitment: slow down, gather info). (3) Split the party: scout ahead vs. search (commitment: divided). (4) Double back to tell someone or gather aid (retreat option). |
| **Information threshold** | Ends when players learn or decide: **The survivors fled; they are not safe yet, and something forced them to move.** |
| **Threads advanced** | **Finding the Survivors** (direction refined, urgency raised), **Survivors' Secrets** (why did they flee? planted mystery). |
| **Pressure mode** | **Suspense**: threat is visible (something scared survivors away), fuse is advancing (survivors are moving further ahead each hour), choices have weight (push forward or gather intel?). |
| **Carry-forward to next beat** | Party knows survivors are alive, purposeful, and *running from something*. They've chosen to follow or investigate. New visible world state: inland deeper, trail splits or becomes unclear, sense that survivors are actively fleeing, not merely lost. **Next beat's opening must inherit:** Which direction did survivors flee? Is the thing they fled nearby or ahead? |
| **Completion** | Party can name: *Survivors are alive, something made them flee, we are getting closer but time is critical.* |

### Cliffhanger-1: The Bloodhawk Ambush (Action + Obstacle)

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Type** | Cliffhanger (Action-heavy; tests direction chosen in Development-1) |
| **Trigger** | **How Scene Resolves from Development-1:** As party chooses to follow or scout, they move into terrain (narrow canyon, dense grove, water crossing). A flock of [[bloodhawk]]s (or similar aerial predator creatures native to Aruhe) erupts from hiding, drawn by the survivors' passage earlier or by the party's movement. The birds are territorial, aggressive, and carry the scent of recent kills. |
| **Actors and stakes** | **[[Bloodhawk]] flock:** 4–6 creatures, not a lethal threat alone but dangerous in number; each deals 1d6 or similar damage in D&D 5e terms. **Real stakes:** Injury to party, delay while fighting or evading, noise drawing unwanted attention. Can party push through or must they detour? |
| **Stakes visibility** | Birds circle and dive; party hears shrieks; they see blood matted on wings (the birds have fed recently—on what?). Immediate, observable threat. |
| **Off-ramps and counterplay** | (1) Combat: fight the flock (risky; wounds, time cost, noise). (2) Evasion: run or hide (cost: lose trail temporarily, survivors' head start grows). (3) Bargain/distract: throw meat or bait; use magic to scare them off (if table allows; reveals capability). (4) Clever trick: lead them away from survivors' trail intentionally, then circle back (commitment: trade time for safety). |
| **What changes in fiction** | (1) **If party fights:** minor wounds, time cost ~15 min, but they hold the trail. Afterward, they notice the birds' kill-zone includes scraps of cloth matching survivors' clothing—survivors passed through here and the birds attacked them too. (2) **If party evades:** trail becomes temporarily unclear; party must search or backtrack. (3) **If party bargains:** they gain favor of the birds (strange) or learn the birds avoid a certain area (foreshadowing: the Rival's camp?). |
| **Threads advanced** | **Aruhe's Dangers** (wildlife is real and escalating), **Finding the Survivors** (evidence survivors met same hazard; party is on right track), **The Rival's Threat** (if party learns birds are unusual, controlled, or seem *herded*—foreshadow rival's presence). |
| **Escalation dial** | Early middle session: avoidable with clever tactics; not lethal but painful. Hard enough to hurt, easy enough to overcome. |
| **Carry-forward to next beat** | Party is slightly injured or tired. Trail is confirmed (birds attacked survivors too). Party is now *aware* that Aruhe is actively hostile, and time pressure is rising. The survivors are closer but still ahead. |
| **Completion** | Party has faced and resolved the hazard. They can now state: *Aruhe is dangerous, survivors came through here, we need to move fast.* |

### Development-2: The Rival's Mark (Revelation + Direction Shift)

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Type** | Development (Cerebral) |
| **Trigger** | **How Scene Resolves from Cliffhanger-1:** After the bloodhawk encounter, as party presses on, they reach clearer terrain (ridge, clearing, riverbank) where they find a third clue: survivor tracks *and* a second set of tracks shadowing them—deliberate, avoiding the main path, but circling. A trained scout can spot a campsite, discarded gear marked with an unfamiliar sigil, or a message carved as warning: *"They are not safe here. Go back. —V"* (or similar; Rival's mark). |
| **Actors and stakes** | **[[Rival-Varesh]]** (placeholder): someone pursuing survivors with intent unknown—protection? Capture? Revenge? **Stakes:** Party is not alone in this hunt. Someone else reached this area first, set up a base, and is actively shepherding or hunting the survivors. Party now competes for outcome. |
| **What changes in decision space** | Party learns: (1) They are not the only searchers. (2) The Rival has *resources* and *preparation* (campsite, supplies, scouting skill). (3) The Rival's *motive* is unknown—savior or predator? (4) Survivors may have already encountered or evaded the Rival. |
| **Player options (2+)** | (1) Confront the Rival directly: move to their campsite and demand answers. (2) Follow the Rival's trail covertly to learn their full plan before revealing party's presence. (3) Press ahead to survivors first, then deal with the Rival. (4) Leave a message or trap to slow the Rival, then move fast to survivors. |
| **Information threshold** | Ends when players learn or decide: **We are in a race. Someone else hunts the survivors. We must decide how we compete.** |
| **Threads advanced** | **The Rival's Threat** (revealed, no longer a shadow), **Survivors' Secrets** (why does Rival care? Planted mystery). |
| **Pressure mode** | **Suspense**: Threat is visible (a second player in the game), fuse is advancing (Rival may reach survivors first), choices carry weight (cooperate, race, or sabotage the Rival?). |
| **Discoverable details** | At least two paths to learn about the Rival: (1) Scouting their camp, finding journals or gear. (2) Catching and questioning a scout or hireling. (3) Finding messages left by survivors warning about the Rival. |
| **Carry-forward to next beat** | Party has committed to a competitive strategy (race, confront, covert, sabotage). The world state is now: survivors are a few hours ahead, Rival is 1–2 hours behind or alongside party, all three are converging on the same destination. Next beat must inherit this three-way tension. |
| **Completion** | Party can name: *A rival also hunts survivors. We must reach them first or stop the rival before they do.* |

### Cliffhanger-2: The Rival's Gambit (Confrontation + Pressure)

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Type** | Cliffhanger (Action + Social; tests direction chosen in Development-2) |
| **Trigger** | **How Scene Resolves from Development-2:** Party is moving toward survivors when the Rival *reveals themselves deliberately*. They block the trail or approach in a parley, flanked by hired mercenaries or allies (e.g., [[rival-escort]]). The Rival explains their position: survivors are in *immediate danger* (a collapsed shelter, predator, environmental hazard, or another faction), and the Rival is *already saving them*—or claiming to. Party's arrival complicates things. The Rival makes a choice offer: Join forces, stay back and watch, or leave the area entirely. Refuse, and the Rival becomes openly hostile. |
| **Actors and stakes** | **[[Rival-Varesh]]** and retinue: skilled, prepared, and not hostile *yet*. **Survivors:** heard crying out, trapped, or in shelter beyond the Rival's position. **Real stakes:** Party can cooperate (share the save), fight (risk injury and losing the initiative), negotiate (cost to pride or resources), or withdraw (tactical defeat). |
| **Stakes visibility** | Rival is calm, armed, outnumbered but not outmatched. Party hears survivor sounds in the distance (screams, calls for help, cries from trapped location). Pressure is *immediate*. |
| **Possible approaches** | (1) **Fight:** Combat with Rival's mercenaries. Risk: wounds, delays, survivors in danger. Reward: full agency over survivors' outcome. (2) **Bargain:** Negotiate with Rival—split credit, join forces, negotiate terms. Risk: trust issues, Rival's hidden agenda. Reward: combined strength, faster save. (3) **Sneak:** Circle around, create distraction, rescue survivors while Rival is distracted. Risk: complex, high failure cost. Reward: beats Rival to the save. (4) **Bluff/Parley:** Challenge Rival's claim, demand proof of good intent, call them out publicly. Risk: escalates to fight. Reward: psychological advantage, posture of authority. |
| **What changes in fiction** | **(If party fights):** Mercenaries are defeated or routed. Party reaches survivors first. Rival escapes or retreats. Survivors are grateful to party. Cost: wounds, time, resources. **(If party negotiates):** Rival and party cooperate to save survivors. Survivors see both groups working together. Rival's true motive becomes clearer (but may be obscured intentionally). Cost: shared glory. **(If party sneaks):** They rescue survivors before Rival arrives. Rival confronts them after, demanding explanations or threatening retaliation. Cost: future enmity. **(If party bluffs):** Rival either backs down (losing face) or calls the bluff, leading to combat or negotiation. |
| **Threads advanced** | **The Rival's Threat** (confronted directly; motive becomes clearer), **Finding the Survivors** (survivors are now *visible*, within reach), **Survivors' Secrets** (survivors may reveal why Rival hunts them; alliance, blood debt, artifact, or other).  |
| **Escalation dial** | Late-middle session: Stakes are high. Rival is credible threat. Real injury possible. But survivors are *right there*—the Climax is forming. This beat is the last major obstacle before the final convergence. |
| **Carry-forward to next beat** | **Party fought:** Injured, tired, survivors freed, Rival fled or dead. Next beat: tend survivors, prepare for Climax if Rival has allies approaching. **Party negotiated:** Relationship is tense. Survivors are trusting or skeptical of both groups. Next beat: Climax might be preventing a *third threat* together or unveiling Rival's true nature. **Party sneaked:** Survivors are grateful; Rival is now openly hostile and incoming. Next beat: Climax is defending survivors from Rival's counterattack. |
| **Completion** | Party has engaged the Rival and chosen a strategy. Survivors are now *present* in the fiction. Next beat must harvest all threads: survivors' safety, Rival's threat level, environmental pressure, and party's choices. |

### Climax: The Convergence (Final Test)

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Type** | Climax (Action or confrontation; follows Cliffhanger-2, so final action is appropriate) |
| **Threads harvested** | All four threads converge: (1) Survivors' location and safety, (2) Rival's true motive and willingness to fight/negotiate, (3) Aruhe's final hazard or wildlife surge, (4) Survivors' secrets or revelation that explains everything. |
| **Which player choices made this inevitable** | Every decision from Development-2 onward led here: the party chose to race, confront, or negotiate with the Rival. The Rival chose to reveal themselves or escalate. The survivors' location became known. Now all three forces meet at the same point. The Climax is *not* mandatory if the party chose a different route, but the fiction has conspired to make this confrontation the highest-stakes moment. |
| **Climax shape** | **Final Battle** (if combat with Rival is central), **Negotiation Under Duress** (if survivors are threatened by environment/third party and both party and Rival must decide to save them together or leave them), **Betrayal Cascade** (if Rival's true nature is revealed as survivors' betrayer), or **Reckoning** (if survivors' secrets or the party's actions have built to a moral or tactical reckoning). |
| **Candidate: Defenders of the Survivors** | Party must protect survivors from: (a) Rival's betrayal or capture attempt, (b) A third threat (environmental collapse, wildlife siege, another faction arrival), or (c) Survivors' own poor choices/injuries. All of Aruhe's dangers culminate here. Party faces a *choice under pressure*: protect all survivors even at cost to self, negotiate with Rival for aid, or make hard choices about who to save. |
| **Possible approaches** | (1) **Direct confrontation:** Fight Rival and wildlife/hazard simultaneously. Risk: outnumbered. Reward: full control of outcome. (2) **Strategic alliance:** Convince Rival to join against a greater threat. Risk: trust the Rival. Reward: combined strength. (3) **Sacrifice play:** One PC or NPC makes a deliberate sacrifice to protect survivors and stop the Rival. Risk: permanent cost. Reward: dramatic resolution. (4) **Retreat and bargain:** Offer Rival something they want more than the survivors (information, payment, alliance). Risk: survivors may not escape. Reward: avoids bloodshed. |
| **Partial outcomes and costs** | **(Survivors all rescued, Rival defeated):** Full success. Cost: party is wounded or drained; survivors are traumatized. **(Survivors rescued, Rival escapes):** Partial success. Cost: Rival remains a threat; survivors owe party a debt. **(Some survivors rescued, some lost):** Bitter victory. Cost: guilt, survivors' grief, questions about who was left behind. **(Survivors negotiated to Rival):** Compromise. Cost: party's authority is questioned; Rival's true nature determines if survivors are safe. **(Survivors lost entirely):** Campaign shift. Cost: profound. Only if party made choices leading here; never a forced TPK. |
| **Why it is not mandatory now** | Party could have avoided the Rival entirely (took a different route), sneaked survivors away during the earlier Cliffhanger, or established trust that prevents this confrontation. If the party did any of those things, the session recomputes—this candidate drops, and a new climax forms from what actually happened. |
| **Carry-forward to Resolution** | Party has committed to a resolution (fight, negotiate, sacrifice). Survivors' fates are now known (safe, at risk, escaped, captured). Rival's status is resolved (defeated, fled, allied, dead, escaped). Next beat shows the *aftermath*. |
| **Completion** | Party players can articulate: *We saved the survivors (or lost them). The Rival is (defeated/allied/escaped). Aruhe is still dangerous, but we've earned a moment of rest.* |

### Resolution: The Reckoning (Aftermath & Reflection)

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Type** | Resolution |
| **Scope** | Match the Climax scope: survivors' safety, Rival's fate, and what it cost the party to achieve it. |
| **If survivors are saved** | Party establishes a safe camp. Survivors share their stories: what caused the wreck, why the Rival hunted them, what they carry with them (artifact, knowledge, enemy list). Party tends wounds. A few hours of relative safety. Survivors express gratitude or raise complications (hidden injuries, moral debts, warnings about what caused the wreck). |
| **If survivors are partly lost or captured** | Party must decide: chase after captured survivors (new thread), grieve and regroup, or accept this outcome. Survivors' fate becomes a hook for future sessions or a reminder of the cost of failure. |
| **Threads' final state** | **(Finding the Survivors):** Survivors' condition, safety level, and what they know are now clear. **(The Rival's Threat):** Rival is dead, fled, allied, or captured. Future threat is known or unknown. **(Aruhe's Dangers):** Party has learned the wildlife, terrain, and hazards of the inland region. Aruhe is still hostile but *known*. **(Survivors' Secrets):** Survivors explain what caused the wreck, why the Rival hunted them, what treasures or knowledge they carry, or what dangers still lurk. |
| **Arc echo** | Every thread planted in the Hook and middle shows its final state. Party can look back and see: *We found them. The rival's motive is clear. Aruhe nearly killed us all. The survivors carry a secret we must protect (or a problem we must solve).* |
| **What players can pursue next** | Survivors owe the party a debt (escort them to safety, protect them from new threats, follow them to a destination). Party is injured or drained (must rest, resupply, or recover). Rival's allies may seek revenge. Survivors' secrets may open a new mystery or quest. |
| **Completion test** | Can players name: *We've changed the survivors' fates. This cost us (time/resources/allies/limbs/pride). What do we do tomorrow?* If yes, the session arc is complete. |

## Floating Beats (Prepared Situations, Not Required)

These are situations the DM has prepped but may never fire if the fiction does not call for them. If candidates become obsolete during play, they are dropped. If a new situation emerges, a floating beat can be repurposed.

### Environmental Hazard: River Crossing

- **Trigger:** Party's path crosses a swollen river or blocked water route.
- **Actors:** None; environment is the antagonist.
- **Stakes:** Delay, injury, loss of supplies, or forced detour.
- **Viable responses:** (1) Wade across despite current (risk: swept downstream). (2) Build or find a raft (cost: time). (3) Find a ford upstream or downstream (cost: time + exploration). (4) Magic or clever trick to cross safely (if table allows).
- **Thread advanced:** **Aruhe's Dangers** if it becomes a serious obstacle; **Finding the Survivors** if survivors' trail crosses here (did they cross? were they swept away?).
- **Fallback:** If river is never reached or is bypassed, drop this beat.

### Social Encounter: Aruhe Locals or Refuge

- **Trigger:** Party encounters locals, traders, or a settlement/shelter on the inland route.
- **Actors:** Neutral or helpful NPCs who may know survivors' whereabouts, rival's identity, or Aruhe's dangers.
- **Stakes:** Information (clues about survivors or rival), aid (supplies, shelter, guide), or complications (locals have their own problems, or survivors are *hiding* from locals).
- **Viable responses:** (1) Trade for information. (2) Hire a guide. (3) Avoid them entirely. (4) Help them with a quick problem in exchange for info.
- **Thread advanced:** **Finding the Survivors** (locals have seen them or rival), **Survivors' Secrets** (locals reveal context about wreck or rival).
- **Fallback:** If party never encounters locals, this beat becomes a missed opportunity—no penalty unless survivors depended on locals' aid.

### Wildlife Sign: Predator Territory

- **Trigger:** Party enters the lair or territory of a larger predator (not swarm like bloodhawks, but a singular threat like [[talon-skarn]] or apex beast).
- **Actors:** Apex predator—territorial, not automatically hostile but dangerous.
- **Stakes:** Serious injury or death if party triggers its aggression. Or: survivors may have fled *into* this territory to avoid other threats.
- **Viable responses:** (1) Sneak through carefully (high skill check). (2) Distract or appease the predator (magic, sacrifice, bribery). (3) Fight (high risk, high cost). (4) Circle around entirely (cost: time, survivors fall further ahead).
- **Thread advanced:** **Aruhe's Dangers** (apex threat revealed), **Finding the Survivors** (survivors' trail leads through or around this territory; do they have a way past?).
- **Fallback:** If party avoids this territory or never enters it, drop this beat; it is not central to the arc.

## Pressure & Clocks

| Clock | Current state | Advance trigger | Visible sign | Consequence | Player influence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Survivors' Distance Ahead** | Early: ~6 hours' lead. | Each hour party delays (camp, rest, extended negotiation). | Survivors' trail grows colder; party must search harder to track. | If gap reaches 12+ hours, survivors may establish shelter, die, or be captured by Rival unseen. | Party can push fast (fatigue cost), find shortcuts (scouting), or negotiate with Rival (shared resources). |
| **Rival's Approach** | Early: ~4 hours behind party. | Each hour party uses; Rival moves steadily. | Signs of Rival's camp or scouts appear; party realizes they are being pursued. | If Rival reaches survivors first, party faces a confrontation or loses the race entirely. | Party can set traps, ambush Rival, move faster, or negotiate alliance. |
| **Aruhe's Hostility** | Low → Medium after first wildlife. | Each time party stays in one location too long; each unresolved environmental threat. | Wildlife converges; weather worsens; terrain becomes more treacherous. | If hostility reaches peak, a major environmental event (storm, predator swarm, quake) forces party toward the survivors' location or into the Rival's arms. | Party can placate wildlife (offerings, magic), move quickly to leave territory, or find shelter and wait it out. |
| **Survivors' Health** | Unknown but declining. | Each hour without aid; exposure, injury from original wreck, predation. | Party finds evidence: blood trails, abandoned supplies, desperate messages. | If health clock runs out, survivors die or scatter. Party arrives to rescue corpses or fragments. | Party can accelerate their pace, find medical supplies, or negotiate with Rival for shared aid. |

**Factions & Responses:**

- **Survivors:** Desperate, organized or scattered depending on group cohesion. They move toward perceived safety or a remembered landmark. If they encounter wildlife or the Rival, they either hide, fight, or flee.
- **[[Rival-Varesh]]:** Professional, motivated, outnumbered but skilled. If confronted, they either negotiate, fight, or retreat. Their true motive (protector? captor? bounty hunter?) is revealed during Cliffhanger-2.
- **Aruhe Wildlife (as faction):** Not intelligent or coordinated but territorial and aggressive. They respond to noise, blood, and intrusion. They may herd party and Rival together, creating forced convergence.

**Routes, Shelters, & Retreat Options:**

- Party can retreat to the wreck site to regroup or gather supplies (cost: time, survivors fall further ahead).
- Aruhe locals (if encountered) can provide shelter or guide services (cost: obligation or payment).
- Survivors may have left directions to a known shelter or landmark (requires finding a message or clue).
- Rival's campsite can be used as shelter if party defeats or bypasses them (risk: Rival returns; reward: supplies, comfort, intel).

## PC Touchpoints (Limelight Moments)

Every PC should have at least one moment where their skills, goals, or backstory become central.

### Suggested limelight frames (DM customizes per party):

**For a Tracker or Scout:**
- Cliffhanger-1 (Bloodhawk Ambush): Your tracking skills identify the flock's behavior and find a safe route through or around them.
- Development-1 (Gathering Storm): You spot the Rival's camousite first and must decide whether to reveal it to the party.

**For a Face or Negotiator:**
- Cliffhanger-2 (Rival's Gambit): You parley with the Rival, reading their motives and finding common ground (or exposing a lie).
- Climax: You negotiate with the Rival or survivors, brokering a solution or uncovering a secret.

**For a Martial PC:**
- Cliffhanger-1 (Bloodhawk Ambush): You stand your ground and protect the party from the worst of the dive-bombs, earning respect.
- Climax: You face the Rival in single combat or defend survivors while others handle other threats.

**For a Caster or Problem-Solver:**
- Development-1 (Gathering Storm): Your magic reveals invisible tracks or deciphers a message left by survivors.
- Floating Beat (Environmental Hazard or Predator Territory): Your spell or clever trick navigates a deadly obstacle.
- Resolution: Your insight or magic heals survivors or explains the wreck's true cause.

**For a PC with a Personal Stake:**
- Hook: You recognize a piece of wreckage or a survivor's name—this is *your* mission.
- Climax: Your determination or knowledge of a survivor becomes the key to saving them.

## DM Notes for Play

1. **Prepare for recompute:** After every beat, follow the recompute loop (agency.md): record state, resolve consequences, retire obsolete candidates, promote emergent beats, re-label as needed, offer the next opening. The Beat Map is a *palette*, not a script.

2. **Pressure-point mode:** This session uses **suspense**—visible threats (Rival, wildlife, time) with advancing fuses (survivors' distance, health clocks) and weighty choices (race, confront, negotiate). Avoid hiding the threat (mystery) or springing unannounced bombs (surprise).

3. **Escalation:** Bloodhawk swarm is avoidable with tactics. Rival is credible and outnumbered but not overpowered. Climax converges all threads—survivors *and* Rival *and* wildlife/environment *and* secrets. Each beat raises stakes slightly.

4. **Transitions:** Each beat's carry-forward is the next beat's trigger. There are no time-skips or teleports. If party chooses a different route, that is not a punishment—recompute and pivot.

5. **Floating beats stay floating:** Predator territory, river crossing, local encounter are *only* used if the fiction calls for them. If party finds a shortcut and avoids them, they are not forced back into the chart.

6. **Dramatic spine check:** Every beat should reinforce—from a different angle—that *speed and strategy matter*. The Hook introduces it (survivors are *ahead*, time is *now*). Dev-1 raises it (survivors fled; time is critical). Cliffhanger-1 tests it (wildlife delays the party). Dev-2 shifts it (Rival is also racing). Cliffhanger-2 raises stakes (Rival is right there). Climax harvests it (all three forces converge; no more time to waste). Resolution echoes it (survivors are safe/lost; the race has resolved).
