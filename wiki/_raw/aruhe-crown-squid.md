## The Great Crown Squid

I would rename the **Colossal Tree Squid** the **Great Crown Squid**, usually shortened to **Crown Squid**. “Tree squid” sounds slightly comedic; “Crown Squid” sounds like an animal somebody could genuinely have named after discovering that the crowns of Aruhe’s enormous trees are its habitat. It also preserves Aruhe’s naming pattern: ordinary words for something that becomes horrible once you understand what they mean.

This fits Aruhe extremely well because the island’s established rule is not “invent random monsters,” but **take a recognizable animal and push some authentic part of its nature until it performs the wrong ecological job**.  The Quiet is especially suitable: visibility already dies within a few paces, hearing is unreliable, and its enormous trees and root curtains establish exactly the vertical ecosystem this thing needs. 

### What it is

A mature Great Crown Squid is a **Gargantuan arboreal cephalopod**, approximately **60–80 feet from mantle-tip to the ends of its eight principal arms**, with hair-thin-to-rope-thick hunting tentacles capable of extending another **80–120 feet downward**. The oldest individuals may be larger.

Its mantle is grotesquely broad rather than hydrodynamic: a translucent, lobed structure resembling a swollen squid mantle crossed with a seed pod and a spider abdomen. Much of that apparent bulk is not flesh. It is an enormous collection of gas chambers.

The eight principal arms are the animal’s “legs.” Each is immensely muscular, ending in broad gripping pads and hooked suckers. At rest, perhaps five are anchored to separate trunks while three lie loose. When moving, the animal doesn't swing like an ape. It **flows from tree to tree like an enormous jumping spider**, releasing and placing limbs in a sequence so coordinated that its central body barely seems to move.

Underneath hangs the feeding apparatus: the beak, radula, and a forest of much finer extensible tentacles. Some are only finger-thick despite being dozens of feet long.

The biological ingredients are surprisingly solid. Real colossal squid possess hooked arms and tentacles; the long feeding tentacles seize prey, after which the eight arms surround and guide it to the beak. Colossal-squid tentacle hooks actually swivel, while arm hooks are deeply seated and designed to maintain their purchase on struggling prey. ([Te Papa][1]) Cephalopods already control skin color and texture using chromatophores, reflective cells, and muscular papillae, so its nearly supernatural camouflage is an exaggeration of something cephalopods genuinely do. ([Smithsonian Ocean][2])

### The spider part should be behavioral, not anatomical

Don't give it spider legs attached to a squid. The squid shape being recognizably intact makes it much stranger.

Instead, steal **jumping-spider sensory psychology**.

Jumping spiders really have eight eyes divided into different jobs: narrow-field, exceptionally high-resolution principal eyes and broad secondary eyes particularly good at noticing movement. ([PubMed][3]) The Crown Squid exaggerates this into something horrifying. Two enormous forward eyes perform rangefinding and microscopic target inspection. Three secondary pairs ring the mantle, continuously passing moving objects to the principal pair.

The result is effectively **360-degree awareness**, but not eight identical magic cameras. When something moves, one little lateral eye notices. Then, without its body moving at all, the enormous principal eyes slowly roll around inside the transparent head until they are looking at it.

That movement is the tell.

And there is one anatomical flaw: immediately beneath the beak and between the bases of the arms, the eye fields overlap badly. **The safest place relative to a Crown Squid is horrifyingly close to its mouth.**

That should matter mechanically.

## The gas mantle

This is the one element I would tweak slightly for biological grounding.

A truly lighter-than-air multi-ton animal needs an absurd gas volume. Instead, make the Crown Squid **buoyancy-assisted rather than freely flying**. Its gas mantle removes most of its effective weight, while its arms provide anchorage and propulsion. The animal still weighs several tons if killed, but a living one feels to the trees more like something weighing a few hundred pounds.

There is real precedent for an animal possessing a dedicated gas-filled flotation organ: the Portuguese man o’ war's pneumatophore is literally a gas-filled biological float. ([Q?rius][4]) Biological hydrogen production through microbial fermentation is also real. ([PubMed][5])

So the Aruhe version has **symbiotic fermentative microbes in gas-gill chambers**. Blood brings them carbohydrate-rich waste products; the microbes generate hydrogen-rich gas; specialized countercurrent tissues separate and compress it into the mantle sacs.

It cannot truly fly.

It can, however, do something worse.

**It almost doesn't fall.**

If all eight arms release, the mantle slowly sinks or rises between the trees while the animal spreads its limbs looking for another purchase. This means a four-ton predator can launch across a fifty-foot canopy gap almost silently.

And because the thing supporting it is gas rather than bone and muscle, its locomotion feels deeply wrong. Trees should bend under something this large.

They barely move.

# The modern fear: **you have already been selected**

The arachnophobia is immediate. That's the conscious horror.

The deeper Aruhe horror should be **ubiquitous surveillance and automated targeting**.

Not “someone is following me,” because the Deer-Stalker already owns that psychological territory.

This is:

**Something was watching the entire time. You didn't know because it had no reason to reveal itself until it chose you.**

The party walks beneath it for perhaps several minutes.

Eight eyes have tracked everyone.

It has watched who trails behind.

Who limps.

Who repeatedly separates from the others.

Who carries the most meat.

Who is smallest.

The players never make the momentous decision to enter combat.

One character simply makes a Perception check and fails.

Then:

> Something closes around your ankle. Not a vine. There is a wet little tightening sensation, then a hook punches through your boot. Your feet leave the ground.
>
> You are thirty feet up before anyone understands what is happening.
>
> The branch above you changes color.
>
> It is not a branch.

That's the Crown Squid.

It doesn't roar.

It doesn't threaten.

**A human is not an opponent. A human is approximately the size category of prey it normally lifts out of the undergrowth.**

# Ecology

Aruhe’s gigantic trees solve most of the ecological problem. The established canopy is already hundred-foot-scale in ordinary stretches, with extraordinary growth throughout the island. 

A Crown Squid requires several adjacent mature trees because it rarely trusts its whole mass to one. That gives it a natural range restriction.

| Region            | Crown Squid behavior                                                                                      |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Old Gardens**   | Mature animals usually absent. Trees are too interrupted. Juveniles occasionally hunt the upper terraces. |
| **The Quiet**     | Prime habitat. This should be where the species becomes a major threat.                                   |
| **The Rot**       | Largest and oldest animals. More sessile because prey is abundant.                                        |
| **Grasslands**    | Refuses to cross broad openings except under extreme hunger. No anchor points.                            |
| **River valleys** | Hunts from the jungle bank but rarely extends a grabber over deep water.                                  |
| **Clear Lake**    | Forested margins only. Open shoreline is refuge from it.                                                  |
| **The Grove**     | Does not cross the mourner line.                                                                          |

This also finally pays off the existing Long Reach language that things **hang in the jungle curtain and drop from the root canopy**.  Crown Squids should be one of the things that sentence was warning about all along.

The River Otters create an especially good ecological boundary. A Crown Squid that lets a grabber trail into occupied water is liable to discover six enormous otters pulling the other direction. Mature squids therefore recognize open water as unsafe. That gives Aruhe a nice apex-predator mosaic rather than one monster trivially dominating everything.

On land, though, the Crown Squid is at the top.

Terror-Birds are dangerous prey.

Bear-Elk are very dangerous prey.

Wolfrabbits are snacks.

A humanoid is food.

# Signs that one lives here

The trick is to make the encounter **avoidable after players learn the ecology**.

A DC 14 Wisdom (Survival) check recognizes that something extremely large is using the upper canopy. DC 17 identifies the unmistakable hunting signs. DC 20 Wisdom (Perception), or passive Perception 20, can actually find a motionless adult before it attacks.

The signs are sucker scars **seventy feet above the ground**; circles arranged in sequences no claw could make. Strips of bark removed upward rather than downward. A mature tree leaning thirty degrees from where something uprooted it and then discarded it. A few polished fragments of antler lying on the forest floor despite there being no carcass nearby. And, most useful of all, places where the undergrowth contains lots of prey tracks but **no tracks showing what killed them**.

Nothing walked down to eat them.

They went up.

# Great Crown Squid

*Gargantuan monstrosity, unaligned — CR 17*

**AC** 17 (rubbery natural armor)
**HP** 283 (21d20 + 63)
**Speed** 20 ft., climb 50 ft.

**STR** 24 (+7) | **DEX** 20 (+5) | **CON** 17 (+3) | **INT** 7 (−2) | **WIS** 20 (+5) | **CHA** 6 (−2)

**Saving Throws** Dex +11, Con +9, Wis +11
**Skills** Perception +17, Stealth +11, Athletics +13
**Senses** darkvision 120 ft., passive Perception 27
**Languages** —
**PB** +6

### Traits

**Canopy Camouflage.** While among foliage, branches, or hanging roots, the squid can Hide even when only lightly obscured. If it hasn't moved since the end of its previous turn, it has advantage on Dexterity (Stealth) checks. This is cephalopod camouflage expressed through 5e's normal hiding mechanics rather than *invisibility*.

**Eight-Eyed Awareness.** The squid has advantage on Wisdom (Perception) checks relying on sight and cannot be surprised while conscious unless the creature surprising it is inside its Mouth-Blind Zone.

**Mouth-Blind Zone.** The squid cannot visually perceive a creature within 10 feet directly beneath the center of its mantle unless that creature is grappled by the squid or touching one of its arms. Against the squid, such a creature counts as unseen, and the squid cannot make opportunity attacks against it.

**Spider-Braced.** While at least three primary arms are touching solid surfaces, the squid cannot be knocked prone or moved against its will.

**Buoyant Mantle.** The squid takes no falling damage while its gas mantle remains intact and falls no faster than 60 feet per round. It cannot use this trait to fly.

**Siege Predator.** The squid deals double damage to objects and structures. Nonmagical plant growth never costs it additional movement.

**Selected Prey.** The squid has advantage on its first Hookline attack each turn against a creature that has no conscious ally within 10 feet of it. This is the jumping-spider brain made mechanically visible: **isolation invites attack.**

---

## Actions

**Multiattack.** The squid makes three attacks, only one of which may be a Beak attack.

**Hookline Tentacle.** *Melee Weapon Attack:* +11 to hit, reach 80 ft., one creature. **Hit:** 18 (2d10 + 7) slashing damage, and the target is grappled (escape DC 19). Until the grapple ends, the target is also restrained. The squid can maintain up to four Hookline grapples simultaneously.

A Hookline can itself be attacked: **AC 15, 15 hit points, immunity to poison and psychic damage**. Destroying one ends that grapple but does not meaningfully reduce the Crown Squid's combat ability; it has dozens.

**Crushing Arm.** *Melee Weapon Attack:* +13 to hit, reach 20 ft. **Hit:** 25 (4d8 + 7) bludgeoning damage. The squid may grapple the target (escape DC 19) or push it 20 feet.

**Beak.** *Melee Weapon Attack:* +13 to hit, reach 10 ft., one creature grappled by the squid. **Hit:** 33 (4d12 + 7) piercing damage.

**Reel.** Each creature grappled by a Hookline is pulled up to 30 feet directly toward the Crown Squid.

**Canopy Pounce (Recharge 5–6).** The squid moves up to its climb speed without provoking opportunity attacks, provided it ends that movement touching a tree or similarly massive structure. At any two points during this movement it may make a Hookline attack.

That is the moment the players learn that failing the ambush did not save them.

They look up expecting an enormous animal to lumber after them.

Instead it **skitters sixty feet sideways through the crowns without ever taking its eyes off them.**

# Legendary Actions

A mature Crown Squid gets **3 legendary actions** each round.

**Skitter.** The squid moves up to 20 feet using its climb speed without provoking opportunity attacks.

**Hookline.** The squid makes one Hookline Tentacle attack.

**Reel.** One creature grappled by the squid is pulled up to 20 feet toward it.

**Rip Through (Costs 2).** The squid tears apart a 10-foot cube of nonmagical wood or vegetation within reach. Creatures in that area make a **DC 19 Dexterity save**, taking 18 (4d8) bludgeoning damage and falling prone on a failure, or half damage on success. The destroyed area becomes difficult terrain.

This is how you depict it “ripping trees out of its way” without giving it a weird bespoke tree-uproot subsystem. Mechanically, it is the standard 5e idea of a Siege Monster expressed as battlefield destruction.

# Running the encounter

The creature shouldn't normally descend and exchange attacks with five adventurers.

Its combat loop is:

**Watch → select → hook → lift → reel → beak.**

If the party cuts the line before the victim reaches the mouth, the Crown Squid doesn't become enraged. It becomes **interested**.

That failed grab tells it something about the herd.

It immediately relocates.

Then it tries a different individual.

When actually wounded badly—say reduced below half HP—it stops treating the party as ordinary prey and begins a full jumping-spider pursuit. It stays above them, anticipates routes rather than following directly, crosses gaps with Canopy Pounce, and simply demolishes vegetation that would slow it.

The important distinction from the Terror-Bird is that the Terror-Bird's first explosive charge is its commitment; escaping that charge can end the encounter. Crown Squid psychology is the opposite. The original Terror-Bird design is a patient territorial ambusher that doesn't pursue indefinitely. 

**The Crown Squid likes a chase.**

## Escape is geographical, not numerical

This makes it function as an Aruhe **hazard**, rather than merely a bag of 283 hit points.

Reach broad open ground.

Get into sufficiently deep water.

Pass beneath terrain too low for its body.

Force it to cross a grassland valley with no canopy connection.

Or hurt it badly enough that elephant-sized food stops looking worth the calories.

That last point is important: it is an apex predator, not a fantasy villain. It does not hate adventurers. It doesn't defend Aruhe out of ideology. Even when the Taking Rule is active, the Blight is amplifying a genuine predatory response rather than remotely piloting the animal.

And fleeing into a Grassland cut is wonderfully Aruhe because **escaping the thing in the trees means running into the territory where the Terror-Bird waits for things to leave cover**. The existing Grasslands already treat open sky as bait. 

So the Crown Squid gives the Quiet a new fundamental rule:

**The floor is not where the jungle's largest predator lives.**

The party can spend days learning to watch the trunks, roots, game trails and shadows.

Then Aruhe teaches them to look up.
