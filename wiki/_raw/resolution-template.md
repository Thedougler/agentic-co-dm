# {{title}}

> [!abstract] Resolution
> **Follows:** [[Climax]]
> **Purpose:** Show what the climax changed, fulfill the adventure's promise, give the characters room to react, and leave on one strong final image.
>
> **Outcome:** One sentence describing what is true now because of what the party did.

## Run the Beat

1. **Confirm the outcome.** State what the party actually achieved, lost, escaped, or failed to prevent.
2. **Show the consequence.** Put one visible change in front of them now.
3. **Pay off the stakes.** Deliver the promised reward, reunion, recognition, loss, answer, or other reason this mattered.
4. **Let them react.** Give the characters space for brief choices, conversations, or epilogue moments.
5. **End on an image.** Close once the consequence has landed. Use a stinger only when it grows naturally from play.

> [!narration] Closing image
> Write 2–4 sentences the DM can speak. Show the new status quo through something the characters can see, hear, or experience. Do not narrate a PC's feelings, decisions, or future.

## What Is True Now?

|                    |                                                                           |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Climax result**  | Victory / costly victory / compromise / defeat / escape / capture / other |
| **Objective**      | What was accomplished, prevented, lost, or left unresolved?               |
| **Opposition**     | Defeated, dead, surrendered, escaped, transformed, ascendant, unknown?    |
| **Stakes**         | Who or what was saved, lost, changed, or exposed?                         |
| **Price paid**     | What cannot simply be restored afterward?                                 |
| **Reward**         | What did success, sacrifice, or survival earn?                            |
| **New status quo** | What is materially different when this beat ends?                         |

## Consequences

> [!success] The party
>
> * **Earned:** Reward, leverage, reputation, knowledge, relationship, territory, safety, or opportunity created by their choices.
> * **Paid:** Resources, injuries, obligations, enemies, losses, or compromises that remain meaningful.
> * **Changed access:** What can they now do, reach, request, command, or understand that they could not before?

> [!example] The world
>
> * **People:** Which [[NPC]] or [[Faction]] now acts differently because of the outcome?
> * **Places:** What changed at a relevant [[Place]]?
> * **Power:** Who gained or lost authority, resources, security, information, or influence?
> * **Evidence:** What concrete detail lets the players *see* that their actions mattered?

## Payoffs

| Setup or promise       | Payoff at the table                                              |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Original stake:**    | What resolves it?                                                |
| **Personal stake:**    | Which PC sees the consequence, and how?                          |
| **Promise / bargain:** | Who honors, refuses, or complicates it?                          |
| **Reward:**            | Coin, item, information, favor, title, advancement, access, etc. |
| **Relationship:**      | Which important NPC gets a final beat?                           |

> [!tip] Pay the emotional bill
> If the adventure began because someone needed saving, justice, proof, payment, revenge, freedom, or an answer, show what accomplishing—or failing to accomplish—that goal means before moving on.

## Character Epilogues

Use only as many as the table wants. Ask; do not prescribe.

| Character | Prompt                                                   | What becomes true |
| --------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------- |
| [[PC]]    | What do we see you do before we leave this story behind? |                   |
| [[PC]]    | Who do you seek out—or deliberately avoid—afterward?     |                   |
| [[PC]]    | What tells us this victory or loss mattered to you?      |                   |

> [!question]- Extra prompts
>
> * What unfinished business do you handle before leaving?
> * Who gets the first news of what happened?
> * What do you keep, return, bury, destroy, or give away?
> * What promise do you now intend to keep?
> * What does your character want next?
> * What small image would you like the camera to leave your character on?

## Optional Stinger

> [!warning]- Use only if the ending needs one
> A stinger should **open a door, not erase the ending**.
>
> **Reveal:** One concrete new fact, arrival, image, message, consequence, or threat.
>
> **Why now:** How the completed adventure caused or exposed it.
>
> **Player-facing evidence:** What the characters actually perceive.
>
> **Next vector:** [[Person]], [[Place]], [[Faction]], or question the players could pursue if they choose.
>
> Stop after the reveal.

> [!failure]- Avoid
>
> * Predetermining a PC's choices, emotions, retirement, romance, or future.
> * Making an important victory meaningless just to preserve a villain or sequel.
> * Introducing consequences that do not follow from anything established in play.
> * Turning the resolution into another full adventure.
> * Explaining every unanswered mystery.
> * Dumping several competing hooks when one clear future vector—or simple closure—is stronger.
> * Skipping the reunion, reward, consequence, or other payoff the adventure promised.

> [!info]- Design basis
> *Scripting the Game* treats Resolution as the final beat: a brief afterscene showing what results from the climax, including endings ranging from victory or surrender to escape, capture, a sequel cliffhanger, or a greater threat.  
>
> The template prepares **possible states and consequences rather than predetermined PC actions**, following situation-based prep and the principle that player choices should determine the actual ending. ([The Alexandrian][1])
>
> Its emphasis on immediate consequences, character reaction, rewards, loose ends, and a closing image follows expert advice that epilogues provide closure and make the effects of player choices visible in the continuing world. ([The Alexandrian][2])
