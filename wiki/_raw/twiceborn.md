# Twiceborn

Skin color locks in at birth for nearly every Grung who ever lives. It marks caste, [[grung|gold through green]], and it does not move. A color that shifts, or a Grung carrying more than one color at once, is rare enough to be a landmark event, one an entire island still talks about a generation later.

---

The mechanism underneath caste color is almost embarrassingly simple. Skin toxicity, and skin color with it, comes from diet, the same logic real poison dart frogs run on, and it matches what [[grung|Poisonous Skin]] already says about Grung drawing venom from their own bodies. Eat the right toxins long enough and the color follows. On its own, though, a diet-driven shift never holds. It's unstable, reversible, dangerous to attempt without knowing exactly what you're doing to your own body.

---

What makes a color change permanent is a second, separate thing: an old suppressed rite that seals the diet-driven shift in place. Gold caste kept this rite for itself for generations. Without it, the color drifts, fades, relapses. With it, done properly, done by Gold, it locks forever.

---

Gold's real secret was never "how do you change color," it's how *arbitrary* the whole system is. If caste is just diet plus a sealing rite, it's a monopoly dressed up as sacred birthright and destiny. Gold hides the mechanism. Their legitimacy depends on caste feeling fixed and holy. Grung culture does the actual enforcing: the taboo is real and brutal even though the trick behind it stays mundane.

---

Gold-tier color requires a specific rare toxin that grows in exactly one place: hidden farms deep in [[karath|Karath]]'s interior, maintained by Gold caste alone. Karath already keeps "unknown interior patrols" as an unresolved hook and already runs on controlled toxin dosing for its hatchery captives. The farms aren't a new location. They're the answer nobody's found yet to a secret that island was already keeping.

---

[[ozzeth-the-twiceborn|Ozzeth]] and [[simone-tabarnack|Simone Tabarnack]], both power-hungry, independently found the same suppressed sealing rite and did something no one designed it for: cast it on themselves. The rite's true, original purpose under Gold was reordering *someone else's* caste: a governance and punishment tool built for the ruled. Turned inward, it never finishes. Ozzeth's hands shifted red-and-blue while he cast, already documented and already unexplained on his own page before this idea existed. That flicker is a rite still mid-transfer, forever incomplete.

---

"Twiceborn" isn't Ozzeth's nickname for himself. It's Gold's own official term of censure for anyone caught with the unsealed tell, a brand applied from above, never chosen.

---

Gold cannot move against either of them openly. Punishing Ozzeth and Simone in the open means admitting the rite exists, that it works, that someone can steal caste, the one fact their whole authority depends on nobody knowing. The punishment goes sideways instead, through reputation, not force.

Ozzeth had no reputation standing between him and the brand. "Abomination" stuck immediately, already true and unprompted in his own dungeon-design text. He's called "the mage-abomination" among Simone's lieutenants, a label that existed before this idea gave it a reason.

Simone's record, dutiful, honorable, a real warrior with real loyalty from the ranks, made the same brand impossible to sell. Instead of "abomination," she became a folk hero. Gold settled for begrudging, incomplete acceptance instead: tolerated, never legitimized, always "not pure Gold," not just a slight but a literal fact. She has the sealing rite. She never got near the one farm that grows what gold actually costs.

---

Her own line, already on the page, reads very differently now: *"The caste order is not a choice. It shapes what we are."* Said by someone who chose hers, and has spent every day since defending the idea that she couldn't have.

---

The [[solanges-authority-seal|Authority Seal]] compels any Grung red caste or lower, and nothing else. That fact has sat on the page with no in-fiction reason attached to it. Now it has one: the seal doesn't grant some separate control-magic. It reaches directly into the Grung cultural belief that caste demands obedience, and pulls on it. Non-Grung have no such belief to grab. It's not a cousin to the color-sealing rite, it's the same caste order weaponized and made portable: the very thing that keeps Grung society stable, now usable by whoever wears the ring.

---

Open, not yet decided: does [[jean-claude-tabarnack|Jean-Claude]] know his sister's secret (the one Grung sibling who broke from the clan openly, and the one who broke from it in hiding)? And how visibly different are Ozzeth's and Simone's unsealed tells (his impossible to miss mid-combat, hers controlled enough to pass for years)?

---

Ozzeth didn't just find the rite alongside her. He found it first, went further with it, and became the reason hers stayed stable. Whatever kept it "controlled and hidden for years" (already true on her page) had a source: him, a mentor's hand, whatever it took to stop what happened to him from ever happening to her. Nobody else in the garrison knew, because the maintenance was never garrison work. He was also, before any of this, someone she cared about and looked up to, not just a useful secret-keeper.

---

The [[calveno-sewers-grung-magazines|Calveno]] raid was Simone's own project, not an assignment handed down: a demonstration aimed at the public, at the [[grung|Grung]] who already call her a folk hero, proving she can deliver a raid at a scale and reach none of Gold's own raiders have matched. Numbers, if useful later: roughly a thousand intended, 487 actually taken and shipped ([[nona-black-jaw|Nona]]'s eventual full tally in [[calveno|Calveno]], the 314 the table saw at the end of Session 08 was still climbing when the session closed), 290 landed at [[karath|Karath]], 197 (about 40%) lost to the storm: 48 at sea when two hulls went down, 149 wrecked on [[aruhe|Aruhe]]'s reefs along with their Grung guardsmen, which is exactly the wreck [[aruhe-wreck|The Aruhe Wreck]] already covers. A win loud enough to matter, costly enough to leave a mark.

---

Ozzeth can cast [[sending|Sending]] freely. No item needed, just a man powerful enough to reach across any distance whenever he wants to. When the fight in the primary chamber turns on him, he tries. What reaches Simone is a half-word, or an attempt that fails to land entirely, something that has never happened before. She knows something is wrong hours before she knows what. That gap, dread with no shape yet, sits underneath her biggest public win in the same breath.

---

His death isn't only grief, it's a countdown starting. Whatever he did to keep her unsealed rite stable for years just stopped, with no one left alive who knows how to keep doing it. She has to finish the transformation to true Gold now, meaning she needs the one thing her whole folk-hero legend has never bought her: the toxin from Gold's hidden Karath farms. Grief she doesn't have time for becomes reckless urgency instead: bigger risks against Karath, faster than the plan called for, because caution just became more dangerous than boldness.

---

The fury lands on [[jean-claude-tabarnack|Jean-Claude]] because it's personal, not tactical. His crew killed someone she loved, someone she'd looked up to for years, inside her own operation. Some part of her will never separate that fact from his name again. The existing hunt (played publicly as loose-end elimination, "not for family but as a loose end") keeps that framing on the surface. Underneath it now, it's rage.

---

She may try to pull him back instead of ending him: come home, help me take what Gold's hoarding, rule the Grung together, family instead of exile. What she will never do is hand him the actual secret: that caste is diet plus a rite, not sacred birthright. She wants the throne Gold sits on, not to prove nobody deserves one, which makes the offer itself a trap: it's a crown built on the exact lie his whole arc has been trying to escape.

---

What actually powers Gold caste was never a secret sitting apart from everything else this world already believes about Grung magic. It's the same mechanism the [[solanges-authority-seal|Authority Seal]] already runs on, scaled up to the whole species. The seal doesn't grant control-magic of its own; it reaches into the Grung cultural belief that caste demands obedience and pulls on it. Gold caste is that same trick, permanent and passive, run against everyone at once: Grung culture teaches that only Gold are naturally gifted with strength, speed, wit, and an effortless affinity for magic: shielded and favored by the wild's mana, simply the best of them by nature. That mass belief is the caste order's actual mechanism. What every lower-caste Grung gives up the moment they're born into their color becomes something Gold keeps using for the rest of their lives. Nobody hands it over. Nobody consents. Their own people simply believe it away from them, permanently.

---

Diet and the sealing rite can only ever move the body: skin, toxicity, maybe raw physical capacity if pushed. Neither one touches the belief-field, because the belief-field was never biological. It's the whole species agreeing about who's allowed to have power. [[simone-tabarnack|Simone]] doesn't know this yet. She hasn't so much as seen an [[solanges-authority-seal|Authority Seal]], and she's still working the problem as though diet and rite were the entire mechanism. That's the same lie Gold sells everyone else, and she believes her own version of it.

Her actual gap: what she gets from the [[dravosi-crown|Dravosi Crown]] in trade for the Grung toxin she supplies them was never money or protection. It's a substitute for the 'golden' toxin normally grown only at [[karath|Karath]]'s hidden farms, close enough to fake the diet-shift herself, without ever setting foot near the real thing. The transformation stalled at partial, purple-and-black skin broken by dots and bands of gold, real and visible, never hidden. Gold caste reads it as an impressive soldier's promise, not evidence. Nobody expects their most trusted, most decorated purple to have attempted the one unforgivable theft, so the tell gets seen and explained away by the same reputation that spared her the "abomination" brand Ozzeth couldn't escape.

---

The first real crack in her own ignorance is already sitting in continuity, not waiting on a future session to happen. Gold disparaged the [[calveno-sewers-grung-magazines|Calveno]] raid, but common Grung (the only audience whose belief actually feeds the field) celebrated it as daring and brilliant, real proof. That's the first belief she's ever earned, and it's already been paying into her, off-screen, in the gap since the raid. She's started noticing something new in her own body. Faster. Sharper. Half a step ahead of what her training alone explains. Right now she has no name for it beyond being unusually good with a spear. Any time she reappears, she's allowed to fight a little better than the numbers say and never explain why.

---

Answered, against the open question above. Jean-Claude knows none of it. He fled before any of this started. The rite, the Crown deal, the partial gold: everything happened in his absence. The sister in his memory is exactly the one who turned him and [[pell|Pell]] in: devoted, certain, unchanged. His silence about her toxin (the [[saltwright|Saltwright]] canister, the twenty vials in the [[uncertainty|Surety]]'s cargo, the Calveno sewer stock) has only ever protected his own past, not hers. He doesn't know there's anything larger underneath it worth protecting. When that gap finally closes, it isn't "my sister did something bad." It's discovering that the person he's spent years being furious at for her unbending certainty quietly stopped being certain the moment he wasn't there to see it.

---

Once she does understand what she actually needs (not a finished rite, a species' belief), the endgame move is recruitment, not execution. She goes to her brother directly: come home, rule the Grung together, take what Gold's been hoarding from them. It's the same offer already on this page, now with a real mechanism underneath it instead of just family feeling. If he refuses, which his own arc makes likely, she doesn't kill him. She captures him. He stopped being a loose end the moment she needed him as a piece, and a piece she needs doesn't get left hunted-but-free.
