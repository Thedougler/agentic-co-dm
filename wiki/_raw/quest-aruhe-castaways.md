# Half the Fleet

*Draft fiction, not canon. Canon lives on the wiki. Derived pages: [[sandro|Sandro]], [[nino|Nino]], [[aruhe-wreck|The Aruhe Wreck]], [[aruhe|Aruhe]], [[warren-aruhe-survey|Warren: Aruhe Survey]].*

The rope had worn a groove into Sandro's wrist days ago, deep enough that
he had stopped testing it by the second night out of [[calveno|Calveno]]. The
green-caste boy who checked the knots twice a watch was gentler about it
than the others.
Somewhere in the dark he decided that survival meant he noticed things
like that and said nothing about them, and put them away where he put
everything else he meant to spend later.

The [[vethka|Vethka]] rode low with the weight of them, eleven roped along the
outrigger strut, and the [[grung-npc-statblock|Grung]] crew worked the sail and the paddles in a
rhythm he had learned by the second day without meaning to. The sky went
the color of a bruise on the morning the rhythm changed. Shorter. More of
them, calling faster than the paddles could answer.

The wind found the fleet before the rain did, one long shove out of the
east that laid the Vethka over so far the outrigger cleared the water
entirely. Everyone aboard, captive and captor both, grabbed for whatever
was nearest with the same two hands.

The boat came down flat, hard enough to drive the breath out of him. By
the time his eyes cleared, the boat that had run behind them all morning,
close enough that he had learned three of the faces on it, stood thirty
feet in the air, end over end, coming apart as it went. What reached the
water reached it in pieces.

The thought arrived whole and unhurried: something out there is enjoying
this.

A wall of green water took the mast off at the deck. Gone in a breath.
The green-caste boy's hand closed around his wrist where the rope was,
held for one full second, and then closed around nothing at all. Salt
drove down his throat like a fist forcing its way in. The world turned
over so many times that up and down became a guess, made and unmade and
made again, and then the guessing stopped mattering. His body
carried on without him, arms still pulling, lungs still finding air
between the rolls, long after his mind had gone somewhere else to hide.

---

He came up choking against something that gave under his hands. It took
the better part of a minute to understand what he was holding: a plank,
one of the Vethka's own outrigger struts, and his fingers had decided for
him that they were never letting go of it again. The rain had stopped at
some point without him catching either end of it.

Three of them rode the plank in by the time the current stopped fighting
them for it: him, Tomo, old enough to be somebody's grandfather with a
rope burn scored into the skin of his throat where he'd freed himself of
whatever had held him, and Nino, a dyer's apprentice out of the Beffa
quarter. Nino had ridden three ropes down from Sandro the whole
crossing, and had not said one full sentence since the wave took the
mast.

Land came up grey and low, wrapped in a mist that lay on the water in one
flat sheet and did not move when the wind moved. They swam. Tomo hauled on the plank
one-armed, the other gone useless somewhere in the crossing, and when
Sandro's feet finally found the sand, he stood there ankle-deep with his
legs shaking under him, and the sand was the first solid thing he had
touched in longer than she could make herself count.

Two of the [[grung|Grung]] had come ashore ahead of them. Both dead. He gave the
first a wide arc from a distance, face down at the tideline, out of habit
more than any grief he could locate in himself. The second he nearly
walked into, twenty feet up the beach in the shadow of the treeline.

It lay on its back with its ribs standing open and held apart, and out of
the cavity and out of the mouth came a pale green shoot, one from each,
both of them a foot high and already putting out leaves. The sand around
it lay smooth for a body's length in every direction. Whatever had opened
it had done so where it lay, in the hours since the storm, and had used
nothing that leaves a print.

His legs went and he sat down hard in the wet sand with his stomach
turning over. He got up. He did not tell Tomo or Nino. Whatever the
island was, it had made up its mind about the Grung long before the storm
brought him here, and standing over that one he could not make himself
sorry.

---

The beach curved around a clearing thick with a low, healthy bush, fat
green fruit hanging heavy on every branch, low enough to reach, every one
of them whole and unmarked. Past that, the ground climbed toward a
treeline that stood as close and as dark at the top of the slope as it did
at the bottom. Somewhere behind it, fresh water ran. After two days of salt
and nothing at all, the sound of it alone put tears in Nino's eyes that
had nothing to do with grief. 

Tomo found the bush on the third morning. The fish they'd managed with a
bent hook and a length of shoelace had run thin, and his hands had been
shaking for two days by then, and it had stopped being the cold. He ate
four of the fruit standing up, fast, without once turning one over in his
hand. When Sandro found him at it and said his name once, sharp as he
could make it, Tomo held his eyes and took the next bite while he watched.

Nothing happened that hour, or through the whole grey afternoon. He slept
that night between them same as every night, backs to the
Vethka's shattered hull for a windbreak, and somewhere in the dark Sandro
let himself believe, for that one night, that the fruit had only been
fruit.

In the morning his blanket lay flat and empty. Long scrapes ran through
the dirt from where he'd slept to the treeline, four of them, evenly
spaced, the width of a man's ankle apart. His heels, then, and his
fingernails. Sandro crouched over the start of them and went looking for
what had made them. A cat carries. A boar churns the ground and leaves a
hoof in it. What took him kept to two feet, backward, both his ankles in
its hands, in no hurry at all. It set nothing else down on that sand: no
print, no tail-drag, no knee. He had
worked a fish market for eleven years, and nothing he had gutted or
hauled or heard about walked backward carrying a grown man.

Nino did not stop shaking for the rest of that day. Sandro walked the
length of the scrapes exactly once, far enough to know for certain and not
one step farther. Then he went back to the hull and never walked that
ground again as long as they stayed on the island.

---

They had been keeping the days by notches cut into the same strut Sandro
had come ashore holding, until he lost the count somewhere past twenty and
stopped trying to find it again. What he had instead of a count was a
rule: watch what kills the person beside you, and never do that yourself.

They ate fish, taken from shallows where the water was still only water,
open sky and salt and nothing under it that had ever dragged anyone
anywhere. They never crossed the line where the tideline moss began. They
never touched the fruit, or the flowers on the upper slope that turned
their bright heads to follow whoever walked past them, or the grass that
leaned all one direction when no wind was blowing at all. Nino had gone
almost entirely silent by then, but he had also stopped needing telling
twice about any of it.

They lived in the Vethka's broken hull because it still kept the weather
off them, and because the only way out of it led toward the treeline.
Neither of them had found a reason to go that way worth setting against
four scrapes in the dirt. The mist came onto the beach most mornings and
burned away by midday, then came back every evening at the same hour,
straight into the offshore wind.

Sandro was working the last of the shoelace back through the bent hook,
not watching the water, when Nino's hand closed hard on his arm and
would not let go.

He followed the line of Nino's stare out past the reef break to a
sail. Small and pale, it cut steady against the current, aimed at the one
gap in the rocks that every chart Sandro had ever seen left blank.

They stood in the wreck of the boat that had carried them here, stone
still and silent, and watched it come.
