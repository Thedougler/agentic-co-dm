The chain's western end holds its biggest land. Forested islands sit close together. A lookout loses sight of one only after the next comes into view. The bottom shows through the water's color. Between the islands the shallow shelf runs pale green over sand and darker where grass and coral take over. West of the last island the shelf drops away, and the open sea begins.

Traffic is the point here. [[kalowe|Kalowe]] takes any hull that pays its harbour fee, so a Crown packet, a fishing family and something with a fresh coat of paint over an old name all tie up on the same night. The channels are wide enough for a frigate. They are also shallow enough that any frigate captain hires a pilot, and everyone here has learned to profit from that.

> [!read-aloud]
> The horizon thickens into land. First one green ridge, then a second behind it, then a dozen more stacked back into haze until you cannot tell island from island.
>
> The water under the keel turns from blue to pale green in the space of a boat length, and the hull's wake hisses low across the shallows.
>
> Smoke and hot pitch tang the air off the nearest shore. Somewhere ahead a caulking hammer keeps up a steady knock, and a pilot's skiff is already putting out from the beach to meet you.
>
> *(to the table)* Do you fly a flag going in, or nothing at all?

## Routes & Access

The western approaches are the easiest water in the Midchain, and the busiest. A ship coming off the open sea sees the outer islands early. That early warning is why the free port grew here instead of deeper in the chain.

Pilots work out of every beach with a landing. Their price is local and negotiable. Refusing one is legal and cheap, and that choice causes most wrecks in these waters. Charts of the western channels are broadly right, a claim the rest of the chain cannot make. But the sandbars move with the season, and no chart tracks them.

Run the crossing per `vault/refs/vault/location/references/wilderness-travel-and-exploration.md` and `vault/refs/gameplay-toolbox.md` § Travel Pace.

## Hazards

Shifting sandbars wait on every approach. Mangrove channels close behind a boat at low water, and dire crocodiles hunt the root mats. Coral heads sit a hand's breadth under a calm surface, ready to gut a hull that cuts a corner.

The political hazards are just as patient. A Crown cutter works the outer islands. Press gangs treat an unregistered crew as salvage worth taking. Debt follows a hull from port to port long after the crew spends the coin that caused it.

Nothing here kills a party quickly. That slowness is exactly how a party ends up committed to it.

## Hooks

- [[kalowe|Kalowe]]'s dry dock takes work no registered yard will touch, and remembers who paid late.
- A pilot family on the western approaches will name a channel for the right favour, and name the wrong ship to the Crown for a better one.
- The sandbars moved this season and someone is still selling last season's chart.
