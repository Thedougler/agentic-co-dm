# Lizardfolk Culture

*[[lizardfolk|Lizardfolk]] settlement, hull-crew work, ritual combat, and naming customs across [[shattered-sea|the Shattered Sea]], among the [[peoples-of-the-shattered-sea|Peoples of the Shattered Sea]].*

## The Culture

In [[campaign-overview|the Shattered Sea]], Lizardfolk live in the tidal margins, on mangrove coasts, river deltas, and salt-and-fresh flats. Their homes look temporary to outsiders, but most have stood for generations. Lizardfolk build low in the water, and when the tide pattern shifts, they simply move. The old home already did its job.

At sea, Lizardfolk crew as hull-workers, salvagers, and repair hands. They can hold their breath for a quarter-hour. They work in cold water without slowing down. Their natural armor means fewer wounds from sharp keels and broken planks. A ship with a Lizardfolk crew needs less time in dry dock. [[bastian-ookage|Bastian Ookage]] is captain of the *[[loud-argument|Loud Argument]]* in [[fisks-fleet|Fisk's Fleet]]. He came up through salvage work before Fisk gave him a command.

Lizardfolk pragmatism is real, but calling it cold misses the point. A Lizardfolk parent dies for a clutch without hesitation. Warm-blooded folk mistake that speed for a lack of feeling. A Lizardfolk who sees a hurt ally does not feel worry and then act. They skip that first step. Help still arrives at full speed, even if the face stays still.

Lizardfolk who spend years among warm-blooded crews grow what outsiders call emotional range. Lizardfolk cannot say for sure whether this comes from habit or real feeling, and most stop asking. A Lizardfolk who laughs at a friend's joke after ten years does not know if the laugh is mimicry or joy. Either way, the laugh is real.

Lizardfolk life follows the tide, not the sun. Low tide brings building, foraging, and craft work. High tide brings rest, egg-tending, and story-telling. A visitor who arrives at high tide finds a quiet village, while one who arrives at low tide finds work that would shame most shipyards.

Lizardfolk waste nothing. Bone becomes tools. Hide becomes armor. Shell becomes coin. A Lizardfolk artisan makes work that looks rough by Human taste but holds up better by every other measure. A rare Lizardfolk who learns fine craft from another culture makes something that unsettles: perfect detail applied with a skill that strips the warmth out of the art.

Lizardfolk stay solitary outside their own home. A stranger's ground is just a fact to note, until proven otherwise. Inside a settlement, that same instinct turns into rivalry. Households, crews, and tidal flats all press their own claims.

The Lizardfolk of the [[midchain|Midchain]] keep their own regional custom, apart from the tidal-margin homes above. Warm-blooded traders passing through call it vaguely like their own East. The Midchain Lizardfolk neither claim nor deny the comparison. Their custom favors formal manners, small gestures, and a use-name picked for its sound instead of copied whole from a host culture.

## Customs & Traditions

A ritual bout settles rivalry tension before it turns into a feud. The fight draws a crowd and ends fast. The loser's grudge ends the moment the bout does. The custom spread well. At sea, Lizardfolk crews turn any spat, real or made up, into a reason for a bout on deck. Warm-blooded crewmates who once found this odd now place bets on it.

Lizardfolk ritual combat found a wider stage in [[the-kessen|The Kessen]], a standing fight league open to every Midchain people, not Lizardfolk alone. The league runs on the tide, not the season, with bouts clustering at low tide when a town's work pauses anyway. Crews and households enter as teams, not lone fighters. A loss lands on the full team until the next round, never on one fighter alone. Betting runs open and unashamed. Lizardfolk entrants tend to be the toughest draw in the bracket. [[bastian-ookage|Bastian Ookage]] carries this custom into Fisk's Fleet. His crew still remembers him running the book on deck.

## Naming

Lizardfolk true-names in their own tongue are plain and useful, names like *Holds-Still, Sees-Under-Water, Bites-Twice.* Among other peoples, Lizardfolk take a use-name instead, one warm-blooded tongues can say, often borrowed from the culture around them. The use-name is not a mask; it counts as a tool, and Lizardfolk see no gap between a useful tool and a real one.

Lizardfolk place-names in Common come from Malay and Austronesian roots, with open syllables, soft consonants, and words tied to water, names like *Tanjung, Teluk, Rawa, Pasang.* This holds for the tidal-margin homes, but the Midchain custom differs: its use-names favor short, clipped sounds that strike warm-blooded ears as vaguely East Asian, names like *Ookage, Ren, Sato*, reflecting the region's own custom instead of the tidal-margin one above.
