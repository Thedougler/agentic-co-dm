# Session 12 — The Rule-Bound Route

> [!abstract] At a Glance
> **Stakes.** Reach the memorial line without making a living claim, losing Jean-Claude, or giving Skarn a clean opening.
> **Contest.** The party must travel under rules it did not write while deciding whether those rules preserve judgment or replace it.
> **Change.** The group reaches the memorial approach together, loses time or resources, changes its formation, or abandons the route.

> [!narration] Initial Narration
> Oren's route is not a road. It is a chain of small decisions through living green: a fallen branch used as a marker, a strip of bare earth between roots, and fruit left where it dropped instead of pulled from a stem. The party can see the darker forest beyond the last rise, but the easiest-looking path crosses a living tangle that bends toward every hand that comes near it.

## Open on Action

Oren explains three rules before the party moves: take what the island has already given, do not claim living things, and leave protected places whole. Celia adds that the rules are survival practice, not worship. The party's carried Ghost Plums, Jean-Claude's body, the Spinner, and the urgency created by Skarn all create reasons to bend them.

## Run the Beat

1. **Name the pressure.** The route is passable, but the most direct choices are also the choices most likely to become claims.
2. **Let them engage.** The party can move slowly, hand off fruit and Jean-Claude, use fallen material for markers, take a longer dry edge, or reject Oren's guidance.
3. **Resolve the contest.** Resolve each meaningful risk with the fiction first. Use **Wisdom (Survival) — `DC 14`** when the party's route choice is genuinely uncertain. **Success:** preserve position and time. **Failure:** lose time, separate a pack, or expose a living claim without blocking progress.
4. **Hand back the choice.** The party decides whether to continue toward the memorial, turn back, or create its own route with a visible cost.

## Opposition

The opposition is Aruhe's response to living claims, the party's urgency, and [[talon-skarn]]'s continuing pressure. Oren does not command the party. Celia does not sanctify the route. Both can be overruled, and both explain the cost they see.

## Pressure

| Pressure | Tell | Consequence |
| -------- | ---- | ----------- |
| **Carried fruit** | Fallen fruit is harmless; a living stem tightens toward a hand. | A picked fruit creates an Aruhe claim and changes the next approach. |
| **Jean-Claude** | Moving him through the narrowest edge slows the group and makes every pause visible. | The party must choose between speed, care, and leaving someone behind. |
| **Spinner** | The object keeps Skarn's attention even when the route looks safe. | Crissdalynn can be protected by formation, concealment, or distance, each with a cost. |
| **Trust** | Oren stops before every boundary and answers questions instead of pretending certainty. | The party can follow, test, replace, or reject his judgment. |

## Resolution

- **If the party follows the route:** It reaches the memorial approach with a coherent formation and a clear understanding of the rules' cost.
- **If the party violates a rule:** Aruhe raises pressure around the claim, but the party retains choices about retreat, repair, or pressing forward.
- **If the party abandons Oren:** He returns toward the camp or follows at a distance only if the party has not made a claim that endangers the survivors. The party can still reach the approach by its own route.
- **If the party turns back:** The memorial remains unexplored, and Skarn's second attempt can occur at a different vulnerable moment rather than being forced here.
