## Current State

The Kessen remains open to every people of the [[Midchain]]. The eight standing teams below matter to the table, but they are a roster rather than a governing body. The league's common name began as Vort Kessen's ring-title after his third championship and stuck harder than his birth surname.

## Active Agenda

**Goal.** Keep the next tide-calendar cycle contested by crews and households from across the Midchain.

**Why.** The Kessen's meaning depends on shared participation and the tribal weight of a team loss.

**Planned action.** Run the next bouts, preserve the eight-team roster, and let rival styles and betting keep the league visible.

**Needs.** Teams, a ring or bout site, a tide calendar, officials, and spectators willing to carry the league's reputation.

**Opposition.** Any force that prevents teams from entering or turns the ritual into private violence.

**Next signal.** A team arrives at a new bout site, a tide date is announced, or betting shifts around an upset.

**Player opening.** The party can enter as a team, back a roster, investigate a disputed bout, or use the public league to build a relationship.

**If completed.** The Kessen remains a shared Midchain institution rather than a legend owned by one champion.

### Chain of Action

**Who decides:** The unnamed league officials schedule bouts and maintain the tide-calendar cycle.

**Who interprets those decisions:** Team captains choose rosters, tactics, and how to carry their tribe's reputation.

**Who actually carries them out:** Fighters enter the ring while crews, households, and spectators enforce the social weight of the result.

**If leadership disappears.** The teams may still fight, but the cross-Midchain league loses the coordination that makes it an institution.

- **Visible sign.** A team travels with its home colors and a public bout reputation.
- **Rumor.** The name Kessen belonged to Vort before it belonged to the league.

## History

The name began as Vort Kessen's ring-title after his third league championship. It became the common name for the standing cross-Midchain league, while Vort later retired to [[The Mud Pits]].
