## Current State

The Glass Debt operates where Midchain back routes open toward the [[central-strait]], [[tail|the Tail]], and the [[drowned-maw]] western approaches. Smaller pirate ships take prizes; the Glass Debt changes how convoys behave. Its hull began as a Crown-built heavy escort, and every formal mark has been made ambiguous rather than erased.

## Active Agenda

**Goal.** Disrupt convoy routes and take prizes without exposing the ship to a fight it does not need.

**Why.** Its size and upkeep make casual raiding too expensive; fear and rerouting are more valuable than every individual cargo.

**Planned action.** Blockade a route, force convoys to change course, and strike only when a prize cannot answer the frigate's guns.

**Needs.** A crew, supplies, good wind, and routes that open toward the Central Strait and Drowned Maw approaches.

**Opposition.** The [[dravosi-crown]] and any convoy that can bring enough force to make the frigate's upkeep or escape impossible.

**Next signal.** Convoys reroute, smaller pirate ships scout ahead, or a blue-white lantern appears where no frigate should be.

**Player opening.** The party can evade it, disrupt its route, or treat its presence as a strategic threat rather than attempt a direct fight.

**If completed.** Convoys alter their routes and the Glass Debt becomes the controlling danger on the affected water.

## People & Structure

No captain, officers, or crew are named in the source. The page describes the ship and operating pattern, not a command structure.

### Chain of Action

**Who decides:** The unnamed command crew chooses routes and convoy targets.

**Who interprets those orders:** Officers and gunnery crews turn the target into a blockade or strike.

**Who actually carries them out:** The frigate's crew operates the guns, rigging, and boarding actions.

**If leadership disappears.** The ship's crew may retain the vessel, but the source establishes no successor or internal command dispute.
