> [!narration] Ferran Balt
> The man behind the counter is compact and precise, moving between the shelves and the counter without pause, adjusting stock an inch here, a cord there, never still. Pale for a village that lives outside. Younger-looking than you'd expect; in a village where the sea ages everyone, that smoothness draws a second look. His right hand carries ink past the knuckle, not the merchant's smudge but the deep-set stain of someone who writes every day, ground into the outer edge. The ledger on the counter is open. When you enter, he doesn't close it. He angles it. Near the far end, a notch cuts into the inside edge of the counter, freshly carved, wood pale at its center, the surface around it worn smooth, sanded down and begun again. Nobody has asked him about it. His hand moves the ledger angle as you approach, the motion automatic. His face is already forward, already polite.
