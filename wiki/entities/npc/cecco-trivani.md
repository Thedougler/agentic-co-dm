# Cecco Trivani

# Cecco Trivani

**Wants:** to get through one complete sung line, start to finish. He has never made it past three syllables, and something in the shop is always one heartbeat from cutting across him again.

**Quote:** "Welcome to Il-" (the door bell)

> [!narration] Narration
> A young man works down a shelf of wind-chime birds, tallest to shortest, humming low while he sets each one straight. Sandy hair, combed wet and parted dead center, like he dressed for a recital nobody scheduled. He's pinned his cuffs back with mismatched buttons, saved from a set he's still hunting the matches for. He hears the door and turns fast. He plants his feet shoulder-width and laces his fingers at his sternum, the way a soloist waits for the downbeat. His chest rises. He rocks up onto his toes with the breath and opens his mouth on the first syllable.

**Roleplay Concept:** a Broadway understudy who never once goes on, plus a shop apprentice who restocks shelves in eighth notes.

**Opening move:** restocking the chime-birds, breath already drawn · Cecco speaks first · "Welcome to Il-" The door bell cuts him off mid-word, or [[Serena]] calls a price across the floor first. He is sure the next one is finally the one. He goes right back to restocking, calm as ever.

**Lore Sheet:**

- Apprenticed at [[Il Preludio]] under [[Fioravante "Fio" Selmerin]], learning the stock the same way he learns everything: in the gaps between interruptions.
- He is the only one of the shop's four who consciously wants to sing. The others carry the same affliction and never reach for it.
- Keeps a private tally, never spoken aloud, of how many syllables he's ever gotten out in a row. Best count: three.
- Never bitter about the timing. Certain, every time, that this evening is finally the one.

**Voice & Delivery:** earnest, always sounding one breath from starting something bigger. Speaks in short, complete bursts, like he's leaving room in the sentence for a line that never arrives. Laces his fingers at his sternum and rises onto his toes just before he means to speak. Emotional default: patient, certain his moment is coming later tonight. It never does.
