### When posture changes

Little surprises Old Faas. He gives short, clear answers without raising his voice, and danger does not make him hesitate because he cannot feel fear after falling from a hay loft at seven. This is not a death wish: he still works carefully, but he will not recognize fear as the signal that stops another sailor. He tells three different stories about losing his original legs—a festival bull near the Bridge, a shaky scaffold at the Beffa, and a current under the [[Mercatura]] dock—with equal certainty. Do not confirm or deny any account; confronted with all three, he says, “Ja. A busy week.” He elaborates once if asked but does not realize others find the pattern strange.
```
````

During the Murrat expedition, Old Faas described the outcome as plainly as a storm: *“He took us to Murrat. Wanted a live one. Went ashore with six crew. The surgeon saw four of us when we came back.”* He was not one of the four who needed [[alys-kuiper]]'s care. He returned from shore leave in [[calven-and-calveno]] with two well-fitted peg legs, shorter and less steady standing still but faster on the rigging than before.

His true cause of injury remains unknown, even to the DM. He never mentioned the absence of fear to a captain. The roleplay note is based on Scruffy the Janitor: literally fearless, inexplicably immortal sailor with flat affect and sparse speech.

No combat stats are recorded in the source.

### Voice

Old Faas uses short, clear sentences with no raised voice. He describes terrible events as ordinary work, elaborates once when asked, and lets a pause carry whatever others would dramatize. **Ask:** “He took us to Murrat. Wanted a live one.” **Refusal:** “No. That is not a story for the deck.” **Under pressure:** “Ja. A busy week.”
