# Rael Corven

![[rael-corven-banner.webp]]

Flag Captain of the *[[red-lady]]*, [[fisks-fleet]] flagship. Deceased, his soul is inside [[delmar-fisk]].

Sea-worn and solid, close-cropped dark hair going iron-grey at the temples. His clothes are plain and properly fitted. A jaw set somewhere between resolve and fatigue defines his face, along with eyes that have stopped performing patience. His hands show the sorcerer's work, with faint luminescence at the knuckles. He speaks rarely, and everything lands like a ruling.

Rael captained the *Red Lady*, the flagship, and was a sorcerer. He was Fisk's flag captain and the one closest to the Admiral operationally. They met as young crewmen on the [[Midchain]]. Fisk read people. Rael read conditions. Together, they missed little.

He was the only captain who entered [[Umberlee]]'s shrine with Fisk, finding the Pearl by [[magic-in-the-shattered-sea|Detect Magic]], and stood at Fisk's side when it ended. He drowned when Umberlee closed the water over all five ships. His soul passed into Delmar. Rael's voice is the loudest of the five captains inside Fisk because he was there, knows what they took, and saw it last before the water closed in.

His father was a merchant sailor, precise and careful. His philosophy was simple: prepare for the worst, work toward the best. The Navy sank his ship on false grounds. They called the cargo pirate salvage. Rael learned what had happened and what they took. He never forgave the Navy. He doesn't separate the officers from the institution.

His father's philosophy survived him. Rael learned it young through training rotations and victualling plans, stocking the magazine against contingencies he hoped never to meet. The *Red Lady* ran the way it ran because Rael treated preparation as a moral position.

**When Active in Delmar.** Rael takes control most frequently when an ally drops to 0 HP in combat. He manifests with steady, commanding presence that feels both maternal and hard. He stops performing and starts moving people with authority, warmth present but no longer in the lead. His forefront skills are intimidation and history. An environmental trigger (a woman in a dark red dress) causes him to go still and scan the face searching for recognition he will not find. The charm drops. He was loudest of the five because he was inside the shrine with Fisk when the fleet sank.

**Sorcery**
**Tradition.** Arcane. **School.** Divination. **Spell analogue(s).** *Detect Magic*.
**Detect Magic.** [[magic-in-the-shattered-sea|The spell]] reads as Divination. A faint luminescence traces the knuckles and fingertips, cooler than candlelight.
**Counterspell.** [[magic-in-the-shattered-sea|The spell]] applies.

**Soul Binding**
**Tradition.** Divine. **School.** Necromancy. **Spell analogue(s).** *Soul Cage*. **Homebrew element.** The deity binds the captains' souls into a living vessel, and stress brings them forward rather than command.
**Detect Magic.** [[magic-in-the-shattered-sea|The spell]] reads as Necromancy. A deep cold is present behind Delmar's eyes, as though a second presence looks out from the same face.
**Counterspell.** A deity imposes the binding rather than an active caster, so [[magic-in-the-shattered-sea|the spell]] cannot affect it.

The Cloak of Useful Things went down with the *Red Lady* at the [[Shelfworks]] eastern shelf descent, alongside the [[pearl-of-souls]]. Potentially recoverable as part of a deeper Shelfworks dive.

## Voice

My name is [[rael-corven]]. Flag Captain of the Red Lady.

I keep the ledger. I always have. The magazine holds six barrels of salt beef, six of biscuit, and six of black powder. I count them twice before every crossing.

I have known Delmar longer than anyone else still alive. We were crewmen together on someone else's ship before either of us commanded anything. He read people. I read conditions. Between the two of us, little got missed.

Slow ships sailed south, seeking safer straits. Say that clean at the end of a long watch and I'll believe you're not tired yet.

The sealed magazine contains six barrels, six spare flints, and six feet of slow match, all coiled and dry. That's the whole magazine.

Everyone has value, whatever their station. That was never a speech. That was just how I ran a ship.

I don't raise my voice. I have never needed to.

*(calm)*

Get her off the line now. You two, on the count. I'll cover.

*(commanding)*

Everyone back a step. That's not a threat yet. It's about to be one.

*(terrified)*

…No. That woman is not her, and she cannot be here.

*(delighted)*

Someone found the flagon again. Good. I was starting to forget what steady felt like from the outside.

*(sarcastic)*

One skull carries five captains, and somehow I'm the one everyone quotes at parties.

*(furious)*

The Navy signed the order that drowned my father. I don't split hairs between the hand and the flag it flew under.

*(whispered)*

There's a woman in a dark red dress I go looking for in every crowd I pass through. I have never once found her.

*(held tone)*

We stock the magazine for the crossing we hope never happens. That's not caution. That's arithmetic.

*(shouted)*

BACK. NOW.

*(persuasive)*

The count is sound. I have never once come up short, and I am not starting tonight.

*(rapid)*

Six barrels, six flints, six feet of match, one crossing, no room for a seventh mistake, and I still can't tell you why anyone finds that amusing.

*(wry)*

My hands glow at the knuckles when I channel. That's the only part of me that ever admitted to feeling anything.

*(earnest)*

I'm not humorless. I decided, long ago, that a joke costs the same breath as a warning, but only the warning keeps a crew alive. Counting the magazine twice was never fear. Refusing to forgive an institution that drowned a good man was never bitterness. It was arithmetic each time, and I'd make the same trade again tonight.

*(dry)*

Steady's not a mood. It's a discipline. Somehow that's the part everyone finds funniest of all.

*(quiet)*

That's me. Rael Corven, Flag Captain of the Red Lady, keeper of every tally that never mattered again, five faces behind one man's eyes, and still, after all this, the driest man on any ship I ever sailed.
