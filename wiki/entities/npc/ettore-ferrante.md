## At a Glance

Ettore is the man the others will not leave behind: he cannot walk, and getting him out means carrying him.

- **Role.** A wreck survivor on the ledge under the smoking skylight in the [[lava-tubes]].
- **Nature.** A heavy, careful Calveno bridge-toll clerk, polite and endlessly sorry.
- **Wants.** Not to be left behind, and to see his older brother [[carlo-ferrante|Carlo Ferrante]] again.
- **Home.** The lava-tube ledge. Before that, one of the four mats at [[spoke-ring]].
- **Condition.** His left shin broke when they slid down into the tube, running from a terror-bird. He has 4 HP, Speed 0, and cannot stand.

## At the Table

- **First meeting.** He greets the party from where he sits and apologizes before anything else: for the smell, the leg, his weight. He names the others: his son Luca, Piero the net-mender, Gianni the cooper. Asked why they are underground, he points up: the birds.
- **Opens up when.** Anyone promises to carry him. After that he stops apologizing.
- **Shuts down when.** Anyone mentions the woman in the woods. He looks north-east, the way Carlo went, and stops talking.
- **Priority.** Luca. He will send the party away with Luca rather than slow them down.
- **Shares.** That Carlo walked toward the voice from Spoke Ring nineteen days ago, and he thinks Carlo is dead. He keeps back how much the leg hurts, and he goes grey and silent when moved.
- **Voice.** Exact, polite, and sorry, the way a toll clerk talks. He says "it is arithmetic" when he means it is hopeless.
- **Lines.** "If you carry me, I will not complain. I swear it." · "No, take the boy first. I can wait." · "Leave me. It is arithmetic. I am too heavy."
> [!narration] First meeting
> On a shelf of black rock, a heavy man lies propped against the wall with his leg splinted straight out in front of him. He pushes himself up on his arms, sags back, and lifts a hand in apology. "Forgive me. I would stand, but the leg has other opinions."

## Secrets

- **He blames himself.** He was the slowest when the bird charged, and he is the reason the other three cannot climb out. He believes the whole group would have reached the coast by now without him. It is not true: without the party, they would never have left the ledge.
