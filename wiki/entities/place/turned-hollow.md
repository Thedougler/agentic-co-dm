# Turned Hollow

![[turned-hollow-overview.png|Turned Hollow overview: the dig pits around the wrong tree]]

## Overview
> [!narration] Narration
> A bowl of dug earth breaks the forest roof, about seventy paces from rim to rim and three stories deep at its middle, bare to the sky while the trees stand dark all around its edge. Its floor is nothing but digging: hundreds of pits, some knee-deep and some deeper than a tall man, each with its heap of spoil beside it, and the turned soil between them stays bare, with no grass taking hold. The pits crowd tighter and cut deeper toward the center, where one great fig rises out of the deepest hole, twice as tall as the bowl is deep, its trunk a hollow cage of fused roots inside a skirt of hanging roots a few long strides wide. Gold plates and pale bones are grown into its bark, and when air moves across the bowl the hanging roots sway and the plates tick against one another. A Grung skeleton hangs whole in that skirt at shoulder height, roots looped around its waist and one wrist, and at the bottom of the central pit, cut root ends have healed over into dark knots.
>
> Dead Grung lie in and beside the pits in rough rings. At the rim they have sunk into the soil until only shell plates and belt buckles show; toward the middle they lie half-buried; around the fig they still hold their shape under torn packs, with rust-red root-saws and short spades on the ground beside them. Torn plates with gold script along their edges lie in the dirt of every ring, and a long crimson feather lies across a spoil heap near the middle. The air smells of turned earth and wet iron, and the only sound is the creak of the fig. The packed dirt of a forest aisle ends at the south rim. At the north rim the ground climbs into dark trees, where a guava leans its branches over the path, one heavy pink-gold fruit hanging at arm's height on its stem and another lying split and browning on the roots below. Two leafless ropes hang among the leafy vines in those trees, and past the north rim, a long stone's throw through the trunks, a ring of black flowers shows on the forest floor.

## At a Glance

Turned Hollow is a canopy break in [[the-quiet]] of [[aruhe]], a bowl about 200 feet from rim to rim and 30 feet deep at the center, dug over and over by Grung expeditions until the whole floor is pits and spoil. It sits between [[star-cut]], about an hour south along the north star-cut aisle, and [[memorial-grove]], whose black-flower ring shows through the trunks about 100 feet past the north rim. Star Cut is a straight packed aisle aimed at a slit of stars, and Memorial Grove is a round clearing whose soil is bound and whose graves no root crosses; Turned Hollow is the round clearing between them whose ground everyone dug and nobody left.

**The DM truth.** The newest Gold-caste orders on [[two-grave-orders]] told Grung expeditions to find and destroy two graves "beneath the memorial tree." Generation after generation of expeditions came north up the star-cut aisle, reached this Hollow first, took its great [[strangler-fig]] for the memorial tree, and dug beneath it. Digging living ground and sawing through the fig's roots are living claims under [[taking-on-aruhe]], so the fig and the island's response killed the diggers where they dug. Each replacement party then dug under the same wrong tree. The fig fed and grew, which is why Grung bones and gold [[grung-authority-seal]] plates are grown into its bark. No grave lies under the fig; the true memorial tree stands in [[memorial-grove]] just past the north rim.

**Rule of the place.** Inside the fig's 20-foot root curtain, movement draws the roots and stillness only delays them. The fig lashes at whatever moves inside the curtain. A creature it already holds that stays still is not squeezed further, but when nothing inside the curtain has moved since the fig's last turn, the fig draws one held creature into its hollow trunk. The limit is that stillness buys time, not safety. Players can test it before they depend on it: a rolled stone, a pack swung on a rope, or any moving object tossed into the curtain gets seized, and the whole skeleton hanging in the roots shows what happens to prey that waited.

**Pressure and trajectory.** If nobody intervenes, the Hollow keeps doing what it has always done: any party still following the two-grave orders reaches this bowl first, digs under the fig, and adds a new innermost ring. Skipping the Hollow by circling through the Quiet wood costs the clearest evidence of the order trail and the plain view of the Grove from the north rim.

## If the party

- **Arrive from the south.** The packed dirt of the north star-cut aisle runs about an hour on foot from [[star-cut]] through deeper [[the-quiet]] and opens onto the south rim. The whole bowl lies in daylight below them, and no check is needed to see its layout.
- **Cross straight to the north rim.** The pit-riddled floor is Difficult Terrain, so the 200-foot crossing costs 400 feet of movement. A straight line through the center passes the trunk and the curtain; swinging wide of the curtain keeps them out of the fig's reach, but anywhere within 60 feet of the trunk they are inside its blindsight. This is the short route, it passes the newest dead and their seals, and it is open sky the whole way.
- **Walk the rim.** A strip of firm lip ground runs around the bowl where the oldest pits have slumped shallow. It is not Difficult Terrain, it stays about 100 feet from the fig and outside every reach it has, and the forest edge overhangs most of it. Going round to the north rim this way is about 300 feet instead of 200, and it passes only the oldest dead and their oldest seals.
- **Go round through the Quiet wood.** The party skips the bowl entirely. The wood is Difficult Terrain and heavily obscured beyond 10 feet, the detour adds about half an hour, and they see none of the Hollow's evidence.
- **Get pulled, shoved, knocked Prone, or run blind near a pit.** A creature that is pulled, shoved, knocked Prone, or running blind within 5 feet of a pit edge makes a **Dexterity save — `DC 10`**. On a success it keeps its footing at the edge. On a failure it falls in, takes `1d6` bludgeoning damage, and lands Prone at the pit bottom; climbing out costs 10 feet of movement.
- **Take cover.** Spoil heaps give half cover. They do not hide anyone from the fig, whose blindsight finds a creature crouched in a pit or behind a heap within 60 feet.
- **Search an open pit.** **Intelligence (Investigation) — `DC 10`** → On a success, they find that the pit bottoms hold only turned soil, rock, and fig roots that were sawn through and healed into knots; there is no grave cut, no coffin, no grave goods, and no bones older than the diggers themselves. On a failure, they spend 10 minutes in the open bowl and see dirt and roots without reading them as a failed search for graves.
- **Read a single seal.** Resolve on [[grung-authority-seal]]. An inner-ring seal read this way gives the words "two graves beneath the memorial tree" on its own.
- **Sort the seals by ring.** **Intelligence (Investigation) — `DC 15`**, with Advantage for a character who knows Grung authority marks, such as [[jean-claude-tabarnack]]. On a success, they see that the rim seals repeat "go inland and report," the middle rings carry replacement orders sent after earlier parties stopped reporting, and the innermost dead around the fig carry the order to find and destroy two graves beneath the memorial tree. On a failure, they spend 10 minutes in the open bowl and learn only that the wording changes from ring to ring, without the order of the layers. See [[two-grave-orders]].
- **Study the fig.** **Intelligence (Nature) — `DC 15`** → On a success, they know it as a [[strangler-fig]] that hunts, that the bones and gold plates in its bark are its old kills, and that the skeleton in the curtain died held and waiting. On a failure, it reads as an ancient fig grown around old remains.
- **Stand at the north rim and look north.** No check is needed. Through the trunks about 100 feet on, they see the ring of black flowers of [[memorial-grove]] and the single fruit tree standing inside it. That second tree is the memorial tree the orders meant.
- **Enter the root curtain, or cut someone free of it.** The rule of the place applies, and the fig's numbers resolve on [[strangler-fig]]. Cutting a root that holds you or an ally is fighting back, not a claim. Cutting roots to clear a path, or prying seal plates out of the bark, is cutting living growth and is a claim.
- **Pick the living guava.** Picking the heavy [[giants-guava]] from its living stem is a living claim, and the picker is marked (see Who). The fig is about 100 feet away and does not respond to it, but the two [[vine-lash]] at the north rim do.
- **Take the fallen guava.** Taking fruit already on the ground is receiving, not a claim. **Intelligence (Nature) — `DC 15`** → On a success, they see it has turned; it is spoiled and gives none of the [[giants-guava]] effect. On a failure, it looks like an ordinary fallen guava; eating it is a sour mouthful and nothing more.
- **Dig, including to bury the dead.** Digging new earth anywhere on the floor is a living claim, burial included, and the digger is marked.
- **Carry Grung remains out of the Hollow.** Whether taking a body out counts as carrying off a kill under [[taking-on-aruhe]] has not been tested. The DM rules it at the table and records the ruling there. Loose spent seals on the ground are not living and can be taken freely.
- **Fly over the bowl or climb into open sky.** Flight skips the pits and the Difficult Terrain. The bowl is a canopy break, and [[bloodhawk]]s own open sky over Aruhe; anything flying or climbing in the open bowl is exposed, and the drop from the rim is a straight stoop lane of 30 feet or more for a diver. The guava canopy over the north rim path, and the forest edge over most of the rim walk, break that dive line. A flyer must also keep 20 feet from the fig's trunk and crown, which stand about 30 feet above the rim.
- **Turn invisible or hide.** Invisibility and Stealth do not stop the fig, which senses by blindsight within 60 feet, and they do not stop claim responders, which find a marked creature by scent and root-touch.
- **Leave north.** The path climbs a few minutes uphill under the guava's canopy to [[memorial-grove]].
- **Leave south.** The packed aisle runs back about an hour to [[star-cut]].

## Who

- **The strangler fig.** One Huge [[strangler-fig]] rooted in the deepest pit at the center. It is the only living thing in the bowl that acts. As a predator it lashes only at what moves inside its 20-foot root curtain, following the rule of the place. As a claim responder it attacks marked creatures within 60 feet, using the full reach and Trail-Pinch on its owner page.
- **Two vine lashes.** Two [[vine-lash]] hang motionless as ordinary vines in the Grove-edge trees at the north rim (False Appearance). They are claim responders only; they do not hunt people walking the north rim path unless someone is marked.
- **The Grung dead.** No living residents. The dead of many expeditions lie in and beside the pits in rings, oldest at the rims and newest around the fig, each where it dug. Their tools lie where they dropped them. The exact number of dead is a canon gap; the DM sets it if a player counts.
- **Absence.** No living Grung has come back out of this bowl on any evidence the Hollow holds. The rust on the newest root-saws shows that even the latest party has been dead for some time.

**Claim response here.** This is the island's rule from [[taking-on-aruhe]]. A creature that makes a living claim (picks from a living stem, cuts living growth to take it, digs living ground, or carries off a kill) is marked until the next dawn. Aruhe's responders find a marked creature within 60 feet by scent and root-touch even if it is Invisible, and attack marked creatures before anyone else. Fighting back against something that attacked you or yours is not a claim; taking is. Here the responders are the fig, when a marked creature is within 60 feet of it, and the two north-rim vine lashes, which wake at the end of the taker's next turn and target marked creatures. Responders stop when no marked creature is within reach, after 1 minute, or when reduced to half their hit points.

**Location moves.**

- **The fig reaches.** The trigger is a creature or object moving inside the curtain. The roots swing and the gold plates in the bark tick as the fig moves. The party can bait the roots with a moving object, go still to buy time, or pull a held friend free with the check on [[strangler-fig]]. A creature the fig engulfs and kills ends as bones in its bark, and the Hollow gains a new innermost ring.
- **The island answers a claim.** The trigger is a living claim made in the bowl or at the north rim. The two leafless ropes at the north rim uncoil at the end of the taker's next turn, and if the taker is within 60 feet of the fig its roots turn toward them. The party can run past reach, wait out the minute, or drop a responder to half its hit points. The taker stays marked until dawn, so the same responders, and any others met before dawn, go for them first.
- **A bloodhawk stoops.** The trigger is a creature flying, climbing high, or moving loud and exposed in the open bowl while a [[bloodhawk]] holds this sky lane; whether an adult is overhead on a given visit is the DM's call, and the crimson feather proves one hunts here. The party hears the paired thump-thump of its wings before the dive. They can break its dive line under the guava canopy or the rim's forest edge. A bloodhawk that seizes a body carries it away, and the kill may turn up later where no ground trail leads.
- **The Hollow is dug again.** The trigger is anyone digging under the fig, whether a Grung party following the orders or the party itself. Fresh spoil lands on the heaps and fresh root ends show in the central pit. The diggers are marked, the fig responds, and if they die the rings gain another layer.

## What

- **The pit field.** *"Hundreds of pits, some knee-deep and some deeper than a tall man."* Every pit is a Grung dig, 3 to 6 feet deep, from generations of expeditions searching for the two graves under what they took for the memorial tree. The floor is Difficult Terrain, pit edges carry the fall hazard in If the party, and spoil heaps give half cover. The soil stays bare because it has been turned again and again. Searching an open pit shows that the diggers found nothing but roots and rock.
- **The central pit.** *"Cut root ends have healed over into dark knots."* This is where the newest parties dug deepest, sawing through the fig's roots with their root-saws to get beneath it. Each cut was a living claim, and each one fed the fig. The pit sits inside the curtain, so reaching it means moving within the fig's reach.
- **The rings of dead.** *"At the rim they have sunk into the soil until only shell plates and belt buckles show."* The dead lie where they dug, in order of age. The rim bodies are the oldest expeditions, the middle rings are the replacement parties, and the innermost dead around the fig, *"still hold their shape under torn packs,"* are the newest, sent to find and destroy two graves. Their rust-red root-saws and short spades are what cut the roots in the central pit, and their rusting iron is the wet-iron smell in the air.
- **Spent seals.** *"Torn plates with gold script along their edges lie in the dirt of every ring."* These are spent [[grung-authority-seal]]s, and they sort by ring into the three layers of [[two-grave-orders]]: report at the rim, replacement in the middle, destroy the two graves beneath the memorial tree at the center. They no longer compel anyone. Loose seals can be taken without a claim; sorting them is the check in If the party.
- **The fig's bark.** *"Gold plates and pale bones are grown into its bark."* The [[strangler-fig]] drew killed diggers into its hollow trunk and grew around what was left, seals included. The plates tick against one another when the roots sway. Prying a plate out of the bark cuts living growth and is a claim.
- **The hanging skeleton.** *"A Grung skeleton hangs whole in that skirt at shoulder height."* This digger was held while the fig was engulfing someone else. The fig can engulf only one creature at a time, and this one died still and waiting, which is the rule of the place made visible.
- **The north-rim guava.** *"One heavy pink-gold fruit hanging at arm's height on its stem."* A living [[giants-guava]] tree leans over the north rim path, and its canopy shades that path from open sky. Picking the hanging fruit is a living claim that wakes the two vine lashes.
- **The fallen guava.** *"Another lying split and browning on the roots below."* It has turned and is spoiled. Taking it is safe, but it gives none of the giant's guava effect, which pushes a hungry party toward the living fruit.
- **The leafless ropes.** *"Two leafless ropes hang among the leafy vines in those trees."* They are two [[vine-lash]] at the Grove edge, motionless and indistinguishable from vines while they stay still. **Intelligence (Nature) — `DC 15`** → On a success, a character knows leafless hangers of that thickness as vine lashes that wrap and squeeze. On a failure, they read as dead vines.
- **The black-flower ring.** *"A ring of black flowers shows on the forest floor."* It is the edge of [[memorial-grove]], about 100 feet past the north rim, with the true memorial tree over the two unmarked graves at its center. Seeing it from the rim is the directional clue that the Grung dug under the wrong tree.
- **The crimson feather.** *"A long crimson feather lies across a spoil heap near the middle."* It is a [[bloodhawk]] primary. Bloodhawks own the open sky over Aruhe's canopy breaks, and this bowl is one.

## Where

- **North:** [[memorial-grove]], a few minutes uphill; its black-flower ring is about 100 feet past the north rim, up the path under the guava.
- **East:** Canon gap. Unbroken [[the-quiet]] forest, with no established site; the DM decides.
- **South:** [[star-cut]], about one hour on foot along the north star-cut aisle through deeper [[the-quiet]].
- **West:** Canon gap. Unbroken [[the-quiet]] forest, with no established site; the DM decides.

## Why

- A party comes here because the star-cut aisle leads here, and because anyone following the Grung order trail ends up in this bowl.
- A party stays because the Hollow holds the whole order trail in one place: the rings of seals show how an order written by someone who never saw Aruhe kept sending people to die under the wrong tree.
- A party cares because the Hollow is the last choice before the real memorial. It shows what obeying [[two-grave-orders]] costs, and from its north rim the true memorial tree is in plain view. The Hollow works as the Grove's decoy and graveyard; whether [[Hinewai]] knows it draws diggers away from her graves is a canon gap for the DM.
