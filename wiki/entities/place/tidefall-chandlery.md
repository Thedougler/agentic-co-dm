# Tidefall Chandlery

> [!narration] Tidefall Chandlery
> The chandlery counter is arranged for inspection papers as much as for rope and naval stores. A young clerk keeps the posted prices close at hand and checks each form in order, and he looks more distressed by a missing line than by a difficult customer. The counter smells of rope, paper, and naval stores, and the prices on the board are the only prices in the room.

## At a Glance

Tidefall Chandlery is [[calders-tooth-and-port-tidefall|Port Tidefall]]'s Crown-sanctioned naval outfitter, and the first provisioning stop for any vessel that has cleared the Inspection Pier.

- **Who is here.** [[nigel-strutt|Nigel Strutt]], the Crown's clerk, at the counter with the forms.
- **Danger.** No boat leaves the chandlery stocked without correct papers, and the port's inspection regime enforces it: a missing line stops the order, and a vessel that sails short of naval stores is the Crown's problem, not the clerk's.
- **Draw.** Naval stores at posted prices, a clerk who will correct a form rather than refuse a customer, and a straight answer about which Crown rule governs a purchase.

## Features

- **The counter.** Arranged so papers and posted prices are both to hand. Nigel works through a form in order and does not reach past a missing line to the next one.
- **The posted prices.** The charter prices for rope, canvas, pitch, naval stores, and provisions. They do not move for a difficult customer or a good story.
- **The charter.** The Crown's provisioning rules, which Nigel knows word for word, and which decide what a vessel may buy and how much of it.
- **The forms.** Inspection papers, provisioning orders, and the corrections that hold a purchase until they are right. Nigel's station sits in the port's inspection chain rather than beside it.

## At the Table

- **Provision after inspection.** Present the vessel's papers and Nigel takes the order. He says, "Of course I can help; we only need to correct this line before I release the order."
- **Correct a form.** The fix is one line and one moment of the clerk's attention. A party that argues the rule instead of fixing the line stands at the counter until the queue behind them moves.
- **Ask which rule governs a purchase.** Nigel answers from the charter, exactly, and this is the fastest way to learn what the port will and will not let a vessel buy.
- **Try to move the price.** Refused. The prices are posted and the charter is the charter; a story about the urgency of the crossing does not change either.
- **Bring false papers.** He checks each form in order, and a forged line is exactly the kind of thing an orderly clerk reads twice.

## Secrets

**Secret.** Nigel is not a gatekeeper by temperament, he is one by charter: his distress at a missing line is the port's discipline working through him, and a party that treats him as an obstacle loses the fastest route to legal provisioning in Port Tidefall. He corrects paperwork rather than refusing customers, so the tell is the posted prices at his elbow and the form he will not skip, and the find is asking him which rule applies instead of asking him to make an exception.
