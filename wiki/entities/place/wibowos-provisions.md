# Wibowo's Provisions

> [!narration] Wibowo's Provisions
> An efficient provisions counter turns crew numbers and days at sea into an exact order. The shelves hold ship's cakes stacked in oiled paper, vent-rock salts in wax-sealed jars, and sacks of grain with the miller's chalk still on them. A long bake-oven door is propped at the back of the shop, and the smell of hot biscuit comes through it while the counter works.

## At a Glance

Wibowo's Provisions is [[suryo-wibowo|Suryo Wibowo]]'s victualling shop on [[kalowe]]'s first island, where a captain's crew count becomes an order that will or will not outlast the crossing.

- **Who is here.** [[suryo-wibowo|Suryo Wibowo]], at the counter, taking crew numbers, days, and destination before he quotes anything.
- **Danger.** A short order leaves port looking complete. Suryo names the shortfall out loud, and a captain who hides it sails hungry.
- **Draw.** Ordinary provisions, [[long-passage-biscuit|Long-Passage Biscuits]] baked in the back room, [[wakerock-salts|Wakerock Salts]], and credit on remembered terms for captains he knows.

## Features

- **The counter.** Suryo works and talks at one speed, studies a list without drama, and asks the question a captain hoped to avoid: how many crew, how many days, where are you headed.
- **The shelves.** Ship's cakes, grain, salted goods, and sealed jars of [[wakerock-salts|Wakerock Salts]], with the miller's chalk still on the sacks.
- **The bake oven.** [[long-passage-biscuit|Long-Passage Biscuits]] are baked in the back room, which is what puts hot biscuit smell in a shop full of dry goods.
- **The credit book in his head.** Suryo extends credit to captains he has dealt with three times or more and keeps the terms in memory rather than in a ledger, which is why a new customer settles honestly or not at all.

## At the Table

- **Provision a voyage.** Give the crew count and days at sea and Suryo reads the order back, then says precisely where it comes up short. A party that argues with the arithmetic instead of adding supplies sails with the same hole in the hold.
- **Hide a shortfall.** Buying the list cheap and claiming it covers the crossing is **Charisma (Deception) — `DC 15`**; a failure means Suryo names the gap at the counter, in front of whoever is standing there.
- **Ask for credit as a stranger.** Refused flatly: he does not extend credit on a guess. The third honest order is the one that earns terms.
- **Buy the biscuit.** Long-passage biscuit is sold by the barrel and by the sack, and it is the one thing on the shelves that keeps a hungry crew working rather than complaining.
