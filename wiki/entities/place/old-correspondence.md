# Old Correspondence

## Overview

> [!narration] Narration
> A single stone cottage sits alone on the islet, a lamp burning in its one window day and night. Salt-bleached grass and wind-bent scrub cling to the low ground, while gulls call from the seaward ledges and crabs work the tide pools below. Inside, an old man writes in careful, unhurried script, an enormous stack of crossed-out drafts beside him. A narrow path runs from the landing shore to the cottage door; rats rustle under the floorboards, and vultures circle the roof some mornings.
>
> *The scribe*: *(dry)* Almost done.
>
> He said the same thing last decade.

Old Correspondence's sole resident has been drafting the same letter for as long as anyone can remember, addressed to a name nobody's ever managed to place.

## At a Glance

Old Correspondence is a single-resident islet in [[midchain-north|The Northern Midchain]], between [[munai|Munai]], [[nemwe|Nemwe]], [[yssenmoor|Yssenmoor]], and [[drovannis|Drovannis]]. The one resident scribe controls the cottage and its lamp. The islet is known for a decades-unfinished letter to an unknown recipient; the western or eastern route can be skipped if the party only wants the scribe.

## If the party

- **Arrives.** Follow the narrow shore path to the cottage; the party finds the lamp lit and the scribe still writing.
- **Reads the drafts.** Sort through the crossed-out stack to confirm that the letter has been rewritten for decades, but the intended recipient remains unidentified.
- **Searches the floorboards.** Disturb the rat nests and find the swarm's biting resistance among the discarded drafts.
- **Waits until morning.** Watch vultures circle the cottage roof and stoop toward anything that goes still on the open rock.

## Who

One old scribe lives in the stone cottage, writing the unfinished letter and answering interruptions with dry patience. A swarm of rats nests under the floorboards among the discarded drafts and comes up biting if anyone pulls papers from the stack. Gulls nest along the seaward ledges, crabs work the tide pools, and vultures circle the cottage roof some mornings; none is a resident with a claim to the cottage.

## What

- **“A lamp burning in its one window day and night.”** The cottage is continuously occupied and serves as the islet's only fixed landmark. The find is the scribe inside, still writing.
- **“An enormous stack of crossed-out drafts.”** The same letter has been rewritten for decades. The find is evidence of the unknown recipient's importance to the scribe, not the recipient's identity.
- **“Salt-bleached grass and wind-bent scrub.”** Wind and salt spray keep the islet's thin soil cropped low. The find is clear sight across the whole islet from the cottage window.
- **“Sea purslane” in fleshy, salty clusters.** The plant mats the tideline rock and is edible raw. The find is enough to cut a day's rations for a party that knows to look.
- **“Gulls and crabs” at the shore.** Gulls nest along the seaward ledges and crabs work the tide pools below. The find is ordinary shoreline noise and tide-pool traffic.
- **“A swarm of rats” under the floorboards.** The rats nest among discarded drafts and bite when anyone pulls papers out of the stack. The find is a living hazard protecting the papers.
- **“Vultures” over the cottage roof.** They circle some mornings and stoop at anything that goes still too long on the open rock. The find is a warning that the exposed ground offers no forgiving shelter.

## Why

A party comes to learn who the scribe is writing to, use the islet as a quiet landmark or stopping place, or investigate why the letter has remained unfinished for decades. The party stays only while the scribe's work, the draft stack, or the island's isolated position matters more than the exposed shore.
