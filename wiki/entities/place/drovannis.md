# Drovannis

## Overview

> [!narration] Narration
> Floating platforms and rope perches bob in a sheltered lagoon. Roosting seabirds fill them. A robed druid moves between the perches, murmuring to birds that know her.

Drovannis is a druidic bird sanctuary. Each year, seabirds return here carrying small lost objects from the [[shattered-sea|Shattered Sea]].

---
## At a Glance

Drovannis is a migratory bird sanctuary in the [[midchain-north|Northern Midchain]], connected to [[corrigans-rest|Corrigan's Rest]], [[old-correspondence|Old Correspondence]], [[verdant-teeth|The Verdant Teeth]], and [[midchain-west|The Western Midchain]]. A druidic order controls access to the lagoon and tends the birds. Skipping Drovannis costs the party the chance to identify a lost object, follow a bird-carried clue, or learn what the sanctuary's keepers protect.

---
## Who

The druidic order tends the sanctuary and its floating perches. Roosting terns and cormorants fill the platforms by midday. Shore crabs work the mud at low tide, reef sharks patrol the open-water channel, and giant octopuses den beneath the pilings.

---
## What

- Salt-marsh reed and wind-bent scrub crowd the lagoon's rim and root the driftwood pilings. Reaching solid ground means pushing through a reed wall.
- Blue mussels crust every piling below the waterline and can be pried loose at low tide.
- Roosting terns and cormorants fill the platforms shoulder to shoulder; a wrong step or raised voice sends the colony up screaming.
- Each year the seabirds return carrying small lost objects from the [[shattered-sea|Shattered Sea]]. The druidic order keeps the objects for tracing or return.
- Shore crabs work the exposed mud, reef sharks patrol the channel, and giant octopuses can seize a hand dipped below the pilings.

## Why

The party comes to ask the druids about a lost object, follow a clue carried by a returning seabird, or seek shelter in the lagoon. They stay to sort the year's objects, earn the order's trust, or navigate the perches without disturbing the colony. They leave when the sanctuary's protectors deny access or when the channel's wildlife makes salvage cost more than the clue is worth.
