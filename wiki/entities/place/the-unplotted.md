# The Unplotted

> [!narration] The Unplotted
> The Unplotted is a green island with a bay flat enough to careen a hull in, its rock arms open in a gap two hundred feet wide. Fresh water runs down to the beach in a cold stream, and fruit hangs untouched on the trees above it, with no bird tracks anywhere on the fruit or the ground beneath. Four wrecks stand bow-in around the bay with their masts still stepped and their decks empty of remains. Soil has packed into the rock above the beach, and roots thread down through cracks that run in straight lines.

## At a Glance

The Unplotted is a named island in the eastern [[midchain]], past the [[doldrums]], that never appears in the same place on two charts and always appears where a ship needs it.

- **Who is here.** Nobody living: the crews of four wrecks are gone without remains. The island is one animal, and it is the only occupant.
- **Danger.** Past the treeline the ground takes hold of everyone standing on it at once, and the bay's heads begin closing. Water, fruit, and a flat bay are the bait.
- **Draw.** A full generous day of fresh water, untouched fruit, and a bay flat enough to careen in — plus four whole wrecks to strip, on an island nobody has claimed.

## Features

- **The bay and its arms.** A dead-flat bay between two rock arms with a gap between them. The arms narrow a step at a time while anything is caught on the interior; a dead ship's spine is wedged in the throat and holds it open.
- **The beach and the waterline.** Sand, the stream mouth, and shade. Nothing happens at the waterline, and time spent there is free.
- **The stream and the fruit.** Cold water off the slope and fruit hanging unpeeled and unbirded. The stream's source sits on the interior, past the treeline.
- **The treeline.** The line that separates a free afternoon from the interior. One step past it and the ground takes every creature standing there.
- **The four wrecks.** Whole hulls, bow-in, masts stepped, decks empty of bones. Ships that strike a reef do not keep their rig, and these came in and stopped. Everything above the waterline on the beach is the lie; the wrecks are honest.
- **The throat.** The gap between the arms, held open by the spine of a ship that has been wedged there for years. It is the one door the island cannot close.

## At the Table

- **Rest at the waterline.** Nothing is taken and nothing attacks, for hours or a full day. A crew that never leaves the beach gets water, fruit, and a safe anchorage and never learns what the island is.
- **Step past the treeline.** The ground grips everyone standing on the interior at once, with no saving throw, and the arms begin narrowing. Resolve the grapple, the escape, and the closing gap on [[island-mimic]].
- **Burn something.** Fire frees every creature the ground is holding within 30 feet of the flames, which is the one thing sailors already know about mimics that still works at this scale. A burned grove also tells the island the crew understands.
- **Reach the wrecks.** Boats or flight reach them. Each wreck is a cache of naval stores from a crew that is not there, and any of them can be jammed spine-first into the throat to hold the gap open.
- **Take the gap before it closes.** A hull driven into the narrowing throat either wedges and becomes the next doorstop or grounds hard in the arms. The gap floors at the width of the wreck already in it.
- **Fight it.** Nothing aboard can kill the island. Hurt, it stops being patient, and at the end of the fight it lets go, grinds its arms back open, goes dormant, and is somewhere else within the season.

## Secrets

**Secret.** The Unplotted is not an island but one animal: an [[island-mimic]] old enough to have taught its own doctrine to smaller mimics — stop being a chest, be the shelter a crew chooses. It follows shipping traffic, which is why three charts disagree about where it sits and none of them stays wrong for long. Everything above the waterline is the lie and the wrecks are the truth, and the tell is that the island is never where it was chartered to be and never once fails to be exactly where a ship needs shelter.
