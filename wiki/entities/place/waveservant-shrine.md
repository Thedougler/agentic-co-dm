# Kalowe Waveservant Shrine

## Overview

> [!narration] Narration
> A salvaged timber roof covers a stone niche in the causeway wall at Kalowe's reef gap. Every hull passes close enough to reach the basin, where a Waveservant records what the vessel owes. Salt wind moves around a small shrine that makes tribute feel like ordinary harbour work.

## At a Glance

The Kalowe Waveservant Shrine sits at the only navigable gap through Kalowe's reef. It collects tribute from every hull and keeps a written record of the debt.

## If the party

- **Bring a hull through the gap.** The party passes within reach of the shrine's basin and is expected to pay tribute.
- **Pay the tribute.** The Waveservants record the payment and treat the blessing as a receipt.
- **Skip the tribute.** The default becomes public on the ledger and can attract an appointment before worse arrives.
- **Ask what the ledger knows.** The attendant knows ships by what they owe, while the Chain Council's raised rate is buying silence about the Pearl theft.

## Who

- A Waveservant attendant keeps the basin and ledger at the gap.
- The [[chain-council]] controls the local political pressure around the raised tribute.
- Every arriving hull becomes visible to the shrine through its payment or default.

## What

- A stone niche in the causeway wall.
- A salvaged timber roof, tribute basin, and written ledger.
- A blessing recorded as a receipt of payment.

## Where

- **North:** [[kalowe]]; the shrine is built into the causeway wall at the reef gap.
- **East:** The reef-gap water; no separate destination is canonized.
- **South:** Reef water beyond the navigable gap; no separate destination is canonized.
- **West:** The remaining causeway and Kalowe's islets; the exact street route is not established.
- Every hull using Kalowe's navigable gap passes within reach of the basin.

## Why

The party cares because the shrine controls the social and religious cost of entering Kalowe by water. Tribute buys a clean arrival; refusal creates a public debt that the Waveservants, the Chain Council, and other harbour powers can use.
