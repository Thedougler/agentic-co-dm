# Otanu

## Overview

> [!narration] Narration
> A thin grey plume rises steady from the island's central cone, bending east on the trade wind, and a faint tang of sulfur drifts down with it. Along the shore, fishers glance up between casts, the way sailors elsewhere check the sky. One fisher mutters while already hauling in the net: “It's leaning wrong today.” Nobody argues with her.

The vent stays active. Locals have built their weather-reading practice around its smoke. Near the vent's throat, the ground stays hot enough to kill anyone who strays off the safe path.

---
## At a Glance

Otanu is an active volcanic island in the [[midchain-north|Northern Midchain]], connected to [[central-strait|The Central Strait]], [[quillane|Quillane]], [[vetau|Vetau]], and [[murrat|Murrat]]. Local volcano-watchers control the safe paths by reading the smoke. Skipping Otanu costs the party the chance to use that reading, gather sulfur moss remedy, or cross the island without treating the vent as a live hazard.

---
## Who

Local fishers and volcano-watchers read the plume as weather and navigation. Wild goats graze the crater rim at dawn, hawks hunt them from the vent's rising heat, giant crabs work the tide pools after dark, and giant scorpions den in the cooled lava tubes.

---
## What

- Ash-fed scrub and wind-bent pandanus hold the upper slopes above coarse sand and tide pools.
- Sulfur moss grows near the steam vents and is boiled into a joint-ache remedy traded in small clay jars at [[vetau|Vetau's]] market.
- The active smoke plume is the island's warning and weather signal. A wrong lean changes when locals haul in nets or refuse to sail.
- Near the vent's throat, black soil and hot ground make the safe path the only reliable approach.
- Wild goats pick along the crater rim at dawn, hawks ride the rising heat, giant crabs work scorched tide pools after dark, and giant scorpions emerge from warmed lava tubes.

## Why

The party comes to read the vent before sailing, collect sulfur moss remedy, or cross the island by the local safe path. They stay to learn why the plume leans wrong, trade at the tide pools, or watch the crater rim. They leave when the smoke warns of unsafe passage or when the heat and lava tubes make further exploration cost more than the island offers.
