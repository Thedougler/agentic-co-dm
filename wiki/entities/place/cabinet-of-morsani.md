# The Cabinet of Morsani

> [!narration] The Cabinet of Morsani
> A lantern marks the door, the only notice the shop offers. Inside, shelves run deeper than the room seems to allow, and magic and rare things gather beneath a layer of dust while rings catch the light on the dealer's fingers. Brass polish sits thick in the air over something older, and a locked case stands behind the counter.

## At a Glance

The Cabinet of Morsani is [[prospero-morsani|Prospero Morsani]]'s curio shop in [[calven-and-calveno|Calveno]]'s Velo Quarter, where magic and rare objects are sold together with the stories of the people who lost them.

- **Who is here.** [[prospero-morsani|Prospero Morsani]], wearing thirty-one rings, and nobody else; his hours move with his mood.
- **Danger.** Nothing here attacks. The risk is a sale made on a story, and the locked case: an object's history is the price of buying or selling it, and a piece whose account does not hold up gets refused.
- **Draw.** Unusual gear at fair prices — the Compass of the Drowned, three quivers of trick arrows including [[the-fishwife|the Fishwife]], [[the-chimney|the Chimney]], and [[lamplighter|the Lamp Lighter]] — and an antiquarian who will tell a party what their own object really is.

## Features

- **The lantern.** The shop's only notice, hung at the door. Prospero opens when it suits him, and the lantern is how anyone knows the Cabinet is trading.
- **The shelves.** Magic and rare objects, dusted but deep, set out in no order a customer can read. Some came from estates, some from corpses, and each one arrived because its owner could not keep it.
- **The rings.** Thirty-one rings on the dealer's fingers, each one holding the story of an object's previous owner, the reason it moved on, and what somebody loved about it.
- **The locked case.** Behind the counter, holding the thirty-second ring attached to nothing. It is the piece the collection is missing, and Prospero has been waiting for it for a long time.

## At the Table

- **Offer an object or its history.** Prospero examines what the party brings, asks a new guest to describe themselves, and then describes the item that matches what the guest was too guarded to admit. A true account of loss draws his full attention.
- **Shop for unusual gear.** The Cabinet's stock has included the Compass of the Drowned at `400 gp`, trick arrows by the quiver, and a +1 dragonhide belt. He never pressures a sale; he opens a case and begins.
- **Ask an object's provenance.** He recounts the history slowly and completely, including the owner whose name is missing, which he treats as the most important part.
- **Try to pass off a story that does not hold.** He reads people the way a merchant reads ledgers. A false account ends the conversation and the object stays on the shelf.
- **Bring what the thirty-second ring wants.** A story or an object that completes the set is the only thing that makes him stop treating a visitor as a customer.

## Secrets

**Secret.** Prospero is not selling objects so much as distributing them: each ring he wears is a story he holds until he can place the piece in the right hands, and the locked case's thirty-second ring is for the item that completes the collection. The tells are the rings he turns slowly while speaking, the questions he asks before the sale, and a locked case he does not offer to open. The find is bringing a true account of loss rather than coin.
