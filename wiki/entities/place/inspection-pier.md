# Inspection Pier

## Overview

> [!narration] Narration
> The Inspection Pier stands in the approach to Port Tidefall, beneath cliff tiers that descend from the fort and trade shelf to the docks. Incoming ships stop here before entering the harbour. Crews answer questions, show papers, and wait while their vessel receives a recorded rating.

## At a Glance

Inspection Pier is Tidefall's official arrival point and the first stop for ships entering the western Strait. It sits between the sea approach and the port's docks; no alternate local arrival point is established.

## If the party

- **Presents papers.** The crew answers questions and shows its papers before receiving a recorded vessel rating.
- **Contests the rating.** The party contests the Crown's inspection record and draws the attention that follows opposition to inspection law.
- **Refuses the stop.** The party does not enter through Tidefall's official approach; no other safe or lawful arrival route is established here.
- **Watches the approach.** The party can see which ships submit to the stop and which ships turn away, but no additional observation procedure is established.

## Who

Incoming crews wait for inspection, and Crown inspectors ask questions, examine papers, and record vessel ratings. No named inspector or fixed headcount is established on this page.

## What

- **Ship papers.** Arriving crews show the documents that establish their right to enter.
- **Recorded ratings.** The pier records a vessel rating that can shape later Crown attention.
- **Approach and docks.** The pier stands between incoming water and Tidefall's docks beneath the island's cliff tiers.
- **Black nooses.** Black nooses hang over the gate into Tidefall, making the harbour's threat visible without explaining when they were raised.

## Why

The party comes here when it needs to enter Tidefall by the official route, present papers, or decide whether Crown inspection is safer than refusal. The recorded rating matters because the Dravosi Crown uses inspection law and harbour stops to make its authority enforceable across the water.
