# Fifita's Slip

> [!narration] Fifita's Slip
> On Kalowe's lagoon side, a boatyard has been made to look like storage. Hulls sit behind whatever cover the island provides, covered in tarred canvas that is tied down at every eyelet, and the work proceeds slowly enough that a stranger might mistake the place for an abandoned shed. Fresh shavings sit under the canvas edge, and the lagoon water inside the slip is the colour of tea.

## At a Glance

Fifita's Slip is [[noa-fifita|Noa Fifita]]'s unofficial boatyard on [[kalowe]]'s lagoon side, where hulls are repaired outside the harbour books.

- **Who is here.** [[noa-fifita|Noa Fifita]] alone with the hull he is working; his family's chandlery and its patriarch [[taufa-fifita|Taufa Fifita]] are the gate in.
- **Danger.** The yard exists because the work stays off the ledger: a boat found here with unpaid tribute or a Crown writ becomes a question for [[kalowe]]'s council, and Noa leaves rather than answer for a customer.
- **Draw.** Hull work with no entry in the harbour books, and a yard that asks no questions about where a boat has been.

## Features

- **The slip.** A lagoon-side yard dressed as storage, with hulls under tarred canvas and work that never announces itself. There is no sign, no desk, and no posted price.
- **The cover.** The [[taufa-fifita|Fifita]] chandlery buys the rope, pitch, and timber that keeps the yard working, and its counter is where an introduction to Noa is made or refused.
- **The work.** Noa works alone, slowly, and surely. He sets one price by the hull and does not haggle; a customer who will not pay it watches him leave with his tools.
- **The lagoon approach.** A boat can reach the slip from the water without crossing the harbour. Shallow water over the lagoon side takes a dinghy or a hull Noa already knows the draft of.

## At the Table

- **Arrive with an introduction.** A word from the Fifita counter, or a prior arrangement with Noa, gets the yard open and a hull quoted. Without one, the yard stays as empty as it looks.
- **Approach cold from the water.** Noa is not there, and nothing is quoted. A party that comes back with an introduction is charged for the extra trip.
- **Bring a boat with history on it.** Noa quotes by the hull, not by the job, and he does not ask what the damage came from. A hull carrying a Crown writ or an unpaid tribute puts the yard at risk, and he will refuse the work if he thinks the port will come looking.
- **Press him on price.** He repeats the number once, and then he leaves for the day. The price does not come down.
