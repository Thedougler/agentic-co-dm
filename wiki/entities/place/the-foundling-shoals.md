# The Foundling Shoals

## Overview

> [!narration] Narration
> Calm water covers a pale sand reef, utterly plain at a glance. Small waves lap at the sand with a soft, steady hiss. Eelgrass lies flat in the deeper channels, while bladderwrack and sea lettuce mat the bars exposed by the low tide. At dawn, a woman wades out and checks one sandbar the way others check nets.

For generations, infants arrive at the Foundling Shoals, safe but mysterious. The local fisherfolk raise them as a sacred duty.

## At a Glance

The Foundling Shoals is a tide-flat reef north of [[sparhold|Sparhold]], between [[quillane|Quillane]], [[tor-vessane|Tor Vessane]], and [[otanu|Otanu]]. The local fisherfolk control the reef's foundling watch and raise each unharmed infant as a sacred trust. The reef is known for unexplained infants appearing on its sandbars; a party can bypass the shoals, but doing so leaves the origin and timing of the next foundling unknown.

## If the party

- **Arrives at dawn.** Follow the fisherfolk's watch and find a woman checking the sandbars before the tide covers them.
- **Investigates a new arrival.** Search the safe infant's landing place and learn what the tide left around the child before the fisherfolk carry out their sacred duty.
- **Crosses at low tide.** Walk the exposed bars to gather food, but plan around the deep channels before the incoming tide closes the route.
- **Forages.** Strip sea lettuce, bladderwrack, or sea purslane from the bars, and fill a bucket with cockles and mussels inside an hour.
- **Crosses a channel on the incoming tide.** Reef sharks can cut off a sandbar faster than a wader expects; giant crabs wait in the softest sand near channel mouths.

## Who

The local fisherfolk keep watch over the sandbars and raise every unharmed infant that appears. One woman checks a sandbar at dawn. Gulls and crabs work the tide line, vultures circle anything stranded, reef sharks run the deep channels on an incoming tide, and giant crabs bury near the channel mouths. No named resident owns the reef.

## What

- **“A pale sand reef, utterly plain at a glance.”** The reef is a tide-flat nursery whose bars appear harmless until the water moves. The find is the next safe landing place or the next channel cut off by the tide.
- **“Eelgrass beds” in the deeper channels.** Eelgrass lies flat with the current and gives cover to anything hunting the shallows. The find is movement hidden below the surface.
- **“Bladderwrack and sea lettuce” on exposed sandbars.** The fisherfolk strip handfuls to eat fresh or dry for winter stores. The find is edible shore food.
- **“Cockles and mussels” in wet sand.** A low-tide walk with a bucket fills it inside an hour. The find is a day's easy shellfish gathering.
- **“Crabs” at the tide line and “Vultures” circling low.** Crabs work the shore at dusk; vultures stoop toward anything the tide leaves stranded. The find is warning that stranded creatures draw attention.
- **“[[reef-shark|Reef sharks]]” in the deep channels.** They run the channels on an incoming tide and can cut off a sandbar faster than a wader expects. The find is a moving boundary between bars.
- **“Giant crabs” in the softest sand.** They bury near channel mouths, and a misplaced step can put a boot into a waiting claw. The find is a hazard beneath an apparently empty bar.
- **The foundling offering.** The fisherfolk's sacred trust is represented by `![[foundling-shoals-offering]]`; the find is the community's material response to each unexplained arrival.
