# Vault of the First Factor

## Overview
> [!narration] Narration
> Pale limestone rises above the jungle canopy as a spire whose water routes do not match public charts. No marked landing waits below; only a narrow cleft, a cold draft, and the faint mineral taste of sealed stone suggest a way in. Somewhere above, a metal edge knocks once against rock, then the jungle swallows the sound.

---
## At a Glance

The Vault of the First Factor is an uncharted limestone spire in the [[Midchain]] jungle, sitting somewhere beyond the mapped approaches to [[calven-and-calveno]]. The site is not confirmed by any current chart or official. The place's rule is compounded obligation: credentials and old debts matter more than weapons. Skipping the site costs the party the chance to trace surrendered collateral, identify the Concordat's old creditors, or gain leverage over the [[tessarine-concordat]].

---
## If the party

- **Approach by water.** The party follows oral directions toward a route that fails to match public charts. They find the spire only if they compare the current waterline to a debt record or collateral mark; otherwise the jungle offers no reliable landing. The cost of a failed approach is lost time and a route that may not recur.
- **Climb the limestone.** The party can climb toward the cold draft and the metal knock. The spire yields a narrow cleft rather than a visible door; the cost is exposing their arrival to whatever maintains the old defences.
- **Present a Tessarine credential.** A credential may identify the party as a debtor, creditor, or trespasser, but current canon does not establish which credentials open the vault. The immediate result is a change in the site's response, not safe access.
- **Search the approach.** The party can compare ledger gaps, oral traditions, and physical collateral marks. They find a route fragment or an old debt trail, but not proof of the vault's contents.

---
## Who

No confirmed inhabitants are present. The [[tessarine-concordat]] may have left old collateral and magical defences here, but no sitting official confirms the vault exists. If the party arrives, the first active pressure is the site's credential system: the old warding responds to declared standing before it responds to force.

---
## What

- **“Pale limestone rises above the jungle canopy.”** The truth is an uncharted limestone spire. A careful survey confirms the stone and the height but not the vault's existence.
- **“Water routes do not match public charts.”** The truth is that the approach is concealed by contradictory channels and oral directions. Comparing the route against a debt record can reveal a usable fragment.
- **“A cold draft” and “sealed stone.”** The truth is a narrow cleft or concealed entrance associated with the old depository. Finding it gives the party a way to test the vault without placing the site on a public map.
- **“A metal edge knocks once against rock.”** The truth is a tell of the vault's credential-sensitive defences or a remnant of its locking system. The sound identifies a mechanism, not its safe response.
- **Compounded obligation.** The vault may hold masterwork weapons, magical family heirlooms, or locked trade-bars surrendered by captains, governors, and merchants. None of those contents is confirmed; a matching debt record or recovered heirloom is the first find.

## Why

The party comes to follow a physical collateral trail, test whether the First Factor's legend preserves a real site, or recover leverage over the [[tessarine-concordat]]. They stay to match a debt, credential, or heirloom to the vault's history. They leave when the site offers only contradictory hearsay, or when the cost of pressing an unconfirmed defence exceeds the value of proof.
