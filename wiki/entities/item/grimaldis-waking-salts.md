# Grimaldi's Waking Salts

> [!narration] Narration
> A palm-wide tin has a crooked lid that never seals flat. Grey salt with green flecks is pressed beneath waxed paper scored into four doses. The smell arrives a foot ahead of the tin: ammonia and crushed peppercorn, sharp enough to sting the throat. Tip it and the salt hisses against the paper, loose where somebody dug out a quarter.

*Consumable (Salts), uncommon.* [[ilaria-grimaldi]] mixes these for ship's surgeons who must get a concussed deckhand upright before the next wave takes the rail, and sells them to anyone who asks plainly.

**Four doses.** As an action, break one dose under the nose of a creature within 5 feet that is Charmed, Frightened, Incapacitated, Paralyzed, or Stunned by an effect that allows a saving throw to end it. That creature immediately repeats that saving throw with Advantage, ending the effect on itself on a success.

The target need not be conscious or willing. A dose works on a creature at 0 Hit Points, but does not stabilize it or restore Hit Points. A second dose against the same effect grants no further save. An effect with no repeat save, poison, disease, or a condition caused by damage does not answer to the salts. An open tin goes flat inside 10 days.

## At the Table

[[ilaria-grimaldi]] sells four-dose tins at [[kalowe]] to ship's surgeons and harbor crews, including the [[Takowan]]. Use a dose when a failed save has left an ally Charmed, Frightened, Incapacitated, Paralyzed, or Stunned and the effect permits another save. It is not healing, stabilization, or a cure for poison or disease.

## Connections

- [[ilaria-grimaldi]] makes and sells the salts.
- [[kalowe]] is the harbor market where crews buy them.
- [[Takowan]] buys them by the crate for shipboard emergencies.

## Provenance

The recipe is [[ilaria-grimaldi|Grimaldi]]'s own, developed over 15 years of hauling crews out of the water at [[kalowe]]. She presses the salts into tins by the dozen, keeps the price flat for harbor crews, and never asks who a tin is for.
