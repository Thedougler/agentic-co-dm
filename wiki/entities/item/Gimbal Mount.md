# Gimbal Mount

> [!narration] Narration
> A yoke of black iron the size of a helmet, hung inside two brass rings set at right angles to each other.
>
> The cradle inside carries a lead ring at its base and four thumbscrews around the rim.
>
> A wagon goes past outside, the counter shakes, and the rings take the shake while the cradle holds where it sits.
>
> The brass at the pivots shines on both sides where hands have turned it.

## Mechanics

> [!mechanic]
> **Gimbal Mount [HB].** Bolting the yoke down takes ten minutes with [[smith's tools]] and four bolts into a rail or a beam. The cradle takes a device or a weapon of up to 60 pounds and holds it with four thumbscrews.
>
> The rings keep the cradle level through any pitch or roll a seaworthy hull survives, and it swings a full half-circle by hand. An attack made with a weapon seated in the mount ignores disadvantage from an unstable or moving surface.
>
> **Edge cases:** the mount cancels the deck's motion and nothing else. A prone shooter, a blinded shooter, and a target behind cover all roll exactly as they would ashore. Seating or unseating a device costs an action.
>
> **Limitations:** it adds nothing to attack or damage rolls, and it holds nothing heavier than 60 pounds. A device in the cradle travels no further than the mount, and the mount travels no further than the four bolts holding it down.

## Provenance

The pattern comes off a Crown swivel-gun yoke, which [[Kettil Ormsson]] built for years in a northern yard before he came south. He makes them one at a time now and never asks what goes in the cradle.
