# Gale-Cut Storm Sail

> [!narration] Narration
> Canvas folded into a crate too small to hold it, with white fold lines standing on their own. The weave runs against itself in a herringbone, hand-stitched, and every panel seam carries roping paler than hemp. A merchant's mark and tally are chalked into one corner, neither matching the papers nailed to the lid. Lift a fold and the whole bolt slides toward the loading door while the air above it turns cold and moving.

*Wondrous item, rare.*

**Gale-cut course.** Bending the sail onto a mast takes 10 minutes and a crew of two. A vessel with the sail set and drawing keeps full travel speed and holds any course its helm can steer, whatever the wind does, in weather short of a named storm. Everyone aboard has Advantage on checks to steer or hold that course.

The sail works on any vessel from a ship's boat up to a two-masted trader, and on one mast at a time. In a dead calm it fills anyway. A second gale-cut sail on the same vessel adds nothing. It drives the hull but turns no wave and sheds no rain; a hull breaking up beneath it still breaks up, and anyone on deck takes the weather in full. Furled or stowed, it is ordinary canvas. It grants nothing on land or below the surface. If cut or holed, it is inert until a sailmaker who knows the herringbone repairs it.

## At the Table

The sail is unsold in a back-room crate at [[Moretti & Sons]], held by [[giacomo-moretti]]. Its chalked tally names tonnage no ship on the [[Mercatura]] carries, while the [[tessarine-concordat|Tessarine]] factor house has never claimed it. The sail helps a vessel cross open water in [[uncertainty]], but it does not protect a hull or crew from the weather itself.
