# Ninefold Block

> [!narration] Narration
> An oak block the length of a forearm, iron-strapped, with nine sheaves stacked inside the shell where a rigging block carries one.
>
> Touch a single sheave and all nine turn together, and they go on turning after the hand comes away.
>
> Its strop takes a splice of cable thicker than a block this size should ever carry, and it smells of hot iron instead of tar.
>
> A line already runs through it, coiled on the boards, and the coil lies tight against the floor.

## Mechanics

> [!mechanic]
> **Ninefold Block [HB].** Reeving a rope through the block and making it fast to an anchor point takes 1 minute. While a creature hauls on that line, treat its Strength score as 21 for how much weight it can lift, drag, pull, or hold, and for any Strength check to shift whatever load that line holds. Two or more creatures hauling the same line stack their pull as usual.
>
> **Edge cases:** the load travels only along the line, toward the block. A portcullis, a hull section, a fallen beam, or a sunken crate all qualify. The block itself needs an anchor point that can take the weight, and a cleat, spar, or bollard that cannot will tear out.
>
> **Limitations:** it does nothing on a creature that resists, grants no bonus to attack rolls, damage, grapple contests, or Athletics used against a creature, and adds nothing to a Strength saving throw. Whatever it hauls travels at a walk and no faster. Cutting the line ends the effect at once, and a hauler off the line gets no help at all.

## Provenance

Blocks like it were rove in the [[Arsenal]] yards for the House dry docks, where a lifting gang costs more per day than the block does once. This one came to [[Moretti & Sons]] in a wreck lot with its own line already through it. Giacomo prices it at what a yard gang costs for a season and has yet to move it.
