# Wakerock Salts

> [!narration] Wakerock Salts
> The tin is two fingers across. Salt crust has welded the lid. Grey crystal fills it to the brim, ground as coarse as damp sand, with three scoops pressed into the surface and one of them already gone. Crack the lid and a bilge-ammonia sting carries the length of a galley. The metal on the underside is warmer than the shelf it sat on.

## At a Glance

*Consumable (salts), uncommon.*

Wakerock Salts wake a sailor out of a dead watch and break a mind that has been taken: one dose under the nose, and the charm, fear, or sleep is off.

- **Effect.** Ends the Charmed or Frightened condition on the target and wakes them from any sleep, magical or ordinary.
- **Doses.** Three to a tin, one dose per use.
- **Price.** `75 gp`; sold at [[wibowos-provisions|Wibowo's Provisions]] and [[moretti-and-sons|Moretti & Sons Chandlery]].
- **Made from.** Grey cut out of the vent rock under [[kalowe|Kalowe]] and mixed with clove to stop it caking.

## Properties

**Wake a creature (Magic action).** Hold one open dose under the nose of a willing or incapacitated creature within 5 feet. That dose goes flat at once. The creature ends the Charmed or Frightened condition on itself and wakes from sleep, magical or ordinary.

**Edge cases.** An unwilling creature that is neither Charmed nor Frightened refuses with no roll. A creature charmed twice inside an hour by the same effect gets nothing from the second dose.

**Limitations.** The salts restore no hit points and end no other condition. They do nothing for a creature at 0 hit points, and nothing at all past arm's length. An open tin left without its lid goes flat by the end of the day.

## At the Table

- **Break a charm on a comrade.** Magic action, one dose, target within 5 feet. The charmed ally turns on whoever charmed them with the rest of their turn.
- **Bring someone up off a dead watch.** Bosuns keep a tin for exactly this, and the ammonia smell reaches the length of a galley, so everyone aboard knows it came out.
- **Count the scoops.** Three to a tin, and the pressed depressions show how many are gone, which makes a tin an honest measure of how bad the week has been.
