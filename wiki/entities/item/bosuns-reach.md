# Bosun's Reach

> [!narration] Narration
> A short, heavy arrow has a head like a folded fist, with three barbs pinned flat against the shaft under a wire ring. Behind the fletching sits a wooden spool no wider than a coin, wound with silk line so fine you must turn it to the light to see it. A leather thumb-loop at the free end is worn shiny from use. Somebody has climbed on this one already and wound it back for the next person.

*Weapon (Arrow), uncommon.*

**Bosun's Reach.** This single-use consumable is fired as a normal ranged attack at a wall, hull, beam, spar, or similar solid surface within the weapon's normal range. An unattended object has AC 10; the DM can set a higher AC for a small or awkward target. On a hit, the wire ring shears, the barbs spring open, and the spool pays out up to 60 feet of silk line to the shooter's hand. The anchored line holds 400 pounds. Climbing it costs movement at half speed, as with any rope.

The barbs hold until someone cuts the line or works the head free from the far end, which takes an action and a successful DC 15 Strength (Athletics) check from a creature standing beside it. Against a creature, the arrow hits as an ordinary arrow for 1d6 Piercing damage, the line falls slack, and the shot is wasted. The head bites timber, brick, mortared stone, and packed earth. Rotten timber, dry-stacked stone, ice, and sail canvas tear during the first 10 feet of climbing as the climber's weight comes on. The line cannot be rewound in the field, does not retract or climb by itself, and the head is not magical after landing.

## At the Table

[[ettore-lupo]] keeps five odd shafts in a felt-lined drawer below his counter at [[Casa Lupo]]. He shows them only when someone asks for a thing he does not stock, and this is the one his hand reaches for first. It solves [[jean-claude-tabarnack]]'s scouting problem when the crew cannot follow him up. One still hangs from a warehouse wall in [[le-paludi]], line and all.

## Provenance

The arrows are made to order for [[ettore-lupo]] out of three shops' work. A turner makes the shaft from [[savia-brentino]]'s straight-grain offcuts at [[Carpenters' Slip]]; [[marta-orsini]] compounds the sprung head at [[Studio Orsini]]; and the silk line comes from [[giacomo-moretti]]'s small-quantity stock at [[Moretti & Sons]]. Ettore buys them in fives twice a year and can identify the buyers of the previous batches.
