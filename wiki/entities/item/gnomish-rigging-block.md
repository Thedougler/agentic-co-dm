# Gnomish Rigging Block

> [!narration] Narration
> An iron frame no bigger than two palms, holding a pair of brass sheaves that spin without sound. The axle housing carries a small wax reservoir plugged with cork, and the whole assembly weighs less than it looks like it should. The sheaves are polished to a dull gold where rope rides them, and the frame shows no rust despite salt crust on every bolt head.

## Mechanics

> [!mechanic]
> **Gnomish Rigging Block [HB].** Functions as a Block and Tackle: when attached to a solid anchor point and used with rope, halves the force needed to lift or move a load. A creature using this block has advantage on Strength checks to lift or haul cargo through it.
>
> The self-greasing axle (a wax reservoir feeds the bearing over months) means no maintenance in salt air, no jamming, and no squealing. Weighs 2.5 lb (half a standard block and tackle). The wax reservoir lasts roughly six months of daily use before needing refill (1 sp).
>
> **Limitations:** the advantage applies to hauling and lifting through the block only — not to grappling, climbing, or any check where the block is not rigged as a mechanical advantage. The brass sheaves are quieter than iron but not silent — a creature listening for rigging noise hears it within 30 feet.

## Provenance

The gnomish crew of the [[nimmik-vollask]] built these for their own cargo cranes first, then started selling them once other crews saw the cranes work. Every block carries a stamped mark on the frame — the same glyph the gnomes put on everything they make.
