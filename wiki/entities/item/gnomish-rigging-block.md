# Gnomish Rigging Block

> [!narration] Narration
> An iron frame no bigger than two palms holds a pair of brass sheaves that spin without sound. The axle housing has a wax reservoir plugged with cork, and the assembly weighs less than it should. Rope has polished the sheaves to dull gold. The frame shows no rust despite salt crust on every bolt head.

*Tool (Block and Tackle), common.*

**Self-greasing block.** The block functions as a Block and Tackle: when attached to a solid anchor and used with rope, it halves the force needed to lift or move a load. A creature using it has Advantage on Strength checks to lift or haul cargo through it. The wax reservoir feeds the bearing for roughly 6 months of daily use before needing a 1-sp refill. The block weighs 2.5 pounds, half a standard block and tackle.

The mechanism needs no maintenance in salt air, does not jam, and does not squeal. Its brass sheaves are quieter than iron but not silent; a creature listening for rigging noise hears them within 30 feet. The Advantage applies only to hauling and lifting through the rigged block, not grappling, climbing, or checks where the block is not providing mechanical advantage.

## At the Table

[[cotter-foss]] sells the blocks from the rail of the [[nimmik-vollask]], whose gnomish crew uses identical blocks on its cargo cranes. The crane that shoots bundles across the gap is Cotter's sales pitch. The block helps any crew hauling cargo, raising sail, or rigging a crane, including the party's own ship maintenance.

## Provenance

The gnomish crew of the [[nimmik-vollask]] built these for its cargo cranes first, then sold them after other crews saw the cranes work. Every block carries the stamped glyph the gnomes put on everything they make.
