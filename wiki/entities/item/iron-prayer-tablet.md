# Iron Prayer Tablet

> [!narration] Iron Prayer Tablet
> A tablet of dark iron, no larger than your palm, its surface covered in finely carved prayers in a script too old or foreign to read at a glance. Decades of handling have worn the edges smooth and polished the center more than anywhere else. The iron has taken on a soft sheen, and the weight sits heavy in the hand for something this small.

## At a Glance

*Document, common.*

The Iron Prayer Tablet is a mundane iron slate that hands the party a written [[valkur]] litany, a false temple provenance, and a few coins of collector interest.

- **What it is.** A cast iron tablet, palm-sized and heavier than it looks, covered in a carved script no one at the table reads at a glance.
- **Where it turns up.** Unplaced loot: a salvage lot, a shrine stall, a chandler's back shelf, or a dead sailor's kit.
- **Why it matters.** It is evidence of private prayer aboard ship, which the [[waveservants]] read as an unlicensed practice and collectors read as temple salvage.

## Properties

**Mundane.** No magical properties, no attunement, no bonus. The tablet is an object with writing on it.

**Read the litany.** The script is a working sailor's shorthand, not a priestly hand. A character who studies the tablet can work it out: **Intelligence (Religion) — `DC 15`** (Study). Success names the text as a [[valkur]] litany for wind and courage; failure spends the time and leaves the hand unread.

**Recognized at a glance.** A [[waveservants|Waveservant]] or any sailor who mutters [[valkur]] at the rail reads the first line without a check.

## At the Table

- **Find it in salvage.** The tablet is priced as temple scrap, worth coppers to most buyers and a purse to the right collector. A buyer who reads it knows exactly what he has.
- **Show it to the Waveservants.** They name the litany at once and take an interest in where a private slate of that weight came from.
- **Pry at the iron.** The back is a curved hull plate with a rivet hole at one corner, which no shrine casting would have.

## Secrets

**Secret.** The tablet is not temple work. It is cast-off hull iron cut by a Scatter sailor who prayed quietly rather than paying at a basin, and the litany is his own copy for wind and courage. The center is polished because he thumbed the same spot for years. The curved back, the rivet hole, and the roughness under the sheen prove it to anyone who turns the tablet over.
