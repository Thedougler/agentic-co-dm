# Roka's Hidden Ledger

> [!narration] Roka's Hidden Ledger
> The journal is unremarkable at first glance, leather-bound and middle-brown, soft from years of handling, an expedition ledger any timber crew foreman might keep. But the entries are carefully organized: expedition dates recorded alongside client names and crew counts, the stated purpose noted, and then a column marked only with creature sketches and tally marks with prices beside each one. The sketches are competent — a [[grung|Grung]] warrior, a jungle cat, something smaller with too many legs. The leather carries the smell of aged fieldwork, and the pages are thick and yellowed at the edges. Beside one sketch the words "[[the-live-hold|live hold]] buyer: `80 gp`" sit in Roka's precise handwriting, and a later margin note reads: *"Boundary scout ([[ussa|Ussa]]) paid `15 gp`. Cut-line drifted north. Repeat within season."* The final entries are from last week.

## At a Glance

*Document, mundane.*

Roka's Hidden Ledger is the private half of his business: what he actually took, who paid for it, and who was paid to look the other way.

- **What it is.** [[roka-sten|Roka Sten]]'s expedition journal, kept separately from his public client roster.
- **What it proves.** Live captures bound for [[the-live-hold|The Live Hold]], prices per specimen, and payments to a boundary scout to misreport timber-cutting lines at the [[verdant-teeth]] fringe.
- **Where it is.** On Roka's person or in his case, not in the shop's records; the public ledger at [[spar-and-snare-outfitters|Spar & Snare Outfitters]] does not mention any of it.
- **Wanted by.** Anyone with a case against trafficking in [[sparhold]], and Roka himself, who stops cooperating the moment someone reaches for it.

## Properties

**Private record.** A mundane journal with no magic, no attunement, and no effect on its own. It is evidence, and the only copy of the evidence.

**What the entries carry.** Expedition dates, client names, crew counts, the purpose each party stated, sketches of creatures with tally marks and prices, and margin notes on payments made outside the trade.

**Reading it.** A reader who understands the trade can follow the pattern in one pass: which expeditions were logged as timber and which came back with live stock. Nothing is code; Roka wrote the truth because he never expected the book to leave his coat.

**Custody.** The journal has no duplicate. Taking it ends Roka's cooperation and gives the party the evidence; leaving it in his hands keeps the lead alive and the proof out of reach.

## At the Table

- **Reach for the ledger.** Roka stops cooperating at once and prioritises recovering or concealing it over anything else, including the lead he was trading. Asking to read it is the same as threatening his business.
- **Read it after taking it.** The entries name the [[the-live-hold|Live Hold]] as the buyer at `80 gp` a specimen and [[ussa|Ussa]] as the paid boundary scout. Both names turn the journal into leverage with two parties who want it quiet.
- **Name the bribe out loud.** The margin note is the part that ends Roka's trade in the settlement: he was paying to move a cut line north, and the scout took `15 gp` to write the report the other way.
- **Use it as leverage without shipping it.** A party that quotes the entry's wording proves it holds the book without showing it, which is enough to get a favour from anyone the journal can hurt.
