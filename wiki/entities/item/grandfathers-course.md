# Grandfather's Course

> [!narration] Grandfather's Course
> The journal's leather cover warps thick with water damage. Salt stains run down the spine like tidemark rings. The pages bloat and stick when turned, the paper swollen, but the spine stays legible: "Grandfather's Course" in gold leaf faded to brass. Inside, the handwriting shows decades of nautical observation. Compass bearings and star positions fill the pages. Notes on currents and shallows. The careful record of someone who read the sea like others read maps. The last entry stops mid-sentence, dated the week before the voyage that was the captain's last.

## At a Glance

*Document, mundane.*

Grandfather's Course is a captain's working journal: decades of bearings, currents, and soundings in a hand that recorded them exactly, pawned for the price of a meal.

- **What it is.** A water-swollen navigation journal of compass bearings, star positions, currents, and shallows, written over decades by [[perrin-black-jaw|Perrin]]'s grandfather.
- **Price.** Pawned for `3 sp` and still unredeemed since Wintertide.
- **Held by.** [[costin-bruma|Costin Bruma]], on the pawn shelves behind the counter at Tally & Trade in [[sparhold]], tagged and drying in harbour air.
- **Wanted by.** [[perrin-black-jaw]], to whom it is worth far more than the slate number.

## Properties

**Mundane document.** No magic, no attunement, and no effect on its own. A character who studies it can use its bearings, star positions, current notes, and shallow soundings to navigate the waters the captain recorded, which makes it a working reference rather than a curiosity.

**Condition.** Water-swollen pages and a warped cover. Turning them takes care; the ink survives, and the spine stamp is still legible.

**Not a chart.** The journal records one captain's tracks over decades rather than a survey of the sea, so it is exact where the captain sailed and silent everywhere else.

## At the Table

- **Read it cold.** A stranger sees a ruined journal and a deadwood pawn three months past deadline, worth the three silver on the slate and nothing more.
- **Recognise the hand.** Perrin knows his grandfather's writing at a glance, which turns the pawn into a recovery rather than a purchase.
- **Buy it back.** Costin holds it for the slate number and names no price twice the same way. He has handled the object and calculated the totals out loud, and he will turn urgency into a reason to raise his number rather than lower it.
- **Use it in the water.** The journal's current notes and shallow soundings are the kind of local knowledge a navigator would pay for, in the waters it covers.
