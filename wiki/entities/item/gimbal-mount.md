# Gimbal Mount

> [!narration] Narration
> A yoke of black iron the size of a helmet hangs inside two brass rings set at right angles. The cradle carries a lead ring at its base and four thumbscrews around the rim. A wagon passes outside and shakes the counter; the rings take the shake while the cradle stays put. Brass shines on both sides of the pivots where hands have turned it.

*Gear (Mount), common.*

**Steady cradle.** Bolting the yoke down takes 10 minutes with smith's tools and four bolts into a rail or beam. The cradle takes a device or weapon weighing up to 60 pounds and holds it with four thumbscrews. The rings keep the cradle level through any pitch or roll a seaworthy hull survives, and it swings a full half-circle by hand. An attack made with a weapon seated in the mount ignores disadvantage from an unstable or moving surface.

The mount cancels deck motion and nothing else. A Prone or Blinded shooter, or a target behind Cover, is resolved normally. Seating or unseating a device costs an action. The mount adds nothing to attack or damage rolls, holds nothing over 60 pounds, and cannot carry the device farther than the mount or the mount farther than its four bolts.

## At the Table

[[kettil-ormsson]] keeps a working mount on the counter at [[Ormsson's Fittings]] to demonstrate the rings. Every gun crew in [[kalowe]] wants one, and the yard buys them in pairs, so the shelf stays empty more weeks than not. It cancels motion-based disadvantage, not the other hazards of firing from a deck.

## Connections

## Provenance

The pattern comes from a Crown swivel-gun yoke that [[kettil-ormsson]] built for years in a northern yard before coming south. He now makes the mounts one at a time and never asks what goes in the cradle.
