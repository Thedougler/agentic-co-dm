# Ashwalls
> [!narration] Narration
> Ashwalls are cold-water volcanic spires with steam vents and warm cave mouths in black stone. Sulfur and salt hang over wreckage lodged in the lee. The caves are the first solid shelter heading west and the last safe lee returning east.


## At a glance
Cold-water volcanic spires: last solid ground heading west and first safe lee returning east; feels hot, sulfurous, and opportunistic.

## Aspects
`volcanic lee, wreck salvage, false rescue`

## Hooks
- Reach the lee after the Galewall; decide whether apparent rescuers are rescuers.
- Salvage a wreck without treating every useful handhold as safe.

## Current state

Pilot families and repair crews work the lee. They treat the spires as a reckoning point, not a destination. Outbound they are the last solid ground before the [[Galewall]]. Inbound they are the first proof of survival.

## Geography

North sits the [[Crown Islands]]. East sits the [[Central Strait]]. South sits the [[Midchain]]. West sits the [[Galewall]]. Water darkens from warm green to cold deep blue before the spires rise.

The Ashwall lee is repair water. Volcanic vent caves stay warm through cold sea air.

## Who

Giant bats roost in warm vent caves. Giant scorpions hold the same warm fissures; a crack that looks like a handhold can run back into occupied dark. Giant vultures work the cliffs after wrecks. Crews tell harpy stories about voices in the storm gaps.

[[Duvane]], a repair-crew carpenter, took a claw and a sting while checking a fouled spar. Two days of poison followed. Ashwall repair work now sends two hands: one to watch, one to work.

## Active powers

Ashwall pilot families keep log books of practical signs. No formal destination authority is named.

## Hatch sign

Crews distinguish two eruptions of lightning. Vertical cloud-to-stone fire is ordinary storm. Lateral fire that branches through ash and holds for a breath is the hatch sign. [[Arclight Phoenix]] birds hatch inside the volcanoes; lightning opens the remaining egg, and the bird flies west into the [[Galewall]] and does not return until it dies in the weather. Old families record: lateral vent-fire, westward departure, expected increase in Galewall activity.

One lightning-burned survivor described a white bird of stormlight in the rigging before the mainmast fell. Burn marks ran along contact points rather than down from a single strike. Iron fittings magnetized hard enough to pull nails.
