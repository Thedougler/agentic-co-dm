## Situation

The Grung raiding fleet moves through reef geography toward [[Karath]], carrying captives while its clans' water power shrinks. The party must choose how to pursue a force that can use channels and supply pressure as much as direct combat. The route matters because following the fleet can save captives and reveal where Grung power is failing.

## What the party knows

> [!narration] Player-facing brief
> The raiders are not taking a straight sea road. Their narrow craft vanish among reefs and channels, and captives are being carried toward Karath. Every day of pursuit spends water and distance while the fleet searches for another passage.

## What is really happening

The fleet is using reef geography and a captive route toward Karath while the clans' water power shrinks. The source does not establish the fleet's exact position, composition, route time, or what happens to the captives on arrival.

## Stakes

* **If the party succeeds.** The fleet is intercepted, its captives can be recovered, and the route's pressure may be exposed.
* **If the party fails.** The fleet reaches more of its route with captives still aboard and preserves its reef advantage.
* **If the party walks away.** The fleet continues toward Karath while the clans' water power remains under pressure.
* **Question.** Can the party force an interception without losing the fleet in the reefs?
* **Question.** What do the captives and route reveal about the Grung clans' shrinking water power?
