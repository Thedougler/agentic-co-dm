# Rattkin Culture

## At a Glance

**Core truth.** Rattkin communities use Runs and Holds to turn ports, bilges, cargo spaces, and drainage routes into a living network. The Warren is their fixed origin point in the current account, but not every Rattkin culture or community is established here.

**Why it matters.** Knowing whether someone is Kin or Dry changes how a Rattkin reads a request, and knowing a person's Run or Hold can open routes that ordinary port maps omit.

**Scope.** This note covers the recorded [[Rattkin]] varieties and vocabulary within the [[the-passage|Passage]] network.

## Current Truth

[[Rattkin]] are the oldest continuous Rattkin presence recorded in every major [[shattered-sea]] port. Their fixed origin point is [[Warren|the Warren]], and their living network is [[the-passage]]. A locked cargo hold is a delay to a Rattkin crew, while a drainage pipe can be a road; a ship's bilge can become a neighborhood.

Rattkin fall into three recorded varieties, each tied to a functional role within a Run:

| Variety | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Black Rat | Climbers, riggers, roofline workers, and elevated port infrastructure workers |
| Brown Rat | Burrowers, Hold-maintainers, and sewer and bilge route mappers |
| Fancy Rat | Social infiltrators and long-term outside contacts |

The vocabulary is shared across the network: **Kin** means other Rattkin; **Dry** means non-Rattkin; a **Run** is a Rattkin community network in a port or city; and a **Hold** is a Passage waypoint or safe house. [[perrin-black-jaw]] is a Fancy Rat Rattkin PC from the [[calven-and-calveno]] Run.

### Limits

* **Exception.** The varieties describe functional roles, not a complete account of every individual Rattkin.
* **Unknown.** The sources do not establish every Rattkin culture or community.
* **Impossible or forbidden.** None established.

## At the Table

* **Players notice.** A Rattkin treats a bilge, drain, roofline, or cargo hold as a connected working space rather than a dead end.
* **This explains.** [[the-passage|The Passage]] can connect places the port's public map treats as separate.
* **This enables.** Once the party learns a Rattkin's Run or Hold, they can ask for access, directions, shelter, or a contact through that network.
* **This warns of.** A route that looks inaccessible to outsiders may already be occupied, observed, or claimed by Kin.
* **Relevant now.** [[perrin-black-jaw]]'s Run affiliation and in-group standing remain live material for his PC arc.

### Party Knowledge

**Known.** The party can recognize that Rattkin use hidden or overlooked routes.

**Suspected.** They may suspect that a local Run can provide access unavailable through public channels.

**Misunderstood.** The party should not assume the three varieties are rigid castes or that every Rattkin community shares one complete culture.

**Last changed.** No witnessed campaign change is recorded.

## If This Is Changing

* **Current pressure.** [[the-passage]] routes are exposed whenever outsiders notice that overlooked spaces connect.
* **Actors.** [[Rattkin]], [[perrin-black-jaw]], and Dry port authorities.
* **Their aims.** Rattkin seek to keep Runs and Holds useful; Dry authorities may seek to regulate or exploit them.
* **Without interference.** Local Rattkin continue using the network while revealing only the routes needed for each task.
* **Visible sign.** A familiar Hold closes, a Run changes its markings, or a Rattkin refuses a previously ordinary crossing.

---

## Consequences

* **Because this is true,** a port obstacle may have a Rattkin route that changes the party's approach.
* **If exposed,** a Hold or Run can become a target for authorities, thieves, or enemies.
* **If disproved,** the party loses confidence in the local account but not necessarily in the wider Passage network.
* **If changed,** [[the-passage]] would alter how Rattkin move between ports.
* **If exploited,** the party can gain access while imposing risk on the Kin who maintain it.
