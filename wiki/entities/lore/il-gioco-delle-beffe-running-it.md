# Il Gioco delle Beffe - Running It

## The Fact

This page holds full table mechanics for the Beffa. It covers how a mark gets picked and how the three-phase skill challenge runs, plus how rival teams split the prize pool once the festival ends. This page is a child of [[il-gioco-delle-beffe]], which holds the festival's premise, current hooks, and consequences.

## The Team Symbol

On Day One, the crew registers its symbol with the committee. Let its members design it themselves: a sigil, a stamp, a wax seal shape, a color band, anything that belongs to them.

Mechanically, the symbol is proof of authorship: the team must leave it at the scene for the prank to count. The last thing the mark finds should be a signature delivered after the fall.

**Beffa doppia (optional):** a rival team can attempt to steal the crew's registered symbol and place it on their own prank. Doing so requires knowing the symbol (possible if they observed registration) and a contested Stealth check against the crew's Perception during Phase 2. It has succeeded exactly twice in the festival's history. Both rulings took three days, and one is still disputed.

## Phase 1 - La Scelta: Design (Day 1)

No checks, pure roleplay. The crew picks its mark and designs its concept, then registers its symbol. Skills the table naturally reaches for include scouting a routine (Investigation or Perception), a market distraction (Performance or Deception), or lifting a prop unnoticed (Sleight of Hand or Stealth), and building a physical piece with a tool check or Athletics works too. A group willing to get inventive might plant false information (Deception or Persuasion) or recruit a local accomplice (Persuasion). Researching a mark's weaknesses (History or Insight) or turning to magic instead (Arcana or a direct spell) rounds out the options.

## Phase 2 - La Preparazione: Setup (Days 2-4, any day chosen)

Each crew member makes one check based on Phase 1 commitments. The crew decides when during the window to strike. Earlier means less risk of sabotage. Later means more prep time and a more relaxed mark.

Complications on failure: a prop is wrong (disadvantage on one Phase 3 check) or the mark spotted something (raise Phase 3 DC by 2). A rival team might notice and try a counter move. A committee adjudicator who observes more than intended raises a pointed question on judgment day.

Rival interference: one rival team may attempt to sabotage preparation with a contested Stealth check against the crew's Perception. If the rival succeeds, assign a complication. If the crew succeeds, they identify the rival and earn a free sabotage action against them in Phase 3.

False-alarm gambit: the crew may deliberately tip off their mark on an early day to lull him into complacency before the real strike. Deception check (DC = mark's Wisdom + 2). Success: the mark's Phase 3 save is at disadvantage. Failure: the mark is now actively suspicious and the DC rises by 3.

> [!check] Perception — Spotting a Committee Adjudicator
> DC 10. Committee adjudicators are bad at staying unseen during Phase 2.
> **Success:** identifies the adjudicator.
> **Failure:** the adjudicator goes unnoticed.

## Phase 3 - L'Esecuzione: The Strike (chosen day)

Each crew member makes one live-pressure check, the execution version of their Phase 2 role. Something can go wrong. The crowd notices, and judges take note. The mark makes a Wisdom save against a DC of 8 plus the number of crew successes in Phase 2. On a failure, the trick fools the mark outright and the prank scores 4-5. Succeeding by 1-4 leaves the mark partially fooled, something felt off, and the prank scores 2-3. Succeeding by 5 or more means the mark spots the trick and the prank scores 0-1, though it still proceeds, and craft and recovery can still win it.

> [!check] Sleight of Hand/Deception — Placing the Symbol
> DC 12. Placing the team symbol during or at the end of the prank without tipping off the mark prematurely.
> **Success:** the crew places the symbol cleanly.
> **Failure:** the mark finds the symbol too early, reducing Surprise by 1.

Recovery check: after the prank, one crew member works to walk it back with a Performance or Persuasion check. Deception or Medicine works as an alternate. Good recovery is worth up to 2 extra craft points from the committee.

## Rival Teams

Present their results at sundown on Day Five: describe them instead of playing them out at the table.

**The Candle Brothers.** Two humans, twelve consecutive entries, four second-place finishes. Technically brilliant, emotionally inert. They will score 13-15 regardless of their prank.

**Serena Voss.** A halfling factor who entered on a dare, going for the [[tessarine-concordat|Tessarine]] factor as her mark. No team, no experience, one very good idea. Roll her score secretly: 2d6+4. If she rolls high, the crew has real competition, and she wants to buy everyone drinks regardless of result.

**Party Unknown.** Registered under a name nobody recognizes and dressed in matching grey coats, this trio declared "Compound / Legendary" and refused to state their mark. Roll their score at Day Five: 1d20. On 1-8, they're disqualified and the crowd loves it. On 9-14, they score 11. On 15-19, they score 16. On a 20, they score 19, which the crew needs to beat or tie to win.

## Running Notes

Ask what each character does, not what the plan is. The crew's plan is their own business. Your job is to assign a skill to whatever they commit to in Phase 1 and hold them to it in Phase 2.

Play the mark: when the prank lands, embody whoever the crew picked. Let good ideas fool you, too. The mark's reaction is the scene.

The window creates tension without any extra effort. On Days 2 and 3, describe marks being jumpy, rivals seen in unexpected places, and committee adjudicators moving through the district. The uncertainty carries the scene on its own.

In Calveno, the Beffa Medallion outweighs the money it comes with: it's social proof that you are clever enough to deceive people in public and charming enough to get away with it, and the Council cannot buy it. [[iacopo-fieschi]] will mention it to [[cosimo-verantio|Cosimo]]. [[prospero-morsani]] will nod at the crew across the Mercatura, and the nod will mean something.
