# Peoples of the Shattered Sea

## At a Glance

**Core truth.** The Shattered Sea's peoples move through ports, crews, islands, and routes in patterns shaped by colonial history, trade, isolation, and the Maw.

**Why it matters.** Demographic presence tells the DM who can plausibly be encountered, hired, questioned, or mistaken for an outsider in each region.

**Scope.** This is a regional sketch, not a census; the named owner pages remain authoritative for each people.

## Current Truth

[[shattered-sea]] is at the edge of the colonial world. The crossing west of the [[galewall]] reaches the known: Chult first, then the kingdoms, records, and recognized law of Faerûn. East of the Galewall lies [[verdant-scatter|the Scatter]], and the Scatter does not ask where you came from. The further east you travel, the less the colonial map means. The [[crown-islands]] are the western homelands transplanted to warmer water. [[kalowe]] and the [[Midchain]] are those homelands losing the ability to enforce anything, and [[tail|the Tail]] and the [[outer-reach]] are what was here before the homelands ever arrived.

**The Crown Islands.**

*Dravosi colonial core. The western homelands with a tropical accent.*

The oldest colonial foothold in the Scatter. The majority population is [[human|human]] and came with the [[dravosi-crown]] flag. [[Rattkin]] are present in every Crown port even though the Crown's relationship with [[the Passage]] is adversarial — the [[Warren]] prefers it that way.

| Species | Presence | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Human | Dominant | Dravosi colonial stock; also Tessarine merchants and independent families. |
| Halfling | Common | Chandlers, bookkeepers, small merchants. |
| Dwarf | Common | Naval engineering, fortification work, armaments manufacture. |
| [[Rattkin]] | Common | Port infrastructure, message networks, [[the Passage]]. Officially discouraged; practically irreplaceable. |
| Gnome | Present | Instrument makers, cartographers, Tessarine ledger work. |
| Tiefling | Present | Mixed colonial heritage. More visible in [[calven-and-calveno]] than in Tidefall. |
| Orc | Present | Dock crews, ship labour, garrison soldiers. Often underranked relative to competence. |
| Dragonborn | Present | Scattered across military and merchant roles. |
| Aasimar | Rare | Occasional pilgrims and Tyr-aligned advocates. |
| [[Aarakocra]] | Rare | Mostly Sentinels. [[high-eyrie]] maintains a small permanent population near the eastern Crown approaches. |
| Other | Rare | The Galewall crossing drops all kinds onto Crown docks. Most do not stay long. |

**The [[central-strait]].**

*The main highway. The most transient, and therefore the most everything.*

Not a place people live — a place people pass through. Port [[calders-tooth-and-port-tidefall]] processes hundreds of ships a year. [[calven-and-calveno]] turns over its dockside population on the tide and skews more diverse than the rest of the [[crown-islands]] — the [[tessarine-concordat]] does not care about heritage, only credit.

| Tiefling | Present | Tessarine merchant families in particular. |

| Dragonborn | Present | Military and merchant both. |
| Water Genasi | Present | Natural affinity for this work. Unremarkable on a working ship. |

| Kenku | Present | Port scavengers, couriers, and fast verbal relay. |
| Aasimar | Rare | — |

**The Midchain.**

*Free ports and local councils. The colonial map stops meaning much here.*

The [[Midchain]] has been occupied by too many different peoples for too long for any one of them to claim it. The free ports and reef-knowledge economy select for people who can work outside recognized law. Humans remain common but are not dominant.

**The [[verdant-teeth]].**

*[[Grung]] territory. The beach is as far as outsiders go.*

The Verdant Teeth interior belongs to the [[grung-clans|Grung Clans]]. The coastline operates a strict beach-trade system: outsiders come to the waterline, goods change hands, outsiders leave. The clans do not negotiate on this.

| Species | Presence | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| [[Grung]] | Dominant | Interior and island communities. All castes present. |
| Visiting traders | Present | At designated beaches only. No overnight presence. |
| [[Tabaxi]] | Rare | Southeastern approaches. The [[Grung]] and the [[Tabaxi]] have a history neither translates clearly for outsiders. |

**The Tail.**

*Fishing communities and salvage crews at the edge of the charts.*

No colonial administration — only pilot families, producing a working meritocracy defined by capability rather than heritage. The Maw's proximity draws the [[sentinels-of-the-eyrie]] and anyone with elemental affinity who has felt the Maw's pull and cannot stay away.

| Species | Presence | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Human | Common | Longline crews, salvage camp labour, staging island residents. |
| Orc | Common | Physical work in hard conditions. |
| Tortle | Common | Long-lived, cold-resistant, comfortable at depth. Ideal salvage crew. |
| Goliath | Common | Cliff-work, heavy salvage, cold-water diving. Tail communities have had Goliath pilot families for generations. |
| [[sea-elf]] | Present | [[halythion]] contact. Surface occasionally near the [[Shelfworks]] approaches. |
| [[Aarakocra]] | Present | Sentinels of the Eyrie maintain watch presence here. |
| Water Genasi | Present | Drawn to the Maw-adjacent water. |
| Air Genasi | Present | Navigation in fog and bad weather. |
| Fire Genasi | Rare | Occasionally drawn here by the Maw's elemental bleed. |
| Halfling | Rare | Provisioning. The staging islands need someone to run the stores. |
| Rattkin | Rare | No major Run. Individual operators working salvage information routes. |
| Triton | Rare | Deep-water presence near the [[Shelfworks]]. Encounter rather than community. |

**The Sunken Crown and Outer Reach.**

*Past the Maw. The oldest maps and the newest ignorance.*

The [[sunken-crown]] is [[Tabaxi]] territory. The [[keth-naar|Keth-Naar]] community has been here longer than any colonial record acknowledges; it does not seek outside contact, it manages it when it arrives. Beyond the Blue Hole, the Outer Reach belongs to whatever was here before anyone started naming things.

| Species | Presence | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Tabaxi | Dominant | Keth-Naar and its island network. Isolated from other Tabaxi populations for generations. |
| [[sea-elf]] | Rare | [[halythion]] trade contact. Respectful, careful, usually carrying specific purpose. |
| Triton | Rare | Deep-water beyond the Blue Hole. |
| Water Genasi | Rare | Drawn to the Blue Hole's elemental behaviour. |
| Other outsiders | Rare | Maw-crossers, pilgrims, salvagers, the lost. Keth-Naar has protocols for all of them. |

**On the Water.**

Ships are their own demographic. A working crew in the Scatter does not look like any port's population — it looks like whoever the captain could hire and keep. A Midchain free-trade crew might be half orc and tortle with a rattkin pilot and a grung surgeon who does not discuss what caste they left. A Crown naval vessel runs formally human with dwarf engineers and a rattkin aboard in an unofficial capacity everyone pretends not to notice. A Passage runner has no species requirement and one rule. The ship is the smallest unit of real integration in the Scatter — it works because it has to.

### Limits

* **Exception.** Presence terms describe likely visibility, not fixed population counts or legal status.
* **Unknown to the world.** The page does not provide a census or complete demographic history for every port and crew.
* **Impossible or forbidden.** No people are categorically absent from all sea routes; isolation describes patterns, not an absolute ban.

## At the Table

* **Players notice.** A port's people, a ship's mixed crew, and the difference between Crown, Midchain, Verdant Teeth, Tail, and Outer Reach presence establish where the party is.
* **This explains.** Why a named ancestry may be common in one region, rare in another, and still plausible on a ship far from its homeland.
* **This enables.** The party can seek a pilot, interpreter, craftsperson, trader, or witness by reading the region's presence pattern.
* **This warns of.** A port's majority does not tell the party who holds authority, and a beach-trade presence does not imply permission to enter inland.
* **Relevant now.** The index connects the party's routes through [[crown-islands]], [[central-strait]], [[Midchain]], [[verdant-teeth]], [[tail]], and the [[outer-reach]].

### Party Knowledge

**Known.** The Shattered Sea has strong regional demographic patterns but ships mix peoples beyond any port's typical majority.

**Suspected.** The party may suspect that presence terms conceal local power relationships and exceptions.

**Misunderstood.** “Present” does not mean welcomed, and “rare” does not mean unknown.

**Last changed.** No session is recorded.

### Common telling

> “A port tells you who lives there. A ship tells you who can leave.”

## If This Is Changing

* **Current pressure.** Colonial enforcement weakens eastward while trade, salvage, migration, and Maw pressure keep moving people through the routes.
* **Actors.** Port councils, colonial authorities, captains, Grung clans, the Sentinels, and the communities listed in each regional table.
* **Their aims.** Authorities want records and control, crews want capability, and isolated communities want to decide when outsiders may approach.
* **Without interference.** Mixed crews and transient ports continue to blur demographic boundaries while regional authority remains uneven.
* **Visible sign.** A new crew composition, changed beach protocol, or unfamiliar port majority appears in a place previously described as stable.

## Consequences

* **Because this is true,** the DM can populate a port or crew from its region without treating the table as a census.
* **If exposed,** a character's ancestry can open or close assumptions about local language, law, work, and welcome.
* **If disproved,** the affected regional table and its owner pages would need reconciliation.
* **If changed,** migration, colonial control, and crew composition would alter the party's route pressures.
* **If exploited,** the party can choose a port, crew, or contact whose regional presence makes a goal more plausible.
