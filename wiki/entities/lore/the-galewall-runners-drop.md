# The Galewall Runner's Drop

## At a Glance

**Core truth.** Missing colonial-era [[dravosi-crown]] payroll records support a story that a privateer hid stolen lockboxes on one of the volcanic [[ashwall-islands|Ashwall Islands]] after the [[galewall]] destroyed the escape ship. The privateer's name, the island, and the cache's survival are unknown.

**Why it matters.** The legend offers a possible treasure lead and a way to connect Dravosi records, volcanic navigation, and a heist whose evidence may still be recoverable.

**Scope.** The claim concerns one early-colonial payroll heist and one unconfirmed cache, not every lost Dravosi shipment.

## Current Truth

An infamous but unnamed privateer robbed a Dravosi payroll fleet during the early colonial occupation. The crew tried to outrun the Admiralty by sailing into the permanent storm belt; the Galewall shattered the ship's masts and forced it back out, sinking and ruined.

Before the wreck disappeared, the crew reportedly rowed heavy lockboxes onto black, sulfur-vented shores of an Ashwall Island. Missing Dravosi payroll records corroborate the heist, but no record identifies the island or confirms a recovery. A massive rat carving may have marked the drop site, though volcanic activity may have buried, eroded, or submerged it.

### Limits

* **Exception.** Payroll records corroborate a missing shipment, not the cache's location or survival.
* **Unknown.** The privateer's name, island, carving, and current state of the lockboxes remain unknown.
* **Impossible or forbidden.** Do not place the cache on a specific island or declare it recovered before play establishes that fact.

## At the Table

* **Players notice.** Missing payroll entries, sulfur vents, shifting volcanic landmarks, and a rat-with-extended-hand carving can point toward the same legend.
* **This explains.** Why old Dravosi records contain a gap and why Ashwall navigators treat certain black shores as unstable.
* **This enables.** The characters can compare Admiralty records, search volcanic shores, or bargain with anyone holding a surviving clue.
* **This warns of.** Toxic vents require protection for extended exploration, and seasonal rock shifts can erase a route or landmark.
* **Relevant now.** The drop is an unraised, speculative lead that can become actionable when a matching payroll record or carving appears.

## If This Is Changing

* **Current pressure.** Volcanic activity and shifting landmarks are steadily destroying or hiding evidence.
* **Actors.** [[dravosi-crown]], treasure hunters, Ashwall navigators, and anyone carrying a surviving payroll clue.
* **Their aims.** The Crown would recover or suppress the loss; hunters would locate the cache; navigators would survive the approach.
* **Without interference.** The carving and any exposed lockboxes remain vulnerable to burial, erosion, or submergence.
* **Visible sign.** A newly exposed carving, payroll fragment, or changed shoreline makes a specific search newly plausible.

## Consequences

* **Because this is true,** a missing payroll entry can be evidence rather than mere accounting error.
* **If exposed,** the Dravosi Crown must account for an old loss and its failure to recover the funds.
* **If disproved,** the records still establish a missing shipment but not a privateer cache.
* **If changed,** the identity of the privateer or location of the cache would redirect Ashwall history.
* **If exploited,** a false carving or forged payroll fragment could lure explorers into toxic vents.
