# Two-Grave Orders

> [!abstract] At a Glance
> **Core truth.** The old Gold-caste orders sent lower-caste Grung inland through [[aruhe]] in layers: first to survey and report, then to replace parties that did not return, and finally to find and destroy two graves. The fragments do not yet explain what the graves contain or why destroying them mattered.
>
> **Why it matters.** The dead were not one failed expedition. Someone kept converting loss into another command, and the party can decide whether to repeat that logic, expose it, or refuse it.
>
> **Scope.** This order trail belongs to the Grung expeditions whose spent [[grung-authority-seal]]s lie along the inland routes; it does not prove that every Grung obeyed or that Aruhe compelled every death.

## Current Truth

The oldest surviving seal fragments repeat an inland reporting duty. They direct red-caste and lower-caste Grung to enter Aruhe, observe what they find, and return the findings to Gold-caste authority. This extends the existing evidence on [[grung-authority-seal]] without changing the seal's spent status.

Later fragments record replacement parties after earlier groups stopped reporting. The wording treats missing Grung as a staffing problem rather than as a reason to stop. The newest readable layer changes the objective: identify the two graves beneath the memorial tree and destroy them. The order trail stops there. It does not name [[Hinewai]], identify the [[memorial-grove|Death Bloom]], or reveal the full mechanism that joins the graves to Aruhe.

The layers show four different relationships to obedience. A seal can impose an order. Grung caste and sealing practice can make authority feel inherited even when rank is not permanent. Survivors such as [[Celia Parel]] and [[Oren Vask]] can choose to follow Aruhe's rules because those rules keep people alive. A person can also refuse an order, a route, a living claim, or the assumption that the dead are expendable.

> [!warning] Limits
> * **Established boundary.** The seal fragments support the progression from reporting to replacement to the two-grave objective; they do not provide a complete translation of every Gold-caste line.
> * **Unknown.** The original Gold-caste motive, the identity of the officer who issued the final order, and what the Grung expected to find under the tree remain unknown.
> * **Forbidden conclusion.** Do not reveal the full Death Bloom or phylactery logic from these scraps alone. The session should establish that two graves matter, not solve why they matter.

## At the Table

* **Players notice:** Spent seals lie beside different generations of Grung dead, and the scraps become more urgent as the bodies become newer: report, replace, find, destroy.
* **This explains:** Why Aruhe contains multiple eras of Grung dead and why the latest survivors heard that someone was coming for graves rather than fruit.
* **This enables:** The party can ask [[jean-claude-tabarnack]] to compare authority marks, ask [[Celia Parel]] to sort the fragments, or question [[Oren Vask]] about the route without needing one perfect translation.
* **This warns of:** The party is being invited to treat a person, a memorial, or a living place as an objective. A command can be old, official, and still wrong.
* **Relevant now:** [[talon-skarn]] is turning the same confusion into leverage around [[fate-spinner]] and the party's carried [[ghost-plum]]s.
