# The Vault of the First Factor

## At a Glance

**Core truth.** Oral tradition claims that the first [[tessarine-concordat|Tessarine Concordat]] envoy built a hidden depository on an uncharted limestone spire in the [[Midchain]], storing collateral seized from debtors who could not meet their notes. The vault's existence and contents remain unconfirmed.

**Why it matters.** If the legend is true, the vault could expose the Concordat's old creditors, recover valuable collateral, or provide leverage over the institution's present representatives.

**Scope.** The claim concerns the interior of the Midchain and the earliest Concordat structure, not the location of every Tessarine holding.

## Current Truth

The [[tessarine-concordat]] keeps its regional wealth concealed rather than displaying it openly in [[calven-and-calveno]]. The legend places a hidden depository on a jungle-choked limestone spire somewhere in the Midchain, holding masterwork weapons, magical family heirlooms, and trade-bars surrendered by captains, governors, and merchants.

No current Tessarine official confirms the vault. Stories say its protections are magical and lethal to anyone without proper Tessarine credentials, but no reliable witness has described the defences or returned with proof.

### Limits

* **Exception.** The vault may be a conflation of several old collateral stores rather than one site.
* **Unknown.** Its exact location, surviving contents, credentials, and defences are not established.
* **Impossible or forbidden.** The current canon does not support placing the vault on a map or declaring its contents recovered.

## At the Table

* **Players notice.** Ledger gaps, old debt records, and oral accounts repeatedly point toward an uncharted limestone spire in the Midchain.
* **This explains.** Why Concordat officials refuse to discuss certain historical collateral and why some inherited objects have no clear chain of custody.
* **This enables.** The characters can investigate debt records, question Concordat factors, or follow a physical collateral trail toward the Midchain.
* **This warns of.** A credential may be the difference between entering the vault and becoming another unrecorded casualty.
* **Relevant now.** The legend is a dormant lead; it becomes actionable when the party encounters a matching debt, heirloom, or Tessarine credential.

## If This Is Changing

* **Current pressure.** The vault's legend is vulnerable to both deliberate Concordat suppression and opportunistic treasure hunters.
* **Actors.** [[tessarine-concordat]], its regional factors, and any debtor or heir seeking lost collateral.
* **Their aims.** Concordat agents would keep the legend contained; outsiders would confirm the site or exploit its contents.
* **Without interference.** The legend remains contradictory hearsay and produces no reliable route.
* **Visible sign.** A new debt record, recovered heirloom, or credential bearing the same early Tessarine mark would make the legend actionable.

---

## Consequences

* **Because this is true,** old collateral may still connect present institutions to forgotten debtors.
* **If exposed,** the Concordat must explain why its earliest envoy maintained a lethal hidden store.
* **If disproved,** searches based on the legend waste time and may damage trust in the source.
* **If changed,** [[tessarine-concordat]] would face a new public claim over its historical assets.
* **If exploited,** a forged credential or debt record could draw investigators into a lethal defence.
