# Aruhe

_The Hungry Isle._

![[aruhe-hungry-isle-v3.jpg|Aruhe / Hungry Isle]]

## Look

From offshore, Aruhe is a vast green volcanic island in deep blue water. Forested ridges fold around a dark crater lake, gold-tan bands cut the lower slopes, pale beaches break the cliffs, and white water works a reef rim. No town, road, field, or smoke marks the island.

## Situation now

Aruhe is roughly 500 miles long and 150 miles across at its waist. Survivors of the Calveno raid are scattered inland, while two survivors remain aboard [[Uncertainty]]. The island is not a single monster or a wasteland; it is an excessive living ecosystem of growth, healing, adaptation, feeding, and rot.

Hinewai's grief at [[Aruhe - Memorial Grove]] binds the island's law. Fallen fruit and genuine offerings may be received. Taking fruit from a living branch, cutting growth, trapping an animal, fishing for a carryable catch, or treating the island as property draws surrounding life into hostility. [[Taking on Aruhe]] adjudicates that response.

## Moves that change the scene

- Land through the reef gap at [[Aruhe - Beach|Western Landing]] and continue inland through [[Aruhe - Old Gardens|the Old Gardens]].
- Follow water from [[Aruhe - Grasslands|the Grasslands]] toward [[Aruhe - River]] and [[Aruhe - Clear Lake]].
- Approach [[Aruhe - Memorial Grove]] from the grasslands, lake edge, or marshes.
- Observe from offshore to preserve distance, but lose information from the landing.
- Move carefully around [[Aruhe - Razer-Grass]], [[Aruhe - Snakewood]], and [[Aruhe - Spiritpollen]].

## Connections

The island's continuous ecosystem runs from Western Landing through the terraces, grasslands, Quiet, river, lake, marshes, mangroves, memorial grove, and lava tubes. Water is the closest thing Aruhe has to a road, but current, terrain, animals, and living ownership prevent it from becoming safe infrastructure.

## Purpose

Aruhe matters because the raid broke here, survivors still need rescue, and a possible [[Tangle]] anchor must be surveyed without treating the island as property. Reaching its heart takes days or weeks, not a short inland walk.
