# The Countless

Sentinels of the Eyrie / Sith-schism reframe, Old-Republic flavor. The
Sentinels follow the currents of fate without disrupting them — record
without conclusion, presence changes the record. **The Countless** are the
schism that kept the reach and dropped the discipline: they use it to bend
fate to their own benefit, and make sure nothing is ever counted against
them. Replaces "the Uncounted" wholesale — name, front, members, method, all
retired, not reused.

---

Long Reach and Long Sight are not the same thing, and conflating them was
the first draft's mistake. **Long Reach** is common — the Eldritch Claw
Tattoo every Sentinel apprentice earns at the end of training. **Long
Sight** is rare and withheld — not normally given to apprentices at all.
Crissdalynn has early, partial access only because the dreidel (Fate
Spinner) unlocked it, and only because Kyzil was worried enough about her
unusually dangerous pilgrimage to arrange that. He never explained to her
what it actually does or how far it reaches. A live secret sitting between
them, not backstory.

---

**[[talon-vantyrus|Talon Vantyrus]]** — the master. Snowy owl [[aarakocra|Aarakocra]]:
pale, cold, regal, against Kyzil's warm tawny-brown. He was Kyzil's own
teacher at the High Eyrie — Dooku to Kyzil's Qui-Gon. He came to see "record without
conclusion" as moral cowardice, people dying in front of an order that only
ever wrote it down. When he broke from the Sentinels he tried to take his
star student with him. Kyzil refused. Vantyrus reads that refusal as a
failure of nerve; Kyzil reads Vantyrus's compassion as ego dressed as
virtue. Neither of them has stopped believing they were right — that's the
wound this whole thread runs on, not a clean villain motive.

---

**[[talon-skarn|Talon Skarn]]** — the apprentice. Peregrine falcon
Aarakocra, incredible speed and ferocity — the one who actually
crosses blades with Kyzil, where
Vantyrus wouldn't. Trained by Vantyrus while openly scheming to kill him;
not a secret between them, an ongoing live contest. Open thread, not yet
answered: what Skarn personally wants out of it — whether he shares
Vantyrus's goal (the Soul Incarnate technique) or his ambition is narrower
and more immediate (raw power, besting Kyzil specifically, something else
entirely).

---

Rule of Two, sharpened past the trope: not passive tradition, active
doctrine. The apprentice studies under the master while actively scheming
to overthrow him. The master teaches her everything while treating her
scheme as the standing threat it is and defending himself against it. If he
ever fails that defense, the failure *is* the verdict — he was never worthy
of what he held. Power in the Countless is proven by surviving your own
student, never inherited by rule.

---

The taken-name idea: Sentinels mark the end of training with the Long Reach
tattoo. The Countless mark their break from the order the same way, with a
name taken to replace the one they carried at the [[high-eyrie|High Eyrie]] —
explains why "Talon Vantyrus" and "Talon Skarn" sound nothing like
"Kyzil." **Talon** as the shared title says what they are twice over — no
ledger anywhere records them, and the bird-of-prey identity is worn as an
epithet, not just a species.

---

The plot engine: the Countless's shared want is the
[[soul-incarnate-statblock|Soul Incarnate]]'s transformation technique — still
a Sentinels-guarded secret
([[sentinels-of-the-eyrie|Sentinels of the Eyrie]], status: canon). Kyzil,
as senior master, is the real opposition — not a bystander to someone else's
confrontation. Working theory, not yet confirmed at the table: Crissdalynn's
illicit Long Sight is valuable to Vantyrus specifically because it's a way
to glimpse toward that secret before the order even notices something's
moving — she's the crack in an otherwise airtight guard, and the reason is
personal to her (her own withheld gift), not incidental.

The party's first meeting with Skarn: not a duel, a theft. He stoops —
silent, high enough to be nothing but a speck against the sun, then
suddenly *there* mid-strike with no warning beat between — and goes for the
dreidel itself, not for blood. Falcon biology backs the read up for free: a
real peregrine's stoop isn't just a strike, it's strike-and-carry, prey
taken away in the talons. One or two vicious exchanges too fast to track
cleanly, then he disengages and climbs out before it becomes a fight he'd
have to finish either way.

Whether he actually gets the dreidel is left to the table, not
predetermined — real dice, the same way this campaign already resolves its
big moments. DCs pitched exceptionally high, biasing the roll toward his
success without guaranteeing it.
