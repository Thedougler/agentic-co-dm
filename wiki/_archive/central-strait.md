# Central Strait

![[central-strait-banner]]

[[master-kyzil|Master Kyzil]]'s search for [[crissdalynn-khinriss|Crissdalynn]] passed through here on his way from [[midchain|Midchain]]. See [[midchain|Midchain]] for the full account.

The Central Strait is the main trade route through [[verdant-scatter|the Scatter]] — a genuine open sea, not a channel, roughly the scale of a real-world inland sea. It runs some five hundred to six hundred miles between the [[crown-islands|Crown Islands]] to the north and [[midchain|Midchain]] to the south, a five-day crossing in a fast ship, and stretches sixteen hundred to twenty-four hundred miles west to east, a week or two end to end for the fastest ship afloat. No reef line or narrows forces a course anywhere along it; it stays open and roughly uniform in width, calmer and more heavily used than the ocean beyond either flanking archipelago, the region's shipping highway rather than a defended channel. Near both shores the water runs clear, pale blue-green, revealing the sandy bottom on good days, with depth from eighty to two hundred feet where the shelf still holds. Toward the middle the shelf drops away, the water darkens, and the charts thin out. Spinner dolphins and flying fish work the lanes within sight of land; past that, for days at a stretch, nothing breaks the horizon at all. The Scatter Current flows steadily east through the whole crossing, adding half a knot to a full knot of speed for eastbound ships.

Almost everything moving between the colonial homelands and [[midchain|Midchain]] uses the Strait. [[tidefall|Port Tidefall]] collects its price at the western end, extending its [[inspection-pier|Inspection Pier]] across the channel. Nothing enters under Crown law without pausing to answer questions and sign documentation. Eastbound, the shores draw back together as the Strait feeds into [[tail|the Tail]], and the current speeds up before [[stenmark|Stenmark]]'s cliffs appear.

## Routes & Access

**The Tidefall Gate.** The western entrance is the navigable water between [[calders-tooth|Calder's Tooth]] and the northernmost [[midchain|Midchain]] reefs. Entering the Gate means entering Crown jurisdiction. Ships that try to skirt it without presenting papers find the Crown patrols already know which reef gap they favor.

**The Blue Lane.** The established shipping route follows the strongest line of the Scatter Current, roughly equidistant between the shores. It remains unmarked but universal: the deepest, fastest line across the Strait, and for most of its length the only company a ship keeps is other traffic running the same line — both coasts drop below the horizon for days before either one climbs back into view.

**The Calven Roads.** Sheltered water south of [[calven|Calven]] island provides one of the better northern-Strait anchorages for ships waiting out weather or tide. [[calveno|Calveno]]'s canal city is visible from here on clear days.

**The Harwick Reach.** Deeper water south of [[harwick|Harwick]] is where Crown patrol frigates stage, close enough to shore that a ship can still see the [[harwick|Harwick Naval Yard]]'s masts in calm conditions.

**Sandtable Shoal.** A sandbank in the middle Strait sits south of [[aldenmere|Aldenmere]], with the bottom rising from two hundred feet to under forty feet over about two miles and shifting the Blue Lane slightly south. A [[kalowe|Kalowe]] operator holds the Crown salvage contract, though the [[harwick|Harwick]] Admiralty remains dissatisfied with this arrangement.

**The Kalowe Run.** The angled approach from the Blue Lane south to [[kalowe|Kalowe]] cuts through the northern [[midchain|Midchain]] reef approaches. Pilots steer by agreed-upon waypoints, not formal channels.

### Landmarks

Every landmark below is a near-shore sighting — something read on the way in or out, at either end of a crossing. Nothing is visible from mid-Strait; that's the whole width away.

**[[fort-crestwall|Fort Crestwall]].** The fortified Crown post above [[tidefall|Port Tidefall]] is visible from the western approach on clear days.

**The Pale City.** [[calveno|Calveno]] becomes visible as a ship nears the northern shore, pale stone facades catching morning light. The city's height above the waterline makes it legible at distances where most settlements disappear.

**The Yard Masts.** The standing masts and shipway cranes of the [[harwick|Harwick]] Naval Yard become visible as a ship nears the northern shore, when the weather cooperates.

**The Eyrie Light.** The [[high-eyrie|High Eyrie]] sea stack is visible from the eastern approach on clear days as a dark vertical mark on the northeastern horizon. Spotting it confirms you hold the right course to avoid the worst of the Spillback. Pilots call it the Eyrie Light because it confirms position, not because it carries an actual light.

**Stenmark Tower.** The Crown signal station sits on the eastern cliff of [[stenmark|Stenmark]], marking the approach into [[tail|the Tail]] and Crown waters. A signal flag warns when [[the-drowned-maw|the Drowned Maw]] acts odd.

## Hazards

Reef sharks hunt shallow water on both coasts. Hunter sharks patrol deeper lanes near [[harwick|Harwick]]. Giant octopuses hide in wreckage around Sandtable Shoal and the Spillback. Rumors say the [[sawek|Sawek]] is the Strait's top predator. It favors the dark southern [[midchain|Midchain]] approaches, but its range is unknown.

Piracy often uses paperwork as a cover, with small crews lurking at the Tidefall Gate where ships pause for checks. Merchants fear the captain of the [[the-bad-receipt|Bad Receipt]] along the south, a Kalowe-built ship that hides behind false papers. When [[the-glass-debt|the Glass Debt]] or [[the-velvet-noose|Velvet Noose]] appear near the Strait, convoys band together and Crown patrols drop their usual routines.

## Geography

The Strait holds a roughly uniform five hundred to six hundred miles north to south along almost its whole length, with no chokepoint anywhere in the crossing. Only at the very eastern end, where the water deepens sharply and the shelf drops away into [[the-drowned-maw|the Drowned Maw]], do the two arcs of [[verdant-scatter|the Scatter]] finally draw together at [[tail|the Tail]]. Wind and tide rule here. Winds blow northeast to southwest, helping eastbound ships but forcing westbound ships to fight the full length. Fog gathers at night in the east and clears by mid-morning. The western mouth stays clearer.

**The Scatter Current** runs east through the full passage. Its strength peaks in mid-channel and the Blue Lane, weakens near reef approaches. At the eastern end it turns north in a bend pilots call the Cold Turn (or the Fool's Assist for those who watched someone else miss it). Miss the turn. Ships that miss it ride north instead of through. Consequences worsen as they approach [[stenmark|Stenmark]]'s cliffs.

## Trade

The Strait is the region's trade center, with deep water and the Scatter Current helping trade flow. Colonial cargo moves through and ships get checked at entry, while free-port traffic uses the route alongside salvage contracts and escorted convoys. Tidefall collects fees and fishing feeds sea predators.

Dravosi checks at Tidefall act as gates, allowing the Crown to tax trade and gather knowledge. Smugglers win when timing is right and papers look good, but delays and seized cargo raise pressure as weather offers chances for illegal trade. Convoy timing affects politics.
