# Giacinto

An elderly gnome craftsman, late 60s, who works a booth in Calveno's [[velo-quarter|Velo Quarter]]. He smells of spirit gum. He sells beret-and-moustache kits that read as "performer" gear to the festival crowds packing the city.

He recognizes his own handiwork on sight, a moustache he's sold by its hand-cut inner edge. He treats a customer's disguise as an ongoing professional relationship: he'll offer to re-seal a lifting edge without prompting, unprompted, in the tone of a doctor checking a healing wound. He isn't suspicious of anyone wearing his work, only professionally invested in it. His working theory about why his kits pass as ordinary in a festival crowd is precise: *"The moustache says 'I am in a costume.' The beret says 'I do not care that you know.' Together? invisible. Don't change anything."*

The kits are more than a costume: paired with a beret and moustache, they let a wearer read as one of the city's performer-travelers instead of drawing notice. A social invisibility field in a Beffa-heavy crowd. This makes Giacinto a key beat for [[jean-claude-tabarnack|Jean-Claude]]'s cover in Calveno.

Currently preparing ten sets of accessories for registered [[il-gioco-delle-beffe|Beffa]] teams this week. A ready reason he's occupied at the booth during festival week.

He wants his craft acknowledged and properly maintained. The moustache's inner-edge seal is lifting on the right side.

## Scene: The Moustache Craftsman

His booth is a narrow stall between a wig maker and a vendor of theatrical smoke, in the [[velo-quarter|Velo Quarter]].

> [!read-aloud]
> A narrow booth between a wig maker and a vendor of theatrical smoke. Prosthetics, false beards, and a row of spectacles cover the boards. The man inside is not wearing them. The spirit gum smell reaches you from three feet away. He has not looked up.

He does not look up immediately. When he does, his eyes go straight to the moustache, not the face. He points. "That's one of mine. Nobody else cuts the inner edge that way. Who are you wearing it for?"

He will not accept "I made it myself"; he is not suspicious of the wearer, only professionally invested. When told the moustache is a disguise with a principle as fundamental as [[grung|Grung]] caste-color identity, one that remained true across fourteen ports, he stops and reframes it: "That's wrong. And I'm going to explain why."

He does not tell the wearer to take the moustache off. He thinks it's doing real work, just not the work they believe. The beret draws more attention than a disguise should; together, the combination reads as "performer," which functions as invisible in a festival city.

**In play:**

- He takes out a small box of spirit gum and starts reapplying it to the moustache's corners before anyone has agreed to this. He has assumed permission.
- If asked how he knows which signals humans read: *"I've made faces for fifty years. I know what people look at."*
- If another PC tries to argue the disguise doesn't work: Giacinto disagrees with them, loudly, on professional grounds.
