# The Drowned Maw

![[the-drowned-maw-narration-appearance]]

![[the-drowned-maw-banner]]

The Drowned Maw is a north-south trench running along the eastern edge of the [[verdant-scatter|Verdant Scatter]], 40 to 90 miles across. The boundary isn't marked on most charts. One watch you're in trade winds, the next there's nothing. Blue-green Scatter shallows give way to something deeper and less defined, a blue with no bottom in it. The surface goes flat, not calm, still in a way open ocean normally isn't, with no current running over it. Seabirds that follow ships across the Scatter turn back at the edge. Fish won't school here, and no sounding line has found the bottom.

Compasses drift within five miles and don't recover until you're through. Most captains sail the crossing by stars and run it fast. Nobody anchors over the Maw. That advice circulates without much explanation, and experienced sailors don't ask for one.

Ships cross routinely. It's the only route east to [[outer-reach|the Outer Reach]], and most have no trouble. The ones that do have trouble come back quiet, which tells its own story.

The western wall, below the salvage tier, is Imperial-restricted water. The [[antheri|Antheri]] built the [[antheri-ruins|Antheri Ruins]] there, sunk to where the mortal sea touches the [[elemental-plane-of-water|Elemental Plane of Water]]. A physical rift opens at the bottom, and every foot it widens is water [[umberlee|Umberlee]] no longer claims. Once quiet, the seepage became a pull the moment the [[pearl-of-souls|Pearl of Souls]] sank near it, and the [[leviathan|Leviathan]] forced through soon after, small next to what's still pushing.

## Geography

**Extent:** a north-south trench 40 to 90 miles across, running the length of the [[verdant-scatter|Verdant Scatter]]'s eastern edge.

**Topography:** the boundary isn't marked on any chart. Shallow blue-green Scatter water gives way, one watch to the next, to a flat, current-less blue with no bottom any sounding line has found. The western wall steps down through the [[shelfworks|Shelfworks]] salvage tier to the [[antheri-ruins|Antheri Ruins]], where a widening rift opens onto the [[elemental-plane-of-water|Elemental Plane of Water]].

## Trade

Salvage is the region's real economy. Crews work the [[shelfworks|Shelfworks]] most weeks for what the Antheri ruins still hold, and the rim lanes collect everyone that trade draws: buyers, rivals, and predators working the same water. Anyone can dive the upper shelf. The lower shelf, past the salvage tier and Imperial-restricted, stays closed.

## Routes & Access

Ships come here for one reason: the Maw is the only route east to the Outer Reach. Most crossings stage from [[tail|The Tail]], the nearest inhabited approach on the western side, before pilots commit to the open crossing. Captains sail it by stars and run it fast. Nobody anchors. Compasses drift within five miles of the edge and stay wrong until the far side, and that advice circulates without much explanation: experienced sailors don't ask for one.

The [[sentinels-of-the-eyrie|Sentinels of the High Eyrie]] have watched the Maw's surface for two centuries from [[high-eyrie|High Eyrie]], and their records have no equal in the Scatter. The [[waveservants|Waveservants]] dislike questions about the depths.

Run the crossing per `vault/refs/vault/location/references/wilderness-travel-and-exploration.md` and `vault/refs/gameplay-toolbox.md` § Travel Pace.

## Hazards

Compass drift and star-only navigation define the crossing, and the no-anchoring rule means a ship in trouble here cannot stop. Rival crews concentrate around salvage traffic.

What lives here sorts itself by depth, and pilots plan around that. Fast surface hunters work the rim and trail any ship that slows to cross.

Where the shelf drops away a thermal boundary forms after dark. Prey rides it upward as the light goes, and giant squid follow.

Below that the layering stops being useful. The Maw has room for kraken and dragon turtles and the [[leviathan|Leviathan]], and no pilot's records say reliably what lives at what depth.

Sailors call the worst of the rim water "Vestra water," named after [[perrin-black-jaw|Perrin Black-Jaw]]'s loss of the [[vestra|Vestra]]. For how she went down, see the Vestra's page. The warning sign worth running at the table: the water goes hot before it kills.

Captains still disagree on the cause, and Perrin's account fits leviathan and dragon turtle equally. The useful lesson is the warning sign: heat comes before catastrophe.

The lower [[shelfworks|Shelfworks]] dive tier is currently abandoned. No crew will work below sixty feet since a heat event at eighty feet seared a crew's bait line instantly, and the [[pearl-of-souls|Pearl of Souls]] signal is only making the water more dangerous. One Maw-crossing ship has already logged a warm-water patch and a dead-fish smell on the Shelfworks approach, which its pilot filed as a vent anomaly.

## Ecology

Nothing about the Maw's depths reads as ordinary open-ocean life. The rift's seepage keeps water moving at depth in ways other trenches don't, and what that current pulls up draws hunters that shouldn't need to feed this often. Fast surface predators trail any ship that slows to cross. Lower down, giant squid ride the thermal boundary the rift sustains after dark, and kraken and dragon turtles work depths past where the layering means anything at all. Whatever the [[leviathan|Leviathan]] forced a path through for may be why so little down there ever starves.
