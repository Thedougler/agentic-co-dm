# The Cave

The Cave should sit beside **The River** conceptually: not another band of Aruhe, but a second geography cutting through several bands at once. Aruhe already distinguishes its concentric surface regions from features such as the River and Grasslands; the Cave works best as the **buried underside of the island**, reachable through scattered collapses in the Old Gardens, Quiet, and Rot without replacing the existing route to the Grove. 

Real lava tubes are an excellent foundation. They form when the surface of a lava flow crusts over while molten rock continues beneath it, then drains away, leaving long tunnels; old systems can branch, collapse into skylights, collect water, and develop ecosystems around penetrating tree roots, bats, and specialized cave invertebrates. ([National Park Service][1])

The crucial tonal decision I would make is this:

> **The Cave is the fear of being trapped in a tunnel after the lights go out.**
>
> Not abstract fear of darkness. Not buried tombs. **A subway tunnel, utility tunnel, collapsed mine, underground parking structure: you don't know where the exit is, your phone would have no signal, the air might be bad, the passage behind you might no longer exist—and then you feel something coming down the tunnel.**

That gives it a modern fear as recognizable beneath the surface as the Deer-Stalker or Wolfrabbit, without literally putting modern technology into the setting.

And its apex predator becomes the thing that makes the metaphor click.

---

## Overview

> **Player-facing arrival**
>
> The hole beneath the roots is not a burrow.
>
> Black stone curves down beneath them in a tube wide enough to walk three abreast. The walls are smooth in long places and savagely folded in others, frozen in the shape of something that once flowed. Roots have punched through the ceiling in pale curtains. Water ticks from their tips.
>
> Thirty feet in, daylight is already only a grey disc behind you.
>
> Fifty feet in, it is gone.
>
> The floor continues downhill.
>
> There are footprints in the mud.
>
> Yours are not the largest.
>
> Somewhere much farther ahead, something strikes the stone once.
>
> Then again.
>
> Then again.
>
> The second impact reaches your feet before it reaches your ears.
>
> Stop when they extinguish the light, mark the wall, continue downhill, or turn around.

The Cave is a vast collection of extinct lava tubes left from the volcanic construction of Aruhe. Some are walking-wide. Others are cathedral chambers. Others narrow until armor scrapes both walls.

They braid, fork, collapse, reconnect, and occasionally open beneath the jungle through skylights whose mouths have been hidden completely beneath centuries of growth.

Unlike the surface, **the Cave has no horizon and almost no landmarks**.

The surface jungle hides where you are.

The Cave hides whether there is still a way out.

---

# Geography

### Not a Sixth Band

The Cave is an **under-region**, in the same sense that the River is an artery rather than another concentric layer of Aruhe. 

Entrances can occur beneath:

* **The Old Gardens:** terrace collapses break into shallow tubes.
* **The Quiet:** root wells and old skylights are swallowed by vegetation.
* **The Rot:** the deepest root systems have penetrated the basalt and formed entire subterranean ecosystems.
* **Near the Clear Lake:** flooded and collapsed tubes exist beneath the old volcanic highlands, but **there is no secret Cave entrance to the Grove**. The Grove remains the last room.

That last point matters. The Cave should create alternate routes and dangerous shortcuts without invalidating Aruhe's established geography.

### The Flow

The oldest major tunnels generally slope **away from the island's old volcanic highlands**.

That means an experienced character can eventually deduce something extremely useful:

**down-tube generally means coastward; up-tube generally means inland.**

Not always.

But often enough to matter.

DC 14 Intelligence (Nature) or Wisdom (Survival) after examining several flow patterns reveals the principle.

This gives the players something better than arbitrary maze rolls. Competence should actually work.

### Scale

The caves are not one continuous dungeon beneath all 500 miles of Aruhe.

Instead there are **dozens or hundreds of old flow systems**, some only a few hundred feet long, some extending for miles, with later collapses occasionally connecting different generations of tunnel.

The known Cave is the interconnected western/central system the party can actually enter.

Large branches commonly range:

* 10–30 feet wide
* 6–20 feet high
* occasional chambers 100+ feet across
* crawlways narrowing below 3 feet
* vertical collapse shafts connecting two or three tube levels

Real wild lava tubes are frequently rough, dark, rubble-filled environments requiring scrambling and route-finding rather than convenient underground hallways. ([National Park Service][2])

# The Modern Fear: **No Exit**

The Cave should repeatedly hit four subconscious notes.

**Your light is temporary.**
Torches have duration. Lantern oil runs out. Magical light works perfectly—but it illuminates only a bubble of stone surrounded by absolute black.

**Your route is fragile.**
The passage you entered through can collapse. Flood. Become occupied. You may know exactly where the surface is and still have no way to reach it.

**The air cannot be trusted.**
Nothing looks different when breathable air becomes bad.

**When something comes, there is nowhere to stand aside.**

That last one belongs to the apex predator.

---

# Apex Predator: **The Blackrail**

I'd call the giant cave centipede **the Blackrail**.

Not because anyone on Aruhe knows what a railway is. The name can come from sailors much later: its polished back looks like parallel strips of wet black iron, and once it commits to a major tube it follows that corridor with horrifying directness.

If you want a less metaphorically suggestive name, **Tunnel-Back**, **Longback**, or **Stone-Centipede** all work, but *Blackrail* is the strongest.

## Player-facing description

> Something is coming.
>
> Not a roar.
>
> A vibration.
>
> Dust jumps from the floor in little rings. Once. Again. Faster.
>
> Then comes the sound: hundreds of hard points striking stone—not randomly, but in sequence. A rolling clatter approaching around the bend.
>
> Two antennae emerge first.
>
> Each is longer than a man.
>
> Then a head plated in wet black-red shell rounds the wall sideways, because the creature is running along it rather than the floor.
>
> The body follows.
>
> And follows.
>
> And keeps following.
>
> Every pair of legs catches the basalt independently. The entire tunnel appears to have started crawling toward you.

## Real Biological Basis

This is one of those Aruhe creatures where reality has already done most of the work.

Large *Scolopendra* centipedes are venomous predators capable of taking vertebrates. *Scolopendra gigantea* has been observed climbing cave ceilings to attack bats and hanging from the ceiling while restraining prey heavier than itself using multiple pairs of legs. ([Natural History Museum][3])

Aruhe simply removes the brakes.

Normal centipede:

* many independently gripping legs
* rapid acceleration
* flattened body for entering crevices
* venomous forcipules
* antenna-driven sensing
* ability to climb
* powerful manipulation of prey with multiple legs
* opportunistic predation

Aruhe:

* **forty feet long**
* enough muscle to move a body several tons in a tunnel
* venom intended for prey the size of deer
* every wall, ceiling and floor is equally usable
* antennae read air movement
* body and legs read vibration through basalt
* can hold several struggling creatures simultaneously

It has not become intelligent.

That makes it worse.

# Blackrail Ecology

**Size:** Huge monstrosity, roughly 30–45 feet long.

**Territory:** solitary. One adult may dominate several miles of interconnected main tube.

**Diet:** enormous cave bats, surface animals fallen through skylights, cave arthropods, anything descending to drink, and occasionally other Blackrails.

**Metabolism:** comparatively slow when resting. It may go days after a major kill without actively hunting.

That helps the ecology enormously: you do not need a tyrannosaur's daily caloric intake under the island.

The cave food web begins primarily with **surface energy imported underground**:

roots → root-feeding insects → predatory arthropods
bats → guano → decomposers → cave fauna
fallen animals → scavengers → predators

Real lava-tube ecosystems can indeed develop around roots penetrating from the surface, while caves elsewhere depend heavily on bats and imported organic matter. ([National Park Service][4])

Aruhe's impossible productivity pushes that system until it can sustain the Blackrail.

---

# How the Blackrail Hunts

It has **three behaviors**, not random encounter-table aggression.

### 1. Listen

A resting Blackrail presses much of its body against stone.

It detects walking through the tunnel.

Normal movement is noticeable.

Running is obvious.

Hammering a piton into basalt might as well be ringing a dinner bell.

### 2. Approach

It does not creep when it knows prey cannot escape.

The party first feels faint rhythmic vibrations.

Then dust falls.

Then comes the leg-rattle.

This is important: **give plenty of warning.**

The horror is not "gotcha, monster attacks."

The horror is:

> *It is definitely coming. What do you do with the thirty seconds you have?*

### 3. Pin

The Blackrail's preferred attack is not biting somebody in half.

It rushes the group, curls around a victim using several body segments, pins them to the wall, and applies its forcipules.

The venom does the killing.

Then it carries the immobilized prey somewhere quiet.

---

# D&D 5e Role

I would eventually build it around **CR 9–10**, depending on the party level you expect in Aruhe.

But its regional encounter should be partly an environmental puzzle rather than a sack of hit points.

Core features:

**Spider Climb.**
The Blackrail climbs difficult surfaces, including ceilings, without making an ability check.

**Tremorsense 120 ft.**
While both it and another creature touch the cave structure.

**Blind Beyond Light.**
Its actual eyesight is poor. Flight, silence, water, or breaking contact with the rock can meaningfully interfere with it.

That gives clever players counterplay.

**Tunnel Rush.**
If it moves at least 30 feet straight through a passage, creatures occupying the passage make roughly a DC 16 Dexterity save.

On failure:

* substantial bludgeoning damage
* knocked prone
* pushed with it or trapped beneath part of the body

On success:

* reach an alcove/side passage
* or take half/no damage depending on final balance

Essentially:

**the Blackrail is an oncoming train encounter.**

### The thing players learn

It cannot turn sharply at full speed.

It cannot fit everywhere.

The tiny holes they hated fifteen minutes ago are suddenly safety.

That is exactly the Cave's design language:

**claustrophobia becomes shelter.**

# Major Hazards

## 1. Dead Air

Some low chambers collect unbreathable gas.

Dormant does **not** require the volcano still to be erupting; cave atmosphere can become dangerous through poor ventilation and biological processes. Volcanic regions also demonstrate why carbon dioxide is particularly dangerous in depressions: it is colorless, odorless, and can accumulate in low areas. ([US Geological Survey][5])

### Tell

* flame burns poorly
* headache
* breathing becomes strangely deliberate
* dead cave insects collect below a particular elevation

**DC 14 Intelligence (Nature) or Wisdom (Survival)** recognizes the signs before prolonged exposure.

After **1 minute** in a dead-air pocket:

**DC 14 Constitution saving throw.**

Failure: **poisoned** while remaining in the chamber and for 10 minutes after reaching clean air.

A creature that fails again while already poisoned **begins suffocating** until removed from the pocket.

The point isn't invisible save-or-die gas.

There are clues.

---

## 2. Drumstone

Sometimes the "floor" is actually the thin roof of an older tube beneath the current one.

The first indication is that footsteps sound slightly hollow.

**DC 15 Wisdom (Perception)** notices before putting significant weight on it.

If a Medium or larger creature crosses normally:

**DC 14 Dexterity save.**

Failure: the crust collapses.

Fall **20–40 feet**, taking standard falling damage, landing prone amid rubble in a lower passage.

Success: reach stable rock at the edge.

This is grounded directly in the way lava-tube ceilings collapse and create skylights. ([US Geological Survey][6])

More importantly:

**the party has now been vertically separated.**

And everyone heard the collapse.

So did everything else.

---

## 3. The Squeeze

Some routes narrow to spaces barely large enough for a humanoid.

Use the normal 5e **squeezing** mechanics rather than inventing a subsystem.

The important regional trick is choices:

> Large tunnel: easy movement. Blackrail can follow.
>
> Crawlway: miserable movement. Blackrail cannot.

The Cave continually asks:

**How much comfort are you willing to exchange for safety?**

A character wearing a pack may have to push it ahead.

A shield may need to be unstrapped.

Nobody loses equipment automatically.

The pressure is enough.

---

## 4. Flood Pulse

Surface rain can enter through skylights and cracks far uphill.

The party doesn't necessarily know it is raining.

Tell:

> The trickle beside your boots suddenly reverses direction around a stone.
>
> Then another thin stream appears from a crack that was dry before.
>
> The air coming downhill gets colder.

One minute later, the pulse arrives.

In a narrow active drainage tube:

**DC 15 Strength saving throw.**

Failure:

* **10 (3d6) bludgeoning damage**
* knocked prone
* swept 40 feet downstream

Success:

* half damage
* hold position

A creature swept into a wall pocket or grate of roots may become **restrained**, escape DC 14 Athletics or Acrobatics.

Not every flood fills the entire cave. Most are violent pulses traveling through the lowest channels.

---

## 5. Root Wells

Where a surface tree has broken through the roof, hundreds of pale roots hang into the Cave.

These are among the only places that feel alive.

Water drips.

Blind insects feed.

Cave crickets jump.

Bats roost above.

There may even be a faint green-grey glow from luminous Rot sap in the deepest systems.

These locations are:

* sources of water
* vertical navigation clues
* rich concentrations of prey
* **Blackrail feeding grounds**

A DC 13 Wisdom (Survival) check at a Root Well determines whether the recent damage is insect feeding...

...or whether the roots were stripped clean by something forty feet long rubbing against them.

# Navigation

I would avoid "make a Survival check every ten minutes or you're lost."

Instead, use **junction decisions**.

At an important fork, present actual information:

### Left

* slopes downward
* stronger airflow
* scattered bat guano
* tunnel broad enough for something Huge

### Right

* slopes upward
* root penetration
* knee-deep water
* passage narrows after forty feet

### Forward

* no airflow
* floor heavily scratched
* faint rhythmic impact from somewhere ahead

Then allow Nature/Survival to interpret the evidence.

**DC 13:** understand elevation/airflow/basic use.

**DC 15:** identify animal traffic and likely connection to the surface.

**DC 18:** reconstruct the original lava flow and predict where a branch probably rejoins another tube.

Players can also simply:

* mark walls with chalk
* leave rope
* map intersections
* use *find the path*
* use familiars
* use divination

**Those things work.**

Aruhe is frightening because nature is hostile, not because the DM secretly deletes player competence.

---

# Light

No supernatural darkness necessary.

Ordinary darkness is better.

A torch creates 20 feet of bright light and then dim light beyond it.

Everything outside that radius still exists.

The Cave should make the party understand the difference between:

> "I cannot see anything."

and

> "I can see perfectly clearly for forty feet."

That second sentence becomes much scarier once something is moving at 60 feet per round.

---

# Sound

Unlike The Quiet, which suppresses warning sound and makes predators unnaturally close, the Cave should do almost the opposite. The Quiet already owns the horror of predators closing without audible warning. 

The Cave **lies with sound**.

Every noise carries.

A whispered conversation appears thirty feet away.

A dropped buckle cracks through three chambers.

The Blackrail's legs may sound as though they're ahead when they're actually moving in a parallel tube behind the wall.

**DC 14 Wisdom (Perception)** can determine the approximate direction of a major sound.

On a failure, you know its apparent direction—but echoes may have lied about which connecting passage produced it.

Not "you hear nothing."

**You hear too much.**

---

# Lesser Fauna

The Cave does not need a zoo of additional combat monsters.

Keep most things ecological.

### Cave Bats

Huge by ordinary standards but not giant-monster huge: perhaps 3–4 foot wingspans.

They leave at dusk through skylights and feed over Aruhe's canopy.

A colony suddenly taking flight is an excellent signal:

**something disturbed them farther inside.**

### Root Roaches

Hand-sized pale cockroaches clustered around root wells.

Mostly harmless.

They vanish into cracks seconds before a Blackrail arrives because they sense vibration before the party does.

That makes them valuable environmental information.

### Glass Crickets

Pale cave crickets with elongated legs and antennae.

They go absolutely still when large vibrations enter the basalt.

Again: warning system, not encounter filler.

### Juvenile Blackrails

One to three feet long.

Dangerous vermin, potentially modeled from the existing **Giant Centipede** stat block with improved HP.

The truly useful detail:

> If there are juveniles here, the adult is not.

Adult Blackrails are violently cannibalistic toward unrelated young.

Players can learn ecology and make decisions with it.

---

# Landmark: The Great Bore

The signature location should be **The Great Bore**.

A nearly straight lava tube perhaps three miles long, 25–40 feet across, gently sloping inland.

No junctions.

No real cover.

Easy walking.

A fantastic shortcut.

The floor has been polished almost smooth by repeated passage.

There are paired scratches along both walls at regular height.

The party can cover in a few hours what would take a day or more through the jungle above.

It is the Cave equivalent of the River.

**The best road.**

And something else already owns it.

Aruhe's existing River uses exactly that structural trick—the safest, easiest route is also the territory of the thing that dominates that environment. 

### The Great Bore encounter

Halfway through:

> Tick.
>
> Five seconds.
>
> Tick.
>
> Four.
>
> Tick.
>
> Three.
>
> Dust sifts from the ceiling.
>
> The marks along the walls suddenly make sense.
>
> They're not scratches from something passing beside you.
>
> They're where the legs touch when it runs.

Then put an old collapsed side blister **120 feet away**.

The question becomes very simple.

**Can they reach it before the Blackrail reaches them?**

That is a much better introduction than rolling initiative immediately.

---

# Landmark: The Daylight Hole

A collapsed skylight beneath The Quiet.

From below, the party can see a perfect circle of green canopy seventy feet overhead.

Rain falls through it.

Roots descend.

Birds never do.

Climbing it is possible:

**DC 14 Strength (Athletics)** with the roots.

Failure by less than 5: no progress.

Failure by 5+: fall from current height unless secured.

This becomes an emergency exit from the Cave—but climbing into the Quiet simply exchanges one set of problems for another.

---

# Landmark: The Drowned Tube

A major tunnel intersected by groundwater.

Its black surface fills the passage from wall to wall.

No current.

No visible far shore.

The roof continues four feet above the water and slowly descends.

The route definitely continues underwater.

This should not be a mandatory path.

It exists to make the players voluntarily say:

**No.**

That is valuable in horror.

---

# Landmark: The Old Mouth

One huge collapsed entrance near the lower Old Gardens.

This may be how the party first discovers the region.

The terraces have partly fallen into it.

Wolfrabbits refuse to den below the point where daylight disappears.

Their warrens stop abruptly along one wall.

That tells experienced Aruhe players something before they have seen anything underground:

> Whatever lives down there outranks the thing that hunts up here.

---

# The Taking Rule Underground

The Cave is still Aruhe.

The existing canon establishes the essential principle: **observation is free; removing something living is what fires the Taking Rule.** 

So:

**Safe to take:**

* basalt
* loose stone
* volcanic glass
* shed shell
* guano
* ordinary dripping groundwater

**A take:**

* uprooting cave plants
* cutting living roots to carry away
* capturing a bat
* taking cave insects
* Blackrail eggs
* killing something specifically to provision yourself

And this creates an excellent Cave temptation.

There is water underground.

There are edible animals.

The party may be running low.

And unlike the River and Lake, ordinary stream and spring water elsewhere on Aruhe is already established as not triggering the special water-taking condition. 

So **drinking or filling skins from cave seepage should be safe**.

That makes Root Wells extraordinarily important.

---

# What the Cave Does to Aruhe's Campaign Structure

The Cave should be an **optional high-risk bypass**.

Surface:

> slower navigation
> more forage temptation
> more creatures
> Bear-Elk circuits
> sunlight eventually returns

Cave:

> faster route
> almost no forage
> fewer encounters
> worse individual encounters
> possibility of separation
> Blackrail territory
> exits are uncertain

The players can potentially bypass a known Bear-Elk route by finding a lava tube beneath it.

That doesn't make the Cave "better."

It means they get to choose:

**Do we cross the road patrolled by the enormous animal whose schedule we understand, or climb into the hole where nothing has a schedule?**

That's good exploration design.

---

# The Core Aruhe Transformation

Most importantly, the Cave still follows the island's established principle:

> **Do not invent an arbitrary fantasy monster. Take something real and push its actual nature past the point where it remains comfortable.** 

The Cave itself follows the same rule.

Lava tubes really:

* branch
* collapse
* create skylights
* collect water
* contain darkness
* require scrambling
* grow unusual ecosystems
* carry roots down from the forest above. ([National Park Service][1])

Centipedes really:

* climb cave walls and ceilings
* attack vertebrates
* use venom
* grip prey with many legs
* hunt bats in caves. ([Zenodo][7])

The Blight only asks:

**What if neither of those things had to remain a reasonable size?**

And the modern fear underneath all of it becomes:

> **You are underground.**
>
> **There is only one tunnel.**
>
> **Something is coming down it.**
>
> **And you have just realized the little recess you passed sixty feet ago was not inconvenient terrain.**
>
> **It was the emergency exit.**
