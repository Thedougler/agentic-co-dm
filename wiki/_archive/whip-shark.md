# Whip Shark (Welak)

![[whip-shark-banner]]

[[kalowe|Kalowe]]'s fishermen call it the Welak; colonial sailors call it the whip shark, after the tail. It is a shark by any ordinary reckoning: massive, cartilaginous, built for open ocean. The lower lobe of its tail extends 10–20 feet beyond the upper and terminates in a hooked, sickle-like barb, dense and sharp enough to punch through ship timber. Older specimens have been recorded up to 55 feet long from nose to tail end, with a 35-foot body and 20-foot tail. At the [[shelfworks|Shelfworks]], they patrol the Abyssal Bore perimeter (40–200 ft.), drawn upward from depth by the rhythmic vibrating hum of surface-supplied air pumps. Whip sharks are not supposed to leave the [[the-drowned-maw|Drowned Maw]].

A specimen over sixty feet long attacked the [[uncertainty|Uncertainty]] at anchor in a storm west of [[calveno|Calveno]] during Session 03: three hull impacts, two inches of bilge water, [[jean-claude-tabarnack|Jean-Claude]] tail-swiped and his chest opened before he arrowed the shark from posterior to anterior, and [[crissdalynn-khinriss|Crissdalynn]] seizing sixty feet of it and lifting it bodily from the sea on a contested-strength check the shark lost with a natural 1. Harvested: a four-foot serrated barb spike (Delmar's, assessed at [[kats-curios|Kat's Curios]]), teeth, scales (used to patch the hull), and three fertilized eggs (Jean-Claude's — later sold at [[le-paludi|Le Paludi]] Market for 300gp). This specimen was far west of its known range. That same night, [[perrin-black-jaw|Perrin Black-Jaw]] received a vision of being polymorphed into a whip shark and hunting a whale from the deep, with the word *"Grow"* communicated from below the worked stone floor of the abyss.

## Description

A Welak sighting starts with the tail, not the shark: the lower barb rides visibly proud of the water when the animal surfaces to strike, a silhouette sailors learn to read faster than the dorsal fin. Dock hands who've worked both animals use it to tell a whip shark from the smaller [[hunter-shark|Hunter Shark]] at a glance — that animal trails ships for scraps and circles before it commits, but the Welak doesn't need an invitation and doesn't circle. It surfaces once, fast, and goes for the hull before a crew can reload. Fishermen who've lost gear to one keep the story short: you don't out-swim it and you don't out-gun it, you get below the waterline before the second pass.

## Ecology

The Welak belongs to the [[the-drowned-maw|Drowned Maw]]'s depths, one of the fast surface hunters that trail any ship slowing to cross the trench — the shallower end of what the Maw keeps below the layering. At the [[shelfworks|Shelfworks]], whip sharks patrol the Abyssal Bore perimeter, drawn up from depth by the rhythmic hum of the salvage crews' air pumps rather than blood or bait; the vibration alone pulls them off their usual range. They aren't supposed to leave the Maw at all, and a fed, healthy specimen rarely does. The specimen that hit the [[uncertainty|Uncertainty]] west of [[calveno|Calveno]] was a genuine outlier, tracked well past any known range and big enough that dock talk in [[kalowe|Kalowe]] still treats the sighting as a warning rather than a fluke. Kalowe's own fishermen, who work water closer to the Maw's rim than most free-port crews care to, are the ones who kept the older name for it — Welak — while colonial charts and Calveno underwriters use "whip shark" instead.
