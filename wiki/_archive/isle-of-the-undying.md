# Isle of the Undying

A Midchain island side plot for Season 2, now built as [[otel-karn|Otel Karn]]. Death has stopped taking mortals here. Fatal wounds simply aren't fatal, but they still hurt, and nothing heals. Butchered livestock walk and graze at market despite lacking skin, muscle, or organs to digest the grass with, as if nothing had happened.

---

The islanders discover the truth when a duel to the death ends in a draw. Both combatants take three point-blank shots apiece, through the skull and the heart, and neither one dies. Both still feel every wound, and neither heals from it.

---

Before the horror surfaces, this reads as a paradise. The crew should get a stretch of consequence-free recreational PvP among themselves: worry-free, guilt-free duels and swordplay at [[stillmere|Stillmere]]'s Proving Ground, before the real cost of a world where death doesn't come becomes clear.

---

An archfey, [[otho|Otho]], struck a careless bargain with the island generations ago, a "gift" given without thinking through what eternal, unhealable wounds would actually mean. He's made himself at home since, walking among the islanders in disguise as a beloved local drunk and reveler, the guy who's "always just been around," delighted by the chaos and excess his own gift produced. He isn't malicious, just careless, and happy to exploit what his carelessness built.

---

Leading thread: [[otho|Otho]]'s teaching, before the bargain curdled, took root on the island as an ecstatic practice, flesh not the enemy of spirit, excess pursued as devotion. That doctrine left the island generations ago and eventually became [[khlysty|Khlysty]]'s philosophy. Otho is personally proud of [[shepherd-grigori|Grigori]] as his star disciple, the vessel who carried the office of Korabl further than anyone before him. Fey time-blindness means he has no idea centuries have passed, or that a mortal staying alive this long is unusual at all. Grigori believes he invented his trick himself. He didn't. This island is what Khlysty's ideology becomes carried all the way through: not ecstatic transcendence, just endless, unhealable suffering.

---

Resolution is the crew's to find, with an easiest and hardest path bracketing it, and legitimate ground between the two:

- Easiest: [[otho|Otho]] gets bored fast, not cruel. Real entertainment, a better story, a more interesting game or invitation elsewhere wins him over on his own terms, and he lets the bargain lapse fondly once the joke stops being funny to him.
- Hardest: open combat is the real trap. He's an archfey, and at anywhere near level 10 a straight fight is unwinnable, which needs to read as obvious at the table. Contract law and his true name are the only other adversarial levers, and both are dangerous besides.
- Middle: he sets his own game or trial, a classic fey test of wit, nerve, or luck. Winnable, but it exacts a real price.
