# Umberlee's dominion erosion — the fissure, the Pearl, and what's on the other side

[[umberlee|Umberlee]]'s claim over the sea is categorical, not a matter of strength: material-plane water is hers, full stop. The fissure at the floor of the [[the-drowned-maw|Drowned Maw]] is a breach into the [[elemental-plane-of-water|Elemental Plane of Water]], and as it widens, real trench water is being physically replaced by elemental water. That water is indistinguishable to any mortal sense — same taste, same danger, same look — it simply isn't hers anymore. Every inch the fissure widens is permanent lost ground, and only she can feel it happening.

---

She's not a beloved deity mourning lost devotion — she's a fear-tribute racket, and most sailors would be glad to hear her reach was shrinking. So the erosion isn't just loss, it's loss she can't complain about without handing mortals a reason to stop paying. That's the root of the bind: she needs it stopped, and she can't let anyone know why it matters to her.

---

The Pearl of Souls isn't just a lure — it's a battery of raw, untapped soul-power broadcasting across the boundary, drawing the attention of things on the other side that want to seize it. What's come through so far ([[leviathan|Leviathan]], Ridgeback, Krakling) are the small fry, weak enough to fit through a narrow crack. Something much larger is pushing and scraping at the breach from the other side, actively trying to force it wider — the real engine behind the erosion accelerating, not passive seepage.

---

The boundary itself isn't a special wall built around water specifically. In the Great Wheel cosmology, the Border Ethereal separates the Material Plane from every Inner Plane alike — fire, water, earth, air — a universal structural law, not a targeted containment. What's happening at the Drowned Maw is a local breach of that universal boundary — a puncture in one specific place, not an exception carved out for one hostile plane. That life on both sides is fundamentally incompatible, and that something enormous lives down there, is this campaign's own layer on top — what lies beyond the boundary in the Elemental Plane stays unknown by design. Whether it ever surfaces as a real, escalating threat later in the campaign stays open, not committed either way.

---

Umberlee never admits any of this — not the fear, not the need, not the stakes. She stays entirely in character: withholding what he needs, taking what he already owes, giving Delmar orders without explanation. Her terror is real but only ever inferable from behavior, never stated. This is DM-only knowledge; the table never hears it from her directly.

---

Auralis and Umberlee were never rivals. Real-world Realms cosmology backs this up: Umberlee reads as belonging to the eternal/mythic tier of deity (oceans go back to the Blue Age, deep prehistory before roughly -35,000 DR), while the Antheri only run roughly -2000 to -495 DR — nowhere near old enough to predate her godhood. What they predate is her *claim on the Maw specifically*. Auralis's containment job was never about her; it was always about the Elemental Plane of Water. Umberlee moved into the water above his work sometime after the Antheri fell, the way she claims any patch of sea nobody's actively defending — the same casual, opportunistic way she claims everything. She has never had to think about what's underneath her claim, because in 200 years nothing behind it ever pushed. The thing forcing the breach wider doesn't have to be Istishia himself (he's canonically indifferent to sea-goddess politics, so he wouldn't descend personally to resolve a border dispute at the material level) — more likely something native to his domain that doesn't answer to him, pressing through the widening crack as it opens. Istishia's name gives the place a real cosmological address without committing to a villain yet.

---

Umberlee's horror is that she has no concept of "older than me." Her entire self-myth is built on being first, oldest, absolute — the thing sailors have always owed tribute to. A hole opening in her domain that she cannot explain doesn't read to her like a rival's incursion, it reads like proof that her own myth about herself might be wrong. That is a thing she will never say out loud, not to mortals, not even to her own clergy. She can't investigate it like a detective, because investigating means admitting there's something to investigate. All she has is the only tool she's ever needed before: leverage over mortals.

---

Auralis needs allies because he cannot leave the trench — he isn't inside the machine that holds the fissure, he *is* it. The Antheri soul-pledge chamber "predates Auralis and appears built for repeated use" (see [[antheri]]) — it was never a one-time miracle, it's a process. [[perrin-black-jaw|Perrin]]'s unwitting pact (the Vestra Secret, see [[auralis]]) is that same toolkit run in miniature on a living mortal instead of a ripped-out consciousness — not a second Auralis, a hand he can use topside since his own body can't leave its post. "Grow" reads less like a vague blessing and more like a very old, very literal instruction: become vessel enough to be used. Perrin wasn't chosen for who he is — he was chosen for being present when Auralis finally had enough signal to reach out at all.

---

Perrin has received the most direct contact and the most power, but he isn't the only one Auralis has ever reached. Over the years, Auralis has touched others more lightly, never revealing the source; some did good with the gift and some did ill. No fixed list exists on purpose. Invent one as background flavor whenever useful at the table, always a face the group hasn't met yet, never a retrofit onto an existing NPC.

---

Umberlee's fear does what she's always known how to do: make threats. It escalates toward Delmar specifically, because she already holds the open Pearl debt over him ("bring the Pearl, keep his life"), five captains' souls already riding in his head. She threatens him into serving her cause against this new, uncomprehended erosion, whether or not he ever understands what he's aiming at.
