## Analysis Summary

### Problem with Current Narration
The existing narration reads like a grid inventory of distances ("Twenty feet north...", "Five feet east...", "Fifteen feet out...") rather than a scene a DM speaks with urgency. It lacks:
- Threat established in sentence one
- Punchy, short sentences where danger lands
- A clear live edge (moment a heartbeat away)
- The playable tone of a DM speaking

### Solution Applied
Rewrote following the **cliffhanger opening recipe**:
1. Threat in sentence one: "A man in a grey shirt is drowning in the slack, and two twelve-foot otters are playing tag with him."
2. Fast-paced short sentences showing danger: "His fingers slip. One boot is gone. Blood runs from his split lip."
3. One longer sentence laying out space with handles visible
4. Live edge that's a heartbeat away: man coughing/gasping, otter pushing him, hips sliding deeper

### Key Craft Principles Applied
- Second person, present tense ("you", standing on sand)
- Concrete nouns and strong verbs (whorl, whiskers, stripe, circles, slip, cough, nudge, slide)
- Folded details onto their owners (water streaming from whiskers on the body that owns them)
- Everyday words (no archaic language)
- Speakable distance (20 feet out, not grid coordinates except where game-relevant)
- Rhythm mixing longer sentences with short punchy ones
- One sense beyond sight (wet spray reaching the bank)
- Every sentence earns its place (adds picture, handle, threat, or live edge)
- Hard lines respected (no player emotions, only sensory world; danger shown, not explained)
