# What the characters can perceive

Read this when the scene involves a handled object, a creature with special
abilities, a hidden threat or trap, a secret, or a fact the sources leave open.
The parent page and table state are the world; this file decides which part of
it reaches the players now.

## Four ways a fact reaches the players

A fact can go in the narration only through one of these:

1. **Senses, now.** A character can see, hear, smell, or touch it from where
   they are.
2. **What they already know.** Their background, training, or earlier play
   lets them recognize it. Use only knowledge the character actually has.
3. **What they just did.** A player declared an action and it resolved; show
   what that action exposed.
4. **Public knowledge.** Earlier play or public canon already made it known.

No channel → leave it out. Words like "might", "seems", or "perhaps" create no
channel; use them only for a reasonable reading of something the characters
can actually perceive ("the scratches look fresh").

## How far perception reaches

- A sightline shows the near side. The far side, the inside, and what is
  behind the door wait for someone to look.
- A sound tells direction and loudness. Its source waits for someone to see it,
  unless the sound itself is unmistakable.
- **Objects have states.** Seen: shape, size, color, surface. Held: add
  weight, temperature, texture of the outside. Opened, cut, tasted, or tested:
  only then the inside, taste, or function. Magic, history, and purpose wait for
  study, expertise, or a spell.
- A closed chest with nothing known about its contents is a closed chest. Show
  its lock, its weight on the floor, its scratches; the players open it.

## Creatures and effects: three layers

1. **What shows.** Body, posture, behavior, and effects the characters can
   sense right now: breath frosting near it, moths dropping from the air, grass
   paling in a ring.
2. **A fair reading.** A character's reasonable guess from what shows, worded
   as a guess: "the air near it seems thinner."
3. **The rules.** Exact radius, DC, condition, damage, hit points, hidden
   abilities, and their true cause. These stay with the DM until play reveals
   them.

Narration uses layers 1 and 2. A danger shows itself through buildup and
signs; the players learn the rule by testing it or paying for it.

## First looks stop before contact

A first look at a threat ends on the windup: the tentacle snapping taut, the
wings folding for the dive, the cart tipping on the slope. The grab, the bite,
the steal, and the explosion happen after players act, in the DM's live
narration.

## Hidden things

- A hidden creature or trap appears as the traces it leaves: the floor cleaner
  in front of the statue, grit on the lion's tongue, someone past the arch
  trying not to breathe. Never "a trap waits" or "something lies in ambush".
- A lie, a disguise, or a betrayal shows only its surface and its tells.
- The DM layer already states every truth plainly; narration never adds
  "don't reveal" notes or hints aimed at the DM.

## The players own their characters

The narration shows the world and stops. It never states a PC's action,
speech, thought, feeling, memory, conclusion, or bodily reaction, and never
says what the table understands ("before anyone realizes"). The world can act
on them: rain soaks cloaks, the deck lurches, smoke stings. Their response is
theirs.

## When the sources are silent

- Missing appearance, stock, or layout: get it from the owner skill for that
  page, or return the gap. Leave it out of the prose rather than filling it
  with a genre default.
- Silence is not absence. Write "no guards at the gate" only when a source
  says the gate is unguarded.
- Parent and art disagree: follow the parent and flag the mismatch outside the
  narration.
