## The question

How in-depth should session notes be when preparing to run a tabletop session.

## Both directions fail

Mulligan frames the answer as a balance between two failure modes rather than
a target quantity of notes.

**Under-preparation** produces a lack of compelling plot, undeveloped
characters, and a hollow world.

**Over-preparation** prevents player characters from meaningfully affecting the
lore of the world.

The second failure is the less obvious one: past a certain point, more prep
does not make the session better, it makes the players' choices matter less,
because everything they could affect is already settled.

## The stated method

Prepare enough to create set pieces and elements that players will interact
with, and stop short of meticulous planning that will go unused.

Flexibility and fluidity are what let player characters' actions shape the
story.

## Motivation without direction

Rather than steering players onto a predetermined path, make things happen in
the world: interesting events naturally attract player attention and motivate
players to pursue the hooks attached to them.
