# Brennan Lee Mulligan's DM Style — Improv and Planning

## The ratio

Mulligan describes his split as "80% improv and 20% planning".

That ratio is not a claim that little preparation happens. He notes that on
Dimension 20 the production itself forces heavy prep: "In Dimension 20 we need
to fully map out all of the battlemaps in order to be built in time, even the
digital ones. So, there is a tremendous amount of planning that happens."

The ratio describes what the prep is *for*, not how much of it there is.

## Prep as toys

The governing image is toys rather than script:

> "Like toys, you dump them out and the kids play with them, and then you have
> your planning go towards something you can improvise with"

Preparation is aimed at producing objects that can be played with, not a
sequence to be followed. What gets prepared is the thing that can react.

## What gets prepared

The article lists what he actually builds ahead of time:

- Complete battle maps, physical and digital
- NPC names and personalities
- City names, appearances, and maps
- Detailed worldbuilding elements

## Why not a rigid session plan

> "I would rather have an NPC with a personality ready to go, and then kind of
> wing it, rather than plan my session rigidly, especially because you never
> know how your players will react."

The stated reason is player unpredictability: a rigid plan assumes a response
that has not happened yet. A personality can meet any response; a planned
sequence cannot.
