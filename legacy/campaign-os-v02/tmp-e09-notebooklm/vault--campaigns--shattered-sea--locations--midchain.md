The Midchain lies south of the [[central-strait|Central Strait]]. Low islands crowd close, most a half-day sail apart, with trees showing first on coasts of forest and cliff. Water runs turquoise to blue. You can find fresh water and shelter here, but not the answers.

The chain runs as one long band on a single shallow shelf, widening in the middle where it thickens into three ranks and tapering at both ends. The regions that divide it are a pilot's convenience, not anyone's border, and no two charts draw them the same way.

## Geography

**Extent:** roughly 300 miles end to end on a single shallow shelf, three islands deep at its widest and a single scattered line at both tips. **Topography:** low islands rise straight from the shelf. Forest and cliff face outward on the ocean side, mangrove and reef flat sit in the lee. The channels between them run shallow and shifting, so last season's soundings don't hold this one. Landings favor small boats over deep-draft hulls almost everywhere outside Kalowe's harbor.

## Government

The [[chain-council|Chain Council]] governs loosely, stepping in only when a problem crosses island lines. Every island keeps its own sovereignty, and the Council convenes at [[kalowe|Kalowe]], the largest port, on the most neutral ground available. It moves slow and trusts no one, but once enough islands share a threat it strikes fast.

## Trade

Free ports and a reef-knowledge economy carry the Midchain's trade. [[kalowe|Kalowe]]'s Island Council dry dock takes any paying hull and asks nothing about cargo, and [[sparhold|Sparhold]] undercuts it on price with looser rules still. Salvaged and reclaimed goods move through dealers island to island, one step ahead of whoever is looking for them, which makes the Midchain good ground both for selling contraband and for losing a trail.

## Routes & Access

Charts for the Midchain contradict each other because the Midchain keeps changing the question. Local knowledge is worth more than paper in most of the inner channels. The same back channels that hide a ship also hide teeth. Captains steer clear of deep blue holes unless a pilot knows them by name. [[sawek|Sawek]] lairs look like useful shelter until something moves below the keel.

Prevailing winds turn more westerly near the [[doldrums|Doldrums]]. They create back channels. Westbound ships use these to avoid Strait inspection. Dravosi patrol cutters run intercept routes through the main lanes. Smart captains prefer the less predictable routes instead.

The Midchain is also where small piracy becomes geography. A cutter like the [[knifes-wake|Knife's Wake]] can disappear through channels a frigate captain refuses to enter.

## Hazards

Shifting reefs, hidden channels, [[sawek|Sawek]] blue holes, giant crocodiles in mangrove water, constrictor snakes along root mats, Grung boundary violations, and piracy that turns local route knowledge into a weapon.

The Midchain runs on local knowledge, not charts, use "does the crew have a pilot" as the pressure valve for any crossing. Back channels near the Doldrums offer westbound escape from Strait inspection. A blue hole tests anyone claiming to know these waters. Piracy here hides behind geography strangers can't read.

## Hooks

- Grung raids from the direction of the [[verdant-teeth|Verdant Teeth]] have worsened; the [[chain-council|Chain Council]] has heard enough complaints that the beach trade may become a regional pressure point.
- Back channels can help westbound ships avoid predictable Strait inspection routes. [[the-passage|The Passage]] knows which ones are clean.
- Captains avoid deep blue holes unless a pilot knows them by name. Some hide lairs, some are just drops.

- The [[doldrums|Doldrums]] shift by season, making southern route planning a moving problem.
- The [[verdant-teeth|Verdant Teeth]] trade route is profitable, narrow, and watched.
