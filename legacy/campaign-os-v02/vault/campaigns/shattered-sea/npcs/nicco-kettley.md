# Nicco Kettley

**Wants:** a door and a lock on [[the-bridge|the Bridge]]'s middle tier before the Beffa crowds thin, and he is funding it with stock a supplier has started asking questions about.

> [!read-aloud]
> A board on trestles, wedged between a fry stall and a rope seller, with a grey cat asleep across the middle of it in the one patch of sun that reaches under the arch. He wears a second board strapped across his chest from shoulder to hip, and nine years of that weight have bent his spine forward. He cannot sit down in that rig.
>
> The halfling behind it works around the cat. [[paper|Paper]] cones of loose leaf. A tin of hand-rolled cigarettes with the ends twisted shut. A brass lighter on a chain long enough to reach any customer and not one inch past them.
>
> He is talking to two people at once, and neither of them is you.
>
> *(without looking up)* "Seconds," he says, tapping the board edge. "Perfectly good seconds."

Nicco runs [[la-gatta|La Gatta]], a vending board on the bottom tier of the Bridge in [[calveno|Calveno]]. He came up out of the canal-city halfling trade and has never held a lease. Everything he owns fits in the tin, the trestles, and a locked box he carries home nightly.

He buys short batches and failed runs from [[studio-orsini|Studio Orsini]] at a fraction of what a finished blend costs, twists them shut himself, and sells them at prices no licensed room in the city can match. When one burns wrong, he says so afterward, cheerfully, and offers the next one at a discount.

**Roleplay Concept:** [[halfling|Halfling]] street vendor + dealer in rejected stock who built a gossip empire.

**Opening move:** selling cigarettes under the arch · Nicco speaks first · "Seconds," he says. "Perfectly good seconds."

**Voice and delivery.** Fast, warm, and never still. He talks over the fry stall's noise because he has to, and the volume never drops when the topic turns sensitive. Worldview word: **leverage**. Every fact he holds about a person is inventory he has not sold yet, which in his accounting is nothing at all like a betrayal.

Sample lines: *"That one's ninety, which beats the hundred you paid last week, so we're both winning."* / *"It'll do what it does, and sometimes it does nothing, which is what ninety buys you."* / *"Don't move the cat."*
