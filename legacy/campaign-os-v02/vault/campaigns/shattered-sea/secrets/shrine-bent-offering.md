# Shrine's Bent Offering

*A bent farthing wedged where the tribute basin drains, hidden among years of silt and shadows.*

## Presentation

> [!read-aloud]
> The basin sits at the shrine's center, fed by candle-water and the loose coins sailors fold before tossing in, bent to make them unmarketable, a gift no one else can spend. The water moves constantly, filling from above and draining somewhere below. The air carries the smell of old copper and stone, salt-sharp and mineral-cold.

## Concealment

Bent coins wedged into the drain crack are ordinary. Tribute coins washed down from the basin accumulate there over years, caught on rough stone edges. Nobody seals the crack: a gift freely given must stay irretrievable, and a sealed drain blocks the goddess's acceptance. A bent coin looks like every other tributary offering that couldn't complete its journey.

## Discovery

Passive Perception 14 notices the coin sits at an unnatural angle in the silt, a glint of metal that doesn't match the basin's water-worn patina.

> [!check] Perception — Notice the Coin
> DC 14. **Success:** you spot a bent coin caught in the drain crack, worked into the stone by pressure and time. **Failure:** the drain looks like any other water-worn gap in old stone.

## Reveal

> [!read-aloud]
> Wedged sideways in the crack, worn smooth by years of water pressure, is a farthing bent long ago and left here by a hand you'll never know. It's pierced through the middle, where the metal flattened against a hook or wire. Someone looped cord or thread through it once. The edges are dark with corrosion, but the dent is old enough now to be part of the coin's shape. [[bent-offering-farthing|A bent sailor's farthing]], offerings incomplete.
