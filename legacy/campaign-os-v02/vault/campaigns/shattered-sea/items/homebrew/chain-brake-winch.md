# Chain-Brake Winch

> [!read-aloud]
> An iron drum the width of a dinner plate on a bracket drilled for four bolts, with a crank handle folded flat against it.
>
> A pawl the size of a thumb rides the drum's teeth on a stiff spring.
>
> [[kettil-ormsson|Kettil Ormsson]] lets a foot of [[chain|chain]] run out of it, and the pawl stops the drum dead with a sound like a door bolt.
>
> The chain that came off has fresh oil on it and leaves it on the counter.

## Mechanics

> [!mechanic]
> **Chain-Brake Winch [HB].** Bolting the bracket down takes ten minutes with [[smiths-tools|smith's tools]] and four bolts into a deck or a solid beam. It ships with 60 feet of chain.
>
> A creature within reach of the crank takes an action to haul, drawing up to 800 pounds 20 feet toward the drum. Release the crank and the pawl locks, so the drum gives back nothing until someone lifts the pawl by hand.
>
> **Edge cases:** a creature hauling something that resists makes a DC 13 [[strength|Strength]] check against a target actively pulling the other way, and a failure costs the action with no ground lost. A second pair of hands on the crank adds that weight and skips the check.
>
> **Limitations:** it frees nobody from the [[grappled|Grappled]] condition and lifts nothing off a creature. It wins ground for whatever the chain already holds. Unbolted, the winch is 40 pounds of dead iron with nothing to pull against.

## Provenance

The yard crews use them to walk sprung planking back into a hull, one ratchet tooth at a time. [[kettil-ormsson|Kettil Ormsson]] cuts his pawl springs from salvaged barrel hoop, and no winch of his has ever let go.
