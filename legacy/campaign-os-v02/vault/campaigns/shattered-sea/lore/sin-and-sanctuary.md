# Sin and Sanctuary

*The cosmological law governing fiend incursion, hallowed ground, and the demon's/devil's kiss.*

## The Fact

Sin has mass and gravity. The scale and density of wrongdoing worked into a place draws fiends there and sets how boldly they move.

A place steeped in real cruelty lets them walk in daylight. A clean place keeps them skulking at the margins. The gravity matters as much as the mass. The deeper a soul sinks, the harder it climbs back out.

Common belief holds that a shrine stays safe ground forever once it's blessed, but the claim isn't permanent. A patron (a god, a demigod, an archfey) has to keep actively holding it.

A fiend that sets foot on ground its patron still holds dies there. Its form on this plane burns away, and its soul goes home: a demon to the Abyss, a devil to the Nine Hells. Fiends know this and never test a claim carelessly.

A pack sends its weakest member forward first and watches what happens to it before anyone stronger commits, a tell any onlooker can learn to read without a ritual to name it.

What the common belief misses is that each patron judges by its own dogma, and any patron can stop holding a claim with no visible sign. [[tyr|Tyr]]'s justice tolerates, even blesses, brutal violence against the guilty. A righteous fight is a blessed fight in his eyes. Another patron would recoil from the identical act.

A patron doesn't abandon a straying follower out of nowhere either. It warns first, and the warnings stop only once the follower can no longer hear them. From outside that looks like abandonment, but the god didn't go silent unprompted.

[[corvin-ashe|Corvin Ashe]]'s confrontation at [[sorrowbell|Sorrowbell]] states this outright, once the demon has already walked through the door uncontested.

> [!dialogue]
> **Corvin Ashe**: You cannot enter the house of Tyr!
>
> **The Shape**: Tyr is not here. This is an empty box.
>
> **Corvin Ashe**: I have done Tyr's bidding. My life's work is in his name.
>
> **The Shape**: Your life's work makes Tyr puke.
>
> **Corvin Ashe**: The widow Voss, the first parishioner I buried, was a witch! Committing heresy!
>
> **The Shape**: Lies? In your house of God? No wonder he's abandoned you.

Ashe wasn't cut off first. He simply lost the ability to hear it, and found out only when it could no longer stay hidden.

Fiends, celestials, and gods all read a mortal soul's true state innately, at a glance, with no more effort than noticing a stranger's height. No one lies to any of them. Only mortals get to fool themselves and each other. That precision is what makes a fiend's mockery of a hypocrite land so exactly, and it's the mechanism behind the demon's or devil's kiss.

The kiss is a sanctioned public mark, and it's rare. No matter how bad the crime, a patron still contests an ordinary sinner's fate. Turning his life around works for a bandit. Returning what he stole works for a thief. Even a murderer can repent and eventually earn forgiveness after paying for it.

The kiss belongs only to the soul whose quality forecloses every one of those paths, a line every side senses, never measures. If the soul were still reachable, its patron would intervene to stop the kiss. The kiss landing unopposed is itself the god's confirmation that it fit.

To the fiend delivering it, the kiss is recognition. The mortal was never going to reach their god's side of the ledger, and the Human shape was the only thing that hadn't caught up yet.

> [!dialogue] The Shape
> But we love you. Let me kiss you.

It turns the victim into a physical, undeniable caricature of what they already were, marking the skin where the crimes happened. Its purpose is public. A mortal fully sided with evil loses the ability to hide among the innocent, on the god's own authority, with the fiend as the instrument.

> [!mechanic]
> **Hallowed ground ([[hallow|Hallow]], Divine Evocation).** Sustained by active patron attention; lapses when the patron stops holding the claim, silently and without visible change. **[[detect-magic|Detect Magic]]** (active): "Divine evocation, steady and warm, like a room where a fire has been burning a long time." **Detect Magic** (lapsed): nothing, or a faint residue the caster might mistake for ambient stonework.
>
> **The demon's kiss ([[bestow-curse|Bestow Curse]], Divine Necromancy).** Permanent and patron-sanctioned: the fiend delivers it as the patron's instrument, and the patron's failure to intervene confirms the mark fits. **[[counterspell|Counterspell]]:** not applicable (the patron acts directly, leaving nothing for Counterspell to intercept). **[[remove-curse|Remove Curse]]:** fails; divine origin, permanent, and the mark is the god's own authority expressed on skin.
>
> **Fiend and celestial soul-reading ([[detect-evil-and-good|Detect Evil and Good]], Divine Divination).** Always-on and passive. Fiends and celestials read a mortal soul's true state at a glance. Their mockery lands exactly because they see what is actually there. **Counterspell:** not applicable.
>
> **A fiend destroyed on contested ground.** The patron's active Hallow destroys any fiend that sets foot on claimed ground. The trigger is the patron's claim, which Counterspell cannot intercept.

**What's active:** this law is live right now, not backstory. It governs how boldly fiends behave in any place travelers set foot and whether a shrine's protection still holds when tested. This law is already playing out end to end in [[quest-penance-bar|the Penance Bar arc]] (draft, not yet played), where Tyr has withdrawn from Sorrowbell because Corvin Ashe stopped being able to hear the warnings. The demon walked in uncontested as the proof, and the kiss is due to turn Ashe into a permanent, physical confession of what he already was.

**PC connection:** world-texture with no single PC's thread. It's the standing rule any future fiend, hallowed-ground, or corrupt-clergy arc plugs into, not something one character owns.
