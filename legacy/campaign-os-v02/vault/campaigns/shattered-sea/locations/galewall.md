# The Galewall

The Galewall shows as a dark band across the western horizon, grey and slate-blue, storm systems piled up inside it. The chop picks up well before the edge, the wind builds, the water turns cold. Then the [[ashwall-islands|Ashwall Islands]] rise out of it, black volcanic spires with cloud on their heights and white surf at their feet. Last stop heading west.

Ships cross often enough to keep trade alive between [[verdant-scatter|the Scatter]] and colonial lands, but they fail often enough that port shrines on the western [[crown-islands|Crown Islands]] have boards with names. The Galewall does not stop ships, but crossing it changes the cost of every mistake.

## Routes & Access

Crossing takes three weeks with an experienced pilot. The loss rate is one in three, and good pilots lose less. Ships cross to keep trade alive, but they fail often enough that port shrines have boards with names.

### White-Fire Crossings

Galewall pilots use "white-fire crossing" for total failures. Rigging fuses and spars split outward. Survivors cannot agree if lightning struck the ship or something alive moved through it. The phrase came from a ship that reached the Ashwall lee with its mainmast gone and shrouds burned black.

The survivor's account is steady where it matters. Stormlight gathered ahead of the ship, not above it. It crossed the mast in a bird-shape and struck crew and rigging. This does not prove a phoenix exists. But it made pilots treat moving stormlight as a real danger.

### Fauna

Storms kill more ships than monsters here and wreckage feeds a food chain. Giant sharks follow damaged hulls near the Ashwall lee. Killer whale pods work the cold water on both sides and follow lifeboats and debris for hours. They attack but survivors fear the circling more. Crews tell stories about rocs riding the storms above the spires.

The Galewall creates recovery-lane piracy: specialized predation on damaged ships. Ships that survive the crossing arrive damaged and short-handed. They welcome any sail that looks helpful. A skilled pirate captain can make rescue look like boarding. The [[the-velvet-noose|Velvet Noose]] is worse, a deep-water flagship large enough to make storm-beaten convoys talk before they can fight.

## Hazards

Storms kill ships and crossing losses run one in three. White-fire events happen and wrecks feed predators. Killer whale pods hunt and rocs soar in the storms. Pirates prey on damaged ships.

## Galewall DM Notes (DM Only)

> [!dm] Phoenix causality and crossing pressure
> Treat this as true: arclight phoenix activity sustains the Galewall. The storm belt is not natural weather. It hatches in stone and dies in lightning. Colonial scholars cannot explain it. A mysterious figure described in Ashwall pilot logs as a stormwarden remains intentionally unresolved (a dragon, another actor, or myth). Ships that follow these hints survive at high rates. Keep outcomes consistent even when the explanation stays uncertain.
>
> Crossing losses are normal. Successful passage costs something: resources, cargo, crew trust, or leverage. Use the Galewall to show that the sea pushes back.

Beyond the natural hazards, the Galewall's damaged traffic invites predation.

> [!dm] Recovery-lane piracy
> A pirate admiral's profile is the right template for a Velvet Noose encounter. A pirate captain is enough for opportunistic recovery-lane boarding.
