East of the [[verdant-teeth|Verdant Teeth]] the land thins and spreads. The islands here are smaller, sandier and further apart, sitting on a broad pale reef shelf that runs shallow for miles before dropping into deep blue at the chain's far end. A ship can sail a whole day between landfalls and still never leave soundings.

These are the oldest waters in the chain and the ones charts agree on least. Standing stones, a broken tower, a hull so large it has become an island: the eastern reef keeps things, and does not explain them. Under the shallows off the [[halythion|Teikhinos Reef]] there is a city that has never once appeared on a colonial survey.

> [!read-aloud]
> The islands out here sit far apart and low, pale sand and scrub, each one ringed by water so clear the reef beneath reads like a second map.
>
> On one, a ring of standing stones breaks the skyline. The next carries a tower with its top gone. Nothing moves on either.
>
> Off to starboard the shallows go suddenly, wrongly dark: a hole in the reef with no bottom the eye can find.
>

## Routes & Access

Distances here are longer than anywhere else in the chain and the water stays shallow the whole way, so a grounding strands a ship instead of sinking it. That difference has kept more crews alive than any pilot has. Reaching the eastern end means committing to a run with few places to shelter.

No pilot family works these waters the way they work the west. What passes for local knowledge is a captain who has been out here before and remembers which shallows lie. Charts contradict each other, and where they agree they are often agreeing with the same old error.

Near [[the-drowned-maw|the Drowned Maw]] the reef communities know their own patch by feel (the shelf edge, the fog's season, which reefs shifted last year), but that knowledge doesn't reach past their own waters. Crews and salvage ships move through tight weather windows there, and a patient pirate can plan around the gaps.

Run the crossing per `vault/refs/vault/location/references/wilderness-travel-and-exploration.md` and `vault/refs/gameplay-toolbox.md` § Travel Pace.

## Hazards

Unmarked blue holes that drop out of a pale reef with no warning and hold things that use them as lairs, reef heads in water too clear to judge for depth, and long reaches with no shelter when weather comes. The ruins are their own hazard. Four of them stand intact enough to enter, and none are empty. Crews who take water from the wrong island come back changed in ways they will not discuss.

Giant sharks work the cold upwelling near the Maw. Vultures and rumoured harpies hold the cliffs at the eastern edge. [[the-glass-debt|The Glass Debt]] rules the route when active, and the [[the-velvet-noose|Velvet Noose]] is a worse rumour still.

## Hooks

- [[halythion|Halythion]] has never appeared on a colonial chart, and somebody in the Strait has started asking why the surveys keep coming back short.
- Whole undamaged wrecks come ashore in the east months after they sank somewhere else.
- The eastern ruins predate every record the Crown holds, and one of them is not as abandoned as the others.
- Tight weather windows and compressed routing near the Drowned Maw give a patient pirate ambush geometry.
- Salvage crews staging for the [[shelfworks|Shelfworks]] route most contracts through [[fathomrush|Fathomrush]], the staging island near the Drowned Maw's western edge.
