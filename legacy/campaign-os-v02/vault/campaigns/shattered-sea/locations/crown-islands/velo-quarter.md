# Velo Quarter

![[velo-quarter-map]]

> [!read-aloud] Velo Quarter
> The second bridge is barely a bridge, four planks and a rail, and it sets you down on stone that somebody has swept.
>
> The square opens about eighty feet across and nothing lights it from above. Every scrap of light down here belongs to a shop. Curtains hang where doors should be, each one lit from behind in its own color.
>
> Smoke rolls out of an open doorway on your left, sweet and heavy enough to taste, and nobody inside is troubling to hide it.
>
> A man on a chalked circle in the middle is juggling three hats and a knife, and losing to the knife. The crowd stands three deep and laughs in the wrong places.
>
> The curtain nearest you moves. A hand is holding it open from the inside, waiting to see whether you come in.

## Geography

**Extent**: roughly 900 ft. by 500 ft., a low teardrop of an island with the square at its wide northern end and the bridge landings down at the tail.

**Topography**: six feet above high water at the crown and less at the rim, so a spring tide lays canal over the lower lanes and the shops there set their thresholds a step high. Footing is old ship ballast laid flat, worn slick under the lantern lines where the oil drips. Sight lines end at cloth, since the square stands open to the sky and walled at ground level by curtains, and with no streetlight over it every light belongs to a shop that can pull it inside. The only ways on or off are the south span from [[le-paludi|Le Paludi]] and the east span that reaches [[the-bridge|The Bridge]] in ten minutes on foot. Neither one clears a keeled hull, so cargo comes in by punt or on a shoulder.

## Culture

Colors the canal frontages forbid get painted straight onto the curtains here. Kingfisher blue goes over arsenic green, and a curtain two doors down carries the deep red a House keeps for its own livery. The standing joke is that most of the paint is last season's House stock, bought cheap off whoever ran the cellar. [[giacinto|Giacinto]] builds the booths and sells the wigs, prosthetics and paint that go with them, so a face on this square is a thing a visitor buys for the evening. Nothing sold here carries a license and nothing needs one; a buyer who wants a city stamp on what he is carrying crosses to [[mercatura|Mercatura]] and buys the same thing over again, in daylight, from a man with a seal. Through [[il-gioco-delle-beffe|Il Gioco delle Beffe]] the stages go up across the open ground and stay up for the Window's three days, and the quarter runs at triple its own weight until they come down.

## Hooks

- A blue-caste [[grung|Grung]] in a festival coat works the square's edge most evenings. He watches how the crowd breaks when a performer makes a scene, and his eyes stay on the crowd the whole time. [[jean-claude-tabarnack|Jean-Claude]] reads it as handler work on sight. Approached, he is polite and volunteers nothing: here for the festival, with no goods to declare and no registration to show. His accent puts him in [[karath|Karath]], a long way from [[veth|Veth]].
- An unsigned commission reaches [[kats-curios|Kat's Curios]] by curtain-drop, paying in [[antheri|Antheri]] parts nobody on the square admits to owning. [[catarina-davirelli|Catarina Da'Virelli]] decides whether she asks where they came from before she uses them or after.
