# Sarn's Landing

> [!read-aloud]
> Reef-break into white lace. A dozen weathered boats cluster in the lee of a narrow cove, their hulls salt-scarred but seaworthy, keels grinding against black sand at the tide's turn. Houses climb the slope behind, rope-lashed and rain-wet. At the cove's highest point stands a shrine of bare stone, stripped lightless, squatting like a broken tooth. Smoke rises from the hearth-houses below, carrying salt and char. The villagers work with practiced efficiency. They have learned not to waste time.

## Geography

**Extent.** Roughly 300 feet along the cove's arc, perhaps 400 feet inland to the shrine's point, a sheltered crescent open to the southeast.

**Topography.** The cove rises steeply from the black sand beach in terraced plots, gaining about 60 feet of vertical from waterline to the shrine point. Footing grows treacherous during high tide when waves lap the houses' lower alcoves. The path up the slope is flagstone mortared tight, slick with spray and age. The houses clustered along the slope block sight lines except from the shrine point, which commands a view of the approach and the outer reef. Access comes by beached boat at low tide (the cove empties to mere shallows) or by scrambling the eastern rocks at higher water. The western rock face climbs too sheer to scale.

## Lore

Some years back, Sarn's Landing was half-empty (its kelp beds failed two winters running and the tithe barely scraped together). A man calling himself a shepherd of mercy began "curing" the sick with power from an unconfirmed source. That power was not lantern-oil, not prayer, not any visible means. A Crown chaplain who passed through smelled wrongness and reported it. [[aleksander-malone|Aleksander Malone]] arrived the next fortnight, a cleric of [[tyr|Tyr]] and the Crown's asset for heresy. He identified the shepherd's power as abyssal bargaining, executed him within the hour, then thanked fourteen villagers for their honesty. He executed them as well. The Crown garrison left behind garrison biscuit. Malone departed believing every villager left standing was innocent. This incident is why the Crown restricts Malone to confirmed heresy only.

Eighteen months after Malone's boat pulled away, a smuggling lane opened off the reef. It moves cargo the Crown knows nothing about, node by node. No one has ever traced it back to what happened here.

## Hooks

**If Ignored:** the smuggling lane consolidates. In another season, the network reaches neighboring islands, supply routes thicken, and the Crown's eye turns to Sarn's Landing itself. When inspection comes, the village faces a choice: expose the lane (and the garrison's judgment) or lie quietly while the investigation spreads outward and the smugglers bury deeper.

- **Moral.** The village still argues, quietly, over whether the fourteen who confessed deserved what they got. Some say honesty should have earned mercy. Others say the cure itself was the confession, and the Crown acted on confession alone. Others say nothing at all, which is its own position.

- **Personal.** The smuggling lane's roots trace straight back to what [[aleksander-malone|Aleksander Malone]] did here and to why the Crown still keeps him leashed for it. Malone's career, his limits, his posting restrictions all flow from Sarn's Landing.

- **Opportunistic.** The smuggling lane itself is a real, exploitable resource: profitable, well-run, flying under Crown patrol notice, with operators competent enough to erase their tracks. A party with the right connections or the right problem to move could find themselves very wealthy very quickly.
