# Aldenmere

Aldenmere is the fourth Crown Island, between [[harwick|Harwick]] to the west and [[stenmark|Stenmark]] to the east. It has no fortress or military presence, just a working fishing and farming settlement. [[dravosi-crown|The Dravosi Crown]] claims it on paper and sends a revenue officer twice yearly to collect the fishing tithe, who then departs.

The name comes from the mere, a tidal lake in the island's center. A narrow inlet connects it to the sea. Farms ring the eastern shore. Fishing fleets work both the mere and open water. The town grew on stone-bearing ground and has not moved since.

### Land & Water

The island is lower than [[harwick|Harwick]] and greener than [[calven|Calven]]. Rolling farms and hedged fields cover much of it. A large depression in the center fills with tidal water but never fully drains. This is the mere, roughly a mile across. It becomes shallow in summer and deep in spring. Reed beds, pasture, and alder trees ring it.

Short cliffs rise on the north shore. Beaches and tidal flats stretch along the south. A natural headland shelters the southwestern harbour.

The inland lake supplies protein year-round: freshwater eels, crayfish, and reed-filtered fish harvested on a rotation established long before the Crown arrived. The inlet channel is navigable by flat-bottomed boats at high tide. In storms, the mere serves as sheltered anchorage for small craft.

The deeper sections stay murky year-round. Locals know safe channels from unsafe ones but keep this knowledge to themselves. The mere holds giant frogs in reed beds (a seasonal nuisance for livestock and a danger when wading at dusk) and giant toads along the deeper waterline. The real threat is a giant crocodile population in the inland inlet channel, one the island culls regularly though the population ages and the records slip behind. Locals don't swim in the mere and don't explain why to visitors. Offshore, hunter sharks patrol the southern approaches. Giant crabs occupy northern cliff pools. Giant owls nest in old alder stands, hunting farmland at night (considered good luck by farmers).

### The Town

The main settlement runs along the eastern shore of the mere and back up the slope toward the harbour road. Stone houses, low and practical. A market square doubles as a fish-drying ground in season. The harbourmaster's office handles water, provisions, and shelter with no rating station or registry desk attached. Ships stop here, pay modest berthing fees, and move on.

Aldenmere sends fish, preserved catch, eels, crayfish, grain, root vegetables, orchard fruit, reeds, and provisions toward [[harwick|Harwick]] and [[tidefall|Tidefall]].

## Flora & Fauna

- Alder & reed cover: old alder stands and dense reed beds fringe the mere, screening sightlines between the shore path and the interior farmland.
- Reeds: harvested from the mere's margins for weaving and thatch, one of the island's steady trade exports alongside its catch.
- Freshwater eels and crayfish: the mere's year-round protein harvest, worked on a rotation locals guard closely from outsiders.
- [[giant-owl|Giant Owls]]: nest in the old alder stands and hunt farmland at night. Farmers call a nesting pair good luck.
- [[giant-frog|Giant Frogs]] and [[giant-toad|Giant Toads]]: crowd the reed beds and the mere's deeper waterline, a seasonal nuisance to livestock and a real bite risk to anyone wading at dusk.
- [[giant-crocodile|Giant Crocodiles]] hold the inlet channel in an aging, regularly culled population. [[hunter-shark|Hunter Sharks]] patrol the southern approaches, and [[giant-crab|Giant Crabs]] occupy the northern cliff pools. Locals don't swim in the mere and don't explain why.
