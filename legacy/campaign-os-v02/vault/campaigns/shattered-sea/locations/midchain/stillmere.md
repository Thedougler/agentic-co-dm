# Stillmere

> [!read-aloud]
> Smoke curls flat over a huddle of low stone houses, roofs weighted with driftwood against the wind off the strait. A crowd's noise carries before the village does: laughter, a ragged cheer, steel ringing twice, then a groan that turns to laughter again. A shoulder-to-shoulder crowd fills the green at the village's heart, watching two islanders circle each other with real blades. Fishing nets dry along every wall you pass, salt-stiff and mended so many times the mending is most of the net. Down by the waterline an old man sings tunelessly to a goat. Neither minds the fight. Past the green, unhurried, a woman grinds something in a stone mortar, and the rhythm of it never breaks while you're in earshot.

## Geography

**Extent.** Roughly 300 ft. by 200 ft., a huddle of houses and one packed-earth green set back from the old waterline post the outside world reads as a quarantine marker.

**Topography.** The village sits on the one flat shelf of ground above the tideline, houses built low and close against the wind. Footing runs packed earth and old cobble near the green, softening to sand and marsh grass toward the water. Sightlines run clean across the green itself but close fast past the first row of houses, where the reclaimed quarantine road narrows into bramble. The beach path from the waterline post and the choked inland road toward the old pest houses are the only ways in. Nobody takes the second one without a reason.

## Government

No one rules Stillmere in the way a mainland town would recognize. Disputes go to the Standing Few, a rotating handful of the island's longest-lived residents, not elected, not appointed, just whoever has been listening to grievances the longest and hasn't left.

Succession never happens the way it should. Nobody has died of old age here in living memory, so a seat on the Standing Few only opens when someone goes missing at sea or simply walks off the island and doesn't come back.

Inheritance follows the same rule. An islander's house and boat pass on only once a Standing Will, filed the day they leave, naming who takes over if they don't return, actually triggers. Most residents never file one, on the theory that they'll still be around to decide later.

## Trade

Stillmere's herds are the island's one real export, and the reason a boat this small can undercut every butcher in [[midchain|the Midchain]]. A slaughtered animal is back on its feet within the hour, stripped of skin and organs, grazing again once it can stand. Traders who buy the salted meat cheap never ask why Stillmere never runs short of stock, and the islanders who sell it have stopped offering an explanation.

## Culture

The green at the village center is the Proving Ground, and dueling here settles debts, courtships, and insults alike, all with real steel, because a blade through the ribs stings for a week and heals for none of it, but it doesn't end anything. Visiting sailors join in freely. Losing costs nothing but pride and a limp that never goes away.

The night before a chronic wound gets bound for good, a shattered hand, a gut that won't close, anything past what splinting alone manages, the sufferer and whoever's binding them fast from sundown. They dance the width of the green until they can't stand. Then they sit and confess whatever's weighing on them to anyone still awake to hear it. Islanders call it clearing the vessel and can't say who started the custom or why it's supposed to help. It doesn't, particularly. Nobody's dropped the order since.

## Notable NPCs

- [[otho|Otho]]: found most evenings at the waterline, singing to whichever goat will tolerate him. Everyone assumes he's simply always been around.
- [[gerrit-sallow|Gerrit Sallow]]: the island's oldest duelist, missing three fingers and half an ear from bouts that should have killed him decades apart. He offers coin, real coin, to any outsider willing to try to finish what no islander's blade ever has.

## Hooks

If ignored, Gerrit keeps testing every crew that puts in at the waterline. Eventually a stranger's blade lands somewhere an islander's never has. Whether that finally kills him or just adds one more wound nobody can close, the Standing Few hear about it before the crew leaves.

- **Moral**: Gerrit is asking to die, sincerely, and nobody on Stillmere will grant it. A crew that takes him seriously has to decide whether trying counts as mercy or murder when neither one is possible to prove.
- **Personal**: [[perrin-black-jaw|Perrin Black-Jaw]] watches a Binder walk a patient through the fast-dance-confession sequence before working an unhealable wound. He recognizes the shape of it without knowing why: the same fasting, the same collective fervor his failed Arcana check couldn't name back in Session 03. Nothing on Stillmere confirms the link, but it's the first thread Perrin's had since.
- **Opportunistic**: the Proving Ground pays out real coin on real wagers, and a stranger nobody's seen fight before draws the biggest odds.
