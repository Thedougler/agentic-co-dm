# Corallith

> [!read-aloud]
> The reef around the island pulses faintly in time with something underneath, a rhythm too slow to be tide.
>
> Coral formations you'd swear weren't there on the last chart jut where open water used to be. The air tastes of brine gone thick. A low creak carries up through the hull each time the swell settles.
>
> *(low)* Local pilots don't call it an island. They call it "the sleeper," and they never anchor to it directly.

Corallith isn't built on coral. It's a living creature. Its visible reef is just the surface part. It moves, but so slowly that nobody notices. They only discover the change later.

## Flora & Fauna

- Coral turf and bladder-wrack: the colossus's own outer skin is the ground here, knuckled plates of living coral matted over in slick olive bladder-wrack that hides each plate's edge and turns a quick step into a fall.
- Tide-pool sea grapes: translucent green clusters pool in the coral's hollows at low tide, sweet and salt-sharp. A pilot who anchors nearby fills a barrel before the water turns.
- Ochre sponge: soft ochre sponges root wherever the coral's pulse runs slowest, and a diver willing to cut past the sting sells them by the pound in [[kalowe|Kalowe]]'s market stalls.
- [[crab|Crabs]]: work every tide pool at the base of the plates in the dozens, easy pickings for anyone who wades in with a bucket.
- [[reef-shark|Reef sharks]]: run the flooded channels between the coral's raised knuckles in packs of four or five, patient enough to trail a wading party the whole crossing.
- [[giant-octopus|Giant octopuses]]: den in the deep hollows where the coral's pulse beats loudest, and surface only when something smaller than themselves blunders into the dark.
