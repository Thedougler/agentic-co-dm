# Otel Karn

Otel Karn sits alone off [[midchain|the Midchain]]'s eastern reach, a low green island ringed by a clean white beach and a quarantine post nobody's replaced in generations. A plague swept through decades ago and burned itself out. Pilots still won't land, and most couldn't say why beyond a superstition handed down instead of an explanation. A few hundred people live here now, most of them in [[stillmere|Stillmere]], the one shared quarantine-era village, the rest scattered across outlying homesteads. They descend from the families who never left.

No one governs Otel Karn. The islanders work the same fields and pens their grandparents worked, trade little, and expect no visitors. Anyone who does land past the rotted quarantine rope finds an island that looks like any other, at least until something happens here that shouldn't.

## Geography

**Extent.** Roughly two miles end to end, a single low island a party can cross on foot in an afternoon. **Topography.** The quarantine beach gives way to bramble and a half-reclaimed road, rising gently into pasture and the old pest-house cluster. Nothing on Otel Karn rises higher than the tree line, and sightlines run clear to the water from most of the interior.

## Culture

Outsiders who've heard of Otel Karn at all know it as the island that stayed shut after its plague. Pilots avoid it on a superstition nobody can explain, as if the sickness itself might still be waiting. The plague wasn't the reason. Nothing here has died since, plague-culled or otherwise, and the families who remember why keep it to themselves. Strangers wouldn't believe them anyway.

To a visitor who doesn't know that yet, Otel Karn reads as close to a paradise. Fields get worked without urgency, doors stay open, and the whole island feels unhurried and unafraid. Nobody hurries here. That ease shows plainest on [[stillmere|Stillmere]]'s green, what locals call the Proving Ground, where islanders duel with real steel over debts, courtships, and insults alike. Losing costs nothing but pride and a limp that never goes away.

[[corvenna|Corvenna]] settles disputes with a masked champion and a verdict everyone abides by. The Proving Ground settles nothing on purpose. It's recreation, the one place in the Midchain where a fight has stopped meaning anything is actually at stake.

Older and stranger still, a handful of islanders keep up a decayed devotional practice nobody living can fully explain. They fast and dance the width of the green until they collapse, then confess whatever's weighing on them to anyone still awake to hear it. No one remembers why. Old islanders call the root of it the Turning and say only that it's older than the quarantine, older than most of what else survived it. On [[stillmere|Stillmere]] the same sequence survives in practical form, walked the night before a wound too far gone for splinting gets bound for good, under a newer name nobody remembers choosing: clearing the vessel.

## Routes & Access

No sanctioned pilot runs Otel Karn. Most captains sail wide of it instead of risking the crossing, though nothing in the water here is worse than the rest of the Eastern Midchain. A party that wants to land ties off at the rotted post and walks straight up the beach. Nobody watches the tide line. Run the crossing per `vault/refs/vault/location/references/wilderness-travel-and-exploration.md` and `vault/refs/gameplay-toolbox.md` § Travel Pace if it matters at the table.

## Hazards

A wound taken on Otel Karn doesn't heal. Not from a blade, not from infection, not from time. It still hurts exactly as much as it should. Anyone run through here carries that wound, open and aching, for as long as they stay. Real restorative magic can close it. A bandage or a short rest does nothing.

Nothing native to the island kills outright, which makes a landing crew reckless fast. The real cost only shows up once the adrenaline fades. The wound is still there the next morning, and the one after that.

## Ecology

Otel Karn's interior reclaimed itself the moment the quarantine road stopped seeing foot traffic. Bramble and sea-fig scrub choke the old path enough that anyone crossing it cuts a new one, and beach purslane grows thick enough along the dune line to feed a hungry crew for an afternoon. [[giant-crab|Giant crabs]] work the tide pools below the waterline post, [[swarm-of-rats|rat swarms]] nest in the abandoned quarantine houses, and [[stirge|stirges]] roost in the pest house rafters, ordinary hazards for an island nobody's bothered clearing in generations.

What isn't ordinary is the pasture. Livestock butchered for the table keeps grazing afterward, skinned, deboned in places, missing whatever organs the knife took, cropping grass it has no way left to digest, unbothered exactly as it stood before the blade. [[vulture|Vultures]] circle the interior in slow columns and never once drop, which pilots have always read as confirmation the island's still poisoned. Vultures know dead meat. Nothing on Otel Karn is dead enough to interest them.

## Notable NPCs

- [[otho|Old Otho]], the island's fixture drunk, welcome everywhere and always mid-story. "He's just always been around" is the answer anyone gives when asked how long they've known him, and nobody's had a reason to wonder further before now.

## Hooks

If ignored, nothing here resolves on its own. Otel Karn just keeps not changing. Its herds outgrow what the pastures can support, since nothing already alive properly starves either. The practiced silence around the real reason gets a little more brittle every generation that has to keep lying to the next one.

- **Moral hook:** the islander who lost that duel and still can't stop feeling it has started asking outsiders, quietly, whether they've ever heard of a way to actually die.
- **Personal hook:** [[perrin-black-jaw|Perrin Black-Jaw]]'s failed Arcana check against [[shepherd-grigori|Shepherd Grigori]]'s healing in Session 03 gives him real reason to want an answer this island can hand him.
- **Opportunistic hook:** word that a fight here can't actually kill anyone is worth real money to the right buyer. A debt collector could stage a fake death here. A duelist could get real live-steel practice with no risk of dying. Either one pays well for the introduction.
