# Halewick

Halewick is an ordinary small [[midchain|Midchain]] fishing island. Houses line a swept lane. Terraces climb the slope above it toward a stream that cuts down to the dock, and boats go out at dawn the way they always have. Nothing about the island's own bones has changed. What has changed, in the last three weeks, is what the villagers believe about who runs it. Reality has quietly agreed with them.

> [!read-aloud]
> Small, orderly houses line a swept lane, laundry strung between them at just-reachable height, snapping faintly in the sea breeze. Woodsmoke and drying fish carry on the wind. Somewhere behind a shutter, a broom scrapes stone in a steady, practiced rhythm. Then a bell rings from up the terrace, off-key and delighted, and every villager within earshot stops mid-motion to bow toward the sound.
>
> A cluster of children stop what they're doing to stare, then one steps forward, hands on hips, in charge.
>
> *The child*: *(flat, utterly certain)* State your business with the Emperor's island. We don't get many ships.

## Geography

**Extent.** A single small island, walkable end to end in under a day. **Topography.** A swept lane runs through the village proper. Terraced slopes climb above it toward untended farmland, a stream cuts down to the dock, and low stone walls between the terraces have gone shaggy with bramble and salt grass. Nothing about the shape of the place has changed in three weeks. What's changed is what happens when someone speaks on it.

## Government

[[toby|Emperor Toby]] rules Halewick, and not one subject dissents. Halewick has a real court, with real offices and real succession politics. A Snack Minister tastes the emperor's food first, an office fought over as fiercely as any real ministry once the last one ages out of favor. A dog, formally knighted, serves as Captain of the Guard, and the village's armed adults defer to it without a flicker of irony. Royal decree eats dessert first, always. Nobody remembers a version of dinner where it wasn't. The throne is a fishing boat hauled up onto the square, its gunwale lashed with driftwood into an armrest. Whatever game the emperor currently favors becomes sacred ritual, and the whole village plays along with total gravity. None of this is performance. Every villager, every minister, every subject believes with their whole heart that Halewick has always worked this way.

This isn't the only will shaping the island. [[frankie|Frankie]] carries an identical, untrained power of her own. Her running grudge with Toby, an ordinary playground slight escalated to cosmic stakes, periodically overrides or collides with his decrees wherever the two of them happen to stand.

## Culture

Bedtime falls at 7:00 p.m. sharp, village-wide, adults included. Nobody enforces it. Everyone simply, unshakably believes they have always gone to bed at 7:00. Punishment for a real offense sends the offender to stand in the corner, for exactly as long as the emperor decided at the time. Given a nine-year-old's fuzzy sense of time, that could mean an hour, or it could, technically, mean forever. Nobody here has ever felt a sentence as unjust. Nobody here experiences any of it as strange.

One group stands apart: Halewick's deaf villagers. The compulsion rides on directly perceiving an utterance, not on understanding what it meant. A decree relayed to a deaf villager secondhand, in writing or in sign, only informs them. It never compels them. Every deaf person on the island still carries their original, un-overwritten memory. They watch everyone around them live inside something that isn't real, with no safe way to say so where anyone could repeat it aloud.

## Routes & Access

Halewick's dock takes any ship that stops long enough to ask. No blockade and no armed watch turns anyone away. The real danger is standing in earshot of a child mid-decree, covered under Hazards below. A visitor who neither speaks to nor hears either child directly, using written notes, sign language, or simply staying clear of a proclamation's range, can walk the island and leave it exactly as they arrived.

## Hazards

The emperor's word functions as authorship, a different mechanism than compulsion. Toby doesn't control minds and get obeyed. He asserts a new true fact about reality, and his subjects' will, their bodies, and the ground under their feet bend to match whatever he claimed.

Contradictory commands don't crash anything either. Reality just makes both true at once, no matter how absurd the contortion required, because "impossible" isn't a category that constrains an assertion made at this level.

Range matters only at the instant of speaking. A target has to be within earshot, or reached via [[sending|Sending]] or telepathy, for a new command to land. Once a command lands, it stops needing the child nearby. It becomes a fixed, permanent fact of reality until something, currently unknown to anyone, undoes it. Leaving the island's range stops *new* commands from landing. It does nothing to reverse whatever already has. A visitor caught in range during a tantrum could sail away carrying a permanent, real fact about themselves that nobody in the world knows how to undo.

Pockets of permanently altered reality sit scattered around the island, wherever Toby and [[frankie|Frankie]]'s clashes have concentrated. None of these stays necessarily active, but every one stays permanently, visibly unnatural. Effect intensity concentrates wherever the two children were actually standing when a collision happened, so these scars cluster near whatever the day's argument was about instead of sitting at any fixed boundary.

## Ecology

Toby's edict of "no more monsters" scoured the island clean, literally, the instant he said it. It took every dangerous animal and every biting insect with it, and the thorn bushes went too. Under the postcard prettiness is a landscape edited down to a nine-year-old's idea of nice. Every lawn has nothing sharp in it. Every hedgerow has nothing that bites. Ordinary fish still school past the sandbar. Ordinary crabs still work the tideline at low water. The edict spared nothing that could hurt a child and touched nothing else.

## Notable NPCs

- [[toby|Toby]], the nine-year-old emperor. His word, joyfully believed by every subject, has remade the island's government, calendar, and memory around him.
- [[frankie|Frankie]], Toby's rival. An ordinary playground grudge escalated to cosmic stakes, and her untrained cursed speech now collides and stacks with his wherever the two of them stand.
- [[billy|Billy]], the third child on the island, the only one who grasps that this can't hold. His compelled villagers now carry, word-for-word and unstoppably, the summons that reaches outside travelers.

## Hooks

A handful of Halewick's own people carry word out, wherever a ship finds them, a port or open water alike, compelled by Billy to repeat one line identically no matter who asks or how. *"We will destroy everything if this is not stopped and it leaves our island."* Not gossip, not rumor. A direct, word-for-word summons.

Older, looser talk reaches port well before that summons does: [[word-from-halewick|Word from Halewick]], a garbled, years-old sailor's legend that already names the island without knowing any of what's actually happening on it.

If ignored, the compulsion doesn't wait. Toby and Frankie's clashes keep concentrating new scars into the island's reality. Whatever a visitor was doing in earshot of either child when they last spoke can already be a permanent, unmovable fact about that visitor.

Personal hook: this island's horror is a direct, currently unknown-to-him consequence of [[delmar-fisk|Delmar Fisk]]'s own theft of the [[pearl-of-souls|Pearl of Souls]]. The Pearl's unresolved signal at [[the-drowned-maw|the Drowned Maw]] has been thinning and aligning distant planar boundaries across the [[shattered-sea|Shattered Sea]] as its pressure grows.

About three weeks ago, that alignment landed here, on the boundary between ordinary Common and Truespeak, the primordial language of creation. Neither child carries the years of discipline a trained truenamer spends earning that boundary's respect. Delmar doesn't know Halewick exists. What's happening here is the Pearl's theft finding somewhere else in the world to go wrong.
