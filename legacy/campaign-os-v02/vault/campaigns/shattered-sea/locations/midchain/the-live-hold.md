# The Live Hold

> [!read-aloud]
> A converted warehouse squats on the lower quay, its timber front gouged and blistered where forty years of stock fought the wall on the way in. The door stands open.
>
> Sound arrives before the room does: shrieks, hisses, snapping beaks, and beneath them the scratch and shuffle of clawed feet on stone that never once stops.
>
> Inside runs dim and cool. Reinforced cages climb all three walls in tiers, tanks steam on stone benches down the middle, and the stacks cut the sight lines so you cannot see the back of a building two hundred feet deep.
>
> The air is seawater and raw meat, and behind that the musk of things that have never been under open sky.
>
> A woman in a leather apron works the aisle between the cages, checking locks, topping water, never fully stopping. She has counted you already.
>
> In the far corner, a bird bigger than a hawk turns in a cage built for it, plumage running green to red as it comes about, its left wing folded wrong against its body.
>
> It opens its beak, and the cry breaks like a dropped pane of glass.

## Structure

The Hold is a cargo warehouse converted to hold things that fight back, roughly 200 feet long by 120 wide on [[sparhold|Sparhold]]'s lower quay, its vaulted timber roof ribbed like a ship's hull and its street front scarred by decades of claw gouges and heat stress. The main door faces the water. A smaller rear exit opens toward the storage yards, and [[sabine-corvo|Sabine Corvo]] wires a snare across it every night before she leaves.

Reinforced cages climb the perimeter walls in three tiers, worked from a ladder that runs on a rail the length of each wall. Heated tanks for aquatic stock stand on stone benches down the center aisle. Packed stone underfoot slopes toward drainage channels that carry off washout and waste, and two hands with a hose strip the whole building down in an hour. The cage stacks block every sight line, which makes a warren of an open footprint: a customer three aisles in cannot see the counter, and neither can anyone at the counter see them.

## Atmosphere

Dim and cool by design, which slows a stranger down and settles the animals. The noise never lets up, and the air is seawater and raw meat over the musk of caged things. Sabine works the aisles the whole time she talks to you, checking locks and topping water, and she handles every creature herself before it leaves so a buyer sees exactly what it does under a stranger's hand.

Prices run steep for anything sourced deep into the [[verdant-teeth|Verdant Teeth]]. She does not haggle and does not hold stock on account, so each creature either finds a buyer who can afford it or stays in her cages. The crimson-crowned harrier in the far corner, iridescent and larger than a hawk, took a bad clip to its left wing during capture. Sabine wants 400 gold for it, and has wanted 400 gold for it since the day it arrived two months ago. A pair of salt-drake hatchlings from a coastal Verdant Teeth expedition share a heated tank, neither longer than a hand-span. A raptor bird off the Teeth's fringe holds the cage beside them, mottled and unsold, and it will only take prey that is still moving.

## Hooks

A caged raptor bird beats itself against the bars in a frenzy. One of the salt-drake hatchlings has turned pale and still, its tank water fouled. The disturbance commands focus the moment anyone steps through the door, before Sabine has even finished her greeting.

When a creature is in crisis, so is her merchandise. She won't hide her concern, and a problem she can't solve alone becomes a reason to call on capable strangers.
