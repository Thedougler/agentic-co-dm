# Ussamet

> [!read-aloud]
> At dead low tide, broken columns break the surface where open water sat an hour ago, barnacled and streaming.
>
> The retreating water hisses off stone that shouldn't be dry, and something in the current pulls at your ankles that isn't just the tide.
>
> *(quiet)* Locals watching from their boats won't say how long the ruin stays up. Only that the sea decides, not the calendar.

Ussamet's ruins only break the surface at the lowest tides, and never stay up for the same length of time twice. Local pilots avoid landing on them, believing that whatever sank them isn't done yet.

## Flora & Fauna

- Drift kelp and salt-crust: nothing roots in the stone itself, but kelp snags between the columns at the tide line and dries to a black mat a party has to climb through the moment the water pulls back.
- Mussel clusters: wedged thick in the ruin's joints, and worth the cuts from the barnacles around them to anyone who can strip a handful before the tide turns.
- [[crab|Crabs]]: work the drained pools between the columns in the ruin's last hour above water, easy pickings for anyone willing to share the stones with them.
- [[vulture|Vultures]]: circle the moment the ruin strands something on it, and land before the tide has even finished dropping.
- [[reef-shark|Reef sharks]]: hold the deep water past the ruin's edge and come in fast once the rising tide covers the stones again, so anyone who misjudges the turn is not alone in it.
