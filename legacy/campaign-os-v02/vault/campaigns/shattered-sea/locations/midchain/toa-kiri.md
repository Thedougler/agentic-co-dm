# Toa Kiri

> [!read-aloud]
> They slip off the gunwale one at a time, weighted baskets gripped tight. No one speaks.
>
> Below the surface their hand signs move fast and precise, a whole conversation with no sound at all.
>
> *(hushed)* On the beach, an old woman only mouths the count of baskets. Out loud, she says, the sea keeps its own tally of who talks back.

Toa Kiri's pearl-workers have kept the same silence rule for generations: nothing spoken below the surface, ever. Nobody remembers why exactly, only that those who broke it are the ones who didn't come back up.

## Flora & Fauna

- Screw-pine thickets crowd the interior slopes, roots stilted a foot clear of the ground, thick enough that the diving families cut a single marked trail instead of pushing new ones each season.
- Coconut palms line the lee shore in loose stands; green nuts get husked and drunk on the beach between dives, and the dried meat keeps in the diving huts through the wet months.
- Sea purslane carpets the open ground behind the tideline and gets chewed raw for the salt, the same way it's used on [[marenui|Marenui]] to the north.
- [[crab|Crabs]] work every rock shelf and piling at low tide, thick enough that a basket left overturned overnight comes back full.
- [[hawk|Hawks]] hunt the ridgeline above the diving beach, riding the same thermal every morning the pearl-workers have learned to set their watches by.
- [[reef-shark|Reef sharks]] hold the pearl beds in the shallows the pearl-workers dive, and a [[giant-octopus|giant octopus]] has claimed the drop-off past the last oyster bed: break silence below the surface and the sharks close in, and two of the pearl-workers have lost their catch-baskets to the octopus and never explain why.
