## Part of

[[penance-bar|Penance Bar]]. The shrine stands alone on the point past the last house, where the causeway ends.

> [!read-aloud]
> One room of dark fitted stone holds the chapel, smaller than its bell suggests, with a basin at the far end and nothing on the walls but salt bloom.
>
> The tally-stones outside stop at the threshold.
>
> A line of the same scratched marks continues in chalk along the inside wall, fresher, still growing.
>
> Tallow candles gutter in the draft from a door at the back that never fully shuts.
>
> From behind it comes the scrape of a pen, writing something down.

Fitted stone sits on a rock spur, roofed in salvaged slate and open to weather on three sides. The nave holds a stone basin at the altar end and a bench along each wall, worn smooth by three years of kneeling.

A locked door at the back leads to Ashe's own quarters, where he keeps the true ledger. The chalk tally on the interior wall is the public count. The bound ledger behind the locked door is the real one, and the two don't match, gaining one honest notch every fortieth night regardless of who the public tally names.

Ashe runs the shrine alone, with no permanent staff. The bell in the tower rings only on his hand, and the village has learned to read its timing as a verdict. Nothing has ever answered him at the basin, whatever he tells the village he's praying to. What visits Sorrowbell on the fortieth night doesn't use the door.

### Ashe's Quarters
