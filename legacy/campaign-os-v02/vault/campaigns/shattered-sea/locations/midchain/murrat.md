# Murrat

Murrat sits midway between [[kalowe|Kalowe]] and the [[verdant-teeth|Verdant Teeth]], barely visible at first and looking, from a distance, like any other uninhabited [[midchain|Midchain]] island. Most pilots don't have it charted, and the ones who do avoid it anyway: the [[moucheron|Moucherons]] who control it attack any outsider who lands, with no negotiating and no exceptions.

## Geography

**Extent.** Roughly 1.5 miles across, one irregular limestone mass ringed by a reef shelf with no marked safe passage.

**Topography.** A low central ridge climbs gently from the shoreline, its fresh springs and interior lake feeding the dozens of villages built into the cliffs around it. Forest canopy closes sight to twenty feet within a few strides of any beach, so nothing inland shows from open water. The Moucherons build stone platforms into natural hollows in the cliff faces, linked village to village by [[rope|rope]] bridges. From the water, none of it shows. The reef ring is the only way ashore by boat; the canopy is the only way through on foot, and it hides Moucheron scouts and trespassers alike.

## Government

No island-wide ruler exists on Murrat. Each of the dozens of cliffside villages is its own kin-group, led by an elder who speaks for it in dealings with the villages around it. The one law every kin-group enforces alike is the island's reputation: kin-groups hunt down any outsider who lands and turn every offer of parley away. [[ket|Ket]] is the sole known exception, and only because [[barnaby-rook|Barnaby Rook]] took him captive before that law could reach him.

## Culture

Blood is currency on Murrat. The formal, consensual bloodletting that Moucheron mercenary squads use to settle contracts in the wider Midchain began here as village practice: a kin-group buys skilled work, mends a grudge, or seals an agreement between villages with a measured draw instead of coin. A Moucheron who leaves Murrat to work as a mercenary carries that economy unchanged, trading neighbors for paying clients.

## Defenses

Murrat doesn't need walls. Moucherons fly at sixty feet and see everything below, so a lookout spots any approaching ship before that ship ever spots a village, and villages answer a landing with squads inside minutes. Killing the first squad doesn't end it. More follow. The interior finishes what the villages start: giant snakes, spiders, apes, and frogs hunt anyone who flees the beach inland, trading one danger for another. Even reaching land is a gamble in itself. The eastern reef shelf runs longer than any chart shows, sharp enough to have caused two known wrecks, with a tide that pulls toward it, not away.
