## Structure

One long room off the harbour-side lane on [[kalowe|Kalowe]]'s first island, wedged between the dry-dock offices and the primary berth, close enough that a crew member sent up with a list is back aboard inside the hour. Dry stores run the left wall in roped tiers, liquids the right. The counter cuts across the far end under a slate where Suryo chalks the day's bulk rates. Behind it a doorway opens on the back room, where crates stand stacked tight against the walls under chalk marks of initials, dates, and ship names. The cistern sits out the side door. Water drawn that morning goes out by the jug, or straight into a cask at the counter.

## Atmosphere

The trade here is unglamorous and the shopkeeper makes no apology for it. [[suryo-wibowo|Suryo Wibowo]] works and talks at the same speed. He reads a crew's real needs off an order faster than the captain states them, and he tells an under-ordering buyer so in blunt terms that never turn unkind. Captains he has dealt with three times or more get credit on a handshake and a memory he keeps without a ledger.

The staples move by the ton: ship's biscuit, barrelled salt pork, dried local fish, rice padded against damp, beans, salt and vinegar for the casks. Dried fruit and citrus baskets go out with every long passage, the first for morale and the second because scurvy costs a captain more than the fruit does. Palm oil for a [[galley]] runs clean and cheap. Spices and ground coffee cost real coin, and loose tobacco holds its value at any port with thin through-traffic. Small casks come filled and sealed. A ship's forty comes empty, with the cistern fill priced apart from it. Rum, common wine, and small beer round out the board, the beer safer than untreated water past three days at sea. Anything above fifty pounds dry or twenty gallons wet takes the bulk rate: a tenth off dry stores and cased liquids alike, fifteen percent off a mixed order.
