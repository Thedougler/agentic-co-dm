# Nallowick

> [!read-aloud]
> Peat and reed mat together into an island that gives slightly underfoot, the whole mass rising and falling with the swell beneath it.
>
> No anchor holds here. You tie off to the island itself, and it tows you along with it. The peat mat creaks and sucks at your boots with every step, and the whole place smells of wet moss and standing brine.
>
> *(low)* Pilots who've tracked it for years swear it keeps the same slow circuit, season after season.

Nallowick isn't fixed to any seabed. It drifts a long, slow loop through the [[midchain|Midchain]] on a schedule only a handful of pilots have bothered to chart.

## Flora & Fauna

- Peat moss and marsh reed: sphagnum moss and stands of tall reed knit into the floating mat itself, so every step gives slightly and never dries underfoot.
- Bog cranberries and reed shoots: wild cranberries cluster in the moss's wetter hollows and young reed shoots pull sweet from the base, both worth a forager's stop before the island drifts on.
- Pressed sphagnum: packed moss slows a wound's bleeding and holds a dressing in place, a trick every pilot who's tracked Nallowick a season or two carries and most outsiders don't.
- [[frog|Marsh frogs]] and [[rat|Marsh rats]]: the frogs work the wetter hollows and go loud after dusk, the rats run the reed line and raid an unwatched pack without much fear.
- [[giant-vulture|Giant vultures]]: circle wherever the peat mat cracks open over something dead underneath, and won't scatter for anything short of a torch.
- [[venomous-snake|Venomous snakes]] and [[swarm-of-venomous-snakes|snake swarms]]: den in the drier hummocks and strike out of the reed line with no warning, the one real danger of crossing the interior on foot.
