# Ashwall Islands

![[ashwall-islands-banner]]

The water darkens before the islands appear. It shifts from warm green to cold, deep blue, then black. The spires rise sudden and volcanic, steep-sided. Cloud drags across the upper heights. Surf bursts white against the bases. The air tastes of salt and minerals. A thin sulphur bite comes from the vents.

Sailors on the western passage use the Ashwalls as a reckoning point. They mark the last solid ground before the [[galewall|Galewall]]. They mark the first safe ground on the return. Navigation relies on stars, instruments, and care. Care keeps you alive when nothing lies ahead but water.

The spires are not empty. Giant bats roost in warm vent caves. Giant vultures work the cliffs after wrecks. High-wind crews tell harpy stories about voices in the storm gaps. Most pilots ignore those stories until someone follows a wrong sound inland.

The vent fissures in the high stone also hold giant scorpions. The volcanic heat keeps the crevices warm through cold sea air, and the same crack systems that look like good handholds on the cliff face can run back many feet into occupied dark. Duvane (duvane), a repair crew carpenter, met one while checking a fouled spar. The claw raked his forearm, and the sting drove through his boot. Two days of poison recovery followed. He never saw it before it struck. Ashwall repair work now sends two hands: one to watch, one to work.

## Routes & Access

Sailors running the western passage know the Ashwalls as a threshold. Outbound, they are the last solid ground before the [[galewall|Galewall]]. Inbound, they are the first proof of survival. Ashwall crews treat them not as a destination, but as a reckoning point.

### People & Power

Crews who survive and work the Ashwalls govern them in practice: pilots, repair hands, storm readers, and families whose log books preserve practical signs before theory.

### Trade & Resources

The Ashwalls are not a market destination. They offer: repair lee, storm-edge proof, pilot knowledge, and emergency work. They give crews a last chance to fix a damaged hull.

### The Hatching Sign

Ashwall crews distinguish between two kinds of eruption lightning. The vertical kind runs from cloud to stone. It's ordinary storm discharge. No one marks it. The lateral kind moves sideways through the ash. It branches and holds for a breath before it fades. That is the sign. Crews who see it know what it means. They know roughly how long before something emerges.

Arclight phoenixes hatch inside the Ashwall volcanoes. When a phoenix dies, its egg remains. Lightning opens the egg, and the vents provide that lightning. The bird emerges oriented toward the storm edge. It flies west into the [[galewall|Galewall]]. It does not return until it dies again in the weather.

### The Lightning-Burned Survivor

The Ashwalls are where [[galewall|Galewall]] stories become evidence. A ship that survives the storm belt reaches these spires damaged, wet, and short on certainty. Pilots and repair crews can still read the hull better than the crew can read its own fear.

One survivor described a white bird of stormlight in the rigging before the mainmast fell. The account raised questions. But the Ashwall repairs changed minds. Burn marks ran along contact points, not down from a single strike. Iron fittings had magnetized hard enough to pull nails. Some pilots now watch for bright, lateral stormlight. They treat it as a possible arclight phoenix (arclight-phoenix) sign, not ordinary weather.

### Fauna

Giant bats roost in the vent caves. The heat keeps them warm year-round. The colonies are large. A crew working below a roost at dusk learns to stay away. The same cracks hide giant scorpions in the rock above. The warmth keeps them in dark crevices, even when those crevices look like handholds. Repair crews now send two hands: one watches, one works.

Giant vultures work the clifftops. Their numbers spike after a wreck. Pilots count them. More vultures mean something came through the storm in pieces.

Crews who shelter in the Ashwall channels tell harpy stories: voices in the wrong place, sounds distinct from the wind's direction.

## Hazards

Storm-edge approaches, hard landings, giant bats in warm vent caves, giant scorpions in handhold fissures, giant vultures after wrecks, harpy stories in storm gaps, and arclight phoenix (arclight-phoenix) hatch events that signal [[galewall|Galewall]] activity.

The Ashwalls are intentionally under-detailed as a western orientation point and late-campaign expansion space, not a near-term destination. Pilot lore says the [[galewall|Galewall]] runs on arclight phoenix (arclight-phoenix) activity, and in-setting, this is true. Development levers if the crew pushes west include a hostile-weather expedition zone, Selune (selune) navigation-cult stronghold, and a staging point for [[galewall|Galewall]] crossing arcs.
