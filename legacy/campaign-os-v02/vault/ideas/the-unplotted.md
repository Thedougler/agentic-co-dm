# The Unplotted

[[the-unplotted|The Unplotted]] is a mimic. The whole island is one animal, old past reckoning, grown to island scale, wearing a small tropical paradise the way the chair in Delmar's break room wears leather.

The page already says the thing. A cartographer wrote it as a curiosity: *"The Unplotted never appears the same on two different charts. Yet ships find it right where they need it."* Not a word of that has to change. It stops being charming. Ships find it where they need it because it goes where ships are going to need it. Unclaimed, because claiming it has never once gone well. Charts disagree because it moves. Unlike [[corallith|Corallith]], which drifts once a generation and doesn't care who's aboard, this one is following traffic.

---

Its doctrine is [[elder-mimic|the Elder Mimic]]'s, scaled up and centuries older: *"stop being a chest."* Don't be the thing they come to take, because the thing they come to take gets searched and gets a sword swung at it. Be the thing they come to rest in. Treasure gets opened. Shelter gets *chosen*.

---

Green, fruited, fresh water off a warm stream, and a deep bay that lies flat as glass in any weather. Nobody has claimed the best-looking landfall in the region, and no two charts agree on its location. A becalmed crew will happily hold both facts without ever putting them together.

It sits past the [[doldrums|Doldrums]], in the Southern islands. That's the windless band. Ships come off it slow and short of water. The island is downwind of desperation.

---

The wrecks standing in the bay are the honest part. Everything above the waterline is the lie.

Bow-in, every one. None holed. Masts still stand stepped on two, and a ship that struck a reef does not keep its masts. No bones on them, on the beach, anywhere.

---

The tells, in roughly the order a careful crew earns them. The anchorage is dead flat while there's chop outside the heads. The fruit hangs untouched, ripe past when birds should have stripped it. The wrecks lie bare of crabs. The air stays empty of flies. The stream runs warm. The sand gives a fraction more than sand gives.

---

It's a larder built to wait. Nothing about rushing feeds it better.

It wants people, and it wants them **inland**. Everything the beach offers is bait for a walk. The casks fill faster at the stream's source than its mouth. The fruit is better up the slope. High ground at dusk is where you'd go to see whether anything's out there. The sand is the lip. Nothing happens at the waterline, and it will give them a whole day and a night on the beach and take nothing at all.

The bay being calm enough to careen in is the trap's finest hour. It's the crew's own competence talking them into unrigging their ship inside a mouth.

---

It reacts when they're in the middle of it. Not a clock, a position.

The ground goes first. At island scale the Adhesive is *terrain*: the interior takes hold of everyone standing on it at once, and what looked like loam is a surface that will not let go. The small version is already written on the [[elder-mimic|Elder Mimic]]'s page (*"the moment anyone touches it in object form… the toucher is [[grappled|Grappled]]"*), and this is a hillside of it. The bay closing that comes after is the door, shutting so nobody rows out while it eats.

The usual advantage for the crew of the *Uncertainty* inverts. The people ashore can't move; the people aboard can, and are the only ones who can reach them.

---

Level 5-10, so the danger is never the crew. It's the ship. By level seven somebody can fly, dive, or step out of a closing bay. Every escape route their spells open is one that saves them and abandons the [[uncertainty|Uncertainty]], who can't fly and is the reason they'd stay.

They can hurt it, and should be able to. Burn a grove. Break a headland. Drop something into the stream and watch a hillside flinch. Fire works: the folk warning about mimics is the one true thing sailors know. But fire into ground holding your quartermaster fast is dangerous in its own right. Nothing they have kills an island outright. It can lose a limb and remain an island. A thing patient for two centuries stops being patient once it starts bleeding.

---

A Tier 1 cutter is exactly the ship that goes in. Shallow draught, built for inspection and pursuit. She can nose into an anchorage a frigate's captain would refuse, and her crew know it and are a little proud of it.

Her hull is the one patched with whip-shark scales and [[mending|Mending]] since Session 03. Worth remembering when something starts pressing on it.

---

The chair is a deserter.

It didn't come from a shipwreck and it isn't the island's child. It *left*: it learned the doctrine there and ran, and the reason it has been so patient and so good for so long, unclaimed in taverns for decades, is that it's still running. The island has no idea it exists and would not care if it did. It hunts people.

By the time the *Uncertainty* raises the island, the chair is still just a pest nobody has managed to catch. It's a running joke with a bite count, still hiding as a different object every week and half forgotten by anyone still looking. Nobody knows its location when they cross the heads. They find out because it breaks cover in daylight, in front of witnesses. It goes over the side and swims.

That should have been the warning. They'll only understand it as one afterwards.

---

The heads don't seal clean, and the reason has been sitting in the bay in plain sight since they anchored. One of the bow-in wrecks isn't a meal. It's *stuck*. Some captain drove her into the narrows years ago and left her wedged there, so the island has to bait at all now instead of simply closing. The gap she holds is narrow and foul, and it's the only door.

No clean answer exists, only two clocks running at once. Ground that won't release until it's hurt keeps the people ashore in place. A gap a cutter can just about take, badly, with somebody conning her through a dead ship's ribs, pens the ship in. And the island's attention is finite. It's *concentrating*, inland. Everything they do to free their people slackens the throat. Everything they do to the throat brings it back to the beach.

The *Uncertainty* comes out scraped down one side and short of crew, or she doesn't come out.
