# Ash Caldera Dragon

A dead volcanic island in the [[midchain-east|Eastern Midchain]], replacing Iron Choir's stub outright — new name, new everything, the old "cold forges ring themselves out" flavor discarded entirely rather than preserved. Upper slopes are burned-out jungle gone to black glass and ash-drift; the crater glows faintly at night, visible to a ship passing close enough.

---

Hook: a treasure rumor circulating among [[midchain|Midchain]] pilots and salvagers. Something glints in the crater floor on a clear night. A few salvage crews have gone in over the years. None have come back to confirm what they saw.

---

Optional side quest, no plot dependency — a party can ignore it entirely or walk into something that can kill them. Built for the level 5-10 band: meant to be scouted and walked away from early, then returned to once the party's stronger, or attempted early only by a party playing very carefully (retreat, hit-and-run, grab-and-go).

---

Two-session structure, front-loaded danger:

Session 1 — approach and escalation. The ash-slope itself is an obstacle: poor visibility, unstable footing, unmapped heat vents. The party finds physical evidence of prior treasure-hunters (gear, remains, maybe a live survivor as an intel source) that telegraphs this is not a safe pull. Environmental tells build through the session — heat shimmer, a distant roar, ash falling upward once — so the dragon is felt before it's fought.

Session 2 — the caldera. Lair and hoard sit at the crater's core. The fight uses the terrain as a second threat: unstable rock, vents that can be knocked into, ash clouds that can blind either side.

---

Young red dragon — territorial, aggressive, not interested in talking. This is the "dangerous encounter" ask: built to be winnable but genuinely costly, not a foregone conquest. Hoard is fused/molten-slag treasure — gold and gems half-melted into rock — with room for one standout magic item as a hook into later content.

Balance note: CR 10 is a genuinely lethal solo fight for a level-5 party — that's the point. Session 1 should telegraph the danger hard enough that a level-5 party backing off reads as good play, not a railroad.
