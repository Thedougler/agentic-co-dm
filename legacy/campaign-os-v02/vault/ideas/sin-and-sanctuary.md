# Sin and Sanctuary

Sin has mass and gravity. The scale and density of wrongdoing in a place is what draws fiends there and determines how boldly they can act — a place steeped in real cruelty lets them walk in daylight; a place mostly clean keeps them skulking at the margins. And it's gravity, not just weight: the deeper a soul sinks into it, the harder it gets to climb back out.

---

Hallowed ground is a live, revocable claim, not a fixed property of a place. Any patron — a god, a demigod, an archfey — can bless a site, and for as long as that patron actively holds the claim, a fiend setting foot on it invites immediate, violent reprisal: the patron's power lands at once, destroying the fiend's form on this plane outright and casting its soul back to its home plane (the Abyss for demons, the Nine Hells for devils — the standard split holds, no shared afterlife collapse). This is why fiends never test hallowed ground carelessly. A pack pushes its weakest member forward first and watches what happens to it before anyone stronger commits — a tell the party can learn to read, without ever being handed an explicit mechanic for it.

---

Patrons judge by their own dogma, not a universal scale of sin. Tyr's justice tolerates, even blesses, brutal violence against the guilty — the fight is righteous if it's aimed at "bad people." Another patron would recoil from the exact same act. There is no setting-wide checklist of what counts as sin; there's only what each patron actually believes.

---

A patron warns a straying follower before withdrawing protection. The warnings stop only once the follower can no longer hear them — which can look identical to abandonment from the outside, but isn't the god going silent first. [[corvin-ashe|Corvin Ashe]] wasn't cut off out of nowhere; he stopped being reachable, too deep in his own lie to hear Tyr's guidance by the time it mattered.

---

All outsiders — fiend, celestial, or god — read a mortal soul's true state innately: one glance, no study required, no chance of missing it. There is no lying to any of them. Only mortals get to fool themselves and each other.

---

The demon's/devil's kiss is a sanctioned public mark, not a demon's private cruelty — it's reserved for a soul every side already agrees is gone. An ordinary sinner, even a bad one, stays inside the range their patron would still contest: a bandit can turn his life around, a robber can return what he stole, even a murderer can repent, pay for it, and eventually be forgiven. The kiss is only for the soul whose quality makes all of that impossible — and the line for that is deliberately nebulous, sensed rather than measured. If the soul were still reachable, its patron would intervene to stop the kiss; the kiss succeeding, unopposed, is itself the god's confirmation that it's deserved.

The kiss isn't punishment from the demon's point of view — it's recognition. The mortal was never going to make it to their god's side of the ledger; the human shape was the only thing that hadn't caught up to that fact yet. "We love you" because they already belong to the fiends, and letting them keep pretending otherwise would be the crueler thing. It transforms the victim into a physical, undeniable caricature of what they always already were, marking the skin where their crimes were committed — and its sanctioned purpose is public: a mortal fully sided with evil loses the ability to hide among the innocent, on the god's authority, using the demon as the instrument.
