# The Isle of Cursed Speech

A normal [[midchain|Midchain]] fishing island, dressed up over the last three weeks into a nine-year-old's idea of a kingdom. Underneath the pageantry it's still an ordinary small-island community — nets, boats, a dock, families — none of that has gone anywhere, it's just been overwritten with a child's absolute, gleefully-believed monarchy. Two children on this one small island have independently developed "cursed-tongue": words that don't just describe reality, they *become* it, no spell slots, no training, no limit but what the child has words for and how hard they feel it.

---

**The cause, grounded in real [[faerun|Forgotten Realms]] lore, not invented whole-cloth.** The Pearl of Souls' unresolved signal at the [[the-drowned-maw|Drowned Maw]] has been straining the boundary between the Material Plane and the [[elemental-plane-of-water|Elemental Plane of Water]] all campaign (canon: "will draw something larger to the next fissure" already implies more than one breach can open from this). Per real Realms lore, planar collision damages the Weave and produces wild-magic zones where "anything could occur" (sourced: [Weave](https://forgottenrealms.fandom.com/wiki/Weave)). As the pressure at the Maw has grown — impersonally, just things on the far side pushing harder over time, nothing to do with [[umberlee|Umberlee]]'s moods — its reach has started thinning and in places *aligning* other, distant planar boundaries across the [[shattered-sea|Shattered Sea]]. On this island, about three weeks ago, that alignment landed on the boundary between ordinary Common and Truespeak / the Words of Creation — the primordial language the first gods used to speak the multiverse into being (sourced: [Words of Creation](https://forgottenrealms.fandom.com/wiki/Words_of_Creation)). Real truenamers spend years of discipline to wield fragments of that language, and even then it resists them (the Law of Resistance, [Truenamer](https://forgottenrealms.fandom.com/wiki/Truenamer)). What's happening here has none of that safeguard: the Words are leaking straight through two untrained children with no resistance and no limiter at all, which is exactly why it reads as a curse and not a gift.

Because this is one alignment among what could be several, the island doesn't need to be uniquely special on its own terms — just where it happened to land first. Room to seed the same phenomenon elsewhere later without it being an exception to explain away.

---

[[toby|Toby]] declared himself emperor of the island. Nobody voted, nobody resisted — his word simply became the village's real, remembered history: they believe they have *always* had a custom of a child god-emperor, because his cursed speech doesn't just command bodies, it overwrites belief and memory wholesale. Nobody here is play-acting or scared into compliance. Every villager, every "minister," every subject genuinely believes this is how things have always been and takes it completely, cheerfully seriously.

---

Overt fairy-tale monkey's-paw absurdity, not creeping dread — the horror is that it's all delivered with total sincerity and joy. A Snack Minister tastes the emperor's food first, a real office with real succession politics when the last one aged out of favor. A dog, formally knighted, serves as Captain of the [[guard|Guard]], and the armed adults defer to it without a flicker of irony. Dessert is eaten first by royal decree, always, and nobody remembers or can conceive of a version of dinner where it wasn't. Bedtime is 7pm sharp for the entire village, adults included — enforced not by fear but by genuine, unshaken belief that of course everyone goes to bed at 7, they always have. Punishment for a real offense is being sent to stand in the corner — for exactly as long as a nine-year-old decided, which given a child's fuzzy sense of time could mean an hour or could, technically, mean forever; nobody has ever experienced a sentence here as unjust. Court pageantry is obviously a child's idea of royal ceremony — a driftwood throne, a fishing boat hauled onto the square, whatever game he's currently interested in turned into sacred ritual, subjects playing along with made-up rules with total gravity.

---

His edicts are absolute and over-broad the way a child's wants always are, and reality complies literally rather than reasonably. "No more monsters" scoured the island of every dangerous animal *and* every biting insect *and* every thorn bush, leaving something faintly, wrongly sterile under the postcard prettiness. He stopped pirates and raiders for years by telling them to "burn" or "stop breathing" — not a spell, just a true statement the instant he said it. An adult launched twenty feet into the air and 100 feet back on "go away," severely wounded — not obedience, physics simply being what he said it was.

---

**The nature of the power, once it's understood properly: not compulsion, authorship.** He doesn't control minds and get obeyed — he asserts a new true fact about reality and everything rearranges to make it hold, including a person's own will, their body, physics, and logical consistency itself. Villagers "surviving" a fatal wound isn't a heal effect; there was never a wound that mattered once he said so. Two mutually exclusive commands don't crash anything — reality just makes both true simultaneously, however absurd the contortion required, because "impossible" isn't a category that constrains an assertion made at the level of the Words of Creation. No genie's malicious-compliance loophole to exploit — no training to route the phrasing into something safe, just a child's flat, absolute, contradiction-blind certainty.

---

Range matters only at the moment of speaking — a target has to be within earshot (or reached via [[sending|Sending]]/telepathy, since the effect rides the utterance itself, not sound waves) for a new command to land on them. Once it lands, though, it's not a live field that needs the child nearby to sustain — it's a fixed, permanent fact of reality until something (nobody currently knows what) undoes it. Leaving the island's range stops *new* commands from landing on you; it does nothing to reverse whatever already landed. A crew member caught in range during a tantrum could sail away carrying a permanent, real fact about themselves that nobody in the world currently knows how to undo.

---

The deaf are the one clean exception, mechanically real, not hand-waved: the compulsion rides on directly perceiving the utterance itself, not on understanding what was said. A deaf villager told secondhand, in writing or in sign, what either emperor declared isn't compelled by the retelling — it's just information, not the utterance landing. Every deaf person on the island is walking around with their original, un-overwritten memory and beliefs fully intact, aware the rest of their community is living inside something that isn't real, with no safe way to say so where anyone could repeat it aloud. The party's one safe channel into this island without risking their own will and memory: written notes, sign language, anything that isn't the spoken utterance reaching their own ears.

---

**[[frankie|Frankie]]** is the rival — an ordinary playground grudge (took her Royal Best Friend spot, or some equally petty and real kid slight) escalated to cosmic stakes, which is the point: the cause is disproportionately small next to what it's now doing to the island. Neither Toby nor Frankie has any training. Their "duels" aren't clean spellcasting, they're two nine-year-olds arguing and one-upping each other in real time, and reality has no choice but to keep up: "you can't hurt me" / "well NOW nobody can ever hurt me either, forever, no matter what" / "okay but I get to win anyway because I said so FIRST" — increasingly verbose, increasingly absolute, increasingly incoherent, stacking and overlapping instead of cleanly canceling, with whichever claim carries more real fury behind it landing harder. The island absorbs the blast radius of a fight that is, underneath the reality-warping, just a fight over a friendship. Effect intensity concentrates around wherever the two of them are actually standing when they collide, and every place that's happened is left permanently scarred — a pocket where the tideline hangs a foot too high and never recedes, or the grass grows in a spiral nobody planted, not necessarily still active, just permanently changed now.

---

**[[billy|Billy]]** is the third child, the one who understands what the other two can't yet: this is unsustainable and it will consume everything if it isn't stopped. Watching Toby and Frankie's escalating collision, Billy used his own newly-surfaced cursed speech to compel a handful of villagers with one flat, absolute, child-simple instruction: *"Find heroes to save us from ourselves."* Because the compulsion persists once landed, those villagers don't relay the message in their own words — they repeat it, identically, no matter who asks or how, wherever they're eventually found (a port, or intercepted at sea, possibly the party's own ship). The line that reaches the party, verbatim and uncanny every time it's spoken: *"We will destroy everything if this is not stopped and it leaves our island."* Not gossip, not rumor-mill hearsay — a direct, unstoppable, word-for-word summons, and the first real evidence the party gets that something on this island has already gone further than anyone there can fix themselves.

Two separate island horrors, same root cause, if the party ever pieces it together.
