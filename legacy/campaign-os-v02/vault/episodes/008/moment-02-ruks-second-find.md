The hatch stands hauled open and leaning against the kerb, two streets back from [[nona-black-jaw|Nona]]'s cordon, where her runner marked it for the raid, and the noise of the market fails to carry this far.
>
> Ruk comes up out of the shaft in front of you with slack shoulders and empty hands, a fight already over before he got to it. Fifteen feet of iron rungs drop away behind him into lamplit dark. The smell that comes up with him has gone past rot into something flat and cold and hours old. He steps aside on his own and leaves the ladder clear.

## What's True

Ruk found the last two unswept rooms already still: fourteen [[grung|Grung]] dead, killed hours earlier by their own side for running behind schedule. First room, thirty feet of low service magazine, nine Grung in the shallows, six tar-sealed barrels untouched on a dry ledge. Second room, twenty feet on, five more Grung in a line, the barrel lashings cut and the powder tools gone.

> [!mechanic]
> **Contact poison, still live down here.** Grung-touched surfaces (rungs, lashings, anything the crew handled) force a **DC 12 [[constitution|Constitution]]** save on bare-skin contact, or **poisoned for 10 minutes**. Gloves or wrapped cloth negates it. Full table: [[calveno-sewers-grung-magazines|Calveno Sewer Magazines]] § Poison mechanics.

> [!check] Investigation or Insight — Reading the Room
> DC 14, for anyone who goes down. Insight at the same DC for anyone who stays up top and reads Ruk's telling of it.
> **Success:** close, precise wounds, one cut each, delivered to bodies caught mid-motion, every one facing away from the blade. Killed fast by somebody they trusted enough to turn their backs on. The same hand did both rooms.
> **Failure:** the room reads as chaos, the same as everywhere else the raid touched.

> [!quote] Ruk, flat, over the bodies
> "Dead already, killed by their own. I have seen this before, on the mainland. Never twice in one morning."

> [!dm]
> Whoever runs this operation trims its own losses instead of risking a straggler. [[jean-claude-tabarnack|Jean-Claude]]'s player already knows privately that the hand belongs to [[simone-tabarnack|Simone Tabarnack]], his sister. Don't announce it; only his own recognition, voiced out loud, confirms it for anybody else.
