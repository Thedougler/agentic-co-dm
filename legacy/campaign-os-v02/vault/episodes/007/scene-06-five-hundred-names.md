## Opening

**Dramatic Question:** what did the raid actually cost, and where does your crew stand when Nona vows to collect?

> [!read-aloud] Transition
> Morning comes late and gray for everyone who fought yesterday. By the time you're moving again, [[ruk|Ruk]] is already gone, back down into the sewers before anyone else was awake.

> [!read-aloud]
> The Mercatura looks almost ordinary in the early light: stalls opening, boats threading the canals, yesterday's crater cordoned off at one edge like a wound someone's decided not to look at directly.
>
> Wet stone and cold ash still hang in the air from the fires two streets over.
>
> [[nona-black-jaw|Nona]] already has a borrowed table up near the cordon, a stack of old receipts squared in front of her, blank side up, and a jar of ink she hasn't capped since sunrise.
>
> The city survived the night. Now it starts counting what it lost. You're close enough to the cordon to watch a woman in a fishmonger's apron reach the table first and give a name, and by the time Nona writes it down without looking up, the next blank receipt is already squared in front of her, waiting on whoever's next in line.
>
> She glances up once, just long enough to find your faces in the crowd, then looks back down and reaches for the next name.

## Running It

**The setup.** Today belongs to your crew as much as anyone in Calveno: what they find in the swept rooms, what they say to the people asking questions, and who they choose to become while the count climbs. **The scene's toy is the count itself.** [[nona-black-jaw|Nona]]'s growing tally of names sits physical and ordinary: a stack of receipts anyone can walk up to, touch, or question. ### Beat 1: Ruk's Second Find

*Ruk **comes up empty-handed** from rooms already still, and anyone who climbs down can **read** what made them that way.*

> [!read-aloud] Transition
> You leave Nona's table to the crowd still gathering around it and cross to the sewer grate two streets back, where the morning's other business is waiting: the last two rooms nobody's checked since the raid.

> [!read-aloud]
> The grate waits below. The one [[nona-black-jaw|Nona]]'s runner marked for the raid, hauled back and leaning against the kerb now, sits where the cordon's noise fails to carry.
>
> [[ruk|Ruk]] comes up out of the shaft right in front of you, slack shoulders and empty hands, the fight he expected already over before he arrived.
>
> Fifteen feet of iron rungs drop away behind him into lamp-lit dark, down toward the two rooms he has just walked. The smell that rises with him has gone past rot into something flat and cold, hours old and entirely still.
>
> He steps to the side and leaves the ladder clear for whichever of you wants to see it yourselves.

He found both remaining rooms already still: the [[grung|Grung]] inside dead, killed hours earlier by their own side for running behind schedule. Any PC who climbs down can read the room before Ruk's account settles it.

The first room holds 30 ft of low service magazine, 5-ft ceiling, ankle-deep flat water. A dry ledge runs along the right-hand wall. Six tar-sealed barrels sit lashed on the ledge beside a burning work lamp. Nine Grung corpses lie in the shallows, spears still slung, powder scoops and a split crate spilled around them.

The second room sits deeper in the network, reached by a 20-ft culvert crawl. Five more dead line the far wall. Someone sliced and opened the [[barrel|barrel]] lashings where they checked the charge. The powder tools sit missing from the ledge. The tunnel beyond continues into the unswept lower stretch.

> [!mechanic]
> **Contact poison (environmental), still live down here.** Grung-touched surfaces (rungs, barrel lashings, anything the crew handled) force a **DC 12 [[constitution|Constitution]]** save on bare-skin contact, or **poisoned for 10 minutes**. Gloves or cloth wrapping negates it. Full table: [[calveno-sewers-grung-magazines|Calveno Sewer Magazines]] § Poison mechanics.

> [!read-aloud] Down the Ladder
> Fifteen feet of cold iron rungs put you into ankle-deep water, and the water is dead flat. Not a ripple in it anywhere.
>
> The ceiling sits five feet off the surface. Standing straight is not an option down here.
>
> Brine and wet stone first, and under that the sweet acrid smell the raid taught you, gone stale and gone cold.
>
> Thirty feet of limestone and old brick, with a dry ledge climbing up out of the tide along the right-hand wall, and nine Grung lying in the shallows between you and it, powder scoops and a split crate spilled through the water around them where they went down.
>
> Water echo carries a long way in these tunnels. This morning it has nothing to carry.
>
> On the ledge above the bodies, six tar-sealed barrels sit lashed and untouched, and the work lamp beside the stack is still burning with oil left in it.

> [!read-aloud] The Second Room
> The way through is a culvert, twenty feet of it, hands and knees the whole length. It gives back every scrape of your own boots and nothing else.
>
> The water on the far side comes up over the ankle, deeper than the first room, with a slick across it that catches your lamp. Five more of them along the far wall, dropped close enough together that the last one's hand still rests on the shoulder of the one ahead of it.
>
> [[the-passage|The Passage]] stretches farther into the dark.
>
> Someone sliced the lashings on these barrels and folded them back. Nothing sits on the ledge where the powder tools should be.

> [!check] Investigation — Reading the Room
> DC 14, for anyone who goes down into either swept room with Ruk; Insight instead, same DC, for anyone who stays up top and reads Ruk's telling of it.
> **Success:** the wounds are close and precise, one cut each. Each body caught mid-motion, facing away from the blade. Killed fast by someone they trusted. The same efficient hand both times.
> **Failure:** the room reads as chaos, same as everywhere else the raid touched.

> [!quote] Ruk — flat, over the bodies
> "Dead already, killed by their own. I have seen this before, on the mainland. Never twice in one morning."

> [!dm]
> Whoever runs this operation eliminates its own losses to prevent stragglers from talking. [[jean-claude-tabarnack|Jean-Claude]]'s player already knows privately it's [[simone-tabarnack|Simone Tabarnack]], his sister. Don't announce it. Only his own recognition, if he voices it, confirms it for anyone else.

### Beat 2: The Tally

*The count **climbs** all day. Your crew can **speed it** or **stay back**, and they can **verify the numbers** as they go.*

> [!read-aloud] Transition
> Morning bleeds into midday while the tally grows. By the time anyone thinks to check, receipts overflow Nona's borrowed table.

> [!read-aloud]
> Near the crater's cordon, the pile rises as you watch. Nona leans over it, writing names on the backs of old receipts because she ran out of clean paper hours ago. The scratch of her pen fills the quiet.
>
> People keep arriving in ones and twos, voices dropping low as they reach the table to give her a name. Some have been standing at the edge of the cordon the better part of an hour, working up to it.
>
> Whole streets out in the districts haven't sent anyone at all yet. Every person at this table knows it.
>
> Nona hasn't asked any of you for help. She hasn't had a free hand to ask anyone for anything since sunrise. The smell of ink and old paper mingles with ash still rising from three streets over.

> [!quote] Nona Black-Jaw — reading the pile
> "Two hundred."

> [!quote] Nona Black-Jaw — an hour later, without looking up
> "Closer to four."

Someone can spend the afternoon at her table helping canvas the districts and check names against real households, or question whether the count itself holds accuracy.

> [!check] Investigation or Persuasion — Canvassing the Count
> DC 13. Investigation to verify names door to door. Persuasion to get reluctant households to admit someone's missing instead of hoping they turn up embarrassed.
> **Success:** three more households come forward who'd been sitting on it, ashamed or afraid to be first. The number moves faster and cleaner for the help.
> **Failure:** the count still moves, just slower, and one name gets written twice before anyone notices. Nona catches her own error before it matters.

> [!check] Insight — Reading Nona
> DC 12 for anyone watching her work instead of helping directly.
> **Success:** she isn't performing grief for the crowd. Every name gets the same flat attention, whether she recognizes the family or not. This is bookkeeping, the same discipline she's run for forty years. That discipline is exactly why the number holds.
> **Failure:** nothing beyond what's already obvious. She's tired, and the pile keeps growing.

By evening, a full day after the crater fell silent, the number stops at **five hundred**. Every household that was going to report someone missing has done so.

> [!dm]
> Nona's chase-crew vow (Beat 3) and her private [[warren-aruhe-survey|Aruhe survey]] commission to Perrin now share the same waters. The [[vethka|Vethka]] pursuit and [[the-tangle|The Tangle]]'s candidate anchor site both sit past the mole. Whether Perrin tells her about [[aruhe|Aruhe]] now is his player's call. Don't force it.

### Beat 3: The Vow

*Nona **stops counting** and **speaks for The Passage**. Your crew **answers her** or doesn't, on camera.*

> [!read-aloud] Transition
> Evening comes, and the pile stops growing. Nona sets the pen down and doesn't pick it back up.

> [!read-aloud]
> She crosses to the crater's edge with [[enzo|Enzo]] and [[ruk|Ruk]] flanking her, passing bread and dried fish hand to hand to whoever's still standing near the rubble before she says a word. The smell of salt and grain lingers.
>
> When the basket is empty she stops where the whole rim can see her and waits for the talking to die out. The crowd's murmurs thin to silence. She is looking at the five of you when it does.

Nona speaks for [[the-passage|The Passage]] in front of anyone left to hear it.

> [!quote] Nona Black-Jaw — after the count stops
> "I know what this is: to look for someone who was here a minute ago and find nothing, the way my people have for decades. Today it happened to five hundred of yours. The Passage stands with the citizens of Calveno, and I will assemble a crew and make chase."

Three unmarked, dark-hulled [[vethka|Vethka]] carried the five hundred out past the mole on someone else's schedule, a full day before anyone thought to look for the boats that carried them.

**The ending holds.** Nona commits to the chase. Five hundred people vanish. How your crew answers her remains open.

- **Answering the vow.** Someone can commit to her crew right now or ask the hard logistics questions (how many hulls exist, how much lead time has passed, what happens if the Vethka scatter before the *[[uncertainty|Uncertainty]]* clears the mole).
  - **Her answers, if asked.** **Three hulls** (the same three that cleared the mole) and **a full day of lead** already gone. On scattering: it doesn't matter. Three proas that size cannot carry five hundred people anywhere. They ferried them onto something bigger, a hull with room for five hundred, and that vessel is her target. Chasing proas through reef water burns effort to no gain. The big hull requires water, food, rest for five hundred lives. Every port where she can resupply has Passage people in it.
- **Pushing back.** If a PC tries to stop her, let it happen. She hears every argument out. No roll shifts the vow. Someone offers something better than urgency (a heading, a name, a faster hull), and this shapes what chase she assembles.
- **Her one question.** She doesn't argue back. She asks one question, "And who goes instead?", and lets the silence answer it.

Whichever way it lands carries straight into Session 08 (nothing here needs resolving before the table breaks).
