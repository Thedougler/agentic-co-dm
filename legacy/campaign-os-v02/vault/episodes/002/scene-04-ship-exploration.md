# Session 02: The Ship

- [x] Ran

## Opening

DM beat-script for Session 02's HCS Surety tour, guiding the crew through ship roles, room-by-room loot, and crew retention conversations, ending in the brig with Ket, with room contents, stats, and current inventory already landed in full on [[uncertainty|HCS Surety]], [[uncertainty-layout|HCS Surety Deck Layouts]], and [[uncertainty-manifest|HCS Surety Manifest]]. This page preserves the staged dialogue, crew retention hooks, and the Ket brig encounter mechanics that those reference pages don't carry. The beat-script is DM prep, not the play record.

*Fires after [[scene-03-grigori|Scene 03: Grigori]] settles. The Surety now has a Crown patrol cutter, a small crew, and no captain.*

[[geoffrey-draves|Geoffrey Draves]] is the right guide for this scene, which covers what's worth slowing down for. He was the ship's carpenter. He knows every creaking plank, where the copper is lifting, which strakes need re-caulking. He also has opinions, delivered quietly, about what he finds. He's committed to the crew but he worked on this ship and some of that is still in him.

---

## Running It

The tour runs deck by deck. Ship Roles come first, then the Weather Deck, Gun Deck, and Hold. It closes with crew conversations and reflections on the ship as a whole.

## Ship Roles

Geoffrey does not launch straight into a tour. He stops them at the wheel first, with the manner of someone who has been thinking about this since the fight ended.

"Before I show you around. You should know what this ship needs to run."

He ticks it off without lecture. His manner stays practical.

**The Surety runs on ten. Four suffice.** Those four cover:

- Someone at the wheel who makes decisions when things go wrong. **Captain**
- Someone who can read a chart and plot a course without putting them on a reef. **Navigator**
- Someone managing the rigging and the deck crew. **Bosun**
- Someone who knows which planks need caulking before they become a flooding problem. **Carpenter** (he does not gesture at himself, but the pause is its own gesture)

Beyond the basics: a **Gunner** to maintain the 6-pounders and not misfire them into the hull; a **Cook** for any voyage over three days (*"I understand our guest below is useful in a galley,"* he says, without audible irony); a **Surgeon** (we have supplies, but Alys has handled surgery alone); and **Marines** for boarding actions, armed watch, anything requiring a blade and a reason to hold ground.

Then ordinary sailors. Enough bodies to work the lines and keep the watches. *"You need eight to ten to run her properly. You have five of the old crew and me. If everyone stays."*

He says *if everyone stays* without emphasis. That silence is still emphasis.

> [!dm]
> [[thunk|Thunk]], the ship's gunner, is present during this conversation. Geoffrey knows he exists but did not think to mention it. If a PC looks around and asks who handles the guns, Geoffrey says "Thunk does" as if obvious. Thunk waves, standing six foot five, looking pleased to hear his name.

The Captain slot is the scene's quiet center. None of the crew has taken the captain's seat. [[beaumont-sel|Beaumont Sel]] is the obvious candidate by competence (a Tier 2 former privateer captain). But he has his own ship ([[saltwright|Saltwright]]).

> [!dm]
> Beaumont answers questions freely and gives honest assessments of the surviving crew. When the crew sets course, he crosses back to the Saltwright and continues his own route. The crew must put someone at this wheel themselves. If nobody steps forward, Geoffrey eventually says flat: "Someone has to steer," looking at each PC.

## Weather Deck

The crew has already spent a session fighting here. Note the lanterns and the guns.

**The lanterns.** Rook kicked them on in Round 1 to collapse Jean-Claude's Umbral Sight, and they still burn. Someone needs to extinguish them before they burn through the night.

**The guns.** Two 6-pounders mounted bow and stern swivels, all operational. Geoffrey, if asked: *"Crown-issue, both functional, powder's below. I'd recommend not firing them without the charge tables, or the first round becomes educational."* He speaks like a man who's watched someone skip the tables and regretted it.

**The net trap.** If nobody triggered it, it still hangs rigged to the port rail. Geoffrey points it out unprompted: *"He rigged it for boarding defense, the day before we arrived."* The identity of the target needs no elaboration.

## Gun Deck

The ship's interior. Where the crew lived.

**Boarding locker** (8 cutlasses, 4 boarding axes, 6 climbing hooks, 4 loaded flintlocks in a case, rope, 3 smoke signals). Geoffrey: *"Don't ask me why there are more weapons than crew."* Everyone can arm themselves fully here.

> [!dm]
> **The Bastion.** The Surety is already a functional Tier 1 bastion. The Boarding Locker is the crew's **Weapons Locker**. This facility slot is already established.

Geoffrey doesn't use that terminology, but he knows where every weapon sits. The Chart Table is the **Chart Archive** by the same principle. The Chart Archive and Weapons Locker are already established. Crew Quarters (crew berths) and Galley (provisions store) open on demand.

> [!dm]
> The medic's cabinet becomes a **Surgeon's Berth** once someone fills that role. Let this discovery emerge through the tour, not as announcement. Full details: [[uncertainty-layout|Deck Layouts]] § Facility Berths and [[ship-operations|Ship Operations]] § Weekly Management.

**Chart table.** [[crown-islands|Crown Islands]] survey charts cover the eastern waypoints and [[central-strait|Central Strait]] approaches. A compass, dividers, and a 3-month captain's log sit bolted to the surface. The log reads methodical, professional, and increasingly terse. [[beaumont-sel|Beaumont Sel]] appears in three entries. The first runs two paragraphs, the second one, the third four words: *"Sel. Again. Noted."*

> [!dm]
> The log also has the inspection manifests from the last three months. Anyone reading carefully will notice a pattern: ships with [[fisks-fleet|Fisk's Fleet]] registry get flagged more often than average. It's not written down as policy. It's just the numbers.

**Medic's cabinet.** 2 healer's kits, 1 potion of healing, 1 potion of greater healing (2d4+2), surgical instruments in a sealed roll. The surgeon's berth sits pre-built but empty. Alys Kuiper has managed surgery alone. She'll continue managing it, with waning enthusiasm, unless someone hires a surgeon.

**Officer's berth** (two built-in bunks, a shared desk, two locked footlockers).

> [!check] Thieves' Tools — Officer's Footlockers
> DC 12.
> **Success:** 15 gp total, one spare Crown uniform, and an unsigned letter that starts "I told you this run was wrong" and then stops.
> **Failure:** the locks hold.

**Captain's cabin.** Rook watched his crew die from the stern windows in here. The room still carries someone's serious regard for the work.

- *Weapons rack:* spare cutlass, one loaded blunderbuss (4d6, 15-ft cone, one shot). Geoffrey: *"I didn't know about the blunderbuss."* He sounds sincere.
- *Locked chest* (opens instantly with Rook's key, otherwise):

> [!check] Thieves' Tools — Captain's Locked Chest
> DC 15 (skip the roll entirely with Rook's key).
> **Success:** 45 gp, Crown commission papers, a sealed letter from the [[tidefall|Tidefall]] Harbormaster about [[beaumont-sel|Beaumont Sel]]'s manifest, 1 potion of greater healing (2d4+2), and 1 potion of water breathing.
> **Failure:** the chest stays shut. Nothing recovers from this attempt.

> [!dm]
> The letter bears Crown wax. Opening it reveals what people choose. The Harbormaster flagged Beaumont's last three manifests as irregularly registered and asked Rook to detain him pending review.

Rook chose inspection instead of detention, and everyone is here. Any reader will find the irony in that choice.

## Hold

Low overhead (5'8"), six inches of bilge water at the lowest point. Bring a light.

**Inspection storage** (six months of boarding manifests, a fee ledger, and three blank Crown writs in a sealed folder). The writs allow forging Crown authority. Ensign Wouters, if present, examines the blank writs and then looks deliberately away.

**Seized crates (×2, unlabeled)** (listed in the fee ledger as "confiscated goods, pending Crown review"). Nobody ever opened them for Crown review. Rook never meant to send them for Crown review.

> [!check] Thieves' Tools — Seized Crate Padlocks
> DC 10, or bypass entirely with a crowbar.
> **Success:** the padlock gives. See contents below.
> **Failure:** the padlock holds. Nothing recovers from this attempt.

*Crate 1:* silver candelabras (25 gp each), a locked jewelry box, a cask of fine brandy (12 gp, ostensibly seized for unpaid duties), a bolt of [[calveno|Calveno]] silk (20 gp). The Crown never receipted any of this. It sits here unofficially. **Subtotal: ~97 gp.**

> [!check] Thieves' Tools — Crate 1 Jewelry Box
> DC 12.
> **Success:** a small pendant worth 15 gp, a name engraved on the back: "For Mira, from the sea."
> **Failure:** the box stays locked. The crew cannot open it on this attempt.

*Crate 2:* flintlock pistols in good condition (loaded, 15 gp each), Mira's Blade (relink pending, a +1 shortsword with a distinctive notched pommel made for a specific owner), and a sealed package labeled in Rook's hand: *"Contraband (R.B.)"* Inside: twenty vials of [[grung-npc|Grung]] poison tincture (sealed in wax, worth ~5 gp each to the right buyer, 100 gp total as black-market contraband). Geoffrey stares at these and says nothing.

> [!dm]
> Anyone who cross-references the fee ledger against the crate contents will find the discrepancy immediately: the official manifest records a fraction of what's here. Someone lifted the rest. Rook used this to supplement his income.

Jean-Claude recognizes the tincture vials instantly. The preparation method matches what the Alchemist used on deck. They can kill a roomful of people or fund a major operation. The crew decides what to do with them.

> [!dm]
> The pendant in Crate 1 and Mira's Blade share a source. Someone took the blade from an inspection that also yielded the jewelry box. Deliberately unresolved: whether "Mira" names the blade's owner or recipient, or someone else entirely. Settle this when it matters, not before.

> [!check] Thieves' Tools — Locked Cargo Crate
> DC 14.
> **Success:** 150 gp in mixed coin and a folded map. The map shows an anchorage marked "[[galewall|Galewall]]" with a bearing and a rough depth sounding. It has no label and offers no context about its purpose. The sounding suggests shallow water over something large.
> **Failure:** the crate stays shut. Nothing recovers from this attempt.

Whether the crew found this crate in play remains unknown per the session-02 canonical summary. [[uncertainty-manifest|HCS Surety Manifest]] records an 80 gp cargo-crate take as party funds, at a different total than the 150 gp this beat-script staged. The landed manifest is the authoritative figure.

**Hidden stash.** A loose panel under the bunk in the captain's cabin, detectable by the lack of dust in the seam.

> [!check] Investigation — The Captain's Hidden Stash
> DC 13.
> **Success:** 110 gp in small-denomination coin packed in oilcloth, and two unset garnets (20 gp each): this is Rook's real personal savings, the money he didn't want even the purser's ledger to know about.
> **Failure:** the panel goes unnoticed. The stash stays hidden.

**Prisoner brig.** An iron cage bolted to the keel. And hanging from a bent ship nail in the keel timber, a brass birdcage roughly two feet across. The moment someone enters with a light, something inside it moves, fast, toward the bars, proboscis first.

[[ket|Ket]]. A [[moucheron|Moucheron]], taken from [[murrat|Murrat]] by Rook three weeks ago as a specimen. Unarmed, untrained, and actively hostile. Crew members take turns feeding him: a donor reaches an arm through the bars, then pulls back fast or he latches. Nobody has been down since the Saltwright engagement started.

*"You're bleeding,"* he says, if anyone in the crew took damage and hasn't been fully healed. He says it the way you'd note the weather.

> [!dm]
> Full NPC: [[ket|Ket]]. Currently **Difficult** on the hunger track, 36+ hours without a feed, and will attempt to feed the moment anything gets within proboscis range of the bars. He uses Common to invite people closer; he is not being diplomatic, he is being a predator.

> [!mechanic]
> **Cage latch.** DC 8 Sleight of Hand opens it from outside; Ket cannot open it from inside.

> [!dm]
> **If opened:** he feeds on whoever is closest and within reach. He exits for open air (out a gun port, through the hatch), not giving speeches. He wants blood and sky, in that order.
>
> Geoffrey stops at the brig doorway and does not come in. *"Rook brought that back from Murrat. The crew's been feeding it. I don't know why."* He sounds like a man who has been asking himself that question for three weeks.

> [!mechanic]
> **Proboscis reach.** Anyone within 5 feet of the cage: DC 12 [[dexterity|Dexterity]] save or the proboscis finds a gap in the bars. On a hit: 1d6 piercing damage and Ket becomes Satiated until his next scene, and he grins about this, the worst grin.

> [!dm]
> [[ket|Ket]]'s own page records how this actually resolved in play: freed via Ensign Wouters' body as a lure, fed, and released toward Murrat (see [[ket|Ket]] § Session 02, Freed, for the corroborated account). This beat-script preserves the pre-play staging (hunger track, cage DC, feed mechanics) that produced that outcome. The NPC page doesn't restate it.

**Treasury note.** The Surety has immediate running costs. Before dividing individual shares, the crew should set aside ~120 gp into a ship's fund. Rough expenses ahead:

- **Crew wages**: 2 sp per day per sailor. ~6 crew (if everyone stays) costs ~36 gp per month. Geoffrey works for passage and shares, but the others expect pay.
- **Repainting** (to lose the Crown registry): ~75 gp at a working yard like Kalowe, ~150 gp at Tidefall (better work, higher fees, more questions).
- **Port fees, provisions, incidentals**: roughly 5 to 15 gp per port depending on size.

If the crew doesn't raise the treasury question themselves, Geoffrey will, quietly, and only once.

**Powder magazine** (12 cannon charges in sealed copper canisters, 20 pistol charges). Geoffrey stops at the threshold and speaks without preamble: *"No open flames, not ever, not for any reason. That's not a suggestion."* He waits until everyone nods.

## The Crew

By the time the hold tour finishes, the old Surety crew has had an hour to take stock of their situation. They're not hiding. They're not hostile. They're waiting to see what management this is.

Each of them defaults to leaving at the next port. Not out of anger. It's simply what sailors do when a ship changes hands under fraught circumstances. The crew's new captains can change this, one conversation at a time.

[[sem-holst|Sem Holst]] catalogues damage Geoffrey overlooked. He'll itemize what's wrong with the hull and how long repairs take, delivering both numbers unflinching. He assesses whether the crew actually respects the vessel beyond its performance. A PC who understands caulked versus packed seams, or who asks hull questions beyond speed, makes him reconsider. *"Not many privateers bother learning that."* Surprise marks his tone.

**Ensign Wouters** was at the chart table before anyone asked him to be, per prep notes. In play, the boarding took him. The Navigator post is open.

[[alys-kuiper|Alys Kuiper]] is in the medic's cabinet doing inventory, which she finds calming. She announces flatly: healer's kits suffice for two more field engagements, and the surgical instruments are well-maintained except for one probe needing replacement. She isn't asking to stay or leave, just performing necessary work because she's most qualified. If the crew formally names the post (Surgeon, ship's medical officer, or any role marking real work), she stays, and if they commit to staffing and stocking the Surgeon's Berth, she examines the cabinet the way Holst examined the hull: assessing potential.

[[old-faas|Old Faas]] is up in the rigging. He went up right after Grigori gave the nod and he hasn't come down. He is not hiding. He just prefers to be useful in ways that keep him off the deck and out of conversations. He'll come down when needed. The argument to stay is simple: ask for his assessment of the rigging and listen to his full answer. He delivers a detailed, quietly passionate twelve-minute account of every line, block, and splice: condition, value, and repair timeline for each. Anyone who hears him out gets an evaluating look. *"Most people leave before the fore-topgallant."*

[[noor|Noor]] is near the galley, failing invisibly by being too enthusiastic. They stand reading a sailing manual while half-listening to the crew move through the galley above. They're the easiest to recruit. They haven't decided where they're headed and want more than anything a ship bound for somewhere interesting. Any destination the crew names that doesn't appear in the manual makes them thoughtful, which counts in the destination's favor.

[[thunk|Thunk]] came through the locked door last, unhurried, and has since drifted toward the gun deck with the easy gravity of a man returning to something that interrupted him. He is the Surety's gunner. He does not mention this. If the crew skips the hold tour, they may reach their first destination never knowing he was there.

He is enormous. The Surety's interior doorframes did not expect him. He smells of old iron and powder and something faintly sweet, which turns out to be the tallow he packs around the shot pouches. He left blacksmithing because the sea called him. When pressed on what that means, he says *"loud, actually, I cannot sleep sometimes, so I come here instead"* with complete earnestness.

He answers any question with full, earnest detail. Ask him about the guns and charge tables, or his hands: his cadence is direct and slightly formal, like a man translating from a language he learned first. The warmth is real, and so is his enthusiasm for things that explode.

His condition to stay is simple: someone asked. Neither a formal role nor a title, just one conversation where the other person wanted to know.

> [!dm]
> **Thunk is the best hire in this scene and he will not tell them that.** Mechanically: when Thunk mans a cannon, he counts as two crew for determining how many guns the crew can man (already landed: [[uncertainty|HCS Surety]] § Crew). If the crew ever installs a [[carpenters-shop|Carpenter's Shop]] (Smithy facility) on the Surety, he can run it without more staff. He left blacksmithing for the sea, but the forge is still in him.

His voice does the rest of the selling.

> [!dm]
>
> - The accent is Arnold Schwarzenegger. Not a joke, play it straight with short declarative sentences and earnest odd emphases.
> - Sample line: *"Ja, I like this gun. She has good, temperament."*
> - Sample line: *"The charge ratio, I already calculate. You want to hear?"*
> - Sample line: *"I was a blacksmith. But the sea, she is louder."*
>
> The roleplay works backward: asking him something (guns, powder, the work) invites him to share. He doesn't need persuading, noticing, or validation to feel useful.

> [!dm]
>
> - These conversations happen naturally. As the crew moves through the ship, people drift apart and reconnect.
> - Reward any PC who engages: perception to notice what Holst is cataloguing, insight to read what Alys is actually asking for, investigation to give Wouters something useful to work with at the chart table.

## The Ship, as a Thing

After the tour, there's a moment where it settles. This is their ship. It needs a crew of ten to run properly, and they have five survivors and one carpenter, plus whatever the crew brings. It has two functioning cannons and a registry plate that says it belongs to the [[dravosi-crown|Dravosi Crown]]. It has a chart table with the Strait fully mapped. It has 6 tons of usable cargo space and a boarding locker stocked for a crew that no longer exists.

Geoffrey, if no one says anything: *"She's sound. Needs work, but she's sound. I know where the problems are."* He pauses. *"What do you want to do with her?"*

That's the question. Move to Scene 05, Central Strait (relink pending, once its page lands), once the crew sets a course.
