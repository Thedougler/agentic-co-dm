## Opening

Full DM beat-script for [[shepherd-grigori|Shepherd Grigori]]'s galley scene aboard the [[uncertainty|HCS Surety]], played as prepped (source `table_status: complete`). The underlying facts (crew backstory, his condition, the Cap'n Gorgeous/Rupert Knighton reveal) are already absorbed onto [[shepherd-grigori|Shepherd Grigori]] § Player-Known (citing `vault/episodes/002/transcript.raw.md:47-61`) and session-02.md's own two-line "galley" Scene Log bullet. This page preserves the staged read-aloud text, verbatim dialogue ("The Question," "His Condition," "The Pretty One"), the crew NPC roster with gimmicks, and the Threat Threshold escalation design. None of this restates material elsewhere, and it provides far more detail than either summary.

*Fires immediately after [[scene-01-rook-final|Scene 01, Rook Final]] resolves with the party controlling the Surety. Trigger on the first character who goes below.*

> [!read-aloud]
> The smell reaches you at the ladder. Something slow-cooked, heavy with aromatics, filling a dank space that has no business smelling this good. The stove ticks with heat, its iron side beaded with moisture from whatever has been simmering on it for the last hour. Someone has been down here making something out of a Crown patrol galley's stores.
>
> The galley is lit. One man is at the table, and his presence fills the low-ceilinged space the way a fire fills a room. Dark hair loose to his shoulders, a thick wiry beard to match. A coat of deep red and gold, salt-stiff at the cuffs but unmistakably the coat of a senior Dravosi Naval Officer, the sleeves rolled up against protocol. A bottle of fine old wine sits in front of him, already well begun. Plates and goblets circle the table, set as if he was expecting company.
>
> He is tying off a bandage on his forearm, one-handed, without slowing the meal. He looks up when you enter, and his eyes find you in the lamplight. Dark. Still. Unsurprised. His face cracks into a warm smile.
>
> *"The ship is under new management?"*
>
> He asks it the way you ask if the kitchen is still serving. He gestures to the places he has set at the table.
>
> *"You must be starving. Sit. The crew has some questions."*

## Running It

The scene runs beat by beat below. First the crew's entrance, then Grigori's own introduction, then the Murrat backstory. The Question and his Condition follow. The Pretty One reveal closes it. Each beat escalates per the Threat Threshold if the party pushes.

## The Crew

*Behind the locked door. Muffled movement, hushed voices. They sent Grigori because he was the only one not worried enough to hide. Once he's satisfied with the party's answers, he nods toward the door. Two knocks, barely. A moment later the lock turns from the inside.*

## Shepherd Grigori

> [!dm]
>
> - [[shepherd-grigori|Shepherd Grigori]] is a CR 19 undead [[hierarch|Hierarch]] sorcerer: warm, deliberate, unafraid—the ease is not an act, nothing in this room threatens him.
> - He is here because Rook was transit, not purpose.
> - He is mildly irritated about the arm ("Brat-aby" came out with real feeling) but has moved past it, visibly.
> - He is heading to [[calveno|Calveno]] where a young heir the Crown physicians gave up on is waiting.
> - The party is an inconvenience that has become slightly more interesting. He has no plans to hurt anyone unless they make it necessary.

> [!dm]
> **The faith is real** (modelled on the [[khlysty|Khlystý]], a mystic sect that treats excess as devotion, pleasure as prayer, and the body as the vehicle for reaching the divine). The meal is not an affectation. He made something real out of the galley's stores because he takes pleasure seriously. Play this straight: the warmth is true, the healing is true.

> [!dm]
> The horror is that he also considers the binding true. He healed them, they are part of him now, and he sees this as a fair and natural exchange. He is not wrong about the healing. He is wrong about everything that follows from it.

> [!dm]
> The crew stayed behind the door because they were afraid; Grigori stayed at the table because he wasn't. When the crew asked who should talk to the pirates, everyone looked at him and he said "Fine" and refilled his glass. He considers this mildly inconvenient and entirely beneath him, but he does not show it.

**Who he is, if asked:** *"A shepherd. I go where people have problems that cannot be fixed. And I fix them."* Pressed: stories of courts, grateful families, a young heir in Calveno whose physicians had given up. Warm. Specific. Verifiable, if anyone cared to check.

**About the wound:** *"A misunderstanding. Nothing worth discussing."* He does not elaborate.

**About the fight:** *"I heard. These things happen at sea."*

**About the bell / the hatch:** *"He rang for All Hands. I declined on behalf of the crew."* A pause. *"He was not pleased."* He refills his wine.

**About Rook:** he shows no warmth and no anger. The absence of feeling is more specific than dislike.

## Crew Beat — Murrat

*Fires naturally once the crew is at the table. Can be triggered by any Rook question, or let it come up organically between courses.*

Old Faas puts it plainly, without being asked:

"He took us to [[murrat|Murrat]]."

He says it the way you report a weather event you survived.

"Wanted a live one. A specimen, he called it. Went ashore with six crew. Four came back needing the surgeon."

Alys doesn't look up from whatever she's doing with her hands.

"Severe puncture wounds. Blood loss. Two of them I genuinely wasn't sure about."

She pauses.

*"Shepherd Grigori sat with them through the night. I did what I could, but."* She sets her hands flat on the table. *"They pulled through because of him. I'll say that plainly."*

Grigori does not respond. He refills his wine.

> [!dm]
> Don't push the Grigori angle here; let it sit. The crew believes he saved those sailors — because he did — and the warmth they have for him is genuine and slightly uncomfortable given what he is. Alys is not wrong to credit him, but she doesn't know what the credit means.

## The Question

Once the party confirms they're in charge, Grigori sets down his fork.

"Then I have a question. If you will indulge me."

He refills his wine without asking if anyone wants some.

"In my travels I have met three kinds of pirates. The first kind. Strange ones. Their plans make no sense. They take things you would not think to value. Unpredictable. They almost never hurt people by accident, but only because hurting people is the goal."

He rotates the glass once.

"The second kind are businessmen. Hostages, cargo, passage fees, pure mathematics. They prefer you alive because you are worth more. Clean logic. I can respect it."

*"Then there are the robin hood ones."* A slight pause. *"These have a code. They keep their word when it costs them something. They feel genuine shame when their crew misbehaves, which I find charming, actually. Not a common trait in the profession."*

He says, quieter, almost to the wine: *"I am always glad to meet the third kind. Fewer of them walk the world than should."*

He looks at the party. Genuinely curious.

"I ask because the answer tells me what kind of cook I should be."

> [!dm]
> [[intelligence|Intelligence]] gathering in a dinner party coat — he wants to know how dangerous they are. Any real answer works; silence or deflection is the only wrong move. Let the table argue about which type they are — that argument is the scene. If they ask which type he is: "I am a guest; the question does not apply."

## His Condition

Before any deal is done, he raises it without pressure — an expectation that reasonable people share:

"One thing only. The crew. Anyone who wants to leave at the next port, you let them leave. They have had an unsettling week."

He does not negotiate it. He does not threaten if refused. He notes it the way you note something obvious and moves on.

**If the party refuses:** The warmth doesn't leave all at once. It thins. *"That's unfortunate."* He waits. He does not raise his voice. If they press: he stands up slowly. The coat settles differently. The room gets very quiet. He still does not speak first.

> [!dm]
> He can kill everyone at this table without moving from where he stands. He is choosing not to because it would be inconvenient and he finds the dinner pleasant. That calculus changes only if they insist.

## The Pretty One

Before the Calveno question, something occurs to him.

"The tall one. Long hair. Looked like a statue."

He waits.

"Is he still alive?"

When the answer is no:

He sets his glass down.

*"Mm."* A pause. *"That is a shame."*

He taps the rim once with one finger.

*"House Knighton. Very old family. Well connected in the Navy. His father will hear his son died on this patrol, and the vessel is now missing. Commodore Knighton does not solve his problems alone. He sends his own fleet."* A beat. *"The Knights. Those ships do not weigh themselves down with brigs."*

He picks up his glass again.

*"So. [[kalowe|Kalowe]] has no Crown presence, no Knighton contacts I am aware of. Five days for the work, perhaps. [[calveno|Calveno]] has better facilities, two days or less. If you're going to Calveno that would be good for me; I have business there."*

He does not say which he prefers.

> [!dm]
> Grigori does not know [[capn-gorgeous|Cap'n Gorgeous]]'s real name — he recognized the Knighton house crest on the coat, not the man. The Knighton who commands the pursuit fleet is [[rupert-knighton|Rupert Knighton]]: Commodore, the Knight Squadron, Dravosi Crown Navy, and also [[geoffrey-draves|Geoffrey Draves]]'s uncle — the party does not know this. If the party is committed to [[tidefall|Tidefall]] regardless: "Your ship. Your risk."

## Threat Threshold

Grigori is not a threat in this scene unless the party creates the conditions. He becomes a problem if they:

1. Refuse to answer the question and won't be moved
2. Threaten him directly, or threaten the crew in front of him
3. Refuse to let any crew off at the next port

In any of those cases: the warmth leaves the room. He does not threaten. He does not move. He says *"That's unfortunate"* and waits.

> [!dm]
> If combat somehow starts: CR 19, 263 HP, AC 17, CHA 21, Mad Monk's Resilience (CON save to drop to 1 HP vs non-radiant non-crit). He does not die. He thanks them for the meal and walks off the ship.

## After the Scene

Once the party has answered the question and the condition is acknowledged, Grigori glances toward the locked door. Small gesture, barely a nod. The lock turns from the inside and the crew files out — wary, tired, relieved in that specific way of people who had a plan for dying that they no longer need.

Move to [[scene-04-ship-exploration|Ship Exploration]]. The party has a ship, a crew, and a decision to make about where they're going.

## Enemy Roster

No enemy by default — this is a negotiation/mystery scene, not a combat encounter. Grigori only fights if the party forces it (see Threat Threshold); if so, use the CR 19 stat notes above. Source gives no formal stat block, only the CR/HP/AC/CHA/resilience summary transcribed verbatim.

## Challenge Calibration

Not applicable in the combat sense. The scene's real challenge is social: extracting the Knighton stakes, agreeing the crew-release condition. There is no fight to balance.

## Terrain

Low-ceilinged Crown patrol galley belowdecks, table set for the party, crew behind a locked door. No stated mechanical terrain effect.

## Tactical Notes

Not a fight by default. The "tactics" are DM pacing/voice notes. Let the "which kind of pirate" argument happen at the table rather than resolving it with a single roll. Don't push the Grigori-saved-the-crew angle; let Alys's line land and move on. Play the Knighton reveal as a beat, not an exposition-dump — he doesn't say which destination he'd choose.

## Lowering the Stakes

The Question and His Condition are the two pressure points — silence/deflection on the Question, or refusing the crew-release condition, both escalate per Threat Threshold. Otherwise the scene has no time pressure; it runs at table pace.

## Raising the Stakes

A party that engages honestly with the Question and accepts the Condition gets the full Knighton/Rupert Knighton intelligence and a two-destination choice (Kalowe vs. Calveno) with Grigori's own travel-time estimate for each — real planning information for the next leg.

## Stakes

Sets up the destination choice (Kalowe or Calveno) that gates Scene 05 — [[central-strait|Central Strait]], and plants the Rupert Knighton pursuit-fleet threat that follows from Cap'n Gorgeous's death.

## If Ignored

Not applicable in the sense of a hook to decline. This is a DM-triggered scene fired the moment a character goes below.

If a player tries to have their character stay on deck instead: let it happen. The scene simply proceeds without them, and Grigori asks after the missing party member once. The Question and the Condition still land on whoever is present.
