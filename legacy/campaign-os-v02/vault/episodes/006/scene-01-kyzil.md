## Opening

> [!read-aloud] The City Wakes Up
> Le Paludi's rooftops rise and dip with every foundation the ground swallowed a little differently: warm tile underfoot gives way to cold slate two feet lower, then a flooded gutter Kyzil clears without a glance at the canal it feeds, the water down there barely moving, too narrow for two boats to pass side by side.
>
> Ahead the roofline splits. Left drops into a walled courtyard, a dead end.
>
> Right stays open above the canal walkway, where a woman already has a flour sack cocked back at her shoulder and a boy on the walkway below has a bucket balanced on a cord, timed to a step Kyzil hasn't taken yet.
>
> Behind him, a whistle finds an answer three roofs over. It doesn't stop.

> [!quote] [[il-gioco-delle-beffe|Beffa]] spotter (calling Kyzil's position)
> "Here! Salt Row rooftops, still moving! Someone get ahead of him!"

## Running It

**The setup.** Across the rooftops of [[le-paludi|Le Paludi]], an ambitious [[il-gioco-delle-beffe|Beffa]] team clocked Kyzil as their mark, pranking a foreign monastic master being a career-making coup. Their first attempts failed instantly against a monk who reads vibration, intent, and precision on instinct; failure only escalated them, pulling in more people, then more, until it's the whole city against one straight man. **The scene's toy is the chase's own escalation.** Each beat below is the mob's answer to how the last one landed, reactive by design, until the smallest hand in Calveno gets the one shot none of the rest could land. Keep every failure low-consequence and physically comedic; the tension is dignity, not survival.

**Beat 1: the city wakes up.** The Opening read-aloud above is this beat. Then run one early, simple trap (net, tripwire, bucket):

> [!check] Acrobatics or Dexterity — The First Trap
> DC 10.
> **Success:** clean, contemptuous ease.
> **Failure:** a minor indignity, soaked or dusted with flour, no real setback either way.

**Beat 2: the net tightens.** Three signals converge for the first time.

> [!read-aloud] The Net Tightens
> The whistles change pitch. All night they've answered each other across three streets. Now they all answer from one.
>
> A weighted line drops from the eaves ahead and pulls taut at knee height, closing the rooftop road solid, the only way past it a drop to the walkway below to the still water and echoing canals.
>
> A bucket swings out from a balcony on its own rope, cold droplets already flicking loose from its rim, timed to Kyzil's next jump.
>
> In the alley mouth another pursuer steps out, water balloon cocked in one fist, the other dusted white to the wrist, flour hanging thick enough in the air to taste.
>
> Somewhere behind, a growing crowd gasps in anticipation.

> [!check] Acrobatics, Athletics, or Dexterity — Thread the Coordinated Trap
> DC 14–16 (player's approach picks the skill; DC climbs as the chase goes on).
> **Success:** barely, and it should feel like it.
> **Failure:** he's boxed in for a beat, has to talk or move his way back out.

**Beat 3 — The Balloon That Doesn't Land.** Someone's been holding for the perfect angle.

> [!read-aloud] The Balloon That Doesn't Land
> A woman crouches in a doorway three rooftops down, one water balloon cradled low against her hip and a second wedged in the crook of her arm, both already sweating condensation in the warm air.
>
> She's timed this to the gap where the walkway narrows to a single plank over the canal, the only line Kyzil can take without breaking stride, and her arm cocks back the instant his silhouette clears the last chimney.

> [!quote] The balloon-thrower — as her arm cocks back
> "There he is! Nobody touch him, he's mine, I've been on this roof since dawn!"

![[scene-01-kyzil-thrower-claim]]

> [!check] Acrobatics or Dexterity — Burst It First
> DC 13; anyone within reach of the throw as it releases may attempt this instead of just dodging it.
> **Success:** he catches it on the fingertips before it's fully airborne — it bursts against her own wrist and forearm instead, and the whole street sees the thrower soak herself.
> **Failure:** the throw gets clean past his hand and catches him across the shoulder — cold, undignified, no real setback.

**On a Success, she gets the last word anyway** — the street's already laughing before she can wring out her sleeve:

> [!quote] Soaked balloon-thrower — to the crowd behind her
> "Don't laugh! He barely even looked at it!"

**Beat 4 — Everything At Once.** The street stops coordinating in turns and starts coordinating all at once.

> [!read-aloud] Everything At Once
> Three things leave hands within the same breath: a flour sack swung underarm from a window ten feet up, a second balloon lobbed high and lazy from the walkway ahead, and a fishing net flicked low across the roof-gutter to catch his ankles before any of it lands.
>
> For one stretched-out second the air over the canal is full and none of it has arrived yet: the sack still turning end over end, the balloon still climbing, the net's lead edge still an inch off the tile.
>
> Kyzil is already moving through all three at once, low and turning in the same motion, the whole sequence compressed into less time than it takes to describe.
>
> Somewhere below, a whistle simply stops.

> [!check] Acrobatics or Dexterity — Running the Gauntlet
> DC 16; one roll covers all three, the DC reflecting that they're timed to land together, not survived one at a time.
> **Success:** he clears all three clean, and it's good enough that pursuers on two roofs stop to actually watch instead of throwing.
> **Success by 5+:** he clears them with room to spare — plants a foot on the net's rope on the way through and leaves it hanging the wrong pursuer's ankles instead.
> **Failure:** one connects, the flour sack catching his trailing leg or the net dragging at his heel for a stride. Either way it's a stumble he shakes off by the next roof.

**Beat 5 — The Smallest Hand in Calveno.**

> [!dm] Beat 5 — Perceive, Then Choose
> Not a roll-your-way-out beat: the Perception check below only decides what Kyzil *knows* going in. The ending itself is the table's call, not a die — ask which way he goes before you read either outcome.

> [!read-aloud] The Smallest Hand in Calveno
> A boy no older than seven sits cross-legged on the walkway ahead, rolling knucklebones against the flagstones, the click of bone on stone the only sound out of step with the chase, never once looking up at the chase bearing down on him. The walkway narrows here to a single low rail between him and the canal, no room to go wide around him.
>
> As Kyzil's shadow crosses him, the boy pitches backward off his heels, arms pinwheeling, one hand flung out toward the exact line Kyzil is already running.

> [!check] Wisdom (Perception) — What's In His Hand
> DC 13.
> **Success:** mid-fall, Kyzil clocks it — a coin-sized tin novelty in the boy's fist, the kind hawked at every festival stall this week, coiled to deliver one harmless snap of static. Whatever happens next, he'll see it coming.
> **Failure:** just a falling child and a hand flung out. If there's anything in it, he finds out the same way everyone else will.

**The choice.** Grab the falling boy, or let him land his own fall and keep moving. Play it straight either way — no judgment built into the prose.

**If he grabs him:**

> [!read-aloud] Got You
> Ahead on the canal walkway, a boy pitches backward off his heels, arms pinwheeling, one hand flung out into the exact line Kyzil is running. The hand that's caught a hundred wrist strikes out of the air catches this one too, without breaking stride.
>
> Mid-catch, a coin-sized tin novelty completes its fall from the boy's fist into Kyzil's palm. It's the kind hawked at every festival stall this week, coiled to deliver one harmless snap of static.
>
> The zap that traces his palm is small enough to be nothing, quiet enough that only he hears it, and by the time the giggling mob rounds the corner behind the boy, still upright, still smiling, Kyzil already knows exactly what just happened to him.

> [!quote] The boy on the ground — grinning up at him
> "Got you. You're the only one who's ever gonna know."

**If he lets it go:**

> [!read-aloud] The One That Didn't Land
> Ahead on the canal walkway, the boy who flung himself backward into Kyzil's path rights himself off one palm before he's even finished falling, already laughing, already scrambling backward out of the way. He was never in danger, only ever bait.
>
> Kyzil doesn't break stride. The mob rounds the corner a beat later to an empty walkway and a kid who just shrugs at them, still grinning.
>
> The rooftops thin out ahead, the chase noise receding roof by roof, until the only sound left is his own breath and the tile under his feet. Somewhere past the edge of the district, before the sun fully clears the harbor, Master Kyzil finally gets his morning run in.

> [!quote] The boy — calling after him, delighted
> "Next time, then!"

**The ending is open — the table's choice, not a script.** Either branch, the Beffa chase itself keeps running the rest of the night after this beat closes; don't narrate a citywide "he got got" or "he got away clean" rumor as settled fact afterward — Scene 2 opens the next morning on general chase gossip only, not a verdict on how tonight ended.
