## Opening

> [!read-aloud] Transition
> The cart hauling [[cobb|Cobb]]'s cannon barrel is still creaking toward the dock. Ruk and Enzo fall into step at Nona's sides three streets over, [[calveno-la-vasca|La Vasca]]'s damp giving way to Le Paludi's fish-market brine and noise.
>
> Neither of them says a word yet.

> [!read-aloud]
> Le Paludi's fish market runs at full noise, half the crowd's attention still on the [[il-gioco-delle-beffe|Beffa]] three streets over.
>
> The whole street is thick with brine and fish guts. Gulls work the gutters. Vendors call prices across canal water.
>
> Ruk and Enzo flank Nona close enough to touch, both scanning faces instead of stalls.
>
> She moves at her own pace regardless, one paw testing a mackerel's eye before the vendor wraps it without asking.
>
> A boy at one of the nearby stalls catches Ruk's stare halfway through unrolling a paper-wrapped bundle from inside his coat, and decides he'd instead eat the whole pie than throw it.
>
> Nona hums over a crate of lemons, unbothered, and doesn't look up once.
>
> Then another whistle starts behind her, closer and clear across the canal. She chuckles without turning around. She sets down the lemon and raises her voice to carry.

> [!quote] Nona Black-Jaw (proudly, to every prankster in earshot)
> "Five Beffas, and not one of you has laid a finger on me! I'm right here, darlings. Do make it interesting this year."

## Running It

**The setup.** In the market on her own home ground in [[le-paludi|Le Paludi]], Nona insists on doing her own grocery shopping today, in public, during festival week, despite being a known and valuable target. Five years ago Ruk finally talked her out of fielding a team in the [[il-gioco-delle-beffe|Beffa]]; she has done her shopping in the thick of Beffa week every year since, and has never once acknowledged the timing. Ruk and Enzo's real job is quiet, constant interference against Beffa pranksters who keep trying their luck against her. She notices every attempt and pretends she doesn't, chuckling faintly as if she isn't the reason half of them fail before they start.

**The scene's toy** is the market itself. A crowd thick enough to hide an approach. Loud enough to swallow a whistle. Threaded with the spotters' own relay (one whistle passed roof to roof and stall to stall ahead of every attempt). Players who learn to read that relay turn it against the people using it.

> [!dm]
> The timing is the point. Nona chose market day in the middle of Beffa week because Ruk took the Beffa away from her, and this walk is the team she never got to field. Bait set out in the open. Her "five Beffas untouched" announcement in the Opening is the scoreboard, reading directly at Ruk.

Beyond the one jab she plays as a light joke, she will never say any of this out loud. Play it only in her timing and her private amusement, and if Ruk ever puts it together mid-scene, she orders the lemons.

**The thematic point.** This is a direct preview, in miniature, of the [[the-rattle-statblock|Rattle]] safety valve waiting inside tonight's fight (full mechanic: [[otar-the-foul|Otar's page]]). The [[warren|Warren]] community answers a call for help specifically because it doesn't hide, and Nona walking these streets unescorted-in-all-but-name with Ruk and Enzo is that principle already in motion.

**The turn.** The boast isn't finished. She lets the market have its laugh, then turns back to her own two Guards and hands them the bill, sweet as anything:

> [!quote] Nona Black-Jaw (turning back to Ruk and Enzo, sweetly)
> "And only because you two won't let me take part."

> [!quote] Ruk (eyes on the crowd, not on her)
> "Beffa week, any stranger may toss a pie at you and no one stops him. I cannot keep you safe like that. A pie can conceal anything."

![[scene-03-nona-ruk-pie-protest]]

> [!quote] Enzo (unhurried, tipping his cigar at Ruk)
> "His fault. I could smell a grenade in a pie."

![[scene-03-nona-enzo-his-fault]]

Nona doesn't even slow down:

> [!quote] Nona Black-Jaw (answering Ruk's explanation)
> "The Rattle works because we don't hide, Ruk. The day I stop walking my own streets is the day I lose the right to ask this city to stand up for itself."

Adapt the wording live. The sentiment never budges. She isn't asking permission (she's stating something she's lived by for forty years), and no roll changes her mind.

**If Ruk keeps pushing**, she has one card left and plays it without mercy: her second unbearably boring Beffa, the year she grew tired of watching him hover and took herself out for exactly this walk, and a cupcake meant for her face caught his instead, dead center.

> [!quote] Nona Black-Jaw (if Ruk won't drop it)
> "Say that again, darling. And then tell Enzo whose face the cupcake targeted."

She holds the straight face just long enough to land it, then turns to study the eels hard until she trusts her own mouth again. Whatever this costs Ruk's dignity, she refuses to laugh about it to his face.

**Malicious compliance.** This is Nona's preferred flavor of civil disobedience, and it runs on the same logic as tonight's Rattle: follow the letter while gutting the spirit so the consequences land somewhere absurd. Five years of Ruk's caution, repaid one market day at a time.

**Playing it.** The comedy is in Ruk and Enzo's escalating effort to [[guard]] a woman who is, by design, nearly impossible to protect from something this small. Pranks escalate through the morning, starting clumsy and growing steadily more polished until she finally handles one herself. The basket arrives home full. None of it reads as real danger. The tension lives entirely in Ruk and Enzo's effort exceeding the actual size of the threat, and that gap is the joke.

**Beat 1: market at full noise** The Opening read-aloud above is this beat, ending on Nona's challenge to the market. The exchange above runs in order from there: her turn back at her guards, Enzo's blame, Ruk's explanation (his clip, or hand the line to Ruk's player and let them make it their own), her Rattle answer (no roll; she declines, warmly, every time). Make sure Enzo's line lands whatever else happens (it's the setup the cupcake callback and the whole malicious-compliance joke pay off), and the table doesn't know the history without it. If the player keeps arguing, the cupcake callback above is hers to play. This is who she is, not a lever a good roll moves.

Underneath the whole exchange, Ruk and Enzo are still working. The second whistle from the Opening is still out there somewhere across the canal, and one of them can try to place it before the noise closes back over it.

> [!check] Perception — Mark the Second Whistle
> DC 12, rolled once, any time during the exchange above.
> **Success:** they clock the rooftop it came from before it's lost. Beat 2's intercept starts with the position already called.
> **Failure:** the market swallows the sound whole, same as it swallows everything else here. Beat 2 opens blind.

**Beat 2: the first one** Easy to spot, which is the point.

> [!read-aloud]
> A wiry man drifts stall to stall without buying, eyes on the basket instead of the fish. He's the obvious mark once you know the walk. A paper cone of flour rides in one hand.
>
> On a low roof above him, no higher than the canal below is wide, a friend pays out a string one careful arm-length at a time.
>
> Nona stops at the eel stall directly beneath it, in the cold reek of ice and brine, and takes her time choosing.
>
> The fishmonger looks up. He sees the string, sees her, then pretends with all his heart to be rearranging ice.

> [!check] Athletics or Intimidation — Intercept Him
> DC 10, before he acts. Roll with advantage if Beat 1's Perception check succeeded.
> **Success:** he takes one look and suddenly remembers urgent business two stalls down (doesn't run, doesn't walk normally either).
> **Failure:** he yelps and drops what he was holding. Half the market turns to look as Nona glances over, entirely too pleased: "Well. That's one way to do it. Anyone else want a turn?"

**Beat 3: the professionals** Word spreads quickly. The next attempt is more polished.

![[scene-03-nona-pickpockets|Adult animated art in the Archer style: crisp vector-like linework, bold ink contours, cel-shaded lighting, poster-style staging, expressive grounded faces, richly saturated adventure palette, Renaissance Age-of-Sail setting. > [!read-aloud]
> Figures work single-file through a walkway too narrow for two abreast with the focus of surgeons. The tall one counts stalls under his breath while the short one carries chalk powder low and stiff-armed, dust already catching dry and bitter, the way nobody ever carries anything innocent. This is a practiced routine, choreographed down to the step.
>
> At three stalls the pattern falters. Again at two.
>
> The fishwives have stopped haggling to watch, and one of them is already grinning.

> [!check] Deception or Stealth — Redirect Them Quietly
> DC 12, subtle action required.
> **Success:** they drift toward a different stall entirely, each convinced he chose it himself.
> **Failure:** they catch the redirect half a second too late, hover awkwardly, and retreat anyway, gracelessly. The watching fishwives applaud.

**Beat 4: the one she handles** Whether the guards are winning or losing by this point, let one prankster slip through, not to test them but to give Ruk and Enzo a decision: close the gap and grab the kid before she lets go, or hold their line and watch her finish it herself.

> [!read-aloud]
> A hand darts for the basket. She catches the wrist without looking, mid-sentence to the fishmonger, and holds it open like a gate opened for someone slow. The boy pulls twice, but the grip does not budge. His struggling shifts the brine-stink of the stall thicker into the air, while her voice keeps on discussing mackerel with the vendor.

> [!check] Athletics or Acrobatics — Close the Gap
> DC 12, only if either [[guard]] moves on him before she lets go. Holding position skips the roll and plays the ending below as written.
> **Success:** a hand closes on his collar the same instant hers opens. He's caught outright.
> **Failure:** they're a half-step too slow. He's already through the crowd by the time they arrive.

> [!quote] Nona Black-Jaw (releasing the boy's wrist)
> "You'll want a better grip than that."

**If he's caught,** Nona lets go of the wrist without missing a beat in her mackerel haggling and leaves the boy entirely to his captor. First real catch of the walk, and neither Guard earns a word of credit for it.

**If he's not,** he's gone before either of her [[guard|Guards]] has taken a full step, and the market swallows him whole, same as always.

- This is the Rattle principle in miniature either way. She doesn't hide, which means she was never in danger to begin with. It's the principle made manifest, and it's malicious compliance on top of that. She hands them a real, physical moment on her own timing, and how they use it says more about them than it does about her. If Ruk starts explaining how the boy got past them, Enzo's verdict from the Opening sits on the table (a look, a tip of the cigar, or hand Enzo's player the reprise: *"His fault."*). No clip needed twice. Dry delivery makes this beat funnier.

**Beat 5: same time tomorrow**

![[scene-03-nona-heading-home|Archer-style animated illustration for adults: clean vector lines, strong ink outlines, cel-shaded light, poster composition, expressive grounded faces, saturated adventure color palette, set in a Renaissance Age-of-Sail world. Do not include: any text, watermarks, logos, gore, photorealistic rendering, anime or chibi style, pixel art, or a stock-photo look.]]

**The ending stays the same.** The count of pranks handled doesn't change how it closes, only how tired Ruk and Enzo look getting there.

> [!read-aloud]
> The afternoon's worn the market down to its last hour, vendors' calls slower now, half the stalls already emptying around them, the canal beside it too still to have lost a single word said over it all day.
>
> Eventually the basket's full, lemon rind and brine still sharp in the air around it, and there's nowhere left in this stretch that hasn't heard, one way or another, that today isn't a good day to try anything clever near the small woman by the lemons.
>
> Nona turns for home at last, unhurried as ever, a wrapped fish tucked under one arm.

> [!check] Acrobatics — Catch the Jostle
> DC 8, no confrontation, purely physical; either [[guard]] may roll.
> **Success:** a stray elbow in the crowd knocks the basket sideways and whoever's closer catches it one-handed before the lemon hits the canal. Dinner's intact.
> **Failure:** the lemon goes in anyway. Nona watches it float off without breaking stride.

> [!quote] Nona Black-Jaw (heading home, not turning to check if either of you is smiling)
> "Good work, boys. Same time tomorrow?"

The quiet ends there. Nothing about tonight's fight has announced itself yet. That's the last honest calm the table gets before it does.
