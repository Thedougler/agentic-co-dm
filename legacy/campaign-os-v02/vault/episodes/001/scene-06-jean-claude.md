## Opening

> [!read-aloud]
> You stand on white sand, and you can smell them before you can hear them.
>
> You came out of the Verdant Teeth. Five islands two days south of [[kalowe|Kalowe]], dense rainforest canopy, [[grung-npc|Grung]] territory as far back as anyone has records. Your people run on caste and colour, blue ranks below purple ranks below gold, and the system holds because everyone inside it believes it is the only way. You believed it too, once.
>
> A month ago you ran. A gnome is dead. A sister named Simone made a choice, and you left in the dark with people screaming behind you. You have been moving through the [[midchain|Midchain]] since, one island ahead of the hunter unit Simone assembled. They know your methods. She taught them.
>
> Dense scrub at your back, close enough to scratch. Mangroves to your left, their roots threading down into dark water, the whole tangle breathing salt and wet green and mud in the heat. The air clings to you.
>
> Could you show us who's standing on that beach right now?

*[Let them finish.]*

## Running It

### Beat 1: Flight to the Saltwright

> [!dm]
> If they ask about the hunters: half a mile out, dogs in front, they have a scent and they know it. If they ask about the channel: wide and dark and warm. JC reads water the way a hunter reads tracks, and this channel is swimmable, but 200 feet of open dark water with dogs on the bank is not a plan. The ship is the only option that doesn't make things worse.

> [!read-aloud]
> A month of staying unseen. Almost.
>
> They are getting closer. Half a mile in the trees, maybe less. Several of them, moving with the confidence of people who have a trail and know it. In front of them, rampaging through the underbrush, dogs.
>
> The channel ahead is open water. Wide, 200 feet to the opposite shore. Dark enough that you cannot see the bottom.
>
> Then you notice the ship.
>
> A small boxy two-masted brig, moving slow, sitting low and full with cargo weight, stained with years of salt and wear. She is drifting in close to the shoreline, and for a moment, maybe only a couple, she is near enough.
>
> Behind you, the dogs are getting louder.

> [!dm]
> When he goes for the ship, let the player describe the approach and the grab. Jean-Claude has a climb speed and can cast [[jump|Jump]] as a bonus action (no roll unless they want one).

> [!read-aloud]
> The wood is rough and damp under your fingers, the grain swelled with seawater, salt crystals in every groove. You find the gaps between the planking and hold.
>
> The shore moves away. The barking grows thin, then small, then silent. The water around you deepens, stretching all the way to the horizon in both directions, and there is no alarm from the deck above. No boots at the rail. No raised voice. Just the occasional chitter of a monkey.
>
> Trailing off the stern there's a loose rope, the end of it dragging in the water, looped once around the aft cleat and going nowhere. It bobs in the wake like an invitation.
>
> And in the hull beside you, just below the waterline, there is a gap in the boarding. Just wide enough.

## Beat 2: If Jean-Claude Stays Outside (The Reef Shark)

> [!dm]
> If the player decides to stay on the outside, he's got the hull grip or the trailing rope. Let him take either.

> [!read-aloud]
> The water here is warm and shallow, you can just make out the sandy bottom through the green murk, maybe twelve feet down. Seagrass. A few small silver fish catching the light (almost peaceful).

> [!dm]
> Let the comfort land. Then:

> [!read-aloud]
> Something changes. The small fish scatter.
>
> You notice a shape moving in a slow, unhurried arc maybe thirty feet back in the wake (not close yet), just beneath the surface. Dark, low, five feet of muscle with a blunt nose and a caudal fin cutting a shallow V in the water. A [[reef-shark|Reef Shark]]. It's following the disturbance. Following you.

> [!mechanic]
> **Reef Shark escalation.** CR 1/2, 22 HP (not hunting, curious). Run the escalation at your pace:
>
> 1. Holds at thirty feet, circling. Nature/Survival DC 12: reef sharks don't typically attack prey this size unprovoked. *Typically.* Fail: he doesn't know that, and the not-knowing reads on his face. Move to step 2 with the fear already there.
> 2. If he stays still, it closes to fifteen feet. The eye, white and flat, finds him. "It isn't moving away."
> 3. If he kicks or splashes, it startles back then resumes the arc tighter (ten feet). The curiosity is more focused.
> 4. If he's on the rope, it noses the trailing end and tugs once. The tug travels directly into whatever body part Jean-Claude has looped it around.
> 5. Escalation ceiling: it does not bite. It bumps (one firm, investigative nudge against his foot or the rope, enough to rattle his teeth). This is the universe suggesting, firmly, that the hull gap is right there.

> [!dm]
> If he still won't go in after the bump, that's fine (cut away to another player). The Reef Shark loses interest when it decides he's not food. Give him a moment of dignity about it when you return. The hull gap is still there.

## Beat 3: In the Hold

> [!dm]
> When he takes the gap:

> [!read-aloud]
> The wood is just wide enough. Inside, it smells of bilge water, rope, and something alive.

> [!dm]
> Shift to both Perrin and Jean-Claude: neither expected company. JC is soaking wet, electric blue, and smaller than Perrin anticipated; Perrin is a [[rattkin|Rattkin]] in a fish hold with stolen gems and an unexplained blade. Play the confusion before any alliance (two people with no reason to trust each other, communicating only in whispers and gestures).

> [!dm]
> Don't rush this moment. It ends on its own when the ship moves.

> [!read-aloud]
> Perrin. Something electric blue and small drops through a crack in the hull directly above your head and lands in the hold with you.
>
> Above, the ship shifts her weight. Not the sea. Not a wave. Something coming alongside in the dark. At the wheel, [[beaumont-sel|Beaumont]] goes quiet.

Continues in [[scene-07-the-hold|Scene 7: the Hold]].

## Enemy Roster

[[reef-shark|Reef Shark]], CR 1/2, 22 HP. See the Reef Shark escalation mechanic in Beat 2 (not a lethal threat: the escalation ceiling is a bump, never a bite).

## Challenge Calibration

Not a combat encounter. The Reef Shark's escalation is a pacing tool pushing Jean-Claude toward the hull gap. No initiative rolls. The source gates the beat entirely on the player's choices (stay still, kick or splash, grab the rope) plus the one Nature/Survival check in the escalation mechanic above.

## Terrain

Open channel water off a beach in the Verdant Teeth (200 feet wide, dark, bottomless-looking). Shallow warm shoal water near the Saltwright's hull (about 12 feet deep, Seagrass, sandy bottom). The ship's hull (rough damp wood, a boarding gap below the waterline, a trailing stern rope looped on the aft cleat). The hold below decks (bilge water, rope, cramped).

## Tactical Notes

Jean-Claude has a climb speed and can cast Jump as a bonus action. No roll needed for the initial ship grab unless the player wants one. Player behavior paces the Reef Shark escalation entirely (staying still vs. kicking/splashing vs. being on the rope) rather than a fixed schedule. The hunters (half a mile out, dogs in front, on a live scent) are the off-screen pressure forcing the ship choice. The DM never resolves them directly in this beat, only cites their proximity to close off the swim-the-channel option.

## Lowering the Stakes

If the player won't go in after the Reef Shark's bump, cut away to another player. The Reef Shark loses interest when it decides he's not food. Give him a moment of dignity on return. The hull gap remains available whenever he's ready.

## Stakes

Getting spotted or caught by Simone's hunter unit before reaching the ship. The beat's entire pressure is escape, not combat. The Reef Shark cannot bite per its own escalation ceiling, so the only real stake in Beat 2 is pacing/tension, not damage.

## If Ignored

This is a DM-triggered forced beat, not a hook the party can decline. The hunters are closing, and the ship is "the only option that doesn't make things worse."
