# Playing the Game (5e SRD): Core Mechanics

Part of [[playing-the-game|Playing the Game (5e SRD)]].

## Abilities

Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, Intelligence, Wisdom, and Charisma are the core abilities. All creatures have them. Each ability measures a physical or mental trait.

| Ability      | Score Measures …                    |
|--------------|-------------------------------------|
| [[strength\|Strength]]         | Physical might                      |
| [[dexterity\|Dexterity]]       | Agility, reflexes, and balance      |
| [[constitution\|Constitution]] | Health and stamina                  |
| [[intelligence\|Intelligence]] | Reasoning and memory                |
| [[wisdom\|Wisdom]]             | Perceptiveness and mental fortitude |
| [[charisma\|Charisma]]         | Confidence, poise, and charm        |

### Ability Scores

Each ability has a score from 1 to 20. Some monsters can have a score as high as 30. The score shows how strong that ability is.

| Score | Meaning                                      |
|-------|-----------------------------------------------|
| 1     | The lowest a score can normally go. If an effect reduces a score to 0, that effect explains what happens. |
| 2-9   | Represents a weak capability.                |
| 10-11 | Represents the human average.                |
| 12-19 | Represents a strong capability.              |
| 20    | The highest an adventurer's score can go unless a feature says otherwise. |
| 21-29 | Represents an extraordinary capability.      |
| 30    | The highest a score can go.                  |

### Ability Modifiers

Each ability has a modifier. You apply it whenever you make a D20 Test with that ability. The modifier comes from the ability's score.

**Round Down.** Whenever you divide or multiply a number in the game, round down. Some rules make an exception and tell you to round up.

| Score | Modifier |
|-------|----------|
| 1     | −5       |
| 2-3   | −4       |
| 4-5   | −3       |
| 6-7   | −2       |
| 8-9   | −1       |
| 10-11 | +0       |
| 12-13 | +1       |
| 14-15 | +2       |
| 16-17 | +3       |
| 18-19 | +4       |
| 20-21 | +5       |
| 22-23 | +6       |
| 24-25 | +7       |
| 26-27 | +8       |
| 28-29 | +9       |
| 30    | +10      |

## D20 Tests

Sometimes the outcome of an action is uncertain. When that happens, the game uses a d20 roll to decide success or failure. D20 Tests take these forms:

- Ability checks
- Saving throws
- Attack rolls

Follow these steps:

1. **Roll 1d20.** Aim for a high roll. With Advantage or Disadvantage, roll two d20s instead of one. Keep only one result: the higher die for Advantage, the lower die for Disadvantage.
2. **Add Modifiers.** Add these modifiers to the number rolled on the d20:
   - **The Relevant Ability Modifier.** Which ability modifier applies depends on the specific D20 Test.
   - **Your Proficiency Bonus If Relevant.** Each creature has a Proficiency Bonus, a number added when a D20 Test uses something, such as a skill, that the creature has proficiency in. See "Proficiency" below.
   - **Circumstantial Bonuses and Penalties.** A class feature, a spell, or another rule might give a bonus or penalty to the die roll.
3. **Compare the Total to a Target Number.** Add up the d20 roll and its modifiers. If that total equals or beats the target number, the D20 Test succeeds. If not, it fails. The Game Master sets target numbers and tells players whether their rolls succeed. A Difficulty Class (DC) is the target number for an ability check or a saving throw, while an Armor Class (AC) is the target number for an attack roll.

### Ability Checks

An ability check represents a creature using talent and training to overcome a challenge. Forcing open a stuck door or picking a lock are both ability checks. Subtler tasks count too, like entertaining a crowd or tracking a quarry through the wilds. The GM and the rules call for an ability check when a creature tries something that could plausibly fail. That something must not be an attack. When the outcome is uncertain and interesting to the story, the dice decide the result.

#### Ability Modifier for Ability Checks

Each ability check takes its name from the modifier it uses. A Strength check uses your Strength modifier, and an Intelligence check uses your Intelligence modifier. Different situations call for different ability checks, and the GM picks whichever ability fits best.

| Ability      | Make a Check To …                                          |
|--------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| Strength     | Lift, push, pull, or break something                       |
| Dexterity    | Move nimbly, quickly, or quietly                            |
| Constitution | Push your body beyond normal limits                        |
| Intelligence | Reason or remember                                          |
| Wisdom       | Notice things in the environment or in creatures' behavior |
| Charisma     | Influence, entertain, or deceive                            |

#### Proficiency Bonus for Ability Checks

Add your Proficiency Bonus to an ability check when two things are true. The GM says a skill or tool proficiency is relevant, and you have that proficiency. Say a rule calls for a Strength (Acrobatics or Athletics) check. You add your Proficiency Bonus to that check if you have proficiency in Acrobatics or Athletics.

#### Difficulty Class for Ability Checks

The Difficulty Class of an ability check represents the task's difficulty. The more difficult the task, the higher its DC. The rules provide DCs for some checks. The GM sets the DC for the rest.

| Task Difficulty   | DC | Ability/Skill | Effect on Failure |
|--------------------|----|----------------|--------------------|
| Trivial            | 5  | The specific check in play sets this | The specific check in play sets this |
| Easy               | 10 | The specific check in play sets this | The specific check in play sets this |
| Medium             | 15 | The specific check in play sets this | The specific check in play sets this |
| Hard               | 20 | The specific check in play sets this | The specific check in play sets this |
| Very hard          | 25 | The specific check in play sets this | The specific check in play sets this |
| Impossible         | 30 | The specific check in play sets this | The specific check in play sets this |

This table sets the DC. First pick the ability or skill and the consequence of failure for the check in play.

### Saving Throws

A saving throw (also called a save) represents an attempt to evade or resist a threat. A fiery explosion or a spell trying to invade your mind are two examples. You don't normally choose to make a save. Instead, your character or a monster is at risk, so you must make one. The effect that caused the save spells out its result.

If you don't want to resist the effect, you can choose to fail the save without rolling.

#### Ability Modifier for Saving Throws

Each saving throw takes its name from the modifier it uses. A Constitution saving throw uses your Constitution modifier, and a Wisdom saving throw uses your Wisdom modifier. Other saves work the same way.

| Ability      | Make a Save To …               |
|--------------|----------------------------------|
| Strength     | Physically resist direct force |
| Dexterity    | Dodge out of harm's way        |
| Constitution | Endure a toxic hazard          |
| Intelligence | Recognize an illusion as fake  |
| Wisdom       | Resist a mental assault        |
| Charisma     | Assert your identity           |

#### Proficiency Bonus for Saving Throws

You add your Proficiency Bonus to your saving throw if you have proficiency in the ability that save uses.

#### Difficulty Class for Saving Throws

The effect that causes a saving throw sets its Difficulty Class. Or the GM sets it directly. A spell that forces a save draws its DC from the caster's spellcasting ability and Proficiency Bonus. Monster abilities that call for saves specify the DC.

### Attack Rolls

An attack roll determines whether an attack hits a target. The attack succeeds whenever the roll equals or exceeds the target's Armor Class. Attack rolls occur in battle most often. But the GM might also ask for one in other situations, such as an archery competition.

#### Ability Modifier for Attack Rolls

| Ability   | Attack Type                                                                                   |
|-----------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Strength  | Melee attack with a weapon or an Unarmed Strike                                                 |
| Dexterity | Ranged attack with a weapon                                                                     |
| Varies    | Spell attack (the spellcaster's spellcasting feature sets the ability used)         |

Some features let you use different ability modifiers from those listed. The Finesse property lets you use Strength or Dexterity with a weapon that has that property.

#### Proficiency Bonus for Attack Rolls

You add your Proficiency Bonus to your attack roll in two cases: when you attack with a weapon you have proficiency with, and when you attack with a spell.

#### Armor Class

A creature's Armor Class represents how well the creature avoids taking a wound in combat. Character creation sets a character's AC. A monster's stat block lists its AC directly.

**Calculating AC.** All creatures start with the same base AC calculation: base AC = 10 + the creature's Dexterity modifier. Armor, magic items, spells, and more can then change a creature's AC.

**Only One Base AC.** Some spells and class features give characters a different way to calculate their AC. A character might have two or more such features. If so, that character must choose only one to use. Only one base calculation can be in effect for a creature at a time.

#### Rolling 20 or 1

If you roll a 20 on the d20 (a "natural 20") for an attack roll, the attack hits. This happens no matter what modifiers apply or what the target's AC is. Players call this a Critical Hit.

If you roll a 1 on the d20 (a "natural 1") for an attack roll, the attack misses. This happens no matter what modifiers apply or what the target's AC is.

### Advantage/Disadvantage

Sometimes Advantage or Disadvantage modifies a D20 Test. Advantage reflects positive circumstances around a d20 roll. Disadvantage reflects negative circumstances.

Special abilities and actions most often grant you Advantage or Disadvantage. The GM can also decide that circumstances grant Advantage or impose Disadvantage.

#### Heroic Inspiration

Sometimes the GM or a rule gives you Heroic Inspiration. If you have it, you can spend it to reroll any die right after rolling it. You must then use the new roll.

**Only One at a Time.** You can never have more than one instance of Heroic Inspiration. Say something gives you Heroic Inspiration and you already have it. If so, you can give it to a player character in your group who lacks it.

**Gaining Heroic Inspiration.** Your GM can give you Heroic Inspiration for many reasons. Most often, it's when you do something heroic or entertaining in character. It's a reward for making the game more fun for everyone at the table.

Other rules might let your character gain Heroic Inspiration without the GM's decision. [[human|Human]] characters start each day with it.

#### Roll Two D20s

When a roll has either Advantage or Disadvantage, roll a second d20 along with the first. Use the higher of the two rolls if you have Advantage, and use the lower roll if you have Disadvantage. Say you roll an 18 and a 3: use the 3 with Disadvantage, or the 18 with Advantage.

#### Advantage and Disadvantage Don't Stack

If two or more situations grant Advantage on the same roll, you still roll only two d20s. If two or more situations impose Disadvantage on that roll instead, the same rule applies: only two d20s.

If circumstances cause a roll to have both Advantage and Disadvantage, the roll has neither. You roll one d20.

The same rule applies when many circumstances impose Disadvantage and only one grants Advantage. It also applies the other way around.

#### Interactions with Rerolls

Say you have Advantage or Disadvantage, and something in the game lets you reroll or replace the d20. You can reroll or replace only one die, not both. You choose which one.

Say you have Heroic Inspiration and roll a 3 and an 18 on an ability check that has Advantage or Disadvantage. You could spend your Heroic Inspiration to reroll one of those dice, not both of them.

## Proficiency

Characters and monsters excel at different pursuits. Some wield many weapons skillfully, while others handle only a few. Some read people's motives well, and others unlock the secrets of the multiverse. All creatures have a Proficiency Bonus, which reflects the impact that training has on a creature's abilities. A character's Proficiency Bonus increases as the character gains levels, and a monster's Challenge Rating sets its Proficiency Bonus.

A creature applies this bonus to a D20 Test whenever it has proficiency in the relevant skill, saving throw, or item. It also applies to spell attacks and to a spell's save DC.

| Level or CR | Bonus |
|-------------|-------|
| Up to 4     | +2    |
| 5-8         | +3    |
| 9-12        | +4    |
| 13-16       | +5    |
| 17-20       | +6    |
| 21-24       | +7    |
| 25-28       | +8    |
| 29-30       | +9    |

### The Bonus Doesn't Stack

You can't add your Proficiency Bonus to a die roll or another number more than once. Say a rule lets you make a Charisma (Deception or Persuasion) check. You add your Proficiency Bonus if you're proficient in either skill. You don't add it twice, even if you're proficient in both.

Occasionally, a rule multiplies or divides a Proficiency Bonus, doubling or halving it, before adding it in. The Expertise feature, say, doubles the Proficiency Bonus for some ability checks. Whenever a rule uses the bonus this way, it can multiply the bonus only once and divide it only once.

### Skill Proficiencies

Most ability checks involve using a skill. A skill represents a category of things creatures try to do with an ability check. The GM has the final say on whether a skill is relevant in a situation.

If a creature is proficient in a skill, it applies its Proficiency Bonus to ability checks that use that skill. Without proficiency in a skill, a creature can still make ability checks with that skill. It just doesn't add its Proficiency Bonus. Say a character tries to climb a cliff, and the GM asks for a Strength (Athletics) check. If the character has Athletics proficiency, they add their Proficiency Bonus to the check. If not, they make the check without it.

| Skill           | Ability      | Example Uses                                                              |
|------------------|--------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Acrobatics      | Dexterity    | Stay on your feet in a tricky situation, or perform an acrobatic stunt.   |
| Animal Handling | Wisdom       | Calm or train an animal, or get an animal to behave in a certain way.     |
| Arcana          | Intelligence | Recall lore about spells, magic items, and the planes of existence.       |
| Athletics       | Strength     | [[jump\|Jump]] farther than normal, stay afloat in rough water, or break something. |
| Deception       | Charisma     | Tell a convincing lie, or wear a disguise convincingly.                   |
| History         | Intelligence | Recall lore about historical events, people, nations, and cultures.       |
| Insight         | Wisdom       | Discern a person's mood and intentions.                                   |
| Intimidation    | Charisma     | Awe or threaten someone into doing what you want.                         |
| Investigation   | Intelligence | Find obscure information in books, or deduce how something works.         |
| Medicine        | Wisdom       | Diagnose an illness, or determine what killed the recently slain.         |
| Nature          | Intelligence | Recall lore about terrain, plants, animals, and weather.                  |
| Perception      | Wisdom       | Using a combination of senses, notice something that's easy to miss.      |
| Performance     | Charisma     | Act, tell a story, perform music, or dance.                               |
| Persuasion      | Charisma     | Graciously convince someone of something.                    |
| Religion        | Intelligence | Recall lore about gods, religious rituals, and holy symbols.              |
| Sleight of Hand | Dexterity    | Pick a pocket, conceal a handheld object, or perform legerdemain.         |
| Stealth         | Dexterity    | Escape notice by moving quietly and hiding behind things.                 |
| Survival        | Wisdom       | Follow tracks, forage, find a trail, or avoid natural hazards.            |

Character creation sets a character's starting skill proficiencies, and a monster's skill proficiencies appear in its stat block.

### Saving Throw Proficiencies

Proficiency in a saving throw lets a character add their Proficiency Bonus to saves that use a specific ability. Proficiency in Wisdom saves, say, lets you add your Proficiency Bonus to your Wisdom saves. Some monsters also have saving throw proficiencies, as noted in their stat blocks.

Each class gives proficiency in at least two saving throws. This proficiency represents that class's training in evading or resisting threats. Wizards, say, are proficient in Intelligence and Wisdom saves, training to resist mental assault.

### Equipment Proficiencies

A character gains proficiency with different weapons and tools from their class and background. Equipment proficiency covers weapons and tools:

**Weapons.** Anyone can wield a weapon. Proficiency makes you better at wielding it. If you have proficiency with a weapon, you add your Proficiency Bonus to attack rolls you make with it.

**Tools.** If you have proficiency with a tool, you can add your Proficiency Bonus to any ability check that uses the tool. If you also have proficiency in the skill used with that check, you have Advantage on the check too.
