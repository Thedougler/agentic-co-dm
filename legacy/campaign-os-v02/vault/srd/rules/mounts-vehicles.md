# Mounts and Vehicles (5e SRD)

A mount can help a character move more quickly through the wilderness, but its primary purpose is to carry gear that would otherwise slow them down. The Mounts and Other Animals table shows each animal's carrying capacity. See "Monsters" for the animals' stat blocks (not contained in this source document).

## Mounts and Cargo

An animal pulling a carriage, cart, chariot, sled, or wagon can move weight up to five times its base carrying capacity, including the vehicle's weight. A vehicle pulled by two or more animals has a combined carrying capacity equal to the sum of all the animals' capacities.

## Barding

Barding is armor designed for a mount. Any armor on the Armor table (see the main Equipment page) functions as barding. The cost is four times the normal cost, and it weighs twice as much.

## Saddles

A saddle comes with a bit, a bridle, reins, and any other equipment needed to use it. Military Saddles grant Advantage on any ability check made to remain mounted. Exotic Saddles let you ride aquatic or flying mounts.

## Large Vehicles

The Airborne and Waterborne Vehicles table below provides statistics for large vehicles. The following notes apply.

### Speed

A ship sailing against a strong wind moves at half speed. In a dead calm (no wind), waterborne ships can't move under sail; oarsmen must row them instead. Keelboats and Rowboats work on lakes and rivers. If traveling downstream, the current (typically 3 miles per hour) increases the vehicle's speed. You can't row these vehicles against any strong current, but draft animals on the shores can pull them upstream. A Rowboat weighs 100 pounds and two people can carry one.

### Crew

A ship larger than a Keelboat or Rowboat needs a crew of skilled hirelings (see "Hirelings" on the main Equipment page) to function. The crew size depends on the ship, as shown in the table above.

### Passengers

The table lists the number of Small and Medium passengers the ship can accommodate using hammocks. A ship outfitted with private accommodations can carry one-fifth as many passengers. A passenger pays 5 SP per day for a hammock, but prices can vary from ship to ship. A small private cabin costs 2 GP per day.

### Damage Threshold

If a vehicle has a damage threshold (see "Rules Glossary", not contained in this source document), it's noted in the table above.

### Ship Repair

You can repair a damaged ship while in port. Repairing 1 Hit Point of damage requires 1 day and costs 20 GP for materials and labor. In locations with abundant supplies and skilled labor, such as city shipyards, the repair time and cost drop by half.
