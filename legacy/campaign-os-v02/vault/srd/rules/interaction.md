# Playing the Game (5e SRD): Actions and Interaction

Part of [[playing-the-game|Playing the Game (5e SRD)]].

## Actions

When you do something other than moving or communicating, you typically take an action.

| Action    | Summary |
|-----------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Attack    | Attack with a weapon or an Unarmed Strike.                                        |
| Dash      | Move again up to your Speed.                                                       |
| Disengage | Your movement doesn't provoke Opportunity Attacks for the rest of the turn.       |
| Dodge     | Attack rolls against you have Disadvantage. [[dexterity\|Dex]] saves use Advantage. Ends if you fall or your Speed is 0. |
| Help      | Help another creature's ability check or attack roll, or administer first aid.    |
| Hide      | Make a Dexterity (Stealth) check.                                                 |
| Influence | Make a [[charisma\|Charisma]] or [[wisdom\|Wisdom]] check to sway attitudes.         |
| Magic     | Cast a spell, use a magic item, or use a magical feature.                         |
| Ready     | Prepare to take an action in response to a trigger you define.                    |
| Search    | Make a Wisdom (Insight, Medicine, Perception, or Survival) check.                 |
| Study     | Make an [[intelligence\|Intelligence]] check to recall facts.                      |
| Use       | Use a non-magical object.                                                         |

Beyond these, class features and abilities offer more. For unlisted actions, the GM decides if it works and calls for a D20 Test.

### One Thing at a Time

The game uses actions to govern how much you can do at one time. You can take only one action at a time. This principle is most important in combat.

Actions arise in other situations too. In social interactions, try Influencing a target or reading body language. You cannot do both in the same turn.

### Bonus Actions

Some abilities grant a Bonus Action. Use it only when an ability says you can. You get at most one Bonus Action per turn. If you have more, pick one.

### Reactions

A Reaction is a fast action in response to an event. It can happen on your turn or another's. Opportunity Attack is the most common one. After you use a Reaction, wait until your next turn to use another.

## Social Interaction

During adventures, you meet creatures willing to talk. You might convince a burglar to confess or flatter a guard. The GM controls these non-player characters.

An NPC's attitude is Friendly, Indifferent, or Hostile (defined in the Rules Glossary). Friendly NPCs help. Hostile ones hinder.

Social interactions progress in two ways: through role-playing and ability checks.

### Role-playing

Role-playing means deciding how your character thinks, acts, and talks. You do this throughout the game, especially in social interactions.

Choose an active or descriptive role-playing style.

The GM shapes NPC reactions from their personality and your actions. A cowardly bandit crumbles under threats of jail, while a stubborn merchant resists badgering and a vain dragon craves flattery.

Listen to how the GM describes each NPC. Their goals tell you how to reach them.

Offer what NPCs want or appeal to their fears and goals. This builds trust and gains information. Insulting a proud warrior or scorning a noble's allies will likely fail.

### Ability Checks in Social Interaction

Ability checks shape social outcomes. Your role-playing can shift an NPC's attitude. The GM may roll to introduce chance, calling for the Influence action.

Use skills your party is good at. A [[rogue|Rogue]] with Deception should handle a tricky guard.

## Exploration

Exploration means entering dangerous, mysterious places.

### Adventuring Equipment

Equipment serves many purposes. A [[ladder|Ladder]] reaches high places, [[torch|Torches]] and light reveal hidden details, [[thieves-tools|Thieves' Tools]] open locked containers, and [[caltrops|Caltrops]] slow pursuers. Weapons serve beyond combat, such as using a [[quarterstaff|Quarterstaff]] to trigger a button you fear touching.

### Vision and Light

Sight affects detection, accuracy, and spellcasting. Vision-obscuring effects hinder you.

#### Obscured Areas

Areas can be Lightly or Heavily Obscured. In Lightly Obscured areas (Dim Light, fog, foliage), Wisdom (Perception) checks have Disadvantage. In Heavily Obscured areas (Darkness, heavy fog, thick foliage), you gain the [[blinded|Blinded]] condition.

#### Light

Light comes in three categories.

**Bright Light.** Creatures see normally. Gloomy days, torches, lanterns, and fires provide it.

**Dim Light.** This is shadow. It lies between Bright Light and Darkness. Twilight, dawn, and moonlight are Dim Light.

**Darkness.** This creates a Heavily Obscured area. Face it at night outdoors (even moonlit), in an unlit dungeon, or in magical Darkness.

#### Special Senses

Some creatures sense the world differently. These include Blindsight, Darkvision, Tremorsense, and Truesight (see elsewhere).

### Hiding

Adventurers hide to spy, slip past guardians, or ambush. The GM decides when hiding is viable. To hide, take the Hide action.

### Interacting with Objects

Object interaction is straightforward. Describe your action and the GM tells you the result. Sometimes rules govern what's possible.

#### What Is an Object?

Objects are things: windows, doors, swords, books, tables, chairs, or stones. Buildings and vehicles are not objects (they're made of many objects).

#### Time-Limited Object Interactions

When time is short (such as in combat), a creature gets one free interaction per turn. That interaction must occur during its movement or action. Extra interactions require the Use action.

#### Finding Hidden Objects

To find hidden things, the GM may ask for a Wisdom (Perception) check. Success reveals the object. Searching far away yields nothing.

#### Carrying Objects

Gear and treasure carry without weight concern. Hauling heavy loads or many items can trigger carrying capacity rules.

#### Breaking Objects

You can break fragile items like glass or paper. Breaking sturdy objects follows separate rules (not listed here).

#### Marching Order

Set marching order when traveling. It shows who hits traps, who spots foes, and who reaches combat first. Change it outside battle as you wish.

### Hazards

Monsters are the primary peril, but others await: burning, dehydration, falling, malnutrition, and suffocation (defined elsewhere).

### Travel

Travel covers long distances over hours or days. The GM may skip travel details or use the pace rules below. For combat speed, see [[combat|combat rules]].

#### Travel Pace

Travel at a Fast, Normal, or Slow pace. On a mount, go twice as far for 1 hour. Then rest before going fast again.

| Pace   | Minute   | Hour    | Day      |
|--------|----------|---------|----------|
| Fast   | 400 feet | 4 miles | 30 miles |
| Normal | 300 feet | 3 miles | 24 miles |
| Slow   | 200 feet | 2 miles | 18 miles |

Each travel pace has a game effect:

**Fast.** Traveling at a Fast pace imposes Disadvantage on a traveler's Wisdom (Perception or Survival) and Dexterity (Stealth) checks.

**Normal.** Stealth checks suffer Disadvantage.

**Slow.** Perception and Survival checks gain Advantage.

#### Vehicles

Travelers in wagons, carriages, or land vehicles choose a pace. Those in a waterborne vessel move at its speed and cannot choose a travel pace. Depending on the vessel and crew, ships travel up to 24 hours per day.
