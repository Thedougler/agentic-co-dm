# Equipment (5e SRD)

## Coins

Characters often find coins on their adventures and can spend those coins in shops, inns, and other businesses. Coins come in different denominations based on the relative worth of their material. The Coin Values table lists coins and how much they're worth relative to the Gold Piece, which is the game's main coin. One hundred Copper Pieces equal 1 Gold Piece.

A coin weighs about a third of an ounce, so fifty coins weigh a pound.

### Coin Values

| Coin | Value in GP |
|------|-------------|
| Copper Piece (CP) | 1/100 |
| Silver Piece (SP) | 1/10 |
| Electrum Piece (EP) | 1/2 |
| Gold Piece (GP) | 1 |
| Platinum Piece (PP) | 10 |

## Armor

The Armor table lists the game's main armor with cost, weight, and these details. Magic armor includes [[any-armor|bonus armor]] and other specially enchanted protective gear.

- **Category.** Every armor falls into a category: light, medium, or heavy. The category determines how long it takes to don or doff the armor (shown in the tables below).
- **Armor Class (AC).** The table's Armor Class column gives the base AC when wearing armor. Wearing Leather Armor gives a base AC of 11 plus the [[dexterity|Dexterity]] modifier, whereas Chain Mail gives an AC of 16.
- **Strength.** A listed [[strength|Strength]] score means armor reduces your speed by 10 feet unless you meet that score.
- **Stealth.** A "Disadvantage" entry in the Stealth column means the wearer has Disadvantage on Dexterity (Stealth) checks.

### Armor Training

Anyone can don armor or hold a Shield, but only those with training use them effectively (see below). Your class and other features determine what armor you're trained with. A monster's stat block lists any armor it's trained with.

**Light, Medium, or Heavy Armor.** Wearing Light, Medium, or Heavy armor without training with it gives Disadvantage on any D20 Test that involves Strength or Dexterity, and prevents casting spells.

**Shield.** The Armor Class benefit of a Shield applies only with training with it.

### One at a Time

A creature can wear only one suit of armor at a time and wield only one Shield at a time. Beyond armor and shields, adventurers equip themselves with [[clothes-fine|fine clothes]] for formal occasions and [[clothes-travelers|traveler's clothes]] for travel.

## Lifestyle Expenses

Lifestyle expenses summarize the cost of living in a fantasy world, covering lodging, food, equipment maintenance, and other necessities.

At the start of each week or month (your choice), you choose a lifestyle: wretched, squalid, poor, modest, comfortable, wealthy, or aristocratic. Pay its price to sustain that lifestyle.

Lifestyles have no built-in consequences, though your GM might use them to set risks or reactions.

**Wretched (Free).** Survive by luck and handouts. You face natural dangers from sleeping outside.

**Squalid (1 SP per Day).** You spend barely enough for necessities. You risk unhealthy conditions and opportunistic criminals.

**Poor (2 SP per Day).** Frugal spending for necessities.

**Modest (1 GP per Day).** Self-support at an average level.

**Comfortable (2 GP per Day).** Modest spending on necessities plus a few luxuries.

**Wealthy (4 GP per Day).** Accustomed to the finer things in life, might have servants.

**Aristocratic (10 GP per Day).** Payment for the best, might have a staff supporting the lifestyle. Others notice the wealth and might encourage sharing it, either legally or otherwise.

## Hirelings

Skilled hirelings are anyone you hire for a service requiring training (in a weapon, tool, or skill). Examples include a mercenary, an artisan, or a scribe. The Hirelings table shows base pay. Some expert workers cost more. Untrained hirelings handle work requiring no training. They include laborers and porters.

### Hireling Rates

| Service | Cost |
|---------|------|
| Skilled hireling | 2 GP per day |
| Untrained hireling | 2 SP per day |
| Messenger | 2 CP per mile |

## Magic Items

Adventures offer the chance (but not a guarantee) of Finding magic items. Hundreds of magic items appear in [[magic-items|Magic Items]]. Common types include [[potion-of-healing|Potions of Healing]], [[potion-of-giant-strength|Potions of Giant Strength]], protective items like the [[amulet-of-health|Amulet of Health]] and [[armor-of-resistance|Armor of Resistance]]. Here's what a character needs to know about using magic items.

### Identifying a Magic Item

Some magic items are indistinguishable from their nonmagical counterparts, while others are conspicuously magical. Handling a magic item is enough to sense its extraordinary nature, but you don't automatically learn its properties.

The *Identify* spell reveals an item's properties fastest. Or focus on one magic item during a Short Rest while holding it. At the rest's end, you learn its properties and how to use them (but not any curse it might carry).

Sometimes a magic item carries a clue to its properties. A ring might have a command word etched inside the band, or a feathered design might hint that it's a *[[ring-of-feather-falling|Ring of Feather Falling]]*.

Wearing or experimenting with an item can offer hints about its properties. Potions require only a little taste to reveal what they do. Other items might need more experimentation: if a character puts on a *[[ring-of-swimming|Ring of Swimming]]*, the GM might say, "Your movement feels strangely fluid." This prompts the player to dive into a river to test the item, and the GM confirms the character swims unexpectedly well.

### Attunement

Some magic items need attunement. You must form a bond with the item to use its magical powers. Without attunement, you gain only nonmagical benefits. A magic Shield without attunement works as a normal Shield but has no magical powers.

**Attune during a Short Rest.** To attune to an item, you must spend a Short Rest focused on only that item while holding it (this can't be the same Short Rest used to learn the item's properties). The nature of your focus depends on the item. Use weapon practice for weapons, meditation for wands, or find another suitable approach. Any interruption to the Short Rest fails the attempt. At the rest's end, you attune to the magic item and gain full access to its magical capabilities.

**No More Than Three Items.** You can attune to no more than three magic items at a time. You can't attune to a fourth item until you end an attunement first. You also can't attune to more than one copy of an item: you can't attune to more than one *[[ring-of-protection|Ring of Protection]]* at a time.

**Ending Attunement.** Attunement ends if you no longer meet the attunement prerequisites or if the item moves more than 100 feet away for at least 24 hours. You also lose attunement if you die or if another creature attunes to the item. You can also end an attunement voluntarily by spending another Short Rest focused on the item, unless the item carries a curse.

### Wearing and Wielding Items

Using a magic item's properties often requires wearing or wielding it properly. Boots and gloves go on your feet and hands, headwear on your head, rings on your fingers, armor on your body, shields on your arm, and cloaks about your shoulders. Hold weapons in your hands.

Most magic items that you wear fit you regardless of size or build. Magic garments adjust easily, or they adapt themselves magically to fit you.

**Stacking Limits.** You can't wear more than one of each magic item type at once. You're limited to one pair of footwear, one pair of gloves or gauntlets, one pair of bracers, one suit of armor, one headwear item, and one cloak. Your GM can make exceptions.

**Paired Items.** Items sold in pairs (boots, bracers, gauntlets, gloves) grant benefits only if you wear both. If you wear a *Boot of Striding and Springing* on one foot and a *Boot of Elvenkind* on the other, neither works.

## Crafting Nonmagical Items

To craft a nonmagical item, gather tools, raw materials, and adequate time. See details below for each need. Once you have everything, you make the item and can use or sell it at its normal price.

**Tools.** The "Tools" section (see [[tools|Tools (5e SRD)]]) shows which tools you need for crafts. The GM assigns required tools for items not listed. You must use the required tool with skill. Anyone helping needs the same skill.

Making an item requires raw materials worth half its cost (round down). If a Plate Armor sells for 1,500 GP, you need 750 GP of raw materials. The GM determines whether appropriate raw materials are available. Basic equipment like [[spikes-iron|Iron Spikes]] can be crafted relatively quickly with the right tools and materials.

**Time.** To determine how many days (working 8 hours a day) it takes to make an item, divide its cost in GP by 10 (round a fraction up to a day). Making a [[heavy-crossbow|Heavy Crossbow]] (which sells for 50 GP) takes 5 days. The days don't need to be consecutive. Characters can combine their efforts to shorten the crafting time: divide the time needed by the number of characters working on it. Normally only one other character can assist, but the GM might allow more.

## Brewing Potions of Healing

A character skilled with the [[herbalism-kit|Herbalism Kit]] can create a *[[potion-of-healing-common|Potion of Healing]]*. This requires using the kit and spending 25 GP of raw material over 1 day (8 hours of work).

## Scribing Spell Scrolls

A spellcaster can transfer a spell to a scroll and create a *[[spell-scroll|Spell Scroll]]*, using the rules below.

### Time and Cost

Scribing a scroll takes time and money based on the spell's level, as shown in the Spell Scroll Costs table below. Each day of inscription requires 8 hours of work. Days of scribing needn't be consecutive.

### Prerequisites for the Scribe

To scribe a scroll, you need skill in Arcana or [[calligraphers-supplies|Calligrapher's Supplies]]. You must prepare the spell on each day you work. Gather any Material components the spell needs. Consume them only when you finish the scroll. The scroll's spell uses your spell save DC and spell attack bonus.

### Cantrips

If the scribed spell is a cantrip, the version on the scroll works as if the caster were the scribe's level.
