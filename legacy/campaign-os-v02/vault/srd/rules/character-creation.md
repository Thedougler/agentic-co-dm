# Character Creation (5e SRD)

## Choose a Character Sheet

A character sheet records a character's details. Use a physical format (printed or hand-written) or a digital one.

## Create Your Character

Steps to make a character:

1. **Choose a Class.** Every adventurer belongs to a class. A class describes a character's vocation and favored tactics.
2. **Determine Origin.** Your origin has two parts: background and species. You also choose your character's languages.
3. **Determine Ability Scores.** A character's six abilities shape much of what that character can do.
4. **Choose an Alignment.** Alignment is a shorthand for a character's moral compass.
5. **Fill in Details.** Using the choices made, fill in the remaining details on the character sheet.

Choose a class and write it on the character sheet. See Classes Overview for the full list of classes.

#### Write Your Level

Write the character's level on the character sheet. A character starts at level 1 by default. Levels rise through adventuring and gaining Experience Points (XP).

**Write Your XP.** Record Experience Points. A level 1 character has 0 XP.

**Starting at a Higher Level.** A GM might start a character at a higher level. If starting at level 3 or higher, write the chosen subclass on the character sheet (see "Starting at Higher Levels" below).

#### Note Armor Training

A class might give armor training. Note it on the character sheet. It lets the character wear that armor well and gain its defense.

### Step 2: Character Origin

Your origin means choosing a background, a species, and two languages. Your background is the place and job that shaped the character most.

#### Choose a Background

Choose a background and write it on the character sheet. This choice shapes Step 3 (ability scores).

#### Table: Ability Scores and Backgrounds

| Ability      | Background |
|--------------|------------|
| [[strength]]     | [[soldier\|Soldier]]    |
| [[dexterity]]    | Soldier    |
| [[constitution\|Constitution]] | Soldier    |
| Intelligence | [[acolyte\|Acolyte]]    |
| Wisdom       | Acolyte    |
| Charisma     | Acolyte    |

**Record Your Feat.** A background grants a feat. Write the feat on the character sheet.

**Note Proficiencies.** A background gives proficiency in two skills and one tool, and a class gives proficiencies too, so record them on the character sheet. The class's features table shows the Proficiency Bonus, which is +2 at level 1.

#### Choose Starting Equipment

Background and class both give starting equipment. Spend any coins from this step on gear right away. Record your equipment and any coins left over.

#### Choose a Species

Choose a species: [[dragonborn|Dragonborn]], [[dwarf|Dwarf]], Elf, [[gnome|Gnome]], [[goliath|Goliath]], [[halfling|Halfling]], [[human|Human]], Orc, or [[tiefling|Tiefling]]. Write the species and its traits on the character sheet. Record your Size and Speed (both depend on species). Note Size as its first letter only.

#### Imagine Your Past and Present

Let background and species inspire the character's past. Consider who raised you, your dearest childhood friend, and any pet from your childhood. Did you have a past love? Did you join a guild or religion? What part of your past drives you to adventure now?

#### Choose Languages

A character knows at least three languages: common plus two rolled or chosen from the Standard Languages table. Knowing a language means the character can communicate in, read, and write it. Class and other features might grant more languages.

### Step 3: Ability Scores

Generate a set of six numbers, then assign them to the six abilities.

#### Generate Your Scores

Use one of three methods:

**Standard Array.** Use the six scores: 15, 14, 13, 12, 10, 8.

**Random Generation.** Roll four d6s and record the total of the highest three dice. Do this five more times, for six numbers total.

**Point Cost.** 27 points to spend on ability scores, per the Ability Score Point Costs table. Example: a score of 14 costs 7 of the 27 points.

#### Table: Ability Score Point Costs

| 11    | 3    | 15    | 9    |

#### Adjust Ability Scores

After assigning scores, adjust per background: the background lists three abilities. Increase one of those scores by 2 and a different one by 1, OR increase all three by 1. No increase can raise a score above 20.

#### Determine Ability Modifiers

Determine ability modifiers using the table below and write the modifier next to each score.

|-------|----------|-------|----------|
| 3     | −4       | 12-13 | +1       |
| 4-5   | −3       | 14-15 | +2       |
| 6-7   | −2       | 16-17 | +3       |
| 8-9   | −1       | 18-19 | +4       |

### Step 4: Alignment

Choose an alignment and note it on the character sheet. The game assumes player characters aren't of an evil alignment. Check with the GM before making an evil character.

#### Alignments

Alignment mixes morality (good, evil, or neutral) with a stance on order (lawful, chaotic, or neutral). See [[alignment|Alignment]] for the nine alignments and unaligned creatures.

### Step 5: Character Creation Details

Fill in the rest of the character sheet.

#### Record Class Features

Write down the level 1 features from the class's feature table. Some class features offer choices. Read all features and make any offered choices.

#### Fill In Numbers

**Saving Throws.** For saving throws with proficiency, add the Proficiency Bonus to the relevant ability modifier and note the total. Non-proficient saving throw modifiers equal just the relevant ability modifier.

**Skills.** For skills with proficiency, add the Proficiency Bonus to the associated ability modifier and note the total. Non-proficient skill modifiers equal just the relevant ability modifier.

**[[passive-perception|Passive Perception]].** Note this score, including all modifiers that apply to Wisdom (Perception) checks.

**Hit Points.** Your class and Constitution modifier set your Hit Point cap at level 1.

#### Table: Level 1 Hit Points by Class

| Class                                         | Hit Points  |
|------------------------------------------------|--------------------|
| Barbarian                                       | 12 + Con. modifier |
| Fighter, Paladin, or Ranger                     | 10 + Con. modifier |
| Bard, Cleric, Druid, Monk, Rogue, or Warlock    | 8 + Con. modifier  |
| Sorcerer or Wizard                              | 6 + Con. modifier  |

The character sheet has space for current Hit Points, temporary Hit Points, and Death Saves.

**Hit Point Dice.** Your class lists your Hit Die type. At level 1, you have 1 Hit Die. During a Short Rest, spend them to heal and track which you used.

**Initiative.** Write the Dexterity modifier in the Initiative space.

**Armor Class.** Without armor or a shield, base Armor Class is 10 + Dexterity modifier. If starting equipment includes armor or a Shield (or both), calculate AC using the "Equipment" rules. A class feature might give a different way to calculate AC.

**Attacks.** Write your starting weapons in the Weapons & Damage Cantrips section. For proficient weapons, add your ability modifier and Proficiency Bonus to attacks. Use the same modifier on damage.

**Spellcasting.** For spells, calculate your saving throw DC and spell attack bonus. Your spellcasting ability modifier comes from the feature that granted you spell access.

**Spell Slots, Cantrips, and Prepared Spells.** Check your class features table for spell slots, available cantrips, and prepared spells. Note your choices.

### Gaining a Level

When gaining a level, follow these steps:

1. **Choose a Class.** Most characters advance in the same class. A character can instead gain a level in another class using "Multiclassing."
2. **Adjust Hit Points and Hit Point Dice.** Gain one Hit Die. Roll it with your Constitution modifier (at least 1), then add to your HP. Or use the fixed value below for your class.

   Fixed Hit Points by Class table:

   | Class                                          | Hit Points per Level |
   |-------------------------------------------------|----------------------|
   | Barbarian                                        | 7 + Con. modifier    |
   | Fighter, Paladin, or Ranger                      | 6 + Con. modifier    |
   | Bard, Cleric, Druid, Monk, Rogue, or Warlock     | 5 + Con. modifier    |
   | Sorcerer or Wizard                               | 4 + Con. modifier    |

3. **Record New Class Features.** Note the features gained at the new level in the class features table. Make any choices offered by a new feature.
4. **Adjust Proficiency Bonus.** Proficiency Bonus increases at certain levels per the Character Advancement table and the class features table. When it increases, increase all numbers on the character sheet that include the Proficiency Bonus.
5. **Adjust Ability Modifiers.** When a feat raises a score to an even number, update that modifier everywhere. If Constitution rises, each level grants 1 bonus HP. Example: 17→18 Constitution at level 8 gains +1 modifier, then +8 HP total (1 each level) plus the level's normal HP.

### Tiers of Play

Gameplay shifts across four distinct tiers from level 1 to 20. Tiers set no rules but mark how play changes.

| Tier | Levels | What Changes |
|------|--------|--------------|
| Tier 1 | 1-4   | You're an apprentice with rare gifts. Learn your class basics and choose a subclass. Threats span farms, villages. |
| Tier 2 | 5-10  | You're a seasoned adventurer. Spellcasters gain major spells like *[[fireball\|Fireball]]* and *[[raise-dead\|Raise Dead]]*. Martial heroes strike 2-3 times per round. Threats now menace cities, kingdoms. |
| Tier 3 | 11-16 | You grow powerful. Spellcasters wield reality-warping magic. Others gain extra attacks and dramatic effects. Threats imperil regions. |
| Tier 4 | 17-20 | You reach the height of your class, a true legend. Worlds or the multiverse itself rests on your decisions. |

## Starting at Higher Levels

The GM can begin you above level 1. Level 3 is ideal if your group has played before.

### Creating Your Character

Use the standard steps here, then apply Level Advancement rules. Begin with the base XP for your level. Example: starting at level 10 means 64,000 XP.

**Bonus Feats at Level 20.** After level 20, gain one feat per 30,000 XP above 355,000. Epic Boon feats fit well, though any qualifying feat works.

### Starting Equipment

Your GM decides if you get more gear, including magic items. The table below is a guide. Check what gear costs too much now. Firearms start too pricey but become available later.

#### Table: Starting Equipment at Higher Levels

| Starting Level | Equipment and Money                                            | Magic Items                               |
|----------------|------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| 2-4            | Normal starting equipment                                        | 1 Common                                   |
| 5-10           | 500 GP plus 1d10 × 25 GP plus normal starting equipment          | 1 Common, 1 Uncommon                       |
| 11-16          | 5,000 gp plus 1d10 × 250 GP plus normal starting equipment       | 2 Common, 3 Uncommon, 1 Rare               |
| 17-20          | 20,000 GP plus 1d10 × 250 GP plus normal starting equipment      | 2 Common, 4 Uncommon, 3 Rare, 1 Very Rare  |
