# Multiclassing (5e SRD Character Creation)

Multiclassing lets you gain levels in many classes. Choose a new class when you level up instead of your current class. Combine class abilities into a unique character concept.

## Prerequisites

A new class needs a score of at least 13 in the primary ability for both that class and your current class. Example: a [[barbarian|Barbarian]] adding [[druid|Druid]] needs [[strength|Strength]] 13+ and [[wisdom|Wisdom]] 13+.

## Experience Points

Level costs use your total character level, not your level in one class. Example: a level 6 [[cleric|Cleric]] / level 1 [[fighter|Fighter]] levels up to 7 together. You can then add a Fighter or Cleric level.

## Hit Points and Hit Point Dice

Gain Hit Points from a new class as described for levels after 1. Only the first class grants level 1 Hit Points.

Pool your Hit Dice from all classes together. Dice of the same type combine (Fighter and [[paladin|Paladin]] both have d10, so a level 5 Fighter/level 5 Paladin has ten d10). Track other types apart (a level 5 Cleric/level 5 Paladin has five d8 and five d10).

## Bonus

Use your total character level for your Bonus, not your level in one class. Example: a level 3 Fighter / level 2 [[rogue|Rogue]] has the Bonus of a level 5 character, +3.

## New Class Proficiencies

When you first join a new class, you gain some (not all) of its starting proficiencies. See each class's description for details.

## Class Features

Each level grants that class's features for its level. Some features have multiclassing exceptions. See each class's entry. Extra Attack, Spellcasting, and Armor Class features have rules below.

### Armor Class

When you can calculate [[armor-class|Armor Class]] in different ways, choose one and apply it consistently. Example: a [[monk|Monk]]/[[sorcerer|Sorcerer]] with both Unarmored Defense and Draconic Resilience selects one AC calculation method.

### Extra Attack

Extra Attack from many classes doesn't stack. You get at most two attacks with this feature. Only another feature that grants more attacks lets you exceed this. The [[warlock|Warlock]]'s Thirsting Blade (which grants Extra Attack) also doesn't let you exceed two attacks.

### Spellcasting

Spellcasting capacity mixes combined levels and individual levels. These rules apply once you have Spellcasting from two or more classes. If you have it from only one class, follow that class's rules.

**Spells Prepared.** Determine prepared spells per class individually, as if you were single-classed in that class. Example: a level 4 [[ranger|Ranger]]/level 3 Sorcerer can prepare five level 1 Ranger spells plus six Sorcerer spells of level 1 or 2 (and four Sorcerer cantrips). Each prepared spell links to one class, and you use that class's spellcasting ability to cast it.

**Cantrips.** If a cantrip scales at higher levels, use your total character level, not your level in a single class, unless the spell says otherwise.

**Spell Slots.** Total your levels like this:

- All levels from [[bard|Bard]], Cleric, Druid, Sorcerer, and [[wizard|Wizard]]
- Half your levels (round up) in Paladin and Ranger

Look up this total in the Level column of the Multiclass Spellcaster table; spell slots at that level cast spells of the right level from any class you hold the Spellcasting feature in. The table may grant you slots higher than the spells you prepared. You can use those high-level slots to cast lower-level prepared spells, gaining any effects they gain from a higher slot level (like *[[burning-hands|Burning Hands]]*'s extra damage).

Example: a level 4 Ranger/level 3 Sorcerer is a level 5 caster for spell slots (all Sorcerer levels plus half Ranger levels): four level 1 slots, three level 2 slots, and two level 3 slots per the table. You can't prepare level 3 spells or level 2 Ranger spells, but you can use the level 3 slots to cast your lower-level prepared spells with enhanced effects if they allow it.

**Pact Magic.** If you have both the Warlock's Pact Magic feature and a Spellcasting feature, your Pact Magic slots cast spells prepared from Spellcasting classes, and your Spellcasting slots cast prepared Warlock spells.
