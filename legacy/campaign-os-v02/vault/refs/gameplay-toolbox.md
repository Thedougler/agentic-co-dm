# Gameplay Toolbox

## Travel Pace

A group of characters can travel overland at a Normal, Fast, or Slow pace. During any journey stage, the predominant terrain determines the characters' maximum travel pace, per the Maximum Pace column of the Travel Terrain table below. Certain factors can affect a group's travel pace.

**Good Roads.** A good road increases the group's maximum pace by one step (Slow → Normal, or Normal → Fast).

**Slower Travelers.** The group must move at a Slow pace if any group member's Speed is reduced to half or less of normal.

> [!mechanic]
> **Extended Travel** — Characters can push themselves to travel more than 8 hours per day, at risk of tiring. At the end of each additional hour of travel beyond 8 hours, each character must succeed on a [[constitution|Constitution]] saving throw or gain 1 [[exhaustion|Exhaustion]] level. The DC is 10 plus 1 for each hour past 8 hours (so hour 9 = DC 11, hour 10 = DC 12, etc.).

**Special Movement.** If a party can travel at a high Speed for an extended time (e.g., via a spell like *[[wind-walk|Wind Walk]]* or a magic item like a *[[carpet-of-flying|Carpet of Flying]]*), translate Speed into travel rates as follows:

- Miles per hour = Speed ÷ 10
- Miles per day (Normal pace) = Miles per hour × number of hours traveled (typically 8)
- Fast pace = Miles per day × 1⅓ (round down)
- Slow pace = Miles per day × 2/3 (round down)

If the characters are flying, or their special movement lets them ignore Difficult Terrain, they can move at a Fast pace regardless of terrain.

**Vehicles.** Characters traveling in a vehicle use the vehicle's speed in miles per hour to determine travel rate, and don't choose a travel pace.

### Travel Terrain table

| Terrain | Maximum Pace | Encounter Distance | Foraging DC | Navigation DC | Search DC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Arctic | Fast* | 6d6 × 10 feet | 20 | 10 | 10 |
| Coastal | Normal | 2d10 × 10 feet | 10 | 5 | 15 |
| Desert | Normal | 6d6 × 10 feet | 20 | 10 | 10 |
| Forest | Normal | 2d8 × 10 feet | 10 | 15 | 15 |
| Grassland | Fast | 6d6 × 10 feet | 15 | 5 | 15 |
| Hill | Normal | 2d10 × 10 feet | 15 | 10 | 15 |
| Mountain | Slow | 4d10 × 10 feet | 20 | 15 | 20 |
| Swamp | Slow | 2d8 × 10 feet | 10 | 15 | 20 |
| Underdark | Normal | 2d6 × 10 feet | 20 | 10 | 20 |
| Urban | Normal | 2d6 × 10 feet | 20 | 15 | 15 |
| Waterborne | Special† | 6d6 × 10 feet | 15 | 10 | 15 |

\* Appropriate equipment (such as skis) is necessary to keep up a Fast pace in Arctic terrain.
† Waterborne travel rate depends on the vehicle carrying the characters (see Vehicles above).

## Creating a Background

A character's background represents what the character did prior to becoming an adventurer. Creating a unique background, or customizing an existing one, can reflect a campaign's theme or world elements, or help a player craft a character's backstory. Steps to build a background:

**1. Choose Abilities.** Choose three abilities appropriate for the background:

- [[strength|Strength]] or [[dexterity|Dexterity]] — ideal for backgrounds involving physical exertion.
- Constitution — ideal for backgrounds involving endurance or long hours of activity.
- [[intelligence|Intelligence]] or [[wisdom|Wisdom]] — ideal for backgrounds focused on cerebral or spiritual matters.
- [[charisma|Charisma]] — ideal for backgrounds involving performance or social interaction.

**2. Choose a Feat.** Choose one feat from the Origin category (source references "Origin Feats" for examples — not detailed further here).

**3. Choose Skill Proficiencies.** Choose two skills appropriate for the background. There needn't be a relationship between the skill proficiencies a background grants and the ability scores it increases.

**4. Choose a Tool Proficiency.** Choose one tool used in, or often associated with, the practice of the background.

**5. Choose Equipment.** Assemble an equipment package worth 50 GP total (including unspent gold). Don't include Martial weapons or armor — characters get those from class choices.

## Curses and Magical Contagions

A curse is a magical burden lasting a specified time or until ended by some means. A magical contagion is an adverse effect of magical origin that is contagious by definition.

### Curses

Curses typically take one of these forms:

**Bestow Curse.** The simplest curses come from the *[[bestow-curse|Bestow Curse]]* spell; effects are limited and can be ended by *[[remove-curse|Remove Curse]]*. *Bestow Curse* is a useful benchmark for gauging other curses' potency: a curse lasting 1 minute equates to a level 3 spell; one lasting until dispelled equates to a level 9 spell.

**Cursed Creatures.** Some monsters are associated with curses, whether through their origin or their ability to spread curses (e.g., werewolves). The DM decides how a spell like *Remove Curse* affects a creature with accursed origins — e.g., a mummy created through a curse might be destroyed permanently only by casting *Remove Curse* on its corpse.

**Cursed Magic Items.** Created deliberately or arising from supernatural events; detailed elsewhere (source refers to a "Magic Items" section not included here).

**Narrative Curses.** May manifest when a creature violates a taboo (breaking a vow, defiling a tomb, murdering an innocent) and receives supernatural punishment. Effects are DM-designed, or a customized version of another curse type. The affected creature should know why it's punished and be able to learn how to end the curse — likely by symbolically righting the wrong. How *Remove Curse* interacts with a narrative curse is DM's call — it might only suppress the effect temporarily. Narrative curses should feel rare, potent, and rooted in campaign lore.

**Environmental Curses.** Some locations are so suffused with evil that anyone lingering there is burdened with a curse. Example: Demonic Possession.

> [!mechanic]
> **Demonic Possession** — A creature targeted by Demonic Possession must succeed on a DC 15 Charisma saving throw or be possessed by a bodiless demonic entity. Whenever the possessed creature rolls a 1 on a D20 Test, the demonic entity takes control and determines the creature's behavior thereafter. At the end of each of the possessed creature's later turns, it makes a DC 15 Charisma save, regaining control on a success. After finishing a Long Rest, the creature makes a DC 15 Charisma save; on success, the effect ends. A *[[dispel-evil-and-good|Dispel Evil and Good]]* spell, or any magic that removes a curse, also ends the effect.

### Magical Contagions

Credited to alchemists, potion brewers, and areas of wild magic. An outbreak can form the basis of an adventure as characters search for a cure and try to stop its spread.

> [!mechanic]
> **Rest and Recuperation** — If a creature infected with a magical contagion spends 3 days recuperating (no activities that would interrupt a Long Rest), it makes a DC 15 Constitution saving throw at the end of the recuperation period. On success, it has Advantage on saving throws to fight off the contagion for the next 24 hours.

The following example contagions can have their DCs, effects, and other characteristics altered to suit a campaign.

> [!mechanic]
> **Cackle Fever** (Magical Contagion) — Affects Humanoids only (gnomes are immune); often from cheaply made potions/elixirs. Effects begin 1d4 days after infection:
>
> - *Fever*: the creature gains 1 Exhaustion level, which lasts until the contagion ends on it.
> - *Uncontrollable Laughter*: while it has the Exhaustion condition, the creature makes a DC 13 Constitution save each time it takes damage other than Psychic. On failure, it takes 5 (1d10) Psychic damage and has the [[incapacitated|Incapacitated]] condition while laughing uncontrollably; it repeats the save at the end of each of its turns, ending the effect on a success (automatically succeeds after 1 minute).
> - *Fighting the [[contagion|Contagion]]*: at the end of each Long Rest, an infected creature makes a DC 13 Constitution save. After 3 successes, the contagion ends and the creature is immune to Cackle Fever for 1 year.
> - *Spreading the Contagion*: any Humanoid (except gnomes) starting its turn within a 10-foot Emanation from an infected creature must succeed on a DC 10 Constitution save or become infected too. On success, that Humanoid can't catch it from that specific infected creature for 24 hours.

Sewer Plague trades laughter for exhaustion.

> [!mechanic]
> **Sewer Plague** (Magical Contagion) — From fouled potions/alchemical waste; incubates in sewers/refuse heaps, sometimes spread by otyughs, rats, etc. Any Humanoid wounded by a carrier, or that contacts contaminated filth/offal, must succeed on a DC 11 Constitution save or become infected. Effects begin 1d4 days after infection:
>
> - *Fatigue*: gains 1 Exhaustion level.
> - *Weakness*: while it has any Exhaustion levels, it regains only half the normal HP from spending Hit Point Dice.
> - *Restlessness*: while it has any Exhaustion levels, finishing a Long Rest neither restores lost HP nor reduces Exhaustion.
> - *Fighting the Contagion*: daily at dawn, the infected creature makes a DC 11 Constitution save. Failure: gains 1 Exhaustion level. Success: Exhaustion level decreases by 1. At 0 Exhaustion, the contagion ends.

Sight Rot works through water rather than touch.

> [!mechanic]
> **Sight Rot** (Magical Contagion) — Any Beast or Humanoid drinking tainted water must succeed on a DC 15 Constitution save or have the [[blinded|Blinded]] condition until the contagion ends.
>
> - *Fighting the Contagion*: A *[[heal|Heal]]* or *[[lesser-restoration|Lesser Restoration]]* spell ends it immediately. A character proficient with a [[herbalism-kit|Herbalism Kit]] can spend 1 hour to create one dose of nonmagical ointment; applied to the eyes of an infected creature, it suppresses the contagion for 24 hours. Three doses/applications totaling 72 hours of suppression ends the contagion.
> - *Spreading the Contagion*: any Humanoid making skin contact with an infected creature must succeed on a DC 15 Constitution save or also become infected. On success, that Humanoid can't catch it from that specific creature for 24 hours.

## Environmental Effects

> [!mechanic]
> **Deep Water** — Swimming through deep water (more than 100 feet deep) is challenging due to pressure and cold. After each hour swimming in deep water, a creature lacking a Swim Speed must succeed on a DC 10 Constitution save or gain 1 Exhaustion level.

Extreme Cold pushes the same clock through weather instead of depth.

> [!mechanic]
> **Extreme Cold** — At 0°F or lower, an exposed creature must succeed on a DC 10 Constitution save at the end of each hour or gain 1 Exhaustion level. Creatures with Resistance or [[immunity|Immunity]] to Cold damage auto-succeed.

Extreme Heat runs the same mechanic in reverse.

> [!mechanic]
> **Extreme Heat** — At 100°F or higher, a creature without access to drinkable water must succeed on a Constitution save at the end of each hour or gain 1 Exhaustion level. DC is 5 for the first hour, +1 per additional hour. Creatures in Medium or Heavy armor have Disadvantage on the save. Creatures with Resistance or Immunity to Fire damage auto-succeed.

Frigid Water covers full immersion, not exposure.

> [!mechanic]
> **Frigid Water** — A creature can be immersed in frigid water for a number of minutes equal to its Constitution score before suffering ill effects. Each additional minute requires a DC 10 Constitution save or 1 Exhaustion level gained. Creatures with Resistance/Immunity to Cold damage, or naturally adapted to ice-cold water, auto-succeed.

**Heavy Precipitation.** Everything within an area of heavy rain or snowfall is Lightly Obscured; creatures there have Disadvantage on Wisdom (Perception) checks. Heavy rain also extinguishes open flames.

**High Altitude.** At 10,000+ feet above sea level, each hour spent traveling counts as 2 hours for determining travel duration (see Travel Pace, Extended Travel). Creatures can acclimate by spending 30+ days at that elevation; can't acclimate above 20,000 feet unless native to such environments.

> [!mechanic]
> **Slippery Ice** — Difficult Terrain. A creature moving onto it for the first time on a turn, or starting its turn there, must succeed on a DC 10 Dexterity save or have the [[prone|Prone]] condition.

**Strong Wind.** Imposes Disadvantage on ranged weapon attack rolls; extinguishes open flames; disperses fog. A flying creature in strong wind must land at the end of its turn or fall. A strong wind in a desert can create a sandstorm imposing Disadvantage on Wisdom (Perception) checks.

**Thin Ice.** Weight tolerance of 3d10 × 10 pounds per 10-foot-square area. When total weight on an area exceeds tolerance, the ice breaks and all creatures on it fall through into frigid water below (see Frigid Water).

## Fear and Mental Stress

Adventurers tend to be less susceptible to fear/mental stress than common folk, but certain creatures/effects can still terrify or fray even a stalwart adventurer's mind. If using these rules, discuss them with players at campaign start.

### Fear Effects

Use the [[frightened|Frightened]] condition as baseline for supernaturally frightful encounters. Fear effects typically require a Wisdom saving throw, DC scaled to how terrifying the situation is:

| Example | Save DC |
|---|---|
| A harmless yet terrifying apparition appears when a sarcophagus is opened. | 10 |
| A magical trap creates an illusory manifestation of one character's worst fears, visible only to that character. | 15 |
| A portal to the Abyss opens, revealing a nightmarish realm of torment and slaughter. | 20 |

Typically a Frightened creature repeats the save at the end of each of its turns, ending the effect on a success.

At DM discretion, a Frightened creature might additionally (examples, not automatic):

- Be forced to take the Dash action each turn, moving away from the fear source.
- Suffer attacks against it made with Advantage.
- Be limited to only one of: move, take an action, or take a Bonus Action, on each of its turns.

### Mental Stress Effects

For intense mental stress, Psychic damage is the recommended emulation. Save is usually Wisdom, sometimes Intelligence or Charisma. On a successful save, the DM may allow half damage instead of none.

| Example | Save DC | Psychic Damage |
|---|---|---|
| A character ingests a hallucinogenic substance distorting perception of reality. | 10 | 1d6 |
| A character touches a fiendish idol that tears at the mind, threatening to shatter it. | 15 | 3d6 |
| A magical trap flings a character into the Far Realm until the end of their next turn. | 20 | 9d6 |

**Prolonged Effects** (possible consequences of mental stress exposure):

- *Short-Term*: the character has the Frightened, Incapacitated, or [[stunned|Stunned]] condition for 1d10 minutes (possibly with alarming behavior or hallucinations). Suppressed by *[[calm-emotions|Calm Emotions]]*; removed by *Lesser Restoration*.
- *Long-Term*: Disadvantage on some or all ability checks for 1d10 × 10 hours, from unwillingness/inability to exert a particular set of abilities. Same suppression and removal as Short-Term.
- *Indefinite*: a long-term effect that lasts until removed by *[[greater-restoration|Greater Restoration]]*; can be suppressed by *Calm Emotions*.

## Poison

Poisons come in four types:

**Contact.** Smeared on an object, stays potent until touched or washed off. A creature touching it with exposed skin suffers its effects.

**Ingested.** Requires swallowing an entire dose (delivered via food/liquid) to suffer effects. DM may decide a partial dose has reduced effect (e.g., Advantage on the save, or half damage on a failed save).

**Inhaled.** Powders/gases take effect when inhaled. Blowing/releasing subjects creatures in a 5-foot Cube to the effect; the cloud dissipates immediately after. Holding one's breath doesn't help — it affects nasal membranes, tear ducts, etc.

**Injury.** Applied as a Bonus Action to a weapon, ammunition, or similar object; stays potent until delivered through a wound or washed off. A creature taking Piercing or Slashing damage from a coated object is exposed.

**Purchasing Poison.** In some settings it's illegal; illicit dealers or unscrupulous apothecaries may keep hidden stashes. Characters with criminal contacts may acquire it easily; others may need extensive inquiries and bribes.

> [!mechanic]
> **Harvesting Poison** — A character can attempt to harvest poison from a venomous creature that is dead or has the Incapacitated condition. Takes 1d6 minutes, then a DC 20 Intelligence (Nature) check using a [[poisoners-kit|Poisoner's Kit]]. Success: harvests one dose (no more can be harvested from that creature). Failure: no poison extracted. Failure by 5+: the character is subjected to the creature's poison.
