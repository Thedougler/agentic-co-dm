# NPC Guidance

Read before writing any moment where an NPC talks to the party, filling a run
guide's `## NPCs` roster, or deciding how a piece of information reaches the
table. [[npcs|NPCs]] are the table's steering: they hand the party direction, and gossip
around them keeps the world moving. Both go wrong the same way — by talking
without giving the party anything to act on.

## 1. One voice at a time

One NPC addresses the party. Every other NPC present is texture: available to
be talked to, never driving the scene.

Never write two NPCs conversing with each other in front of the party. A
moment whose opener stages an argument, a briefing, or a back-and-forth between
two NPCs turns the table into an audience — the players wait for the exchange
to finish instead of acting inside it. Give the second NPC a job for their
hands instead, one clause, and let the party reach them if they want to.

An NPC-to-NPC exchange is legal only when it is short, minimal, and does not
involve the party — a line thrown over a shoulder while the work continues, not
a scene the party watches.

The speaker is whoever wants something from the party right now. That want is
already on the page (`.claude/skills/composing-beats/references/runtime-surface.md` § Prep-entity floor § 3, the Toy fields); the entrance and
first line are [[prose-and-character-craft|Prose and Character Craft]]'s § NPC entrance.

## 2. The ask — subtle or explicit

Every speaking NPC has an **ask**: what they want the party to do next. It sits
beside the want, never replacing it — the want is the motivation the DM
improvises from, the ask is the line the party can act on.

The ask runs on a register scale, and the register is a choice about how much
the party is being steered:

- **Subtle** — an aside, a complaint, a half-finished sentence, a thing
  mentioned while doing something else. The party decides it is a lead.
- **Direct** — a stated request with a reason and, usually, a cost or a
  deadline. The party decides whether to take it.

A subtle ask that no player picks up is not a failed ask — it is a clue, and
clues come in threes (`.claude/skills/composing-beats/references/audits.md` § Three-Clue Rule). Escalate the register on a
later channel rather than repeating the same line louder.

## 3. NPCs are one channel, not the channel

A secret is written before its delivery method is chosen
([[creating-secrets-and-clues|Creating Secrets and Clues]]), so the same fact can arrive as a document, a
physical trace, a rumour, or an NPC's line. Note which NPC is *able* to say a
given secret if the party presses; never bind the secret to that mouth.

An NPC who exists only to deliver information is a text box with a name. When
every reveal in a session routes through someone talking, move at least one to
another channel.

## 4. Chatter needs a draw or it is noise

Overheard talk — gossip, a crier, an argument through a wall, two dockhands
who never notice the party — earns its place only when it points at something
the vault already keys: a live thread, a named NPC, a place the party has been,
a rumour with its own page (`vault/refs/vault/lore/references/rumour.md` owns those). Chatter invented for
atmosphere alone gives the table nothing to hold and teaches players to stop
listening.

Deliver it where it becomes actionable, not ahead of that. A fact the party
cannot use for three sessions is not foreshadowing when it arrives as a
throwaway line; it is a fact they will have forgotten.

Chatter does not need a live thread to be worth folding in. A line that lands
on a known name or a place the party has walked is already relevant — that is
the gate, not whether an objective is currently open.

One or two overheard lines in a moment is a living street. More is a wall of
voices the DM has to perform and the players have to filter.

## 5. Failure modes

**The vending machine.** An NPC who answers every question fully and asks
nothing back. Give them something they want from the party first, and let the
information cost a beat of conversation.

**Early-loaded lore.** History delivered before the party has any use for it.
Cut it to the one sentence that changes what they do next; the rest keeps until
it is load-bearing.

**Chatter as noise.** Ambient lines written for texture with no referent. Cut
them or point each one at something already on a page (§4).

**The quest-giver as railroad.** One NPC stating the session's intended path.
Split the direction across channels (§3) and let a refusal be a real branch —
what happens when the party says no belongs on the page
(`.claude/skills/composing-beats/references/audits.md` § The If-Ignored Requirement).

**The crowd scene.** Three NPCs with speaking lines in one opener. Pick the one
who wants something (§1); the rest get a clause each.
