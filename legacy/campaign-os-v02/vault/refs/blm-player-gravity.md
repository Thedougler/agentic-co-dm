# Anchoring Prep to Player Gravity

## The tension being resolved

A player wants a narrative arc for their character while also wanting that
character to be unaware, in-world, of living inside a story. Mulligan is
quoted stating this directly:

> "I want to be living in a story, immersed into a character that is not,
> to their knowledge, living in a story."

The source treats that gap — shape versus in-character unawareness — as the
preparer's problem to resolve, not something to be solved by asking players
to accept a plotline.

## Water and irrigation

Mulligan characterises the group at the table as water, quoted directly:

> "They are like water. They are going down the hill as fast as they can,
> seeking the path of least resistance."

The preparer's role, on this account, is to irrigate the slope: place
obstacles so that the route arrived at is one worth watching. The source
draws an explicit distinction against railroading here — the water's route
is genuinely its own, and what the preparer built is the channel, not the
route itself. The obstacles that do this work are drawn from what a
character is already invested in, which is what keeps the guidance from
registering as constraint.

## Backstory as the reliable anchor

Mulligan is quoted characterising backstory material as "plot hooks you'll
bite every time," set against a free-floating mystery, which a party can
decline. The source's stated reasoning: a draw attached to something a
character is already invested in — a sworn enemy appearing, for instance —
does not depend on the party opting in the way an unconnected draw does.

The stated practical consequence of this: knowing what the party is
invested in is what makes preparation predictable enough to be worth doing
in advance.

## Session Zero as the source of that knowledge

Both sources converge on Session Zero as the point where this information
is gathered. The roundtable source frames Session Zero as where the
character goals and fears that later conflicts attach to are learned, so
that whatever gets built afterward connects to something already
established rather than to an assumption. The framework source states the
same sequence as a loop: Session Zero identifies character goals and
fears, world conflicts are built around those, and the source claims this
loop makes prepared material land with more reliability than a draw with
no such anchor — because it wasn't free-floating to begin with. The
framework source's own characterisation of this loop: "Freedom is real;
the rails are invisible."

## Required output: the Link of Relevance

The framework source states that all NPC and encounter generation must be
anchored to at least one character's backstory, and that a "Link of
Relevance" field is a required output of that generation step — every
prepared NPC or encounter must record what backstory element it connects
to.

**Gap in the source:** the framework source directs the reader to a
document titled "Player-Gravity-Wells" for "the current gravity map" — the
live record of what each character is invested in. That document is not
included in either source recreated here, and its contents are not stated
by either source. What a gravity map actually records, beyond being
referenced as the anchor point for the Link of Relevance field, is left
unstated.
