# Gameplay Toolbox — Combat Encounters

Features that make an encounter more interesting/challenging:

- *Changes in Elevation* — terrain features providing elevation change (crate stacks, ledges, balconies) reward clever positioning and encourage jumping, climbing, flying, or teleporting.
- *Defensive Positions* — enemies in hard-to-reach or defensive spots force ranged-attacking characters to move around.
- *Mixed Monster Groups* — different monster types working together combine abilities (like characters of different classes/origins), making a diverse force more powerful.
- *Reasons to Move* — features like chandeliers, kegs of gunpowder/oil, and rolling stone traps encourage movement by both sides.

## Combat Encounter Difficulty

### Step 1: Choose a Difficulty

- *Low* — likely one or two scary moments; characters should win with no casualties (may need healing resources). Rough guideline: a single monster is a low-difficulty challenge for a party of 4 whose level equals the monster's Challenge Rating.
- *Moderate* — absent healing/resources, could go badly; weaker characters might be taken out of the fight; slim chance of a character death.
- *High* — could be lethal for one or more characters; survival requires smart tactics, quick thinking, and possibly luck.

### Step 3: Spend Your Budget

Every creature has an XP value in its stat block. Deduct each added creature's XP from the budget as you build the encounter. Spend as much as possible without going over; a small unspent remainder is fine.

Examples:

- *Example 1*: Low-difficulty, four level-1 characters → budget = 50 × 4 = 200 XP. Options: 1 [[bugbear-warrior|Bugbear Warrior]] (200 XP); or 2 [[giant-wasp|Giant Wasps]] (100 XP each = 200 XP); or 6 [[giant-rat|Giant Rats]] (25 XP each = 150 XP).
- *Example 2*: Moderate-difficulty, five level-3 characters → budget = 225 × 5 = 1,125 XP. Options: 2 [[druid|Druids]] (450 XP each) + 9 [[stirge|Stirges]] (25 XP each) = 1,125 XP; or 1 [[wight|Wight]] (700 XP) + 1 [[warhorse-skeleton|Warhorse Skeleton]] (100 XP) + 6 [[skeleton|Skeletons]] (50 XP each) = 1,100 XP.
- *Example 3*: High-difficulty, six level-15 characters → budget = 7,800 × 6 = 46,800 XP. Option: 2 [[adult-red-dragon|Adult Red Dragons]] (18,000 XP each) + 2 [[fire-giant|Fire Giants]] (5,000 XP each) = 46,000 XP.

## Troubleshooting

- *Many Creatures* — more creatures raises the risk of a lucky streak dealing more damage than expected. If an encounter has more than 2 creatures per character, include some fragile ones that die quickly. Especially important at character level 1–2.
- *Adjustments* — a player's absence may warrant removing creatures to preserve intended difficulty. Die rolls and other factors can also make an encounter easier/harder than planned; adjust on the fly (creatures flee = easier; add reinforcements = harder).
- *CR 0 Creatures* — use sparingly, especially ones worth 0 XP; use swarms instead if you want many CR 0 critters present.
- *Number of Stat Blocks* — best encounters often pair one creature type with another (e.g., fire giants + hell hounds). Referencing more than 2–3 stat blocks in one encounter can be daunting, especially for complex creatures.
- *Powerful Creatures* — a creature with CR higher than party level might one-shot a character with a single action (e.g., an [[ogre|Ogre]], CR 2, can kill a level 1 [[wizard|Wizard]] in one blow).
- *Unusual Features* — if a monster has a feature lower-level characters can't easily overcome, consider not using it against characters below the monster's CR.
