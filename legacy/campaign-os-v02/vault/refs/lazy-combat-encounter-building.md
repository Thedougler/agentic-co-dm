# Lazy Combat Encounter Building for 5e (Lazy GM Resource Document)

This method helps build and improvise dynamic combat encounters based on the fiction of the game.

## Start with the Story

Good combat encounters begin with the story. Instead of building combat encounters as fixed components of the game, let combat encounters evolve naturally from the story taking place at the table.

Begin by asking the following question: What monsters make sense given the current location and situation? Then let that question guide the creation of a list of monsters and NPCs that might show up at a given location, and in what quantity.

Instead of predefining scenes as combat, roleplaying, or exploration, let the characters' approach determine what happens. Maybe they fight the guards at the gatehouse. Maybe they sneak past. Maybe they try to play the part of hired mercenaries. But let whatever happens come from the choices of the players.

## Average Character Hit Points

Gauging the level of challenge in an encounter often comes down to comparing the amount of damage a monster can deal to the hit points of the characters. Hit points vary widely between classes, but the following formula estimates an average character's hit points at a given level: **(Level × 7) + 3**.

This formula gives a rough estimation of how tough characters of a given level are, and helps gauge how dangerous a specific monster will be, as well as judging the potential deadliness of traps, hazards, and other effects that deal damage.

## Potential Deadliness

Assuming the chosen monsters aren't dealing damage that easily overwhelms the characters' hit points, an encounter's difficulty usually doesn't need worrying about — unless the encounter is potentially deadly. Gauge an encounter's potential deadliness, including the single-monster CR cap, with [The Lazy Encounter Benchmark](vault/refs/lazy-encounter-benchmark.md).

If the characters in a given game often have an easy time with potentially deadly encounters, treat the characters as though they are one or more levels higher when checking the benchmark — this raises the threshold at which an encounter might become deadly, making the calculation more accurate.

## Character Capabilities Vary

No chart, table, or equation works perfectly to judge encounter balance at any given level, because no such system can account for the wide range of options that affect combat. Any individual character's effective power in a fight can go well beyond what's expected for their given level, especially at higher levels. Other factors that can affect a character's power include:

* Player experience
* Class synergy between different characters in the party
* Magic items
* Environmental and situational circumstances
* Feats and multiclassing
* The total number of actions on either side
* How well rested the characters are

Use the benchmarks for determining whether an encounter might be deadly as a starting point, then trust that gauging what the characters can handle in combat gets more accurate the more they're seen in action during the game.
