# Quest Content Reference

Fuel for *what* a quest is about and *what it pays off with* — sibling to
`vault/refs/vault/quest/references/structure.md`, which covers *how* a quest's arc unfolds. Read this
during the interview when the DM doesn't already have an objective, needs
secrets/clues material, or is filling in Outcome rewards. Every table
here cites its source; use as a spark, not a script — a quest
still needs its own PC-Connection and true stakes named, per the guide's
Hard rules.

## Objective Archetypes

Ten general-purpose quest objectives, standard in RPGs for decades
(`vault/refs/vault/quest/references/templates.md`). Distinct from a *structural
pattern* (`vault/refs/vault/quest/references/structure.md` — how the quest's arc unfolds session over
session): an archetype names *what the task is*. Offer these when the DM
has a situation (an NPC, a location, an opposing faction) but no objective
shape yet.

1. **Kill the Boss** — hunt down and permanently end a monster or
   villain's threat, possibly protected by lieutenants.
2. **Find Something** — steal or hunt for an item, or return one to its
   rightful place; the item may be protected by a boss.
3. **Rescue Someone** — retrieve a captured NPC from a location, or
   (variant) escort someone through danger to a destination.
4. **Kill the Lieutenants** — hunt down several sub-bosses (eliminate,
   capture, or convert), often building toward a later Kill the Boss.
5. **Destroy Something** — enter a hostile location to destroy an object
   (an evil obelisk, a ritual catalyst, a weapon of great power).
6. **Steal Something** — an intrigue-focused heist rather than a dungeon
   crawl; plan the approach, allow failing forward, give the location
   multiple entry/exit paths.
7. **Clear the Dangers** — clear an entire location of monsters/threats,
   not just deal with one known foe.
8. **Collect the Keys** — hunt for a majority (not all) of a set of keys
   before a rival gets them — 3-of-5, 4-of-7, 5-of-9 works well so no side
   can win by holding just one.
9. **Defend a Location** — prepare and hold a location against oncoming
   enemies; keep the spotlight on the party's individual roles, run any
   mass-battle portion off-screen.
10. **End the Ritual** — disrupt an ongoing ritual's multiple components
    (glyph-marked pillars, corrupted pools); variant: defend the ritual's
    performers against a third party trying to stop it.

## Secrets & Clues Fuel

Four prompt categories, each usable as a d10 table
(`vault/refs/ideas/creating-secrets-and-clues.md`). **Core discipline: write
each answer abstract from its place of discovery** — a sentence the DM can
drop in wherever play goes, not gated to a specific Beat or NPC in
advance. Use when `## Secrets & Clues` needs material and the DM doesn't
have any yet; a hidden conclusion still needs the Three-Clue Rule
(`.claude/skills/composing-beats/references/audits.md` §3) once three of these are picked.

**Character secrets** (ties a PC to the world — satisfies the
PC-Connection Requirement directly): family history, a tie to this
location, a haunting ghost/spirit, prophetic dreams, a secret parasite, a
family member involved in the adventure, how the villain relates to the
character, an NPC believed dead who still lives, a childhood ritual
blessing, a previous event tying the character to the story.

**Historical secrets** (local/world history, found in mosaics, statues,
tomes, or elder tales): a dead god's connection to the area, armies that
once battled here, a cruel lord slain here, an ancient civilization that
thrived here, a buried old empire's settlements, a hidden alien
creature/power, a past rebellion, buried primeval mysteries, the
location's former purpose, a monster that once ruled here.

**NPC and villain secrets** (introduces a villain before the confrontation,
learned from heralds, rumors, journals, or a minion's last words): a dark
history, why the NPC thinks they're right, their great accomplishment, a
foe they defeated, what makes them politically untouchable, a great power
they possess, what they desire, their routines, who they love above all,
what they want kept hidden.

**Plot and story secrets** (moves the larger campaign forward, learned
from quest-givers, notes on the defeated, portents, or fleeing NPCs): a
villainous event soon to pass, an impending disaster, a royal
assassination, a newly revealed dungeon entrance, monsters first sighted
this season, an invading army, a dark portent, a natural disaster, an
unnatural being's appearance, an unusual creature seen in the wilds.

## Rewards

What the party walks away with — Outcome should name this, not just
whether the quest succeeded (`.claude/skills/composing-beats/references/composition.md` §
Rewards; `.claude/skills/composing-beats/references/runtime-surface.md`'s "Select magic item
rewards" step). Real quest pages already do this in practice (a bounty
table, branching payouts by outcome) — this just names the categories to
draw from:

- **Items with stories**, not stat blocks alone — an item's provenance is
  part of the reward.
- **Potions, generously** — cheap to grant, high perceived value.
- **Situational-use items** — useful only in a certain time or place; ties
  the reward back to this quest's specific stakes.
- **Social rewards** — status, property, titles; make players feel
  important without another magic item.
- **Costs, not just gains** — a Pyrrhic outcome
  (`vault/refs/vault/quest/references/structure.md` § The Pyrrhic Arc) is
  also a "what the party walks away with," even when it's a debt, a
  vacuum filled, or a Front that starts ticking as a direct result.
