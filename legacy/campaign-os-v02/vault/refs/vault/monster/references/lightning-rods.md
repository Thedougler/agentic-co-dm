# Lightning rods (design philosophy for CR-4+ / party level 5+)

Companion to
[[cr-design|"CR math and monster design method"]]
— read that page for the Four Laws, roles, ability hierarchy, and CR math;
read this page when statting a CR-4+ creature for a party of 5th level or
higher, before finalizing tactics.

---

Justification: past 4th level, a GM who reflexively counters the party's
signature spells and features (immunities, inflated HP, tactics built to
dodge their best options) makes those characters' cool moments frustrating
instead of rewarding. Condensed from `vault/refs/ideas/lightning-rods.md`.

Instead, build monsters as intended *victims* of the party's strongest
plays — showing those abilities off instead of shutting them down:

- **Watch what the party brings.** Note the spells/features players
  actually enjoy using (a stunning-strike monk, a *fireball*-slinging
  wizard, a *banishment*-ready cleric) and design the next big fight's
  monsters around letting those specific things land.
- **Hordes for area effects.** Once the party has AoE (*fireball*,
  *hypnotic pattern*, Destroy Undead), add low-CR hordes that group up in
  perfect position to be blasted — direct-cinema logic over tactical
  realism. `.claude/skills/encounter-prep/references/running-the-encounter.md` §
  Running hordes covers the mechanics.
- **Expendable lieutenants for control spells.** A non-legendary Brute or
  Defender lieutenant with a bad WIS/CHA save is exactly what a
  *banishment* or *polymorph* wants to land on — legendary creatures resist
  this via Legendary Resistance, their lieutenants shouldn't.
- **Fragile damage-dealers for burst classes.** Low AC/HP/CON-save
  Artillery or Skirmisher creatures reward a paladin's smite or a
  sharpshooter's alpha strike with a satisfying one-hit kill.
- **Play to the party's strengths, not just weaknesses.** Place an
  Artillery creature somewhere that looks hard to reach but is trivial for
  a flying/climbing/teleporting character — letting the party show off
  their build is the point, not a loophole to close.
- **Telegraph it.** A less tactically-minded table might not notice a
  lightning rod is there to be exploited — be ready to describe or state
  outright what's at stake if they don't deal with it (see
  [[cr-design|"CR math and monster design method"]]
  § 6 Telegraphing).
