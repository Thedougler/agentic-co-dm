# Campaign Content Reference

Fuel for a campaign overview's pitch/truths/starting-situation, a session
zero, and a one-shot's strong start — sibling reference to
`vault/refs/vault/faction/references/content.md`'s shape. Read this during
the interview when the DM doesn't have a concept yet. Every table here cites
its source inline; use as a spark, not a script — a campaign page still
needs its own PC-Connection and Safety & Tone content named, per the skill's
own rules.

**Front archetypes, villain/antagonist design, and organization concepts
for group PC-Connections already live in
`vault/refs/vault/faction/references/content.md`** (§ Front Archetypes, §
Villain & Antagonist Design, § Organization Concepts) — read there, don't
re-derive them here. This file covers only what's specific to campaign- and
session-zero-level content: the pitch, the truths, the starting location,
running session zero itself, safety tools, individual character
connections, and one-shot strong starts.

## Campaign Pitch

A one-sentence premise (`vault/refs/ideas/spiral-campaign-development.md` §
Campaign Pitch) — the central theme of the campaign, given to players during
session zero so they can build characters around it. Offer these as
starting points or inspiration when the DM has no pitch yet:

Prevent the summoning of the Dragon Queen; prevent the coming of the Black
Moon; end the dark reign of a lich queen; break the political power of a
death knight; kill a vampire lord and his horde; restore light to a vale of
nightmares; restore the prison of a demon prince; shatter a draconic
alliance; save people from a gnoll war band's blood feast; restore light to
a fallen celestial; dismantle an empire; find the seven keys to a set of
gates; prevent a sorcerer king's resurrection; stop a cult; save an heir;
find and seal a world serpent's vault; close a gateway to the Outside;
destroy a legendary weapon; slay an ancient dragon; end an archdevil's dark
pact.

## The Six Truths

Once the pitch exists, name the facts that set this campaign apart from
others and share them with the players
(`vault/refs/ideas/spiral-campaign-development.md` § Six Truths). Six is a
convention, not a hard count — the
template's `<N> Truths` heading is deliberately open. Each truth should be
concrete and table-facing, e.g.: monsters have been sighted on once-safe
roads and whole villages vanish without a trace; a floating obsidian
citadel has appeared over the northern mountains; folk once knew how to
survive this threat but the secret was lost; a named villain is using the
chaos to wage war; prophecy names a specific group who will restore order.

## Starting Location

Ongoing campaigns work best starting in a central location the world
spirals outward from (`vault/refs/ideas/spiral-campaign-development.md` §
Starting Location) — the party sets out from here, and the setting expands
as they explore it. Starting-location archetypes: adventurers' guild,
mining outpost, recent shipwreck, frontier outpost, holy temple, refugee
camp, fortress under siege, great library, planar hub city, crumbling
fortress. A village works well too, and is the most common default.

## Running Session Zero

Full flow (`vault/refs/session-zero-checklist.md`):

1. **Write a one-page campaign guide** before the session: the pitch and
   flavor, the Truths that separate this campaign from others, what
   character options/motivations fit best, any potentially troubling
   themes (feeds § Safety & Tone below), and candidate group patrons.
2. **Describe the theme** at the table, selling the premise.
3. **Discuss safety tools** — see § Safety & Tone.
4. **Decide on a group patron**, if the party wants a shared quest-giver —
   offer candidates, work toward consensus, don't let the choice alienate
   any one player.
5. **Build characters together**, reinforcing the campaign's themes and
   motivations.
6. **Optionally run a short adventure** at the end to introduce the
   characters fast — a single combat with some negotiation/exploration
   works well, characters advancing to 2nd level afterward.

## Character Connections

Two approaches to pre-connecting the party
(`vault/refs/ideas/connecting-characters.md`) — pick one, or let the table
choose:

- **A single shared group connection** — a faction/patron/organization
  that already ties every PC together. For a *group* connection, use
  `vault/refs/vault/faction/references/content.md` § Organization Concepts.
  Best for one-shots (speeds the table up, ties directly into the
  adventure) and campaigns wanting a strong shared draw.
- **Individual pairwise relationships** — each player rolls or picks a
  relationship to the character on their right, going once around the
  table so everyone ends up with two relationships. Relationship
  archetypes: adopted siblings, mentor and student, friendly rivals, sage
  and scribe, priest and acolyte, fellow veterans, ward and guardian,
  spouses, buddy cops, childhood friends, noble and bodyguard, soul
  bound, former prisoners, former criminals, hunted quarry, pact bound,
  apocalypse survivors, savior and saved, business partners, master and
  servant. Personal-relationship variants (rolling player's PC → the
  player-on-their-right's PC): sibling of, saved by, served with,
  protected by, adventured with, friendly rival of, childhood friend of,
  magically bound to, survived with, escaped with, apprentice of, acolyte
  of, idolizes, drinking buddies with, business associate of, lost a bet
  to, indebted to, trained by, dueling partner of, on the run with.

## Safety & Tone

Discuss and agree these with the players, early — during session zero for
an ongoing campaign, or before play for a one-shot
(`vault/refs/safety-tools.md`). Never the DM's private guess.

**Sensitive topics worth naming explicitly** (not exhaustive — add whatever
this specific campaign/adventure might touch): blood, body horror,
burning, cancer, cannibalism, claustrophobia, death by exposure, freezing,
gaslighting, genocide, gore, harm to animals, harm to children,
homophobia, incest, insects, kidnapping, mental domination, murder,
paralysis, physical restraint, racism, rats, real-world religion, ritual
sacrifice, self-harm, sexism, sexual assault, sexual contact, slavery,
spiders, starvation, terrorism, thirst, torture, transphobia.

**Hard lines vs. off-screen content** — for each sensitive topic that comes
up, the table decides whether it's a hard line (never comes up at all) or
off-screen content (can happen, described vaguely, not played out in
detail). Capture each player's individual lines alongside the DM's own.

**The pause cue** — agree a verbal cue (e.g. "pause for a second") any
player or the DM can use to interrupt the scene, break character, and
discuss out-of-character. Use it early and non-dramatically so it doesn't
carry a stigma reserved only for extreme moments.

## Strong Starts (one-shot Pitch)

A strong start opens mid-action rather than with scene-setting
(`.claude/skills/writing-hook-beats/references/hook-beats.md`) — use as inspiration for a
one-shot's Pitch H3, or an ongoing campaign's first session. Examples by
setting: in a settlement — bandits caught mid-robbery, the campaign's
villain inviting the party for a drink, a local ruler assassinated with a
villain taking over; in a sewer — a wererat offering information, a wall
collapsing into a hidden temple; in the wilderness — a satyr stepping out
of a tree, a golden-antlered stag begging for protection from hunters; in
a dungeon/cave — a vampire asking a favor, two bands of goblins fighting
over a hag's favor, a wounded knight begging for help before dying.
