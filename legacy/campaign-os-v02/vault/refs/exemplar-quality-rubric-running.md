# Exemplar Quality Rubric: Running & Trust

Checks R11–R20 from the [quality rubric](vault/refs/exemplar-quality-rubric.md).

## C. Running and improvisation

- **R11 — Encounters carry intent and end conditions.** Every combat states the
  opposition's goal (capture, delay, drive off), tactics or behavior states, and
  a pre-decided end (morale break, flee threshold, phase triggers, fail-cap into
  combat for socials). Check: the runner can answer "what do they do now?" and
  "when does this stop?" from the text. *(Wild Sheep Chase's phased boss;
  Night Hunter's per-event behavior; Silverpine's argument-DC table + 3-failure
  cap; the basilisk's behavior states.)*
- **R12 — Wants before mechanics.** NPCs and monsters lead with motivation —
  a Wants line, a Toy-style goal/method/problem, or Trait/Ideal/Bond/Flaw in
  voiceable first person — so unscripted interaction is runnable. Check: every
  named actor has a stated want findable without reading its history section.
  *(Serpent Kings' Wants: stat line; Pleasantries' Renata block; Wild Sheep
  Chase roleplaying boxes.)*
- **R13 — Speakable, not performable-only.** Player-facing content is delivery-
  ready: read-aloud runs long enough to seed every check or choice printed under
  it and ends on an actionable draw (`callouts` references/read-aloud.md);
  exposition is bulleted NPC speech; rumor/table entries are complete voiced
  lines, never topic fragments. Check: any player-facing line can be read or
  paraphrased aloud as written. *(Potent Brew's Glowkindle bullets; Darker
  Dungeons' voiced rumors; Night Hunter's speakable bullets.)*
- **R14 — Improv scaffolding at every open point.** Where play is open, the
  document hands the runner material: zone word-palettes with signature
  senses, seed/rumor tables of concrete entries, environment-adaptive combat
  principles, named blank-canvas expansion points. Check: no open scene whose
  only support is "improvise". *(Serpent Kings' thematic-area palettes; Darker
  Dungeons' d100 seeds; Wild Sheep Chase's terrain principles.)*
- **R15 — Player deviation pre-handled as permission.** Likely shortcuts, clever
  plans, and absent resources get contingency notes framed as "let it happen,
  here's the fallback" — never as plot defense. Check: the two most obvious
  derailments have a written answer. *(Wild Sheep Chase "Not Like This...";
  Night Hunter's no-pack-animal fallback; Silverpine Troubleshooting.)*
- **R16 — Telegraphs and foreshadowing are planted.** Every trap or ambush has
  an in-fiction tell players can notice; key details are repeated across scenes
  (roughly three exposures) before they pay off. Check: for each hazard, name
  its tell; for each twist, its plants. *(Potent Brew's bisected rat; Silverpine's
  nervous horses; Serpent Kings' trap tells.)*

## D. Trust, intent, and safety

- **R17 — The trust boundary is drawn on purpose.** Mechanics are closed
  (numbers never delegated); fiction, staging, and wording are open — and the
  document says so: check lists declared non-exhaustive with an adjudication
  principle, permission statements written out, scaffolding designed to be
  outgrown. Check: the document states at least once what it deliberately
  leaves to the runner. *(Silverpine's "really just advice... reward
  specificity"; Darker Dungeons' boxed permissions; Serpent Kings' "I Disagree
  With...".)*
- **R18 — Design intent ships with the content.** Units carry their reasoning —
  a Lessons/intent note, an overview naming the target experience and player,
  operator's notes for complex pieces (encounter shape, tuning levers, why the
  signature mechanic exists). Check: a future editor can answer "what is this
  piece for?" without archaeology. *(Serpent Kings' Lessons boxes; Pointy Hat
  overviews; [the Necromaton](docs/exemplars/pointyhat-the-necromaton.md)'s Tips on Running.)*
- **R19 — Safety is inline, procedural, and costed.** Dark-content warnings sit
  where the content is (or gate the document), phrased as a table procedure
  (who checks with whom), with mitigation options whose costs are stated;
  lethality valves are built in-fiction where appropriate. Check: each flagged
  theme has warning + mitigation + cost. *(Pleasantries' spoiler-aware costed
  warnings; Way of the Flesh's gate; Night Hunter's rescue valve.)*
- **R20 — All reachable endings are written.** Success, failure, and refusal
  each get concrete aftermath; consequences and outward-pointing draws are
  pre-designed per ending. Check: enumerate the endings the table can reach —
  each has text. *(Wild Sheep Chase's three endings; Pleasantries' per-ending
  epilogues; Night Hunter's aftermath.)*
