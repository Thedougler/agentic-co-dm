# Influences — the moves, and what stays behind

Techniques the GM has taken from work they admire, stated so each can be applied
to material with no connection to its source. Reach for a named move here when a
passage needs a technique; the source work's characters, places, and plots never
travel with it.

Every entry carries both halves. **Moves** is what transferred. **Stays behind**
is what must not reach this repo's prose — naming the excluded half is what makes
the rest safe to apply. These are techniques available to reach for, not a
procedure to follow: a move is offered, and what a passage does with it is the
writer's call. The `writing-style` skill grows this page; new entries are
appended, never substituted for existing ones.

## Stephen King

The GM's favourite author.

**Prosaic/terror juxtaposition.** Place the horrific directly against mundane,
idyllic detail rather than isolating it in its own atmosphere, so the ordinariness
itself is the source of unease.

**Slow-burn community construction.** Front-load vignette character and place
building with no plot urgency, so a later threat lands against an already-peopled
social fabric instead of a blank setting.

**Surface-vs-substrate reveal.** Establish an idealised surface impression of a
place, then reveal corruption that predates the threat, so the threat reads as an
amplifier of existing rot rather than an unrelated intrusion.

**Fragmented assembly with visible seams.** Build a longer work from originally
separate pieces and leave the transitions compressed and elliptical rather than
smoothing them into continuous chronology.

**Register departure as a mode signal.** Shift to a sparser, more mannered
sentence construction — against the surrounding register — to tell the audience
they have entered a different mode before any exposition confirms it.

**Embedded first-person interlude.** Deliver deep background as a first-person
document set inside a third-person narrative; the shift in person marks it as
supplementary without breaking the reading flow.

**Recurrence-pattern revelation.** Space discrete past incidents through the work
so that only in aggregate does the audience recognise a cycle the present-day
characters have not yet perceived.

**Distributed ensemble with real interiority.** Give secondary figures full
backstory and genuine perspective even when their arc is short, rather than
anchoring everything to a single viewpoint.

**Verb-forward plainness.** Strong direct verbs over adjective and adverb
modification; the first honest word over the fancier synonym.

**Sentence-length modulation as pacing.** Short clipped sentences through high
tension, longer elaborated ones through characterisation and scene-setting — the
ratio is itself the pacing signal.

**Dialogue roughness.** Incomplete sentences, regional idiom, and fragments in
dialogue, set against cleaner narration, so speech reads as overheard rather than
authored.

**Stays behind.** Horror itself. The GM takes King as a craftsman and not as a
genre — "horror doesn't really do it for me." Every move above is a staging
technique that works on any material; none of them is a licence to turn an arc
to dread, and gore, shock, and monster-forward framing are not available as
defaults because the techniques came from a horror writer.

## J.R.R. Tolkien — prose

Taken as prose technique, separate from the film adaptations below.

**Depth via allusion and gap.** Reference past events, names, and objects without
explaining them; the acknowledged gap, not the explanation, is what produces the
sensation of a world larger than the story being told.

**Pseudo-historical framing.** Present invented backstory as the compilation of
an in-world historian or record-keeper rather than as authorial narration, which
launders exposition as documentation and changes its felt authority.

**Interlace.** Interweave concurrent threads by cutting between them at chosen
break points, with small shared landmark details both threads reference, so the
true chronology reconstructs afterwards — suspense now, pattern-recognition later.

**Etymological naming consistency.** Derive names from a root system so that names
from one in-world culture share sound-patterns, letting an audience sort
unfamiliar names by origin purely by ear. The naming decisions themselves belong
to `campaign-domain-modeling`.

**Sparse true archaism.** Achieve an old feel through syntax and cadence rather
than vocabulary, keeping genuinely archaic words rare and marked.

**Register-by-population.** Give each culture a consistent linguistic register so
register itself signals identity, with a plainer viewpoint group mediating the
audience's entry into an elevated one.

**Parataxis for elevated cadence.** Link clauses with "and" rather than
subordinating them; the flattened, list-like structure reads as exalted
independently of the vocabulary used.

**Stays behind.** Archaic diction worn as costume. Verse and songs. Appendix
lore. Extended landscape description whose purpose is to slow the piece into
dwelling — the specific reason the GM prefers the adaptations. Depth arrives by
allusion here, never by travelogue.

## Peter Jackson's adaptations — staging

Kept deliberately separate from the Tolkien prose entry above. The GM prefers
these to the books, and the preference is the point: merging the two produces the
prose the adaptations were chosen over.

**Parallel cutting over delayed simultaneity.** Where prose delays the reveal
that two threads were concurrent, intercut them continuously so the audience
watches them unfold together — trading the retrospective realisation for
real-time tension.

**Visual doubling as connective tissue.** Recurring visual and verbal motifs
across otherwise-separated threads signal "these are linked" without requiring
nonlinear structure.

**Scale-mixing.** Alternate wide framing for geography and scale against tight
framing for character within the same sequence, so spectacle never loses legible
individual stakes.

**Even action distribution.** Where a source's set-pieces fall unevenly,
redistribute spectacle so each instalment carries its own arc and climax rather
than inheriting the original's pattern of highs and lows.

**Match length to density.** Stretching a tightly-scoped source across more
runtime forces imported filler and dilutes pacing.

**Stays behind.** Cutting narrative material to serve pace — which subplots and
threads live or die is the DM's call, never an agent's, and an adaptation move
that licenses excision does not belong in a system that drafts for someone else's
table. The production techniques — forced perspective, scaled rigs, crowd
simulation — are filmmaking craft with no writing analogue. Spectacle for its own
sake: every move here keeps stakes legible *inside* scale, rather than pursuing
scale alone.

## Quentin Tarantino — Inglourious Basterds, Django Unchained

**Rubber-band dialogue.** Stretch a scene far past what its information
requires — let the talk stay mundane and tangential — once the audience knows a
threat is present. The tension comes from the stretch, not from the content of
the conversation, and it tightens for exactly as long as the ordinary talk
continues.

**One-sided-knowledge hold.** Give the audience, or one character, a fact
another character in the room lacks, then withhold the moment of realisation far
longer than the scene naturally would, letting it sit through several more
exchanges before it resolves or detonates.

**Dialogue as set-piece.** Build a conversation with the internal architecture of
an action sequence — opening feint, escalating probes, a mid-scene turn, a
climax — so what is said and concealed does the work a fight would otherwise do.

**Chaptered convergence.** Divide the story into titled, self-contained movements,
each with its own thread and cast, all independently built toward one fixed
future event where they physically meet. Related to interlace above and to
Ritchie's converging caper below; the distinguishing feature here is that the
destination is announced and the chapters are separated openly.

**Shock cut without the ramp.** Keep the surface calm right up to the trigger,
then cut straight to sudden brief violence with no transitional beat. The
abruptness itself is the effect — a build telegraphs and defuses it.

**Multi-party standoff.** Stage three or more opposed parties holding
simultaneous leverage over one another, so any single action cascades; adding a
further party mid-scene forces everyone to recalculate who holds power over whom.

**Anachronistic layering.** Dress or score a period piece with conspicuously
wrong-era material, foregrounded rather than hidden, so the mismatch marks the
work as constructed commentary and the tonal contrast comments on the scene.

**Pastiche as visible citation.** Build openly out of a prior genre's structural
DNA — archetypes, iconography, borrowed motifs — so the work reads at once as its
own story and as an explicit homage, rather than as disguised borrowing.

**Stays behind.** The period-offensive language these films use as a documented
feature. Graphic torture and gore as spectacle.

## Guy Ritchie

**Converging caper.** Run several storylines that appear entirely unconnected for
most of the length, each with its own goal and cast, then engineer a late
collision around one shared object or space so the outcome is built from
previously separate causal chains.

**Achronological reveal order.** Reorder events so the audience learns a fact at
a chosen distance from seeing its consequence. The underlying cause-and-effect
stays simple; the reordering is emphasis, never genuine structural complexity.

**Compounding complication.** Give nearly every scene a new problem or reversal
for at least one faction rather than letting scenes merely advance or explain.
The compounding rhythm produces the sensation of pace independently of how fast
anything is actually cut.

**Overlapping argot dialogue.** Minimal beats between lines, dense wordplay and
insult volleys, and a consistent in-group vernacular specific to the milieu — the
speed and the shared jargon both drive pace and mark who belongs.

**Faction choreography by narration.** When many small factions chase the same
contested object by different methods, a narrating voice plus targeted
flashbacks act as connective tissue, letting the story cut between parties who
have not met without the structure reading as confusion.

**Framing narrator whose account is revised.** Frame the story as one character's
retelling, then interrupt to concede a shown scene did not happen quite that way,
using the correction itself as a beat that recontextualises everything already
shown.

**Stays behind.** Retroactively unmaking events that were already shown as
true — canon here is additive and never contradicts a played fact
(`vault/CLAUDE.md` rule 2), so the revision device belongs to an unreliable
in-world *source* on a page, never to the campaign record itself.

## X-Men: Days of Future Past — dual-timeline structure

**Mutual-dependence linking.** Make each timeline's survival contingent on the
other, so cutting away from either never lowers the stakes in the one left
behind.

**Emotional-anchor bridging.** Route both strands' stakes through one figure who
is central to both, converting an abstract distant catastrophe into a personal
present cost.

**Success-breeds-complication midpoint.** Let the goal be achieved at the
midpoint, then immediately generate a fresh problem as bad as the one just
averted, so momentum never slackens after the apparent win.

**Antagonist as relationship reversal.** Convert the threat from an impersonal
force into a formerly trusted ally, moving the climax from "stop an enemy" to
"confront a betrayal" without adding plot machinery.

**Synchronised cross-cut climax.** Intercut a slow negotiation in one strand
against a kinetic siege in the other, so each cut recharges urgency the other had
let settle.

**Moral choice as climax.** Resolve the central confrontation on an ethical
choice rather than a power outcome, so the structural stakes cash out as
character change.

**The named flaw, kept as a check.** A ticking clock goes slack the moment the
clock-bearing character stops being logically necessary. Whatever supplies
urgency must still require the risk-bearer at the climax, not only to the
midpoint.

## Jujutsu Kaisen

**Bounded escalation.** Push power right to the edge of an established rule
system without ever breaking it, so new power reads as legitimate rather than
arbitrary.

**Costed trade-off.** A temporary power spike is bought by permanently
surrendering a different capability, so escalation carries lasting narrative cost
instead of arriving free.

**Reserved peak technique.** Hold a maximum move back as a rare structural climax
marker rather than a routine tool, and give opponents an explicit counter, so
nothing becomes narratively unbeatable.

**Stated tier hierarchy.** Publish a clear power ranking across the cast, so an
underdog beating a higher rank registers as meaningful rather than confusing.

**Ensemble-wide depth.** Build backstory and motivation across a broad cast
rather than centring escalation on one lead, so any character's loss can carry
full weight.

**Tonal whiplash.** Place a broad comic beat directly against a horror-registered
one with no ramp between them; the contrast intensifies both.

**Stays behind.** Palette and camera-kinetic coding of power tiers — an animation
device. The prose equivalent is King's register departure above.

## Fog Hill of Five Elements — visual reference only

No prose moves. Research found its craft to be animation and cinematography
throughout, with no written analogue reported: continuous-take fight
choreography that never breaks spatial continuity, a high unbroken frame rate
through impacts so strikes read as earned, and ink-wash brushstroke trails that
make motion itself the picture's texture.

Recorded so this stays available as a **visual** reference — for art direction,
battlemap and scene-art prompts — and so a later run does not mine it for prose
technique it does not have.

## Adapting dense source material

Drawn from the contrast between adaptations that worked and one that did not, and
recorded because this repo adapts constantly — story to run guide, wiki to table.

**Emotional truth over plot fidelity.** What survives adaptation is the
underlying emotional and thematic truth, not comprehensive plot coverage.

**Transform for the format.** A change of medium licenses restructuring rather
than compression alone; critics locate the difference between successful and
failed adaptations precisely here.

**Front-load the ensemble.** Establish each member and their private stakes
individually before the main engine engages, so later group peril lands on
people rather than on a group.

**Scale must match scope.** The named failure is cherry-picking fragments across
a sprawling mythology at insufficient runtime — coverage without throughline
makes the world feel smaller than its source.

**Commit to a divergent target, or don't diverge.** An adaptation may pursue a
different emotional target than its source; what critics reward is full
commitment to that choice rather than half-adapting toward both.

**Stays behind.** Authority to cut. These moves diagnose what an adaptation is
doing; which threads live or die is the DM's call, never an agent's — same
exclusion as the Jackson entry above.
