# Mechanic Theatricalization

Techniques attributed to Brennan Lee Mulligan for making dice rolls and skill checks land as dramatic beats rather than procedural interruptions.

## Box of Doom Principles

Public rolls for high-stakes moments serve three functions:

1. **Eliminate fudging.** Rolling in the open builds trust that danger is real.
2. **Create a shared dramatic climax.** The game master declares the DC and the consequences of both success and failure before the roll is made.
3. **Dice as collaborator.** The dice are left to govern roughly 20% of outcomes, injecting genuine uncertainty.

## Degrees of Success

For investigation or social rolls, the game master preps multiple difficulty thresholds in advance — the source's worked example is 10/15/20. A roll that clears only the low threshold still yields a crumb of information that keeps the story moving; a roll that clears a high threshold reveals a deeper secret. No dead ends result from a failed roll.

## Resource-for-Advantage Trade

The game master may make available the option for a player to sacrifice hit points or a spell slot in exchange for advantage on a roll that is personally meaningful to that player's character. Offered this way, the mechanic turns into a high-stakes narrative bet rather than a plain probability roll.
