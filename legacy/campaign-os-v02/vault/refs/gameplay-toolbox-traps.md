# Gameplay Toolbox — Traps

Traps should be used sparingly, or they lose their charm. Too many traps in an adventure makes players overly cautious, slowing the game. The best traps are either fleeting distractions skilled characters overcome quickly, or deadly puzzles requiring quick thinking and teamwork. Traps that are undetectable and inescapable are rarely fun.

**Parts of a Trap** (following the name):

- *Severity and Levels*: nuisance (unlikely to seriously harm characters of the indicated levels) or deadly (can grievously damage them), tied to a level range.
- *Trigger*: what sets it off (pressure plate, trip wire, turning a doorknob, wrong key, etc.).
- *Duration*: rounds/minutes/hours, "until destroyed or dispelled," or instantaneous (resolves instantly). If it resets after activating, that's noted; otherwise the trap is inert after activation.

Caution: a trap that's a nuisance at one level range could be deadly to lower-level characters.

## Example Traps (alphabetical)

> [!mechanic]
> **Collapsing Roof** — Deadly Trap (Levels 1–4). Trigger: a creature crosses a trip wire. Duration: Instantaneous.
>
> A trip wire 3 inches off the ground, stretched between two weak supports, collapses an unstable section of ceiling when crossed. Each creature beneath the ceiling section must succeed on a DC 13 [[dexterity|Dexterity]] save, taking 11 (2d10) Bludgeoning damage on failure or half on success. Rubble creates Difficult Terrain.
>
> - *Detect and Disarm*: Search action + DC 11 [[wisdom|Wisdom]] (Perception) check detects the trip wire and unstable ceiling. Once detected, the trip wire can be cut or avoided with no further check.
> - *At Higher Levels*: Levels 5–10: 22 (4d10) damage, DC 15. Levels 11–16: 55 (10d10) damage, DC 17. Levels 17–20: 99 (18d10) damage, DC 19.

Falling Net is next, lighter in consequence.

> [!mechanic]
> **Falling Net** — Nuisance Trap (Levels 1–4). Trigger: a creature crosses a trip wire. Duration: Instantaneous.
>
> A trip wire (3 inches off ground, between two columns/trees) releases a weighted 10-foot-square Net suspended from the ceiling onto the first creature to cross it. Target must succeed on a DC 10 Dexterity save or be [[restrained|Restrained]] until it escapes (auto-succeeds if Huge or larger). A creature can use an action for a DC 10 [[strength|Strength]] (Athletics) check to free itself or another creature in reach.
>
> - *Detect and Disarm*: Search action + DC 11 Wisdom (Perception) detects the trip wire and Net; then cut or avoid freely.
> - *Destroy the Net*: reducing the Net to 0 HP frees any trapped creature (Net stats under Adventuring Gear, not included here).
> - *Set the Trap*: a creature with [[thieves-tools|Thieves' Tools]] and all components (including a Net) can set it with a DC 13 Dexterity (Sleight of Hand) check; each attempt takes 10 minutes.
> - *At Higher Levels*: heavier Net increases save/Athletics DC: 12 at levels 5–10, 14 at levels 11–16, 16 at levels 17–20.

Fire-Casting Statue trades a net for flame.

> [!mechanic]
> **Fire-Casting Statue** — Deadly Trap (Levels 1–4). Trigger: a creature moves onto a pressure plate. Duration: Instantaneous; resets at the start of the next turn.
>
> When a creature moves onto the pressure plate for the first time on a turn, or starts its turn there, a nearby statue (can look like anything) exhales a 15-foot Cone of magical flame. Each creature in the Cone must succeed on a DC 15 Dexterity save, taking 11 (2d10) Fire damage on failure or half on success.
>
> - *Detect and Disarm*: [[detect-magic|Detect Magic]] reveals an Evocation aura around the statue. Search action within 5 feet + DC 10 Wisdom (Perception) detects a tiny glyph. A Study action + DC 15 [[intelligence|Intelligence]] (Arcana) check identifies the glyph as meaning "fire." An action with a sharp tool defaces the glyph, disarming the trap. Separately, a Search action + DC 15 Wisdom (Perception) check on the floor section detects the pressure plate; wedging an Iron Spike or similar object under it prevents triggering.
> - *At Higher Levels*: Levels 5–10: 22 (4d10) damage, 30-foot Cone. Levels 11–16: 55 (10d10) damage, 60-foot Cone. Levels 17–20: 99 (18d10) damage, 120-foot Cone.

Hidden Pit needs no trigger tool at all, just weight.

> [!mechanic]
> **Hidden Pit** — Nuisance Trap (Levels 1–4). Trigger: a creature moves onto the pit's lid. Duration: Instantaneous.
>
> A 10-foot-deep pit has a hinged lid matching the surrounding floor; moving onto it swings it open, dropping the creature in (lid stays open after). Falling deals 3 (1d6) Bludgeoning damage.
>
> - *Detect and Disarm*: Study action + DC 15 Intelligence (Investigation) check on the lid section detects the pit. Once detected, an Iron Spike or similar wedged between the lid and floor prevents opening (safe to cross); an [[arcane-lock|Arcane Lock]] spell or similar magic can also hold the cover shut.
> - *Escape*: requires a Climb Speed, climbing gear, or magic like [[spider-climb|Spider Climb]] to scale the smooth walls (DM can add handholds/footholds to ease escape).
> - *At Higher Levels*: Levels 5–10: 30-foot pit, 10 (3d6) damage. Levels 11–16: 60-foot pit, 21 (6d6) damage. Levels 17–20: 120-foot pit, 42 (12d6) damage.

Poisoned Darts hide in the walls, not the floor.

> [!mechanic]
> **Poisoned Darts** — Deadly Trap (Levels 1–4). Trigger: a creature moves onto a pressure plate. Duration: Instantaneous; resets at the start of the next turn if it has activated fewer than three times.
>
> Moving onto the plate for the first time on a turn fires poisoned darts from wall-embedded tubes (holes hidden by dust/cobwebs or disguised in decor). Each creature in the darts' path must succeed on a DC 13 Dexterity save or be struck by 1d3 darts, taking 3 (1d6) Poison damage per dart.
>
> - *Detect and Disarm*: Search action + DC 15 Wisdom (Perception) on the walls detects the holes (plugging them with wax/cloth/detritus prevents firing). Separately, Search action + DC 15 Wisdom (Perception) on the floor section detects the pressure plate; wedging an Iron Spike or similar under it prevents triggering.
> - *At Higher Levels*: per-dart damage increases: 7 (2d6) at levels 5–10, 14 (4d6) at levels 11–16, 24 (7d6) at levels 17–20.

Poisoned Needle punishes the wrong key, not the wrong step.

> [!mechanic]
> **Poisoned Needle** — Nuisance Trap (Levels 1–4). Trigger: a creature opens the trap's lock improperly, or fails to disarm the trap. Duration: Instantaneous.
>
> A needle hidden in a lock springs out and stabs whoever opens the lock with anything other than the proper key. The creature makes a DC 11 [[constitution|Constitution]] save; failure: 5 (1d10) Poison damage + [[poisoned|Poisoned]] for 1 hour; success: half damage only.
>
> - *Avoid*: doesn't trigger if opened via [[knock|Knock]] or similar magic.
> - *Detect and Disarm*: Search action + DC 15 Wisdom (Perception) detects the needle. Once detected, an action + DC 15 Dexterity (Sleight of Hand) check disarms it; a failed check triggers the trap.
> - *At Higher Levels*: Levels 5–10: 11 (2d10) damage, DC 13. Levels 11–16: 22 (4d10) damage, DC 15. Levels 17–20: 55 (10d10) damage, DC 17.

Rolling Stone is a different scale of danger, built for higher levels.

> [!mechanic]
> **Rolling Stone** — Deadly Trap (Levels 11–16) or Nuisance Trap (Levels 17–20). Trigger: a creature moves onto a pressure plate. Duration: until the stone stops rolling.
>
> Moving onto a hidden pressure plate releases a 5-foot-radius stone orb from a secret compartment, which begins to roll. The stone and nearby creatures roll Initiative; the stone gets +8 to its roll. On its turn the stone moves 60 feet in one direction, changing course if redirected by an obstacle; it can move through creatures' spaces and vice versa (treated as Difficult Terrain for creatures). Whenever the stone enters a creature's space for the first time on a turn, or a creature enters the stone's space while it's rolling, that creature must succeed on a DC 15 Dexterity save or take 55 (10d10) Bludgeoning damage and be [[prone|Prone]]. The stone stops upon hitting a wall/barrier; it can't turn corners (though passages can be built with curves to keep it moving).
>
> - *Detect and Disarm*: Study action + DC 15 Intelligence (Investigation) on the floor section deduces the pressure plate's function. Wedging an Iron Spike or similar under it prevents triggering.
> - *Destroy the Stone*: Large object, AC 17, HP 100, Damage Threshold 10, [[immunity|Immunity]] to Poison and Psychic damage.
> - *Slow the Stone*: an action + DC 20 Strength (Athletics) check reduces the stone's movement that turn by 15 feet on success; at 0 feet it stops and is no longer a threat.

Spiked Pit finishes the alphabet, and the list, with old-fashioned spikes.

> [!mechanic]
> **Spiked Pit** — Deadly Trap (Levels 1–4). Trigger: a creature moves onto the pit's lid. Duration: Instantaneous.
>
> A 10-foot-deep pit with a hinged, floor-matching lid; moving onto it drops the creature onto sharpened wooden/metal spikes at the bottom (lid stays open after). Damage: 3 (1d6) Bludgeoning (fall) + 9 (2d8) Piercing (spikes).
>
> - *Detect and Disarm*: Study action + DC 15 Intelligence (Investigation) on the lid section detects the pit. An Iron Spike or similar wedged between lid and floor prevents opening; an [[arcane-lock|Arcane Lock]] spell or similar can also hold the cover shut.
> - *Escape*: requires Climb Speed, climbing gear, or magic like [[spider-climb|Spider Climb]] (DM can add handholds/footholds to ease escape).
> - *At Higher Levels*: Levels 5–10: 30-foot pit, 10 (3d6) Bludgeoning + 13 (3d8) Piercing. Levels 11–16: 60-foot pit, 21 (6d6) Bludgeoning + 36 (8d8) Piercing. Levels 17–20: 120-foot pit, 42 (12d6) Bludgeoning + 57 (13d8) Piercing.
