# The Default World

A world the rules assume and never draw. They name its planes, its magic and its peoples. Then they stop at the coast and hand the map over.

## Overview

The rules describe the physics a setting runs on. They leave out what a player would recognise: countries, cities, gods with names, a calendar, a history. Strip the proper nouns from any D&D game and this is what stays behind.

That leftover is still a real place. One Material Plane sits at the centre. Echoes shadow it, elemental realms feed it, and the homes of the gods ring it further out. Magic there is steady and teachable. The same spell behaves the same way for whoever casts it, and a village that has never met a wizard still sits somewhere wizards can exist. Elves, orcs and halflings walk the same roads without anyone explaining why.

This page is the floor a new campaign starts from when the DM has not yet picked a world. The moment a game needs a coastline or a god with a name, it wants a world page of its own.

### Tenets

1. Magic obeys stated rules, and those rules do not bend for the powerful.
2. The Material Plane is one plane among many, and a traveller can reach the others. Distance is not the only direction a thing arrives from.
3. Gods act through mortals. The rules leave their names to the table.
4. Many ancestries share the world as ordinary neighbours.
5. Adventurers are rare. Most people here never gain a level.

## Geography

The rules draw no map at all. This is the biggest gap a new world fills, and the reason nobody can run a bare rule set as a setting.

What the rules do assume about land is practical. Travel runs at stated paces over easy and hard ground. Settlements scale from village to city. The wild country between them stays dangerous enough that armed escort is a paying trade. Any map that honours those three assumptions fits the rules.

The published worlds each answer the question their own way. Toril, Oerth, Krynn, Athas and Eberron all sit on the Material Plane and share its planes and its magic, while differing in almost everything else. [[faerun|Faerûn]] is the one this table uses.

## Peoples & Powers

The rules hand over peoples without placing them. Humans, dwarves, elves, halflings, gnomes, orcs, tieflings, dragonborn and goliaths all live somewhere in this world. Where, though? Nothing says which are common, which are strangers, or which draw a second look in a foreign port, and answering that region by region is the most useful thing a world page adds over the bare rules.

Organisations get the same treatment. The rules name no guild, church, crown or trading house. Every faction at a table belongs to that table.

## Faith & Pantheon

Divine power works as machinery here. Clerics take domains and paladins swear oaths, while warlocks strike their bargains with patrons. The machinery runs without naming one god.

For names, the SRD offers four pantheons drawn from real mythology, and a DM can lift one whole and run it: Celtic gods out of Irish and Gaulish myth, Greek gods off Olympus, Egyptian gods from the Nile, and Norse gods out of Scandinavian myth. One representative deity from each follows below.

| Deity | God of | Domains | Symbol |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Daghdha | Weather and crops | Nature, Trickery | Bubbling cauldron or shield |
| Zeus | The sky, ruler of the gods | Tempest | Fist full of lightning bolts |
| Re-Horakhty | The sun, ruler of the gods | Life, Light | [[solar\|Solar]] disk encircled by a serpent |
| Odin | Knowledge and war | Knowledge, War | A watching blue eye |

D&D's own house pantheon sits outside the SRD. Pelor, Moradin, Corellon Larethian, Yondalla, Kord, Bahamut, Tiamat, Lolth and Vecna come from the Greyhawk list printed in the Player's Handbook. A table can use them freely at home. Reproducing their table in a published document needs a licence the SRD does not grant.

## Cosmology & Planes

The rules describe this part in full, and every world built on them inherits it whole.

The **Material Plane** carries the mortal world. Two **echoes** shadow it. The **Feywild** runs brighter and stranger. The **Shadowfell** runs drained and grieving.

The **Astral Plane** and the **Ethereal Plane** lie between everything else, and most planar travel crosses one of them.

The **Elemental Planes** of Air, Earth, Fire and Water supply the raw stuff the Material Plane got made from. They turn violent toward their borders.

The **Outer Planes** house the powers: the Abyss, Acheron, Arborea, Arcadia, the Beastlands, Bytopia, Carceri, Elysium, Gehenna, Hades, Limbo, Mechanus, Mount Celestia, the Nine Hells, Pandemonium and Ysgard. Wider D&D canon rings them with the Outlands and sets the city of Sigil at its hub, where doors open onto every plane at once.

## History & Calendar

The rules supply neither. `current_date` sits empty on this page for that reason. A world built on this baseline picks its own calendar on its first day, and dates everything from there.

- **(none)** — This baseline carries no history of its own; a world instantiated from it writes its first line the day the DM names a calendar, the way [[faerun|Faerûn]] dates its own history by Dalereckoning.

## Campaigns in This World

## GM Notes

The planes, the peoples, the magic and the four mythological pantheons come from the 5e System Reference Document, which Wizards of the Coast released under Creative Commons Attribution. That material is free to reproduce.

The rest summarises wider D&D canon in original prose: the Greyhawk house pantheon, Sigil and the Outlands, and the named published worlds. That canon stays Wizards' property, so this page describes the material instead of copying it. The same rule governs [[faerun|Faerûn]], and any other published setting the vault documents.

Nothing here is secret. A player can read the page start to finish.
