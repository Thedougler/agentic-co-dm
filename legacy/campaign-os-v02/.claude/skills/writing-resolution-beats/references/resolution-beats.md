# Resolution Beat Reference

Consult this file when establishing the state delta, building a consequence envelope or actual fallout, writing the DM-facing body, or running the validation gate.

The procedure belongs in `../SKILL.md`. This file owns the detailed Resolution model so the main workflow stays short and predictable.

## Resolution invariant

A Resolution is the final Beat of its containing dramatic unit.

Its job is to show what became true because of the Climax outcome. Only a Climax precedes it. The Resolution begins **after the central dramatic question has been answered or permanently obsoleted**.

For sandbox play, preserve the same function while changing the control model:

> **Earned fallout; open future.**

The GM shows the world's causal response to what happened. The players retain control of what they do next.

The Resolution therefore establishes new state without establishing new rails.

---

## Leading concepts

Use these terms consistently while reasoning about the Beat.

**Fallout** — the material difference between the world before the Climax and the world after its consequences land.

**Gravity receipt** — the visible change to a player-gravity anchor caused by the Climax, stated as external circumstance rather than authored PC reaction.

**Consequence envelope** — the PREP-mode deliverable: a response model that prepares the world to react to plausible outcomes without choosing one.

**State delta** — the structured comparison of pre-Climax and post-Climax world state across every dimension that matters.

---

## Core rules

### Climax decides; Resolution reflects

The Climax answers or permanently obsoletes the adventure's central dramatic question. The Resolution begins **after that answer exists**. If the scene still requires the players to answer the central dramatic question, it is still Climax.

### Fallout changes state

The Resolution records the meaningful difference between the world before the Climax and the world after its consequences land. A Resolution that only celebrates, narrates, or says goodbye without establishing what changed is incomplete.

### Prep consequences, not endings

Before the Climax has occurred, prepare consequence rules, affected actors, likely pressure responses, rewards at stake, relationships that may change, and questions the DM will answer after play. Do not prepare a canonical sequence of player actions. Do not decide which consequence state becomes true before play decides it.

### Preserve earned outcomes

A Resolution may complicate a victory, expose a surviving threat, reveal an escape, produce capture, or widen the scale of the problem. It must not erase a player-earned result through unearned GM fiat. The world may answer the players. The Resolution may reveal that the players did not control every consequence. Neither permits silently changing what their successful choices accomplished.

### Cause precedes consequence

Every material consequence must trace to a player choice, a Climax outcome, an established NPC intention, an established faction pressure, an established property of the situation, a previously established threat, or an event that actually occurred during play. The Resolution does not manufacture surprise consequences solely because an ending needs more drama.

### Pay player gravity

Resolution is where player investments receive **receipts**. A gravity receipt shows how what the character cared about has changed because of play. Never dictate how a PC feels about the receipt. Present the changed circumstance. The player owns the reaction.

### End the adventure

A Resolution may leave campaign threads open. It must end **this adventure's** dramatic question. If the page finishes by requiring immediate continuation of the same unresolved contest, the Climax has not actually resolved.

## State delta construction

Build the delta before writing the aftermath. Compare the pre-Climax state with the post-Climax state across only the dimensions that matter.

Check:

- **Objective** — Who controls, destroyed, saved, exposed, recovered, or lost the thing at stake?
- **Opposition** — Who survived, surrendered, escaped, fractured, gained leverage, or ceased to matter?
- **People** — Who lives, dies, disappears, returns, is captured, or becomes free?
- **Relationships** — Which trust, loyalty, debt, rivalry, oath, romance, feud, or alliance materially changed?
- **Position** — What authority, access, reputation, territory, legitimacy, or social standing changed?
- **Resources** — What was spent, gained, broken, stolen, consumed, awarded, or made unavailable?
- **Knowledge** — What became known, public, disproven, secret, or impossible to conceal?
- **World pressure** — What danger ended, persisted, transformed, or became newly relevant?
- **Player gravity** — Which established goal, fear, bond, rival, obligation, or backstory element received a consequence?

For every material delta, establish:

**cause → new truth → visible evidence**

Example:

`The party exposed the admiral's pact → the admiral loses legitimacy → dockworkers tear down his banners before dawn.`

The visible evidence gives the DM something concrete to put in front of the players instead of explaining abstract campaign state.

## PREP branch

Use when the Climax has not happened.

### Identify outcome variables

Identify **2–5 outcome variables** the Climax is actually capable of changing. Phrase each as a question.

Examples:

- Who leaves with the Crown?
- Does Marrow survive?
- Does the city learn what the Council did?
- Which ship remains seaworthy?
- Does Phoebe keep her promise to the Ember Court?

Do not enumerate every combination of answers.

**Complete when:** the variables collectively describe the meaningful uncertainty of the Climax without prescribing how the players resolve it.

### Prepare consequence rules

For each outcome variable, prepare only the consequences the world already gives you reason to expect. Use conditional rules.

Examples:

- **If Marrow survives:** [[Marrow]] retreats to the patron who can still shelter him.
- **If the evidence becomes public:** [[Dockworkers Guild]] withdraws support from [[Admiral Vey]].
- **If the lighthouse falls:** traffic through [[North Passage]] stops until another beacon is established.

Prefer consequences that emerge from existing actors pursuing existing motives. Avoid large branching trees. A Resolution Envelope is a **response model**, not a choose-your-own-ending document.

**Complete when:** for every outcome variable, the DM can determine the relevant consequences after play by applying existing world logic rather than inventing a new ending.

### Build the Resolution Envelope

Prepare a short set of things the DM can look for immediately after the Climax:

**After the Climax, establish:**

- Who or what achieved the central objective?
- Who remains able to act?
- What did the party preserve, sacrifice, expose, or lose?
- What becomes visibly different immediately?
- Which established actor responds first?
- Which player-gravity investment receives a consequence?
- Which unresolved pressure genuinely remains?

Then include only the conditional consequences that the DM could not derive instantly from the linked pages.

Do not include speculative prose describing how the PCs celebrate, grieve, forgive, regret, retire, reconcile, or otherwise react internally.

**Complete when:** a DM who knows the Climax result can turn the envelope into the actual Resolution without consulting a scripted ending.

## COMPILE branch

Use when the Climax outcome is known.

### Establish what actually happened

Read the authoritative play evidence. Prefer, in order:

1. explicit DM ruling;
2. completed session transcript or session record;
3. accepted ingest evidence;
4. already-updated canon pages.

Separate: what happened; what characters learned; what the world can reasonably know; what remains DM-only.

Do not convert speculation into Player-Known fact. Do not promote hypothetical Resolution Envelope branches merely because they were prepared. The prepared envelope is scaffolding. Actual play is authoritative.

**Complete when:** every asserted Climax outcome and every player-caused consequence can be traced to authoritative play evidence or established world behavior. No unused hypothetical branch survives as fact.

### Apply fallout

For each state delta, ask: **What does the world do with this now?**

Resolve only immediate or clearly inevitable consequences. Prefer:

- visible aftermath;
- NPC decisions;
- faction responses;
- changed control;
- rewards and losses;
- altered relationships;
- newly public information;
- immediate institutional consequences;
- short-term environmental effects.

Defer long-range developments whose outcomes still depend on future choices.

Good: `[[The Copper Guard]] arrests the three councillors whose signed orders were recovered.`

Too far: `Within five years the Copper Guard becomes the dominant political institution in the region.`

The first is fallout. The second is a future history the campaign has not played.

**Complete when:** every included consequence is either immediate, already occurring, or inevitable from established conditions without requiring unresolved future choices.

### Pay gravity receipts

For every materially implicated player-gravity anchor, determine whether the Climax changed it. Record the changed external circumstance.

Good:

- `[[Rhea]] kept the promise she made to [[Captain Orso]]: his crew comes home alive.`
- `[[Tomas]] has the title he wanted; [[House Venn]] now publicly recognizes his claim.`
- `[[Neris]] destroyed the laboratory that created her, but [[Doctor Calve]] escaped with the research.`

Avoid:

- `Rhea finally feels worthy.`
- `Tomas realizes power was never what he wanted.`
- `Neris forgives herself.`

The first set gives the players material. The second set authors their characters for them.

Not every PC requires equal spotlight in every Resolution. Every gravity thread materially implicated by the adventure does require accounting.

**Complete when:** every relevant player investment receives its earned external consequence, and no PC's internal response is predetermined.

### Select the Resolution pattern

Consult `resolution-types.md`. Select the pattern that best describes the actual aftermath. More than one may be factually present. Choose one as the primary structural pattern and treat others as secondary consequences when useful.

An escape, larger threat, or ending cliffhanger requires particular scrutiny because each can accidentally revoke closure.

**Complete when:** the selected pattern describes what play produced and does not require changing the Climax result to fit the pattern.

## Special rule — overturning the apparent result

*Scripting the Game* explicitly permits a Resolution to change the apparent meaning of the Final Battle. In sandbox play, interpret that permission narrowly.

A Resolution may establish that:

- the defeated villain had an established escape plan;
- winning one objective cost another;
- the destroyed weapon had already transmitted its message;
- the heroes won locally while a previously established larger force remains;
- the surrendered enemy possesses leverage that survives the surrender.

A Resolution may not arbitrarily declare:

- the defeated villain was secretly invulnerable;
- the destroyed artifact inexplicably reforms;
- the rescued prisoner was never the real prisoner;
- an unrelated greater villain appears solely to invalidate the victory;
- an unseen NPC reverses the party's accomplishment offscreen.

The test is **causality**. If the surprising consequence was already latent in the situation or caused by actual play, reveal it. If it exists only because the ending felt too easy, omit it.

## Sandbox principle — closure without rails

A prepared Resolution should make the world's responses predictable without making player behavior predictable.

Prepare the banks. Let the players determine where the water runs.

For Resolution:

- establish what NPCs want;
- establish what factions protect;
- establish what happens to uncontrolled threats;
- establish what resources can survive or be lost;
- establish which relationships are under pressure;
- establish the consequences attached to player gravity.

Then let the Climax determine which of those facts become relevant.

## DM-facing body contract

Use the repository's existing frontmatter template. The following contract governs the body when the template leaves discretion.

```markdown
# <Resolution Title>

## Resolution

**Landing:** One or two sentences describing the first concrete image, reaction, or changed circumstance after the Climax.

### What Changed
- One causal state change per bullet.
- Link the affected canonical entities.
- Include every material delta and nothing merely decorative.

### Reactions
- Include only NPCs or factions whose immediate response matters.
- One concise reaction per bullet.
- Express intention or action, not biography.

### Gravity Receipts
- One bullet per materially affected PC investment.
- State the external consequence.
- Leave player interpretation open.

### Open Horizon
- Include only genuinely unresolved consequences.
- Maximum three bullets.
- Omit the section if the adventure leaves none.
```

The runnable body normally stays **under 250 words**. Exceed that bound only when removing material would hide a consequential state change the DM needs. Do not add boxed read-aloud prose unless the repository or user specifically asks for narration.

### Runtime compression

The DM-facing note is an execution surface, not a design essay. Prioritize:

1. what became true;
2. why the party's investment now looks different;
3. who responds immediately;
4. what remains genuinely open.

Keep extensive biography, faction history, location description, mechanics, and reusable lore on their owning pages and reach them with `[[wikilinks]]` or precise embeds. The beat page owns only what is unique to **this aftermath of this situation**.

## Validation gate

Run every applicable gate before completion.

### Placement gate

- The Resolution follows a genuine Climax.
- The adventure's central dramatic question has been answered or permanently obsoleted.
- No mandatory continuation of the same decisive contest remains.

### Fallout gate

- Every material post-Climax state change is represented.
- Every represented consequence has an identifiable cause.
- The Resolution establishes new state rather than merely narrating celebration or defeat.

### Agency gate

- No unplayed player decision is presented as fact.
- No PC emotion, belief change, forgiveness, regret, or future choice is dictated.
- No offscreen fiat negates an earned player accomplishment.
- Any escape, reversal, rescue, or greater threat follows established causality.

### Gravity gate

- Every materially implicated gravity anchor is accounted for.
- Gravity receipts describe changed circumstances rather than authored PC reactions.
- Prepared consequences originate from established investment rather than a newly invented reason the PCs must care.

### Sandbox integrity gate

- PREP mode contains consequence conditions rather than predetermined endings.
- COMPILE mode contains actual fallout rather than unused hypothetical branches.
- Future campaign possibilities remain possibilities until play resolves them.

### DM usability gate

- The landing is immediately understandable.
- Each state-change bullet carries one consequence.
- Only relevant actors receive reactions.
- Open Horizon contains no more than three items.
- The runnable Resolution is normally ≤250 words.
- The DM can use it without reading explanatory craft prose.

### Wiki gate

- The current repository template governs schema.
- Named canonical entities are wikilinked where useful.
- Enduring entity information remains on its owning page.
- Hypothetical prep has not been promoted into canon.
- Obsidian syntax is valid.
- No duplicated static content should instead be linked or embedded.

**Complete only when every applicable gate passes.**

## Failure modes

### The Resolution becomes another fight

**Symptom:** Players must still defeat, escape, expose, persuade, or decide something central.

**Diagnosis:** The Climax is unfinished.

**Repair:** Move the unresolved contest into Climax. Resume Resolution after its outcome exists.

### The ending is already written before play

**Symptom:** Prep says what the PCs will do, who they will spare, how they will feel, or which ending occurs.

**Diagnosis:** A script replaced a Resolution Envelope.

**Repair:** Convert predictions into outcome variables and causal consequence rules.

### Victory gets stolen

**Symptom:** The players succeed, then an unseeded twist declares that their success did not matter.

**Diagnosis:** Surprise replaced causality.

**Repair:** Preserve the accomplished objective. Keep only residual consequences supported by prior state or actual play.

### Nothing changed

**Symptom:** The page says everyone celebrates, mourns, rests, or goes home but establishes no changed state.

**Diagnosis:** Atmosphere replaced fallout.

**Repair:** Rebuild the state delta. Write the material changes first.

### The sequel hook arrives from nowhere

**Symptom:** A new mastermind, invasion, monster, conspiracy, or portal appears solely because the campaign should continue.

**Diagnosis:** Continuation replaced consequence.

**Repair:** Connect the future threat to existing pressure or omit it. A finished adventure is allowed to finish.

### The Resolution dictates PC emotion

**Symptom:** The page declares that a character forgives someone, regrets a choice, falls in love, abandons an ambition, or learns a moral lesson.

**Diagnosis:** GM authorship crossed into player authorship.

**Repair:** Present the event or changed relationship that invites the response and stop there.

### Every NPC gets an epilogue

**Symptom:** The Resolution becomes a cast encyclopedia.

**Diagnosis:** Completeness was confused with relevance.

**Repair:** Include only actors whose state changed or whose immediate response changes the situation. Link the rest.

### The page becomes campaign history

**Symptom:** The Resolution predicts months or years of uncontested future events.

**Diagnosis:** Immediate fallout expanded into authored future canon.

**Repair:** Stop at the first point where future player choices or unresolved actors can change the outcome.

### Prepared branches become canon

**Symptom:** An unused conditional consequence appears later as established history.

**Diagnosis:** PREP leaked into COMPILE.

**Repair:** Remove the unused branch and compile only what actual play established.

## Review procedure

When reviewing an existing Resolution:

1. Identify its central dramatic question.
2. Locate the actual Climax result.
3. Extract every factual consequence the Resolution asserts.
4. Trace every consequence backward to a cause.
5. Identify every PC action or emotion it assumes.
6. Identify every materially implicated player-gravity anchor.
7. Test closure.
8. Test the Resolution pattern.
9. Test wiki ownership and duplication.
10. Run the full validation gate.

Report failures by rule. Do not rewrite passing material merely to make it stylistically different.

## Reference basis

This framework combines two compatible models.

### Beat function

*Scripting the Game* defines the Resolution as the final Beat following the Climax and treats its purpose as establishing what the Climax outcome means for the world. It permits happy endings, villain deaths, escapes, captures, surrenders, ending cliffhangers, and greater threats as aftermath patterns. It also explicitly permits a Resolution to change the apparent meaning of the Final Battle — interpreted narrowly for sandbox play.

### Player gravity and agency

The Brennan Lee Mulligan player-gravity framing treats players as pursuing routes that make sense to their characters while the GM shapes the surrounding terrain. Preparation becomes reliable when consequences attach to things the characters already care about. The sandbox translation:

> **Honor the answer. Show the fallout. Leave the next choice open.**
