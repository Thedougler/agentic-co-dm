# Story structures — classic prose-fiction shapes

Full shapes for the prose-fiction cluster of `.claude/skills/draft-story/references/story-structures.md`'s
catalog. Index and cross-links: `.claude/skills/draft-story/references/story-structures.md`.

## Three-act structure

The plain default. Setup → confrontation → aftermath.

```
Act 1 (Setup)          → Establish stakes, hook the party, first threshold
Act 2 (Confrontation)  → Escalating complications, midpoint reversal
Act 3 (Aftermath)      → Convergence, climax, consequences
```

Applies at multiple scales simultaneously:

| Scale | Act I | Act II | Act III |
|---|---|---|---|
| Session | Strong Start / situation established | Complication / escalation | Consequence / cliffhanger |
| Story Arc | Hook / faction activated | Faction executes plan / players respond | Confrontation / aftermath (player-driven) |
| Campaign | World established / players embedded | Factions converge / stakes escalate | Final convergence (if players drive it) |

**Sandbox key**: Act III is *available*, not *inevitable*. Players may
resolve an arc in Act II, skip to Act III early, or let it collapse
without closure — all valid; `quest_status`/session outcome reflects
whichever actually happened. Act II is where most play happens — treating
it as "time before the real stuff starts" starves the richest act.

**Campaign scale**: a 3-session quest maps one act per session; a single
session maps acts to its opening/middle/climax (see
`.claude/skills/composing-beats/references/rhythm-and-pacing.md` § The Session Arc).

**Use for**: most quests and seasons — reach for something else only when
the content demands it.

## Fichtean curve

Rising action immediately, no flat exposition — a chain of crises, each
bigger, then aftermath.

```
     Crisis
        /\
       /  \
Crises     Aftermath
     /
Rising action (starts immediately)
```

**Use for**: heists, chases, sessions opening mid-danger, one-shots where
table time can't afford a slow open.

## In medias res (with flashback)

Start in the middle, fill in how they got here.

```
PRESENT (crisis) → PAST (setup) → PRESENT (development) →
PAST (key event) → PRESENT (climax) → AFTERMATH
```

**Use for**: mysteries, cold opens ("you wake in a cell"), sessions where
the *why* is the reveal. At the table, "past" beats can be flashback
scenes, recovered documents, or NPC testimony.

**Simple variant (no flashback structure)**: drop the party straight into
the most interesting moment and let context fill in through play rather
than a scripted PAST/PRESENT alternation — "the rope breaks on the third
floor of the burning warehouse" needs no flashback scene, just forward
motion. Default opening mode for sessions and scenes; formula: identify the
most interesting moment, start 30 seconds before it.

## Seven-point structure

Dan Wells' plot architecture — plan the ending first, then start the
party in the opposite state.

```
1. Hook        → Party starts in the opposite state from the ending
2. Plot Turn 1 → Call to action, the situation changes
3. Pinch 1     → Pressure applied, opposition shows its hand
4. Midpoint    → Party moves from reacting to acting
5. Pinch 2     → More pressure, the dark moment
6. Plot Turn 2 → Final piece of the puzzle, means to win obtained
7. Outcome     → The objective achieved or definitively lost
```

**Use for**: plotting a quest quickly with a clear spine; multi-session
arcs where the midpoint reaction→action flip gives the party agency.

## Kishōtenketsu (four-act, no central conflict)

Japanese/Chinese/Korean structure that turns on recontextualization, not
opposition.

```
Ki (Introduction)  → Establish characters, place
Shō (Development)  → Deepen without conflict
Ten (Twist)        → A surprising element reframes everything
Ketsu (Conclusion) → Reconcile, new understanding
```

**Use for**: downtime sessions, quiet character-focused interludes,
lore reveals where the twist is understanding, not a fight — a session can
land hard with no combat at all.

## The Hero's Journey (monomyth)

Campbell's 12-stage mythic structure.

```
ORDINARY WORLD → CALL TO ADVENTURE → REFUSAL OF THE CALL →
MEETING THE MENTOR → CROSSING THE THRESHOLD → TESTS, ALLIES, ENEMIES →
APPROACH TO INMOST CAVE → ORDEAL → REWARD → THE ROAD BACK →
RESURRECTION → RETURN WITH ELIXIR
```

**Use for**: a whole campaign or a season-length transformation arc for
one PC — too heavy for a single quest. Stages are checkpoints, not a
script; the party will skip and reorder them. Applied per-PC rather than
to the whole campaign, useful stages narrow to: **Call & Refusal** (what
would make this character refuse to engage fully — play it early),
**The Ordeal** (the character's lowest point, where a core belief is
tested — let it emerge from play, never manufacture it), and **Return
with Elixir** (what the character has gained that they choose to give
back to the world — the arc's payoff).

## Frame narrative

A story within a story.

```
Outer frame: someone tells the story
    Inner story: the main narrative
Outer frame: return, reflect
```

**Use for**: NPC testimony sessions, prophecy/vision sequences, a bard's
tale the party later walks into — and unreliable narrators the table can
catch lying.

## Circular / bookend structure

End where you began, transformed.

```
Opening image → events → closing image (mirrors the opening)
```

**Key**: the return reveals how the party or the place has changed.

**Use for**: season finales returning to the starting town, PC-arc
payoffs, campaigns that close on their first scene's location.

## Non-linear shapes

- **Reverse chronology** — end → middle → beginning; the *why* lands last.
  Fits investigation arcs reconstructing a crime.
- **Parallel timelines** — past A → present A → past B → present B →
  convergence; fits split-party play and generational lore.
- **Mosaic / fragmented** — fragments the players assemble into meaning;
  fits rumor networks and clue-trails (pair with the Three-Clue Rule,
  `.claude/skills/composing-beats/references/runtime-surface.md`).
