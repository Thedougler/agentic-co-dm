# Travel roles — the player-agency lever

Full system: [[wilderness-travel-and-exploration|Sly Flourish's LGMRD
wilderness travel]] — read it, never restate it. This file covers only how
the roles plug into a travel leg's events.

## The offer

Before the leg's first event, offer the three roles to real PCs by name —
Trailhand (Nature or Survival, keeps the party on path), Scout (Insight,
Investigation, Nature, Perception, or Survival, spots trouble first),
Quartermaster (Medicine or Survival, manages food and water). Their
targets run 10-20, default 12, per the LGMRD page; group stealth doubles
travel time. Every leg of 2+ events assigns roles: the roles are the players'
hands on the journey — who watches, who leads, who rations — and turning
them down is itself a choice with consequences the DM may narrate.

**At sea — this campaign's default method — the canon system is
[[ship-operations|Ship Operations]] § Stations Underway:** the Trailhand
is the Navigator, the Scout is the Lookout, the Quartermaster holds the
name, and the Bosun and Captain stations join them — every PC calls a
station per leg, each station's failure arriving as the next complication.
Use those stations and DC tiers directly; the LGMRD trio below is the
overland/aerial form. A PC with sailor's tools or a sea background is the
natural advantage source — established gear only, never invented
(`event-tables.md`).

Match role to thread where the material allows: the PC whose backstory the
leg pulls on (the skill body’s § Spotlight balancing) picks first, and a role that
touches their thread ("<PC name> scouts — and recognizes the sail") beats a role
that doesn't.

## A failed check is the next event, not a punishment

Resolve a failed role check as the next complication, never as a flat
penalty. The failure *is* the
complication that leads into or reshapes an event: the Trailhand's wrong
fork lands the party at the landmark from the far side; the Scout misses
the sail until it's close enough to hail; the Quartermaster's spoiled cask
turns the next stop into a negotiation for water. The DC tier and
consequence severity come from the hazard table in
`event-tables.md`, cited per Hard Rule 2.

## Rolls stay in-fiction

The roles are the main place dice touch a travel leg: resolution rolls,
made through `roll-dice`, against a cited DC tier. Dice never decide what
the party encounters — event selection is derivation, not rolling
(the skill body’s § Deriving events from the party).
