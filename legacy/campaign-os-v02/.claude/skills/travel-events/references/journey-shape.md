# The journey shape — five slots that make a leg epic

Epic is structural, never travelogue. `vault/refs/stories/influences.md`
§ J.R.R. Tolkien keeps depth-by-allusion and interlace while its *Stays
behind* clause rejects "extended landscape description whose purpose is to
slow the piece into dwelling." A leg reads like the road to Mordor when all
five slots are filled, not when the scenery paragraph runs longer. Fill
them in order; each is one to three lines in the leg's travel moment.

## 1. Departure cost

One line: what the party leaves behind, owes, or forfeits by going now — a
contact left mid-negotiation, a berth paid for and abandoned, a rival free
to act unwatched. Departure with no cost is a scene transition wearing a
journey's clothes. The cost is a pressure the table can feel pulling
backward, never a mechanical fine.

- *Thin:* "You set out at dawn."
- *Filled:* "Serane's ledger stays unsigned on the harbourmaster's desk —
  every day at sea is a day her offer cools."

## 2. Landmark

At least one named, steerable thing on the leg — a wreck on a reef spur, a
toll-arch older than the kingdom, a light that shouldn't be burning. Named
means the DM can say it and the players can point at it; steerable means
the party chooses to approach, skirt, or ignore it, and the choice costs or
pays something. The landmark is where wonder lives and where player agency
gets a handle: an event can happen *at* it, but the landmark itself is not
an event and survives being ignored.

Depth-by-allusion is the Tolkien move here: the landmark implies a larger
history in one clause ("the arch's toll-gods have been chiselled blank")
and the page never explains it. When nothing established suggests a
landmark, pick the row of [[table-wilderness-encounters|the wilderness
encounters table]] that resonates with this party's live material and cite
it — picked for relevance, never rolled (Hard Rule 2).

## 3. Events

The typed events, composed per the skill body’s § Deriving events from the party
and § Composition rules. Interlace is the structural option when a leg runs
3+ events: cut away mid-tension to the other thread the leg carries (the
front advancing at the destination, the pursuer's sail on the horizon) and
return a beat later — the cut manufactures scale without a single extra
mile of description.

## 4. Toll

At least one stated, persistent cost of the crossing — never zero: days
against a clock, rations or water, a level of
[[vault/srd/rules/exhaustion|Exhaustion]], gear lost or spoiled, a wound
that stays until treated, a secret revealed under duress.
Numbers come from the toll menu in `event-tables.md` (keyed to
[[gameplay-toolbox|the gameplay toolbox]]), never invented. The toll is
what makes distance real to the table when the distance itself stays vague.

## 5. Arrival changed

One line: how the party arrives different from how it left — carrying a
debt, a passenger, a grudge, a question, one fewer secret, one more scar.
This line is mandatory (Composition rules): a leg that ends emotionally
where it started was a transition, not a journey. It feeds the travel
moment's Landing and gives the destination's first scene its opening
pressure.
