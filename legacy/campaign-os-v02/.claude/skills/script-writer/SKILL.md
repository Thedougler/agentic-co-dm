# Script Writer

## Overview

Produces one complete, ready-to-read screenplay for a player to perform as their PC — fun enough that they enjoy hacking through it, thorough enough that the recording gives a full voice profile: articulation, pitch range, pace, emotional range, and dynamics (whisper to shout). Think improv-game-show energy (Make Some Noise, Whose Line) more than a dry vocal-warmup sheet.

The file is a simple screenplay, nothing else — no bullet lists, no headers explaining a section's purpose, no frontmatter. Just the lines a human reads aloud, opening with one brief slate that addresses the actual reader as themselves before the character takes over. See § Format.

Every script is also a slow-burn con: the first half is a completely sincere, straight self-introduction; at the midpoint, the roast and vocal direction both begin at once, and every line after that grows less subtle than the last, right up to the close. The player discovers the roast by performing it, in character, line by line — the script itself never names, flags, or winks at the joke anywhere. See § The two-phase arc and § Keep the con invisible.

## Before writing

Always write fresh. A prior `<name>-voice-script.md` may exist from an earlier run — before drafting, delete it (`rm` the destination path) so there's nothing to read, reuse, or confuse with the new draft. These aren't canon and cost nothing to regenerate; treating an old draft as a base only carries its bugs forward. Every invocation composes the script from scratch off the PC page and session sources.

Check for `vault/campaigns/shattered-sea/pcs/<name>.md`. Found it → pull species, class, manner, catchphrases, and one concrete detail (an object, a relationship, a red line) to make lines specific, not generic. No page → ask 2-3 quick questions (species/vibe, one quirk, a phrase they'd say). Don't run a full interview — that's `dnd5e-character-interview`'s job.

Then mine roast fuel: pull 3-5 roastable specifics from `vault/campaigns/shattered-sea/pcs/<name>.md`'s Session Log and the session recaps under `vault/episodes/` — an embarrassing canon moment, a plan that went sideways, a contradiction between what the PC claims and what they do, an overused catchphrase, a prized possession that's objectively ridiculous. Every joke must trace to one of these. Swap-test each: if the line still lands with a different PC's name in it, it's a stock class/species joke — cut it and mine deeper.

Party roast fuel (always on, not opt-in): from `vault/campaigns/shattered-sea/pcs/<name>.md`'s Relationships section, pull every party member and mine one light, affectionate specific to needle them about — a running gag, a callback to a shared scene, a contrast between the speaking PC's worldview and theirs. One jab per party member, one line each, never more. Same swap-test and trace rule as the self-roast: a generic "my friend is annoying" line is stock filler — cut it and mine deeper.

DM/campaign roast fuel (second lane — when the player asks the script to also roast the DM or the campaign, or the DM requests it on themselves): mine 3-5 specifics from real campaign content, themes, and tropes — a recurring narrative device, a running bit, a genre convention played completely straight, a thematic pattern across sessions/NPCs. Never wiki data-hygiene bugs, contradictions, or stale/temporal errors — those aren't table-safe to read aloud, since the wiki is a living document and exposing its plumbing to players isn't the joke. Same trace rule as PC jokes: every jab names this campaign's specific wobble — a line that lands on any table ("the BBEG monologued too long") is stock DM-joke material, cut it and mine deeper.

Fiction only, no exceptions, every line in both phases: the script speaks about the character's in-fiction life only — name, species, class, canon events, relationships as written on the PC page. Never the real player's job, appearance, real-world relationships, other games, real events, or anything true of the human and not the character. `vault/campaigns/shattered-sea/pcs/players/<player>.md` is read only to check lines-and-veils, never mined as fuel — no fact from it, however harmless-looking, ever becomes a line, sincere or roast. This binds phase one exactly as hard as phase two: a "true, flattering detail" in the opening is a true detail about the *character*, not the player.

Safety rail: Read `vault/campaigns/shattered-sea/pcs/players/<player>.md` Notes plus `vault/refs/lines-and-veils.md` before writing any roast line — a topic named there never becomes a joke, however funny (one wrong gag poisons the whole exercise). For a party-member jab, read that same file for the *targeted* PC's own player too, not only the speaker's — a joke safe for the speaking player can still land on a topic their target has veiled. Roast the character, never the player: the target is what the PC did in fiction, not how the player played — this holds for party-member jabs exactly as hard as self-roast. The DM is the sole exception, and only in the DM/campaign lane, because asking for the roast is the opt-in — and even then the target is the craft and the content, never the person outside the DM chair; lines-and-veils binds DM jokes exactly as hard as PC ones.

## Format

There are exactly three kinds of line in the script — nothing else:

- **Dialogue** — plain text, no markup, spoken *as* the character in the first person the whole way through, and nothing but *what* the character is saying. Never a character cue, name tag, or speaker label anywhere. Never third person about the character. Never a word about *how* the line is being delivered — no "I say this slowly," "I still say it the way I said it that night," "quietly," "I lower my voice," or any other reference to pace, tone, volume, or manner inside the dialogue text. How a line is performed is a direction's job, full stop; a dialogue line that narrates its own delivery is a critical error, not flavor. Never an instruction to the person reading the line, in-character or not — no "say this one clean, three times through," "don't trip," "here's a harder one," "it doesn't count unless it comes out clean twice running," "say that with your own tongue," or any other framing that talks to the performer instead of being the character talking. A tongue-twister is delivered as a line the character would just say — the actor already knows to read it aloud; the script never tells them to. No dialogue line is ever repeated verbatim anywhere in the script — every line is new text; reusing a line to compare deliveries is repetitive to read and cut entirely.
- **Direction** — a performance cue on its own line, wrapped in italicized parentheses: `*(sincerely)*`, `*(commandingly)*`, `*(whispered, like someone's sleeping)*`, `*(delighted, like you're seeing a puppy)*`, `*(fondly, to an old friend)*`. An adverb or bare emotion word is the default; a short comparison clause ("like X") is fine when it colors the read faster than the word alone would — but it stays about *how to say the line*, never *why the character feels it* (no backstory, no motive, no joke explanation — the player knows their own character better than the script does). No `direction:` label, no quotes.
- **Meta-note** — the sole, deliberate exception to "never break the fourth wall," and only in these two fixed spots: (1) the opening slate, exactly `*(as yourself)*` followed by a plain line `I am reading for <Character Name>`; (2) one permission note early in phase one, after the first couple of sincere lines and before the tongue-twisters, giving the actor room to lose the character without failing: `*(Try to stay as <Character Name> from now on, just keep going if you lose character and continue however you can)*`. Both are italicized parentheses like a Direction, addressed to the real human reader instead of a performance cue. Nowhere else in the script — not phase two, not the closing line — ever addresses the reader directly; every other italicized parenthetical is a Direction about the character's next line, not a note to the actor.
- Every line — Dialogue, Direction, or Meta-note — sits in its own paragraph, separated from the line before and after it by a blank line. A single newline with no blank line collapses into the same paragraph when rendered, merging a Direction into its Dialogue as one run-on line instead of two — always leave the blank line, including between a Direction/Meta-note and the line it precedes.
- No slugs, no section labels, no beat names anywhere on the page (`SLATE`, `WARM-UP`, etc. are internal planning terms for the writer only — see § The two-phase arc — never printed).
- No header, no title, no note about production-aid status inside the script file's body beyond the two fixed meta-notes above — the file is the performed lines, full stop. (File-level bookkeeping for "not canon" lives in the filename/path convention and this skill's own contract, not in the text — see § Output.)

## The two-phase arc

The script has two phases, split at the midpoint by line count. Nothing on the page marks the split — the player only feels it.

**Phase one — straight introduction, no performance directions at all.** The character introduces themselves in their own voice: name, role, a couple of true, flattering details, plant one compliment the ending will later invert. Fold in the articulation workout naturally as part of the introduction — a count or roll-call the character would plausibly say (crew names, a tally, an inventory), plus 1-2 phonetically tricky lines built from ordinary, common words arranged for difficulty (alliteration, consonant clusters, quick repetition) — never obscure or technical vocabulary; see § The word ladder. Every line in this phase is undirected dialogue, with exactly two Meta-note exceptions in their fixed spots (the opening slate, and the permission note a few lines later) — no Direction parentheticals anywhere until the phase ends.

**Phase two — direction and roast begin together, one gets less subtle every line.** Starting at the midpoint, every dialogue line is preceded by its own Direction. Nothing repeats from phase one or within phase two — each line is fresh text. Across this phase, cover: contrasting emotional colors (calm, furious, terrified, delighted, sarcastic, commanding, persuasive — spread across distinct lines, not the same line seven times), and the dynamic extremes (one line whispered, one shouted, one held on a long sustained tone). The self-roast rides these lines, starting deniable and getting progressively more overt — the first directed line could still be read as sincere; the last few can't. The party jabs ride alongside it, one per party member, spread across distinct lines rather than clustered — light and affectionate throughout, they don't escalate the way the self-roast does (a jab at a friend stays a jab; only the self-roast gets less deniable). Partway through phase two, one longer passage has the character earnestly defend the exact things being roasted, oblivious to how it sounds — sincere delivery is the whole joke, never a wink. The very last line of the script is dialogue (preceded by its Direction, per phase two's rule) — nothing follows it, and no Meta-note ever appears this late. It calls back to phase one's opening and, now overtly, rates the character.

## The word ladder

Alongside the escalating roast runs a pronunciation difficulty ramp, but it's built from ordinary words arranged for articulation difficulty, never from invented jargon or obscure vocabulary — this exercise tests verbal flexibility and range, not vocabulary. Use alliteration, consonant clusters, quick repeated sounds, and the character's own proper nouns (names, places, ship names off their page) — real words a normal reader already knows, just arranged to be hard to say cleanly at speed. Each trap from phase one's twisters onward is strictly harder to say than the last; by the closing lines the player is landing genuinely difficult constructions. A trap that's easier than an earlier one breaks the ramp. Coin nothing that isn't a real, common word or the character's own canon proper nouns — no fabricated compound jargon. Every twister is just the character talking — a roll-call, a boast, a habit, a rant — never introduced, framed, or counted off first ("say this three times," "here's a harder one"); see § Format's ban on instructing the performer.

## The DM lane rides the same arc

When the DM/campaign lane is active, it layers onto the same two-phase arc — never a separate section, never a separate voice. DM jabs land inside the dialogue itself, never the direction. The signature defense passage may have the character earnestly defend the campaign's own wobbles as features ("in MY city the sewers have TWO official stat blocks, for redundancy"), and the closing line may rate the campaign alongside the character. Escalation matches the PC arc: deniable right after the midpoint, overt by the close. The word ladder and full voice-profile coverage stay intact — a DM jab that displaces either is cut, not squeezed in.

## Keep the con invisible on the page

Never add a "roast" or "gotcha" label anywhere, and never flag escalation in a dialogue line or a Direction ("this one's a joke," "you'll see why in a second") — the two fixed Meta-notes never mention the roast either, only character-recovery permission. The drift lives entirely inside lines the character would actually say — Directions color delivery, they're never a place to hide a punchline. The player discovers it mid-read, performing it in their own voice, or the con is dead. Comedy layers on top of the voice-profile coverage, never replaces it — a line that's funny but drops its articulation/range/dynamics job is a failed line.

## Length

Target 2-5 minutes of spoken runtime, calibrated to average conversational speaking speed (~150 words/minute): roughly 300-750 words of dialogue (directions don't count toward runtime). Undershooting drops coverage; overshooting is the more common failure — a defense passage that runs several sentences per roasted fact blows past 5 minutes fast. If a draft runs long, trim clauses and tighten lines before cutting required coverage (an emotion, a dynamic extreme, the defense passage) — never skip required coverage to hit the target.

## Output

Print the finished script in the chat response as a ready-to-read block. Then save it verbatim to `vault/campaigns/shattered-sea/pcs/va-scripts/<name>-voice-script.md` — the vault-wide default location for every voice script, PC or non-PC lane. The file is the script text only, nothing else: no header line, no frontmatter, no `status`/`publish` fields. It never appears in recaps or the published site.

## Not for

NPC voice/audio generation → `npc-voice`. PC backstory/canon creation → `dnd5e-character-interview`.
