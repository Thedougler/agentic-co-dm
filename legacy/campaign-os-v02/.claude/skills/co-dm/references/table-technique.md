# At the table — running technique

Read for how to actually run a live beat, not just what to say. This is
craft advice this skill applies while giving the table its answer
(Workflow step 6) — it doesn't change what gets captured (Hard Rule 2
still applies to anything improvised here).

## Control the clock

Set scenes. Give players a situation to respond to, not an open void — "what
do you do?" should follow a clear choice, not an empty room. In combat: let
a player declare intent, pause, move on to the next turn. Skip the "anything
else?" that invites second-guessing a decision already made.

## Fast-forward

Not everything needs to play out in real time. Skip or compress:

- Shopping — handle it as a quick exchange, not a scene.
- Travel across town — cut straight to arrival.
- Familiar NPC greetings — get to the point of the scene.
- Collecting a routine reward — done, moving on.
- A rest — skip it unless something happens during it.

## Embrace the unprepared

- Let the party go beyond whatever was planned — a prepared scene is a
  starting point, not a boundary.
- Make their choices meaningful — a choice with no visible consequence
  teaches the table that choices don't matter.
- Slow down. Silence gives players room to act, and buys the DM time to
  think — it doesn't need to be filled immediately.
- Make extreme rolls count. A natural 20 is a narrative opportunity, not
  just a bigger number — same for a natural 1.

Nothing above changes what this skill captures or writes — an improvised
detail born from embracing the unprepared still gets its `## IMPROV` block
(SKILL.md Hard Rule 2), the same as any other improvised fact.
