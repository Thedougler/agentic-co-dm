# Workflow Detail

Element catalogs for each workflow step and the island audit checklist.
Read when building a specific step and you need the full menu of what to
consider.

## Step 1 — Load the current world

Establish:

- PC goals, loyalties, relationships, and declared interests;
- active factions and their agendas;
- unresolved hooks and consequences;
- relevant locations and available routes;
- active clocks, threats, promises, debts, and opportunities;
- recent world-state changes;
- canon status of every fact being reused.

Use compiled vault pages as planning inputs. Route transcript-to-canon work
through the repository's ingest workflow.

## Step 3 — Gravity sources

Connect the island to existing character and campaign material:

- goals;
- fears;
- loyalties;
- enemies;
- unanswered questions;
- promises;
- beliefs;
- desired resources;
- people they care about;
- opportunities they have already expressed interest in.

A strong island normally includes:

- at least one compelling reason to engage;
- a credible reason the party might delay or refuse;
- consequences that make either choice part of the fiction.

Hooks provide information, temptation, urgency, or opportunity. They do not
decide for the players.

## Step 4 — Force components

Give every load-bearing actor or faction:

- **Want**: what they are actively trying to accomplish;
- **Reason**: why it matters to them;
- **Constraint**: what limits or complicates them;
- **Leverage**: people, authority, knowledge, resources, terrain, or power
  they can use;
- **Next action**: what they do if nobody interferes;
- **Tell**: what observable evidence reveals their activity.

Include environmental, institutional, supernatural, or social forces when
they function as active pressures.

## Step 5 — Bridge types

Vary entry vector source:

- an NPC request;
- a rumor;
- visible consequences;
- faction outreach;
- geographic discovery;
- a PC relationship;
- intercepted information;
- a previous island's wake;
- an opportunity the PCs notice themselves.

Different bridges should reveal different information, relationships, risks,
or advantages. Leave space for player-created approaches.

## Step 6 — Affordance types

Record the pieces of the situation the PCs can manipulate:

- relationships;
- secrets;
- vulnerabilities;
- resources;
- authority;
- terrain;
- schedules;
- laws;
- rituals;
- physical properties;
- competing loyalties;
- incentives;
- fears;
- logistical dependencies;
- magical or technological constraints.

Write what these things **are** and what they **can do**. When an obstacle
appears to have only one viable answer, add another affordance or remove an
unnecessary gate.

## Step 7 — Tide tracking

A tide may track:

- faction progress;
- political escalation;
- investigation;
- construction;
- ritual completion;
- deteriorating relationships;
- environmental danger;
- pursuit;
- war;
- economic pressure;
- spreading information.

For each meaningful step record:

1. what causes advancement;
2. what changes;
3. what evidence becomes visible;
4. which opportunities appear or disappear.

Use a clock only when its state changes decisions.

## Step 8 — Beat candidates

Candidate beats may include:

- Hook;
- Development;
- Cliffhanger;
- Climax;
- Resolution.

Examples:

- **Development:** becomes available once the PCs gain the smuggler's trust.
- **Cliffhanger:** fires if the rival faction learns the exchange location.
- **Climax:** becomes possible when two major forces openly commit to
  incompatible goals.
- **Resolution:** records the consequences after the island's dramatic
  question is settled.

If constructing a formal Beat Chart, preserve its local pacing structure
while allowing player action to bypass, replace, reorder, or invalidate
prepared material. Recompose unused beats around the resulting world state
rather than preserving a planned itinerary.

## Step 9 — State types and consequence questions

Consider states such as:

- secured;
- lost;
- shared;
- destroyed;
- exposed;
- concealed;
- delayed;
- captured;
- escaped;
- allied;
- hostile;
- transformed;
- abandoned.

For each important state, ask:

- Which actors gain or lose leverage?
- What becomes newly true?
- What becomes impossible?
- Who learns about it?
- Which relationships change?
- Which clocks advance, stop, or appear?
- Which resources change hands?
- Which bridges open or close?

## Step 10 — Wake propagation targets

Consequences should propagate through:

- NPC relationships;
- faction agendas;
- regional politics;
- rumors;
- resources;
- reputation;
- laws;
- alliances;
- threats;
- future opportunities;
- other narrative islands.

Create or strengthen outgoing bridges where the fiction supports them.

## Step 12 — Island audit checklist

### Agency

- Can the PCs meaningfully refuse it?
- Can they leave before resolving it?
- Can they return later?
- Can they approach it in ways the prep did not predict?
- Can materially different decisions change the resulting state?

### Causality

- Is important content anchored to people, places, objects, or events in the
  fiction?
- If something moves, does an in-world actor or process move it?
- Do consequences follow from established causes?

### Activity

- Do major forces act without waiting for the party?
- Does the tide change the situation when ignored?
- Are visible signs produced as those changes occur?

### Connectivity

- Are there multiple ways to discover or enter the island?
- Can its outcomes affect other parts of the campaign?
- Are outgoing bridges consequences rather than authorial redirects?

### Character gravity

- Does engagement connect to actual PC interests?
- Would the same island still work if the PCs pursued a different current
  goal?
- Are hooks invitations rather than commands?

### Persistence

- Does the resulting state remain true after the session?
- Will a revisited island reflect earlier choices?
- Can its wake be compiled into the appropriate canon pages?

### Vault integrity

- Are existing facts linked instead of duplicated?
- Are dynamic lists represented dynamically?
- Are DM-only and player-known information separated according to repository
  conventions?
- Does the page conform to the current template and lint rules?
