# Extracting moves from the DM's own writing

The DM's own prose is the strongest evidence there is: it shows what they do when nobody is asking.
It is also uneven, and the profile inherits whatever gets recorded.

## Signature and tic

Every recurring pattern is one or the other.

**Signature** — a choice that carries meaning. It recurs where it is doing work, it survives being
pointed at, and removing it would flatten the passage.

**Tic** — an unconscious habit. It recurs everywhere including where it costs the passage, and the
DM does not defend it when asked.

Only signature is recorded. A tic written into the profile becomes law, and every future draft
reproduces a habit the DM had already grown out of.

Interview question 3 — *anything you would defend if an editor flagged it?* — settles most cases.
Where the DM has not ruled and the pattern is load-bearing, ask; where it is plainly a mechanical
error, it is a tic.

Craft and correctness are different axes. A comma splice that drives momentum through a run of
clauses is signature; the same splice sitting between two unrelated statements is a typo. Judge what
the sentence achieves, never the rule it breaks.

## What to look for

Read the whole sample before naming anything. Then work through:

- **Sentence rhythm** — where the long sentences fall and what they carry, where the short ones fall
  and what they land.
- **Narrative distance** — how close to the character, and whether it moves.
- **Narrator stance** — who is telling this, what they know, whether they address the reader, whether
  they are honest about themselves.
- **Humour placement** — what it follows and what it undercuts.
- **Interiority** — thought rendered directly, reported, or left to the body.
- **What is withheld** — names, outcomes, the contents of a gap the reader must cross.
- **Structure** — how sections open and close, how time jumps, what the headings do.
- **Specificity habits** — where exact numbers, brands, or counts appear, and what they anchor.

Each pattern gets one quoted example from the sample, kept with the entry. An entry with no quote is
an impression, and the next agent cannot check it.

## Age and range

Old writing shows the instincts, not the ceiling. Record the move, never the execution level — the
profile is a description of the voice, not a bar the voice may not exceed.

A partial sample gets recorded as partial in the source note. Silence about range reads as
completeness, and a later run treats one documented mode as the whole voice.

## Where samples came from

Samples handed over for a single extraction are often deleted afterwards. The source note carries the
provenance regardless — the work's title, its date, what was extracted, and that the file is gone.
Provenance that lives only in a deleted file cannot be re-checked, and every entry sourced from it
becomes unverifiable.
