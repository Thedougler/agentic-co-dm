# Spoken Standard

## Spoken-Language Standard

Write for the ear. This standard governs player-facing prose the DM
speaks aloud. Written narrative (`vault/stories/**`) follows its own
rhythm rules (`vault/refs/stories/prose-aesthetic.md`).

Assume:

- the DM may say it once,
- players cannot rewind,
- other information competes for attention,
- complex syntax degrades comprehension,
- a table may be noisy,
- players are preparing decisions while listening.

Therefore prefer:

- short sentences,
- concrete nouns,
- active verbs,
- ordinary syntax,
- one image per clause,
- deliberate repetition of important nouns,
- clear spatial relationships,
- memorable contrasts.

Avoid:

- nested subordinate clauses,
- long adjective chains,
- obscure synonyms,
- excessive proper nouns,
- lore exposition hidden inside descriptions,
- sentences requiring rereading,
- paragraph-scale metaphors.

Read difficult prose mentally aloud before finalizing.

If a sentence is easier to read than to hear, simplify it.

## Prose Length

No fixed word count supersedes function.

Brevity means no padding — it never means less specificity. Never cut a
concrete detail, an established identity, or an affordance to fit a
band; cut filler, restatement, and atmosphere that carries no
information. Prose that is short but vague fails; prose that runs long
because every sentence carries a distinct concrete fact passes.

### Micro beat

1 sentence.

Use for:

- consequence,
- clue,
- reaction,
- tactical change.

### Standard spoken beat

1–3 sentences.

Use for most live narration.

### Major image

1 short paragraph.

Use sparingly.

### Extended prepared passage

Exceptional.

Only use when:

- exact language matters,
- interruption is unlikely,
- the content itself is the event,
- the DM explicitly wants it.

If prose can be replaced by a smaller scene model without losing value, prefer the scene model.

## Precision Over Volume

When players are confused, do not automatically add more description.

Often the problem is insufficient precision.

Weak:

> There are several passages and structures around the chamber.

Better:

> Three ways out: the stairs you entered by, an iron door beside the altar, and a flooded tunnel beneath the balcony.

Twenty extra atmospheric words would not improve the first version.
