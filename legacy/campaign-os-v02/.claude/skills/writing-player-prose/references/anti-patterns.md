# Anti-Patterns

## The Novel Paragraph

Long prose describing everything before players can act.

**Failure:** listeners forget details and lose agency.

## The Five-Senses Checklist

One detail from every sense whether useful or not.

**Failure:** artificial, verbose, low information density.

## The Purple Fantasy Generator

Ornament replaces observation.

**Failure:** sounds generic while communicating little.

## The Camera Pan

Narration slowly traverses every object in spatial order.

**Failure:** physical completeness is mistaken for usable understanding.

## The Hidden Obvious Thing

The agent refuses to name something every character would instantly recognize.

**Failure:** creates fake mystery through communication friction.

## The Conclusion Theft

The narration interprets evidence for the player.

**Failure:** removes deduction.

## The Emotion Theft

The narration dictates a voluntary PC emotional response.

**Failure:** removes characterization.

## The Predetermined Reaction

> Shocked, you draw your weapons.

**Failure:** narrates player decision.

## The Lore Ambush

A location introduction becomes a history lecture.

**Failure:** prevents play from beginning.

## The Spotlight Accident

One mundane clue gets disproportionate descriptive emphasis solely because the agent knows it matters.

**Failure:** metagame signaling.

## The Infinite Continuation

The narration keeps speaking after players have enough information to respond.

**Failure:** DM occupies the conversational space where play should occur.

## The Mechanical Dead End

> Miss. Next turn.

**Failure:** repeated mechanical outputs disconnect the combat from its fictional state.

## The Cinematic Cutscene

The prose decides outcomes because they would make a dramatic scene.

**Failure:** incompatible with sandbox agency.
