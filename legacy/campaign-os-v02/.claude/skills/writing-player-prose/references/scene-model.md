# Scene Model

## Default Doctrine: Prepare the Scene Model, Not a Script

Do **not** default to long boxed text.

Prepared narration assumes a specific:

- arrival,
- pace,
- emotional state,
- viewpoint,
- sequence,
- preceding event,
- degree of urgency,
- player attention,
- dramatic beat.

Sandbox play frequently invalidates those assumptions.

Therefore, when creating campaign material, default to structured narration support.

For example:

```markdown
## Player-Facing

**Immediate read:** Flooded moon-temple; black waist-deep water; unnatural stillness.

**Apparent**
- Bronze doors stand open beneath the waterline.
- Pale votive candles burn while submerged.
- Something recently disturbed the silt behind the altar.

**Actionable**
- Broken stairs reach the balcony.
- The altar provides substantial cover.
- A northern arch continues deeper underground.

**Recognizable**
- Selûnite characters recognize the iconography as pre-Schism.
- Anyone familiar with undead recognizes the lights as resembling grave-lights.

**Pressure**
- Ripples begin spreading from behind the altar.

**Tone**
Stillness → intrusion → awakening.
```

This gives the DM ingredients for narration appropriate to the actual moment.

Use finished spoken prose only when there is a reason the exact language matters.

## When to Use Finished Prose

Generate polished player-facing prose when the requested artifact specifically benefits from language ready to speak, such as:

- an opening image,
- an arrival beat,
- a revelation,
- a dream or vision,
- a ritual declaration,
- a villain's prepared address,
- a prophecy,
- a sensory transition,
- an important environmental transformation,
- a concise scene reset,
- a climatic image,
- a memorable final line,
- an epilogue fragment,
- short read-aloud text explicitly requested by the DM.

Even then, keep it adaptable.

Prefer **one strong paragraph or several short spoken units** over a speech-sized block.

## Player-Facing Section Template

Use where substantial narration support is useful.

```markdown
## Player-Facing

**Immediate read:** <category anchor + defining condition>

### Apparent
- <what anyone present notices>
- <important spatial relationship>
- <defining environmental fact>

### Actionable
- <route / object / opportunity>
- <route / object / opportunity>

### Recognizable
- **<character or qualification>:** <automatic knowledge>
- **<character or qualification>:** <automatic knowledge>

### Pressure
- <what is changing or demanding response>

### Tone
<compact emotional trajectory or atmosphere guidance>

### Reincorporate
- <environmental detail to keep alive during play>
- <environmental detail to keep alive during play>
```

Do not include empty headings merely to satisfy the template.

## Optional Spoken Seed

If useful, add one compact example the DM can steal or paraphrase.

```markdown
**Spoken seed:** Black water fills the old shrine to your waist. The candles beneath it are still burning, and something has just moved behind the altar.
```

The seed is inspiration, not a required script.

## Narrative Islands

When material belongs to a prepared dramatic structure, player-facing prose should support entry from different directions.

For each important island, identify:

- invariant visual or emotional identity,
- current state variables,
- NPC goals,
- environmental truths,
- available approaches,
- entry conditions,
- escalation triggers,
- possible revelations,
- consequences.

Do not tie the island's identity to one opening paragraph.

The DM should be able to recognize and render the island even if players reach it:

- early,
- late,
- secretly,
- violently,
- socially,
- through an ally,
- from an unexpected direction.

## Human Runtime

The human DM is the runtime.

Agents do not perform live narration.

Agents prepare material from which an expert DM can immediately understand the situation and speak naturally.

Therefore optimize source documents for two simultaneous readers:

### Agent reader

The agent needs:

- explicit semantic structure,
- reliable distinctions between hidden and perceivable information,
- clear conditions,
- relationships between entities,
- reusable facts,
- low ambiguity,
- enough context to generate or revise prose correctly.

### Human DM

The DM needs:

- extremely fast scanning,
- strong information hierarchy,
- memorable phrasing,
- compact prompts,
- minimal explanation,
- immediate distinction between what is true and what players can know,
- easy improvisational handles,
- no requirement to read paragraphs aloud verbatim.

Do not sacrifice either audience.
