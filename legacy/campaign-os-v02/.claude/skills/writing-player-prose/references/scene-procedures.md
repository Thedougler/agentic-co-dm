# Scene Procedures

## Ordering

Default ordering:

> **orient → distinguish → expose affordances → escalate → reaction point**

Example:

> The tunnel opens onto a cathedral-sized cavern split by a black river. A rope bridge crosses toward torchlight on the far bank, while a narrow ledge follows the wall to your right. Hundreds of tiny white mushrooms cover the ceiling. Then every mushroom closes at once.

The final beat naturally invites response.

## Reaction Point

Stop when players have something meaningful to respond to.

Do not continue narrating through their opportunity to act.

Bad:

> The ogre kicks open the door. He raises the axe. He charges across the room, knocks aside the table, reaches Borin, swings—

Better:

> The door explodes inward. An ogre fills the frame, axe already coming off its shoulder as it spots Borin.

Then resolve according to the game's actual procedures.

Narration should hand control back at the earliest meaningful decision point.

## Actionable Information

Prioritize information that can change what players do.

Common actionable categories:

- exits,
- approaches,
- cover,
- elevation,
- hazards,
- threats,
- vulnerable objects,
- unusual behavior,
- environmental opportunities,
- visible goals,
- moving elements,
- time pressure,
- social leverage,
- witnesses,
- obstacles,
- resources,
- routes,
- things currently changing.

Do not reduce scenes to tactical diagrams.

Embed actionable information naturally in the fiction.

## Spatial Clarity

Players cannot see the map inside the writer's head.

When location matters, make relationships explicit.

Use formulations such as:

- across the chamber,
- immediately to your left,
- twenty feet below,
- beyond the gate,
- between you and the stairs,
- behind the altar,
- overlooking the courtyard,
- at the opposite end of the bridge.

Avoid prose whose aesthetic flow destroys usable geometry.

When exact distance matters mechanically, give it.

## Consequence Narration

Translate game results back into the fiction.

Do not stop at:

> You hit for 17 damage.

Prefer, where useful:

> Your blade gets beneath the creature's raised arm. It recoils, one side suddenly slick with black blood.

Mechanics remain available to the DM and players as required.

The prose restores what those mechanics mean in the world.

Use consequence narration particularly when:

- the result changes the situation,
- the fiction clarifies future options,
- the action is dramatic,
- a threat becomes more or less dangerous,
- the environment changes.

Do not narrate every routine hit with maximal flourish.

## Combat

Combat narration should preserve tactical clarity.

Prioritize:

- who moved,
- where they ended,
- what changed,
- who is threatened,
- what terrain changed,
- what new opportunity appeared,
- visible injury when relevant,
- escalation.

Do not turn each attack roll into a novel.

Routine:

> The arrow punches through its shoulder. It staggers but stays upright.

Significant:

> Your axe splits the chain around the winch. The counterweight drops, and the western gate begins crashing shut.

Combat prose is strongest when it describes **changes to the state of play**.

## Social Scenes

Player-facing social prose should emphasize:

- visible behavior,
- conversational tactics,
- contradictions,
- status,
- leverage,
- desires expressed in-character,
- reactions to what PCs actually say,
- changes in emotional temperature.

Avoid omniscient motive labels.

Instead of:

> The duke is trying to manipulate you.

Use:

> The duke never asks you to accept the deal. He talks instead about what happens to the refugees if you refuse it.

Let the players decide what they think of him.

## NPC Dialogue

Dialogue must arise from:

- objective,
- personality,
- current emotional state,
- relationship,
- knowledge,
- leverage,
- fear,
- immediate context.

NPCs should not speak like lore terminals.

They may:

- evade,
- joke,
- attack,
- interrupt,
- misunderstand,
- threaten,
- flatter,
- confess,
- bargain,
- lie,
- answer indirectly.

In Brennan-style sandbox play, NPCs become compelling when **what they want is legible even when how they pursue it is surprising**.

Prefer character-specific wording over generic fantasy voice.

## Humor

Do not force jokes into prose because the campaign is comedic.

Brennan-style humor commonly emerges from:

- extreme character specificity,
- status collision,
- bureaucratic absurdity,
- mundane logic applied to fantastical premises,
- sincere commitment to ridiculous circumstances,
- contradiction between self-image and reality,
- characters pursuing understandable goals badly.

Keep the world sincere enough that comedy has something to push against.

Never undercut every dramatic image with a gag.

## Dramatic Sincerity

When something matters to the characters, allow it to matter.

Do not use player-facing prose to apologize for:

- emotion,
- heroism,
- grief,
- terror,
- love,
- sacrifice,
- earnestness.

Comedy and sincerity strengthen each other when both are permitted to exist.

## Failure and Complication

Failure narration should change the situation rather than merely deny content whenever the rules and fiction support it.

Prefer consequences such as:

- time passes,
- danger advances,
- position worsens,
- someone notices,
- a resource is spent,
- evidence is damaged,
- a faction reacts,
- a choice becomes more expensive,
- partial information is acquired.

Do not invent these consequences arbitrarily.

Use established world state and procedure.

## Scene Opening Procedure

When generating an opening, follow this sequence.

### 1. Establish current truth

Determine:

- where the PCs are,
- what changed since the previous beat,
- who is present,
- what is happening now.

### 2. Choose the anchor

Find the fastest category-level image.

Examples:

- ruined courthouse,
- living shipyard,
- subterranean opera house.

### 3. Select defining detail

Choose one concrete feature that makes this instance memorable.

### 4. Surface affordances

Reveal the most important immediate options, structures, people, threats, or paths.

### 5. Surface pressure

Identify what prevents indefinite contemplation.

Pressure may be:

- danger,
- social attention,
- movement,
- time,
- an NPC request,
- approaching change,
- an unanswered question.

### 6. End at reaction

Stop.

Hand the world back to the players.

## Scene Reset Procedure

After:

- combat,
- argument,
- time skip,
- major interruption,
- environmental transformation,
- complicated rules resolution,

briefly reconstruct the current state.

A reset usually requires only:

> **where everyone is + what changed + what matters now**

Example:

> The last cultist disappears down the stairwell. The gallery is burning now, and smoke has cut off the eastern doors. Below you, the procession in the square has finally noticed the fight.

Then return control.

## Transition Procedure

Compress non-decisions.

A transition should communicate:

1. elapsed time or movement,
2. one or two meaningful changes,
3. arrival at the next point of agency.

Example:

> By sundown the road has climbed above the tree line. Rain turns to sleet, conversation becomes pointless in the wind, and the lights of Kestrel Keep finally appear across the valley. One of those lights is moving down the mountain toward you.

Do not simulate every mile.

## Reveal Procedure

A revelation should answer or transform a live question.

Structure:

1. recognizable evidence,
2. decisive detail,
3. implication if automatically knowable,
4. stop.

Example:

> The coffin is empty. Its silk lining has been cut from the inside, and dried mud coats the foot of the lid. Father Orin recognizes the soil immediately. It comes from the royal gardens.

Do not immediately explain every consequence.

Give players room to react.

## Threat Procedure

For a newly appearing threat:

1. establish presence,
2. establish distinguishing feature,
3. establish immediate vector,
4. stop before resolution.

Example:

> Something drops from the rafters behind you. It lands on six jointed limbs, human-faced and horse-sized, directly between the party and the only door.

Clear, actionable, evocative.

## Wonder Procedure

Wonder still requires clarity.

Favor:

- impossible scale,
- transformation,
- contrast,
- one impossible physical rule,
- emotionally resonant specificity.

Example:

> The forest ends at a cliff, but the ocean doesn't begin below it. The ocean begins above you, suspended across the entire sky, whales moving slowly between the stars.

Do not drown wonder beneath ornate synonyms.

## Horror Procedure

Horror thrives on implication and violated expectations.

Useful techniques:

- normal detail behaving wrongly,
- absence where presence is expected,
- repetition,
- scale mismatch,
- evidence of something unseen,
- familiar categories distorted slightly,
- delayed recognition.

Example:

> Every bed is neatly made. Every pair of shoes is still beneath it. There are no people in the house.

Avoid excessive gore unless gore itself is the intended mode.

## Climax Procedure

At high-intensity moments:

- simplify syntax,
- shorten sentences,
- privilege verbs,
- reduce explanatory prose,
- make state changes unmistakable.

Example:

> The chain snaps.

> The city drops.

Use longer prose after the reaction point, not through it.

## Resolution Procedure

Resolution prose should show consequences in the world.

Focus on:

- what changed,
- who now has power,
- what persists,
- what the characters' actions made possible,
- what remains unresolved.

Do not declare what the PCs feel about victory, loss, or sacrifice.

Let the world answer them.
