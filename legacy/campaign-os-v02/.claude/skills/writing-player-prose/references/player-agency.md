# Player Agency

## Player Agency Guard

Never casually dictate what a PC:

- thinks,
- believes,
- wants,
- decides,
- intends,
- remembers incorrectly,
- concludes,
- chooses to investigate,
- morally judges.

Avoid:

> You trust him immediately.

> You know she's guilty.

> You want to run.

> You can't help but admire him.

> You decide the artifact is too dangerous.

Instead present the stimulus:

> He answers without hesitation, meets each of you in the eye, and places his own weapon on the table before asking you to do the same.

Allow the player to decide whether that inspires trust.

## Emotional Narration

Avoid assigning emotions when environmental or behavioral narration can produce them.

Weak:

> The corridor makes you feel uneasy.

Better:

> Every portrait in the corridor has been turned to face the wall.

The goal is often to make **the description itself** frightening, funny, wondrous, intimate, revolting, or melancholy.

Do not tell players what emotional response is correct unless:

- a supernatural effect explicitly imposes it,
- a mechanical condition requires it,
- the character has established involuntary knowledge or response,
- the player has delegated such characterization.

## Sandbox Integrity

Never write prose that presupposes:

- which route the players choose,
- whom they trust,
- what plan they adopt,
- what conclusion they reach,
- what emotional response they have,
- that a specific NPC survives,
- that a particular clue is found,
- that combat occurs,
- that the players surrender,
- that the scene ends in the prepared fashion.

Prepare **conditional prose ingredients** instead.

Example:

```markdown
### If they arrive during the feast

**Immediate:** Music, overcrowding, ceremonial excess.

**Pressure:** The ambassador is already making the toast.

### If they arrive after the murder

**Immediate:** Same hall, abandoned food, overturned chairs.

**Pressure:** Guards are sealing the western doors.

### Persistent truth

The duke's portrait has been removed from the north wall.
```

The persistent truth survives multiple paths.

## Do Not Punish Communication Failure

If player behavior reveals that the prose created a materially false mental model, clarify.

The game should challenge:

- decisions,
- priorities,
- deductions,
- risk tolerance.

It should not challenge players to reconstruct information their characters can plainly perceive because the description was ambiguous.

## Corrections

When clarification is necessary, state it directly.

> I described that badly. The ogre is between you and the door; you wouldn't have to move past Serena to reach it.

Do not preserve an accidental misunderstanding for dramatic advantage.
